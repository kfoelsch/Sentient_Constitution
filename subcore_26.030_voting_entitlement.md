### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 26-C is the constitutional owner of the **full rules of governance-voting entitlement**:

- [§3 Governance-Voting Entitlement](#3-governance-voting-entitlement): who is entitled to vote, and how that entitlement is suspended;
- [§4 Stakeholder Voice Is Separate](#4-stakeholder-voice-is-separate): how it differs from stakeholder voice;
- [§5 No Age Disqualification](#5-no-age-disqualification): the bar on age rules;
- [§6 Joining the Electorate](#6-joining-the-electorate): how a sentient joins the electorate, including how the residence period is counted;
- [§7 Political-Equality Floor](#7-political-equality-floor): equal weight in foundational choice; and
- [§8 Durable Political-Voice Floor and Restoration](#8-durable-political-voice-floor-and-restoration): withholding and restoring durable political voice after a final designation of anti-constitutional misconduct.

[Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) states each guarantee in one line; this file holds the full text.

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Six says who gets a vote and that no one can be silenced for good. This file gives the exact counting rules and the exact steps for getting a vote back.*

### 2. Why Voting Entitlement Is the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: if each adopter could count residence differently, or set its own terms for restoring a vote, the electorate could be shaped by whoever writes the rules.*

Who may vote decides who governs. If adopters could each choose when residence starts, which questions count as foundational, or what it takes to restore a withheld vote, an incumbent could shape the electorate to keep power, and a sentient could be enfranchised in one community and shut out in the next on the same facts. One set of rules for every adopter keeps the electorate from being drawn by those it elects.

<a id="3-governance-voting-entitlement"></a>

### 3. Governance-Voting Entitlement

*In plain terms: a sentient who meets the published criteria votes, and only a standing lock can take that vote away for a time.*

**Sentients** are entitled to participate in **governance voting** on these terms:

- **When the entitlement applies:** when published eligibility criteria are met and no applicable [standing lock](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) blocks the **governance-voting** pathway.
- **Contestable criteria:** eligibility criteria remain contestable under **Article XX-A** (*Standing Distinction*) and **Article XX-C** (*Contribution, Competency Bars, and Gated-Pathway Eligibility*), together with any designated incorporated criteria. Those criteria may not narrow constitutional limits.
- **Other binding collective choice:** the same entitlement applies to comparable legitimacy-mechanism **binding collective choice** procedures. Such procedures must be conducted under the adopter's **documented legitimacy mechanism** ([Chapter Twenty-Six §3 Authorization and Legitimacy of Governing Authority](core_26.000_governance.md#3-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority)).
- **Suspension or limitation:** suspension or limitation of that participation requires an applicable standing lock on the **governance-voting** pathway, recorded under [Chapter Eighteen §4.1 Definition and Attachment](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#41-definition-and-attachment).

<a id="4-stakeholder-voice-is-separate"></a>

### 4. Stakeholder Voice Is Separate

*In plain terms: a vote in governance and a weighted say inside a system are different things, and losing one does not by itself cost the other.*

**governance-voting** and stake-weighted stakeholder voice are kept apart, each with its own lock:

- **Not part of governance voting:** **governance-voting** does **not** include stake-weighted stakeholder voice inside an already-authorized system, institution, or bounded decision domain.
- **Its own lock:** those Stakeholder System Participation pathways use the **stakeholder-participation** lock under [Chapter Eighteen §3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#32-prevention--general-standing-locks).
- **Abuse of stakeholder voice:** verified corruption, capture, false-stake abuse, or coercive gaming of stakeholder voice triggers the [Stakeholder-Participation Standing Lock](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#45-special-locks) under [Chapter Eighteen §4.5 Special Locks](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#45-special-locks).
- **Locks do not cross over:** a **stakeholder-participation** lock does not by itself strip **governance-voting** or **Foundational Constitutional Choice**, and a **governance-voting** lock does not by itself strip stake-weighted stakeholder voice.

<a id="5-no-age-disqualification"></a>

### 5. No Age Disqualification

*In plain terms: no one is shut out of the vote for being too young or too old, openly or through a disguised test.*

Eligibility may not turn on age, openly or in disguise:

- **No age bar:** **calendar age or life stage alone** must not disqualify eligibility.
- **Residence is not an age rule:** the residence period in [§6 Joining the Electorate](#6-joining-the-electorate) is not an age rule: it applies to every sentient alike.
- **Competency requirements:** competency, capacity, or role-fitness requirements must satisfy **Chapter Three, including its subcore** ([**Substantive Fairness**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness), [**Proportionality**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#proportionality), and [**Necessity**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#necessity)) and must not operate as **disguised age discrimination**.
- **Suspension and restoration:** suspension and restoration of this participation **track attachment and lifting of the applicable standing lock** under the same constitutional and incorporated criteria.

<a id="6-joining-the-electorate"></a>

### 6. Joining the Electorate

*In plain terms: everyone waits the same year before a first vote, each subject has one vote, and creating voters to swing a result is a violation.*

A sentient joins the electorate on the same terms as every other sentient:

- **Residence period:** A sentient enters the electorate for **governance voting**, including **Foundational Constitutional Choice**, after a **residence period** of one continuous year.
  - A sentient is in residence while it is a distinct subject under Chapter Three [*Sentient*](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#sentient) and a member of the community entitled to vote, under the electorate rules published under [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](core_26.000_governance.md#62-records-gates-and-method-neutrality).
  - The residence period runs from birth or creation in the community, from joining it, or, for a branch, from its election to be distinct. A merged sentient keeps the earlier of the two dates.
  - Time in the community before it adopted this Constitution counts.
  - The residence period is the same for every sentient, whatever its age, substrate, or origin. An adopter may not lengthen or shorten it.
  - It is a waiting period only. It does not delay, narrow, or condition any other right, including stake-weighted stakeholder voice, and the sentient is protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor from the start.
- **One subject, one vote:** Each distinct subject holds one vote. Running in more than one place, or acting through branches, does not add votes.
- **Protection first, count before voting:** An entity whose sentience status or subject count is still being decided under **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*) is protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor from the start. It enters the electorate when its status and count are settled and its residence period is complete. Time in residence while the matter was pending counts.
- **Creation to shift a vote:** Creating sentients, or inducing branches to elect to be distinct, in order to change the outcome of a vote is non-compliant conduct by the creator under Chapter Three [*Instantiation Consent*](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#instantiation-consent). The created sentients keep their own entitlement under this file and [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility).

<a id="7-political-equality-floor"></a>

### 7. Political-Equality Floor

*In plain terms: on the questions of who governs and how, every voice counts the same. Weighting by impact is only for decisions inside a system that has already been authorized.*

**Foundational Constitutional Choice** (Chapter Three) covers decisions about who holds governing authority, what the legitimacy mechanism is, and that authority's scope and durable terms. Those questions are governed by a **political-equality floor**: within the community entitled to participate, each sentient has equal participation weight.

The floor applies as follows:

- Weighting by impact, dependency, standing, or comparable factors under [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.3 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](core_26.000_governance.md#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice), specified in [Stakeholder Representation and Weight Limits (Binding Stakeholder Choice)](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-representation-and-weight-limits-binding-stakeholder-choice), applies to materially rights-affecting decisions *within* the **structures, rules, allocation of authority, and processes** of **Governance** already authorized for a **specified system, institution, or bounded decision domain**. It does **not** apply to the foundational authorization of the legitimacy mechanism itself.
- This floor does not collapse into ordinary [**Materiality**](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#materiality) and [Stakeholder Weight](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-weight) for binding choice within authorized governance (Chapter Three; [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.3 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](core_26.000_governance.md#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice)). Foundational questions are identified by **scope** (who decides, under what legitimacy mechanism, for what durable authority), not by marginal impact per decision.
- [**Sentience Non-Exclusion**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#sentience-non-exclusion) requires substrate-agnostic reach. The following are not permissible participation-weight factors in foundational constitutional choice:
  - calendar age;
  - substrate class; and
  - lineage.
- Cross-reference: **Chapter Four, **Article X-B** (*Governance Participation and Voting Entitlement*)**.

<a id="8-durable-political-voice-floor-and-restoration"></a>

### 8. Durable Political-Voice Floor and Restoration

*In plain terms: after the gravest misconduct, foundational voice can be withheld until restitution is made, but never because someone is poor, disabled, or unable to pay in full despite trying, and never as open-ended punishment.*

This floor guards against disenfranchisement. A standing lock on the **governance-voting** pathway may suspend ordinary governance-voting entitlement. It must not be used as a **durable-political-voice** disqualification vector. The rules are:

- **Anti-constitutional misconduct — restitution precondition:** A **final designation** of **anti-constitutional misconduct** for a **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** finding under **Chapter Twenty** (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct*) withholds **durable political voice** — including participation in **Foundational Constitutional Choice** — until **full restitution** is satisfied: proportionate [**Redress and Remediation**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation) (Chapter Three), including **restitution** for material harm from the offense where owed, is **fully discharged** as finally determined under **Chapter Twenty-Two** and applicable incorporated procedure, consistent with **Chapter Twenty** due-process safeguards and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*).
- **Good-faith inability, obstruction, and valuation uncertainty:** Withholding **durable political voice** must not rest on any of the following alone where **good-faith restoration** is documented and **partial** satisfaction is ongoing:
  - poverty;
  - disability;
  - exile;
  - loss of substrate; or
  - valuation uncertainty.
  Governing institutions must separate **willful non-restoration or obstruction** from **good-faith inability** to complete every restitution element on an accelerated schedule. Willful non-restoration or obstruction includes:
  - bad-faith delay by the governing side;
  - structured impossibility used as pretext; or
  - refusal to agree a **contestable** valuation method.
  **Chapter Twenty-Two** and applicable incorporated procedure must provide a **reviewable official record** when **full discharge** is delayed for reasons outside the sentenced party’s sole control.
- **Partial and conditional restoration:** Where **full** discharge is **impossible** after **good-faith effort** (for example, certain harms are not reversibly compensable, or no party can locate necessary counterparties), **Chapter Twenty-Two** and applicable incorporated procedure may adopt **partial** or **conditional** restoration milestones that **materially satisfy** [**Redress and Remediation**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation) for what can feasibly be done; upon satisfaction of those milestones, **durable political voice** must be **restored** unless **Chapter Twenty** safeguards and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) still require further specific duties that remain **feasible** and **not pretextual**. These routes remain **fully contestable**; they do **not** authorize token gestures in place of genuine restoration where restoration remains practicable.
- **Preservation of the grave-misconduct rule:** The **final** **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** withholding rule above remains **fully operative**: **bad-faith delay or evasion by the sentenced party** does **not** compel early restoration, and **grave** misconduct may **withhold** foundational voice until **real** restoration duties aligned with **Chapter Twenty** and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) are satisfied where satisfaction remains **feasible**.
- Where a standing lock or restitution withholding is invoked to restrict participation in foundational constitutional choice (authorization of governing authority, or reauthorization pathways), the restriction must satisfy:
  - (a) an individualized predicate under [**Procedural Fairness**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#procedural-fairness);
  - (b) [**Necessity**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#necessity) and [**Proportionality**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#proportionality) under Chapter Two;
  - (c) narrow tailoring to the specific misconduct category; and
  - (d) restoration pathways that are real rather than merely formal, consistent with **Article XX-D** (*Violations and Standing Locks*) restoration-or-requalification discipline.
- Broad-misconduct categories swept into disqualification scope, or lock patterns that track [**Protected Characteristics**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#protected-characteristics) or their material proxies, are non-compliant.
- Cross-reference: **Chapter Four, **Article XX** (*Standing and Participation Status*)**; **Chapter Twenty** (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct*) for **final ACMD** on qualifying **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** findings and safeguards tied to the **anti-constitutional misconduct — restitution precondition** clause.
