### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 26-C is the constitutional owner of the **standard detail of governance-voting entitlement**: how the residence period is counted, how foundational questions are told apart from ordinary binding choice under the political-equality floor, and the full rules on withholding and restoring durable political voice after a final designation of anti-constitutional misconduct. [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) states the guarantees: a one-year residence period that is the same for everyone, one vote per subject, equal weight in foundational choice, and no permanent loss of political voice.

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Six says who gets a vote and that no one can be silenced for good. This file gives the exact counting rules and the exact steps for getting a vote back.*

### 2. Why Voting Entitlement Is the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: if each adopter could count residence differently, or set its own terms for restoring a vote, the electorate could be shaped by whoever writes the rules.*

Who may vote decides who governs. If adopters could each choose when residence starts, which questions count as foundational, or what it takes to restore a withheld vote, an incumbent could shape the electorate to keep power, and a sentient could be enfranchised in one community and shut out in the next on the same facts. One set of rules for every adopter keeps the electorate from being drawn by those it elects.

<a id="3-residence-period"></a>

### 3. Residence Period

*In plain terms: the waiting period before a first vote is counted the same way for everyone, and it delays nothing but the vote itself.*

The residence period in [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) is counted as follows:

- A sentient is in residence while it is a distinct subject under Chapter Three [*Sentient*](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#sentient) and a member of the community entitled to vote, under the electorate rules published under [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](core_26.000_governance.md#62-records-gates-and-method-neutrality).
- The residence period runs from birth or creation in the community, from joining it, or, for a branch, from its election to be distinct. A merged sentient keeps the earlier of the two dates.
- Time in the community before it adopted this Constitution counts.
- The residence period is the same for every sentient, whatever its age, substrate, or origin. An adopter may not lengthen or shorten it.
- It is a waiting period only. It does not delay, narrow, or condition any other right, including stake-weighted stakeholder voice, and the sentient is protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor from the start.

<a id="4-political-equality-floor"></a>

### 4. Political-Equality Floor

*In plain terms: on the questions of who governs and how, every voice counts the same. Weighting by impact is only for decisions inside a system that has already been authorized.*

The political-equality floor in [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) applies as follows:

- Weighting by impact, dependency, standing, or comparable factors under [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.3 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](core_26.000_governance.md#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice), specified in [Stakeholder Representation and Weight Limits (Binding Stakeholder Choice)](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-representation-and-weight-limits-binding-stakeholder-choice), applies to materially rights-affecting decisions *within* the **structures, rules, allocation of authority, and processes** of **Governance** already authorized for a **specified system, institution, or bounded decision domain**. It does **not** apply to the foundational authorization of the legitimacy mechanism itself.
- This floor does not collapse into ordinary [**Materiality**](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#materiality) and [Stakeholder Weight](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-weight) for binding choice within authorized governance (Chapter Three; [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.3 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](core_26.000_governance.md#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice)). Foundational questions are identified by **scope** (who decides, under what legitimacy mechanism, for what durable authority), not by marginal impact per decision.
- [**Sentience Non-Exclusion**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#sentience-non-exclusion) requires substrate-agnostic reach. The following are not permissible participation-weight factors in foundational constitutional choice:
  - calendar age;
  - substrate class; and
  - lineage.
- Cross-reference: **Chapter Four, **Article X-B** (*Governance Participation and Voting Entitlement*)**.

<a id="5-restitution-precondition-and-restoration"></a>

### 5. Restitution Precondition and Restoration

*In plain terms: after the gravest misconduct, foundational voice can be withheld until restitution is made, but never because someone is poor, disabled, or unable to pay in full despite trying, and never as open-ended punishment.*

The restitution precondition in [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) is applied as follows:

- **Good-faith inability, obstruction, and valuation uncertainty:** Withholding **durable political voice** must not rest on any of the following alone where **good-faith restoration** is documented and **partial** satisfaction is ongoing:
  - poverty;
  - disability;
  - exile;
  - loss of substrate; or
  - valuation uncertainty.
  Governing institutions must separate **willful non-restoration or obstruction** from **good-faith inability** to complete every restitution element on an accelerated schedule. Willful non-restoration or obstruction includes:
  - bad-faith delay by the governing side;
  - structured impossibility used as pretext; or
  - refusal to agree a **contestable** valuation method.
  **Chapter Twenty-Two** and applicable incorporated procedure must provide a **reviewable official record** when **full discharge** is delayed for reasons outside the sentenced party’s sole control.
- **Partial and conditional restoration:** Where **full** discharge is **impossible** after **good-faith effort** (for example, certain harms are not reversibly compensable, or no party can locate necessary counterparties), **Chapter Twenty-Two** and applicable incorporated procedure may adopt **partial** or **conditional** restoration milestones that **materially satisfy** [**Redress and Remediation**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation) for what can feasibly be done; upon satisfaction of those milestones, **durable political voice** must be **restored** unless **Chapter Twenty** safeguards and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) still require further specific duties that remain **feasible** and **not pretextual**. These routes remain **fully contestable**; they do **not** authorize token gestures in place of genuine restoration where restoration remains practicable.
- **Preservation of the grave-misconduct rule:** The **final** **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** withholding rule of [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) remains **fully operative**: **bad-faith delay or evasion by the sentenced party** does **not** compel early restoration, and **grave** misconduct may **withhold** foundational voice until **real** restoration duties aligned with **Chapter Twenty** and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) are satisfied where satisfaction remains **feasible**.
- Where a standing lock or restitution withholding is invoked to restrict participation in foundational constitutional choice (authorization of governing authority, or reauthorization pathways), the restriction must satisfy:
  - (a) an individualized predicate under [**Procedural Fairness**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#procedural-fairness);
  - (b) [**Necessity**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#necessity) and [**Proportionality**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#proportionality) under Chapter Two;
  - (c) narrow tailoring to the specific misconduct category; and
  - (d) restoration pathways that are real rather than merely formal, consistent with **Article XX-D** (*Violations and Standing Locks*) restoration-or-requalification discipline.
- Broad-misconduct categories swept into disqualification scope, or lock patterns that track [**Protected Characteristics**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#protected-characteristics) or their material proxies, are non-compliant.
