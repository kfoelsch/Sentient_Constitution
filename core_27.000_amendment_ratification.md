Chapter Twenty-Seven is the constitutional owner of **procedural and custody validity** for amendment and adoption (Tests 2 through 4), review triggers, provisional effect, invalid-change handling, ratification documents, and amendment procedure requirements.


*In plain terms: this chapter is the procedural and paperwork spine for legitimate change — publish clearly, prove custody of the real text, let independent reviewers and the public contest it, and do not let rights-affecting updates snap irreversibly into place before those guardrails have actually run.*

This Constitution may be amended to improve protection, clarity, binding effect, or institutional fitness under changing conditions, subject to **Chapter Twenty-Five** (non-regression), **Chapter Twenty-Six** (supremacy and external-order interface), and the validity conditions in this chapter.
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: a change can be substantively fine and still fail because of how it was made. This chapter holds amendments to a procedure that is real: a published proposal, a clear chain of authority and custody, and a contest path that works.*

**Purpose.** Chapter Twenty-Seven states Tests 2 to 4 and the rules around them:

- the framing rules: heightened review for rights-affecting changes (**section 2**), the burden of justification and public traceability (**section 3**), and the validity floor (**section 4**);
- the three tests: procedural validity and publication integrity (**section 5**), authority-chain and custody validity (**section 6**), and contestability and independent review validity (**section 7**);
- what happens when review is needed or a change fails: review triggers (**section 8**), the provisional effect rule (**section 9**), and invalid-change handling and remediation continuity (**section 10**);
- how a body adopts and how it amends: ratification and adoption (**section 11**) and amendment procedure requirements (**section 12**); and
- the owner non-relocation rule for this chapter (**section 13**).

**Role.** The chapter is the procedural half of change validity:

- [Chapter Twenty-Five](core_25.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-five-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity) states Test 1, the substance test. Procedural steps cannot save a change that fails it (**section 4**).
- A body binds itself to this Constitution only by adopting through this chapter (**Chapter Twenty-Six section 6**).
- [Chapter Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_incorporation.md#chapter-twenty-eight-incorporation-bridge) holds custody, editions, and operative effect.

**Chapter scope.** The chapter covers **section 2** through **section 12**, and **section 13** states the owner non-relocation rule. Escalation wording in this chapter is routing, not a second accountability code. The chapter leaves:

- numeric Contribution and Violation slots to [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#chapter-sixteen-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it);
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designations to [Chapter Twenty-One](core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-one-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation); and
- substantive non-regression to Chapter Twenty-Five.

*In plain terms: when a change is declared invalid, treat it as void for constitutional purposes from that determination forward, spell out what must be rolled back and how **affected** **parties** get a fair remedy — and publish a time-bounded plan that restores protections and preserves evidence.*

Where a change is found invalid, it is treated as non-operative for constitutional supremacy purposes from the point of determination. Decision records must identify scope, affected acts, remediation obligations, and required restoration steps for materially affected parties.

On invalidity determination, adopters must publish a time-bounded remediation plan that:
- restores controlling prior protections or stricter lawful protections
- preserves evidence under [Evidence Preservation](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation) and audit trails
- provides contestable remedy pathways proportionate to impact and dependency

#### 11.1 Deliberate Adoption
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11](#11-ratification-and-adoption) ratification frame.
- Downstream: [§11.2](#112-adoption-document) document requirements; [§11.3](#113-joining-by-additional-parties) joining rules.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Four §2](core_24.000_governance.md#2-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority) (24.000 §2) documented legitimacy mechanism alongside deliberate adoption; [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_28.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority) (28.000 §4) (*who may adopt* — a steward bound by valid adoption is not themselves a qualifying adopter).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: binding adoption has to be explicit, recorded, and auditable to the scale of the system — quiet or partial "we kind of follow it" does not let an operator claim constitutional protection while dodging obligations, and a steward's own-conduct note is not adoption.*

This Constitution binds a steward, organization, federation of bodies, or jurisdiction only when adoption is deliberate, documented, and auditable. Auditability must meet a standard proportionate to system class, material impact, and dependency under **[CS-9.305](corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-9305-part-a-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [CS-9.310 — System Impact Classifications](corpus_systems/cs_09.310_system_impact_classifications.md#cs-9310-part-b-system-impact-classifications)**. Informal, partial, or undisclosed adoption is insufficient to claim constitutional protections as a shield while denying corresponding obligations.

A steward is bound when a qualifying adopter records a valid adoption document under this section and [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_28.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority); listing a steward as a party bound by adoption does not make that steward themselves a qualifying adopter.

When the adopter is a jurisdiction or polity, the recorded mechanism must include a published affected-sentient participation check — contested election, sortition, federated ratification, or another [Chapter Twenty-Four §2.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums](core_24.000_governance.md#22-eligibility-contested-selection-and-democratic-minimums)-compliant path. Silent board resolution, undisclosed executive document, or "we kind of follow it" practice is not enough for a polity to claim constitutional adoption. Organizations, system operators, and system-developer consortia remain able to adopt through the existing deliberate, documented, and auditable path without that polity participation check.

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

*In plain terms: keep a clear paper trail — who adopted, what scope, when it starts, who holds the master copy of the corpus — so nobody can launder authority through ambiguous version drift.*

Adopters must maintain an identifiable adoption document. Examples include **joining** by additional parties (with recorded assent), treaty or compact ratification, corporate or intergovernmental resolution, federated registry entry, or equivalent. The adoption document must state the adopting entity, scope of adoption, and effective date. It must:

- identify the custodian of the authoritative edition of this Constitution and its integrated implementation files.
- be consistent with document-control expectations in [README.md](README.md) and [Chapter Five *Corpus*](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#corpus) (authoritative corpus, edition identifier, custody chain).
- be consistent with **Corpus** in Chapter Five ([core_05.000_definitions_home.md](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#corpus)).
- where [Article I-D](core_06.000_rights_part_a.md#article-i-d-existential-risk-and-ecological-recovery-capacity) (*Existential Risk and Ecological Recovery Capacity*) is in adoption scope, name the reduction, cap, or interruption measures required by that Article in the adoption document or in incorporated implementation it pins — not a core-chosen numeric target.
