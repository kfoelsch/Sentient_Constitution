Chapter Seventeen is the constitutional owner of **contribution consequences**: the effect, if any, of verified contribution at Question 3. Its rules start from verified contribution Standing Records and end at the gated pathway outcomes that Chapter Nineteen states.
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this chapter asks what verified contribution may do. It may support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked. It never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Seventeen decides the effect, if any, of verified contribution at Question 3. It does so in two steps: recency and currentness, then competency bars and clearances for gated pathways that are not locked (**section 3**).

**Role:** The chapter is one of four that together own Question 3, with Chapters [Sixteen](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#chapter-sixteen-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows), [Eighteen](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#chapter-eighteen-violation-consequences--remedy-correction-and-locks), and [Nineteen](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#chapter-nineteen-final-standing-effect-restoration-and-enforcement):

- It decides from the contribution Standing Record only.
- It takes the decision order from **Chapter Sixteen section 4.4** (*Processing Order*). The contribution track and the violation track do not wait for each other.
- Its result meets the violation track only at the final standing effect in Chapter Nineteen, where a lock controls.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- recency and currentness (**section 3.1**); and
- competency bars and clearances (**section 3.2**).

The chapter leaves:

- the decision order to Chapter Sixteen;
- remedy, correction, and lock rules to Chapter Eighteen; and
- the final standing effect to Chapter Nineteen.

It points to them and does not restate them.


<a id="2-why-contribution-consequences-exist"></a>
### 2. Why Contribution Consequences Exist

*In plain terms: verified good work should count for something, but never as a way to buy past harm. This chapter exists to say what contribution may and may not do.*

A system that never recognizes contribution teaches that effort does not matter. A system that lets contribution cancel harm teaches that harm can be paid for. This chapter lets verified contribution support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked, and says plainly that it never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.

### 3. Contribution Consequences

*In plain terms: this section asks what verified contribution may do. It may support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked. It never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.*

This section starts from verified Chapter Fourteen contribution records that apply to the decision. From those Standing Records it asks two things:

- how current the contribution still is; and
- whether any competency clearance for an open gated pathway is met against its published bar.

**Independence:** past help does not set the remedy, the correction duties, the safeguards, or the locks under [§3 Violation, Correction, and Prevention](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#3-violation-correction-and-prevention) and [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#4-lock-design-and-enforcement). The one exception is help given after the violation, which can count as restitution under [§3.1.2 Remedy](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#312-remedy) and so reduces the remedy still owed. Help also cannot cancel or balance out a verified harm—that [no-offset rule](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#44-linked-standing-records-and-no-offset-bridge) from Chapter Fourteen still applies.

**Lock precedence:** an applicable standing lock under [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) and [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#4-lock-design-and-enforcement) controls. Neither recency nor a competency bar or clearance can open a blocked gated pathway, waive the Rights Floor ([§3.6 The Rights Floor Is Not Reduced](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#36-the-rights-floor-is-not-reduced)), erase a violation, or replace remedy or correction.

Decide contribution consequences in this order:

- Confirm recognition eligibility: recognition must be evidence-backed, contestable, reviewable, non-monopolistic, and available on equal standards to informal, unpaid, peer-organized, mutual-aid, maintenance, repair, teaching, and community-stewardship work.
  - Equal standards include separation of duties: under [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement) and [Chapter Fourteen §7.9 Informal and Small-Scope Records](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#79-informal-and-small-scope-records) the verify seat for informal work is met by any independent body with published authority to rely on the Standing Record, not by the formality of the verifying authority.
  - A bar that demands formal-institution verification an informal group cannot reach is the [§3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](#32-competency-bars-and-clearances) gatekeeping pattern.
- Apply recency and currentness ([§3.1 Recency and Currentness](#31-recency-and-currentness)).
- Apply competency bars and clearances ([§3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](#32-competency-bars-and-clearances)); where a lock blocks a gated pathway, no clearance operates on it.
- Hand off each gated pathway outcome to [§3 Final Standing Effect](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#3-final-standing-effect); restoration and reassessment remain under [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment).

<a id="31-recency-and-currentness"></a>
#### 3.1 Recency and Currentness

*In plain terms: how much does an older verified contribution still say about present competence and reliability? The system answers that automatically for Question 3 readiness. No one has to re-weigh ordinary contributions by hand. Manual review starts only when a contribution is contested. Recency does not rewrite Chapter Fifteen measurement, and time alone does not settle an unresolved violation.*

Recency is strictly a **Question 3 clearance and readiness input**. It changes no slot, measurement, or descriptor ([§3.3 Facts and Measurements Stay Fixed](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#33-facts-and-measurements-stay-fixed)), and it does not change a Chapter Fourteen contribution Standing Record or historical recognition.

For a current trust-, role-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-sensitive decision, the standing system must **automatically** calculate currentness weight for each applicable verified contribution. No additional manual approval is required for an uncontested contribution when verified inputs and the published formula (or a stricter published role-specific rule) produce a determinate result. The process must run seamlessly unless the contribution—or the inputs used to weight it—is contested, uncertain, or otherwise requires review under [§4 Automatic Integration, Review, and Continuity](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#4-automatic-integration-review-and-continuity).

Unless a more role-specific published rule is stricter and constitutionally valid, current standing weight uses a 10-year half-life, calculated by whole days:

`currentness weight = 2^(-days / 3652.5)`

Illustrative weights at whole-year ages (exact day counts may differ slightly):

| Age (years) | Currentness weight |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 2.000 |
| 5 | 0.707 |
| 10 | 0.500 |
| 15 | 0.354 |
| 20 | 0.250 |
| 25 | 0.177 |
| 30 | 0.125 |
| 35 | 0.088 |
| 40 | 0.063 |
| 45 | 0.044 |
| 50+ | none (historical only) |

The system applies the weight to each contribution separately. After 50 years, a contribution remains in the historical record but supplies no current clearance weight. Continuing or maintained contribution may be evidenced by current Standing Records rather than treated as a single old event. No recency rule applies to unresolved violation: passage of time alone does not correct, remedy, supersede, or resolve it.

When a contribution or its currentness inputs are contested, the system must still assemble and preserve the automatic calculation, route the contest for authorized review, and keep the result inspectable. Contest does not erase the historical contribution record.

The automatically calculated integration record displays, for each contribution used as a currentness input:

- which contribution record and version;
- the age or continuing-status basis used;
- the resulting currentness weight;
- whether the weight was produced automatically or after contest review;
- whether the contribution is historical-only for clearance purposes; and
- whether a stricter published role-specific rule replaced the default 10-year half-life for this contribution and, if so, which rule and version.

<a id="32-competency-bars-and-clearances"></a>
#### 3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances

*In plain terms: a competency bar is the published standard for a gated pathway. Competency clearance is the standing-effect result that says verified readiness meets that bar. Once clearance is in force and the gated pathway is open, it stays open by default. Some roles need periodic retesting; technical forums decide which ones. A real change in role-relevant capacity — for example after a stroke or a mental-health condition that impairs needed judgment — can trigger reassessment. Clearance never opens a gated pathway that a standing lock still blocks.*

A **competency bar** is the published, gated-pathway-specific qualification standard. Anyone affected must be able to see the rule, check how it was applied, and challenge it if it is wrong. In short, it states what readiness the role or access requires.

A **competency clearance** is the positive standing-effect result when verified readiness meets that bar. In short, it answers: does this sentient currently meet the bar for this gated pathway?

This subsection has four parts:

- [§3.2.1 What a Bar Requires](#321-what-a-bar-requires);
- [§3.2.2 Guarding against Gatekeeping](#322-guarding-against-gatekeeping);
- [§3.2.3 Continuity, Retesting, and Capacity Change](#323-continuity-retesting-and-capacity-change); and
- [§3.2.4 What the Clearance Record Must Say](#324-what-the-clearance-record-must-say).

<a id="321-what-a-bar-requires"></a>
##### 3.2.1 What a Bar Requires

*In plain terms: a bar says what readiness a gated pathway needs, shown by evidence that fits the role. It is only as demanding as the role's reach, others' reliance on it, and the foreseeable risk justify. Informal work can meet it as well as a formal credential can.*

A bar may require evidence of:

- current verified contribution;
- demonstrated skill;
- relevant experience;
- training;
- role-specific qualification; and
- observed reliability.

How hard the bar is must match:

- how much the role can affect others;
- how much others depend on it; and
- how serious the foreseeable risk is if the sentient is not ready.

**Examples.** A peer mutual-aid coordinator may need shown reliability in shared care work, not a formal degree. A water-system or critical-infrastructure operator may need role-specific training plus recent demonstrated skill. A forum panelist or certifier may need verified impartiality-relevant experience and current qualification for that gated pathway. Teaching, maintenance, repair, and community-stewardship records can support clearance when they show the same readiness a formal credential would.

<a id="322-guarding-against-gatekeeping"></a>
##### 3.2.2 Guarding against Gatekeeping

*In plain terms: a bar tests readiness; it does not screen sentients out. Formal and informal experience meet the same demonstrability standard, and the integration record must show how access, fit, and review were handled.*

A bar or clearance process must not become arbitrary gatekeeping, opaque credentialism, reputation substitution, social-prestige screening, insider sponsorship, formal-channel monopoly, or exclusion by inaccessible specialization. Equivalent demonstrability standards apply to formal and informal experience.

Every bar and clearance result must be designed so the integration record can identify:

- the gated pathway and its sensitivity;
- the published bar applied;
- skill and experience fit;
- role proportionality;
- currentness;
- contribution-to-role relevance;
- inclusive access;
- the review route; and
- the reassessment trigger.

The conclusion that a gated pathway is trust-sensitive is itself open to challenge:

- The reason must be published with the bar, in terms of the gated pathway's reach, how much others rely on it, and the foreseeable risk ([§3.2.1 What a Bar Requires](#321-what-a-bar-requires)). The adopter must also publish the list of gated pathways it treats as trust-sensitive.
- Any sentient the bar affects may challenge the conclusion that the gated pathway is trust-sensitive, and the bar itself, before an independent reviewer outside the body that set the bar. A conclusion that the published reason does not support is set aside, and the gated pathway is no longer trust-sensitive for the purposes of [Chapter Fourteen §4.2 Silence Is the Default](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#42-silence-is-the-default).
- No one may require clearances from several gated pathways as a joint condition of one other role, access, or standing. Doing so assembles effects across gated pathways into a comparable artifact, which [Chapter Nineteen §3.1 Anti-Aggregation of Gated-Pathway Effects](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#31-anti-aggregation-of-gated-pathway-effects) bars.

<a id="323-continuity-retesting-and-capacity-change"></a>
##### 3.2.3 Continuity, Retesting, and Capacity Change

*In plain terms: once clearance is granted and the gated pathway is open, it stays open by default. It is reopened, limited, or withdrawn only on published retesting, a real change in role-relevant capacity, a standing lock, or another reassessment trigger.*

**Default continuity.** Once competency clearance is granted and the affected gated pathway is open, clearance stays in force by default. That is not permanent entitlement. Published retesting, a material change in role-relevant capacity, an applicable standing lock, or another reassessment trigger under this section may reopen, limit, or withdraw clearance.

**Periodic retesting.** Some competencies require periodic retesting; others do not. Which gated pathways require retesting, how often, and by what method is set by published standards under [Technical Forum Domains](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#32-technical-forum-domains), including specialized technical forums within their lawful scope. Those standards must be role-proportionate, inspectable, and contestable.

**Material capacity change.** Clearance may also be reassessed when verified facts show a material change in the capacity the gated pathway actually requires — for example loss of motion or language after a stroke, or a mental-health condition that impairs judgment needed for that role. Reassessment must:

- target the role-relevant capacity at stake as it is now, not disability or health status as such, under [Chapter Two §3.1.5 Present Capability and Proactive Help](core_02.000_values_principles.md#315-present-capability-and-proactive-help);
- remain necessary, proportionate, open to review, and consistent with accessibility and anti-discrimination floors under Chapters Three and Four; and
- consider accommodation, support, or a narrower gated pathway before withdrawing clearance, where those options would preserve safe readiness.

<a id="324-what-the-clearance-record-must-say"></a>
##### 3.2.4 What the Clearance Record Must Say

*In plain terms: every clearance result must be readable on its face: what was decided, against which bar, on what evidence, who can contest it, and what would trigger reassessment.*

The integration record must say, in plain view, for each clearance result:

- **The result:**
  - whether clearance is granted, not granted, or granted only with named safeguards.
- **What it rests on:**
  - which published competency bar applied;
  - which verified contribution and other readiness inputs were used; and
  - how currentness under [§3.1 Recency and Currentness](#31-recency-and-currentness) affected the result.
- **How it continues and can be reviewed:**
  - whether the open clearance remains under default continuity or a published retesting schedule;
  - who may contest or review the bar or clearance, and by what route; and
  - what triggers reassessment, including material capacity change where applicable.

Gated pathway outcomes after bars and clearances are stated under [§3 Final Standing Effect](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#3-final-standing-effect).
