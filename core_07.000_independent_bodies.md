Chapter Seven is the constitutional owner of **the standard requirements for an Independent Body** and of **the list of the Independent Bodies this Constitution creates**.

<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this chapter sets the rules that keep certain public bodies free from pressure, and lists the bodies the Constitution creates, with what each one promises sentients.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Seven sets the standard requirements that make a body independent, and lists the Independent Bodies this Constitution creates.

**Role:** The chapter builds on [Chapter Six](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties): an Independent Body meets every Chapter Six requirement, and this chapter adds the protections a body needs when its job is to check, fund, measure, or judge others.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- why independent bodies exist (**section 2**);
- what makes a body independent: naming, the standard requirements, pattern evidence, and scaling (**section 3**); and
- the bodies this Constitution creates, each with its purpose, its one-line guarantees to sentients, and its subcore file (**section 4**).

<a id="2-why-independent-bodies-exist"></a>
### 2. Why Independent Bodies Exist

*In plain terms: some public jobs (keeping money sound, publishing honest findings, supervising banks) only work if the officials doing them cannot be leaned on. This chapter exists to make that independence real.*

Where an official's decision can be overturned by a phone call, the public gets the decision the caller wants. The jobs that most need to be trusted are the ones most tempting to capture. This chapter sets the rules every Independent Body follows (selection by lot, protected funding, open decisions, and no override) and lists the bodies this Constitution creates, with each one's promises to sentients. Each body's operating rules are in its subcore file.

### 3. What Makes a Body Independent

<br>

*In plain terms: some bodies exist to check, fund, measure, or judge others, so they only work if no one can lean on them. Whoever pays a body can steer it, so its funding is protected too, it keeps a reserve so that a political fight over a budget cannot shut it down, and any cut in hard times follows a rule published in advance. Its members are drawn by lot from everyone who passes a published competency test, so no one who wants to steer it can choose who sits on it. Because it often sees personal data, it publishes patterns about groups, never details about individuals, and never turns that data against those who gave it. Outside auditors check its books and its work, and anyone it harms can challenge it before a forum that is not itself affected. It decides in the open, with votes, minutes, and later transcripts published, and no one may order it what to do, even in an emergency. A small community may share or borrow such a body, but none of these protections shrink when it does. Rather than repeat the same protections in every chapter, this section sets them once. A chapter that creates such a body cites this section and adds only what is special to that body.*

**Naming a body:** A body is an **Independent Body** when this Constitution, or designated implementation text, names it as one by citing this section. The designating text states what the body does. It may add stronger requirements. It may not narrow this section.

**Standard requirements:** Each Independent Body must meet all of the following:

- **Outside control:** it sits outside the [Material Control Line](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#material-control-line) of every body or party it serves, funds, checks, measures, or decides about, and of the governing authority except for the budget powers that the designating text expressly gives and the duty to run the appointment draw.
- **No override:** no one may direct the body in how it carries out its duties, and no act of the governing authority or anyone else may suspend any requirement of this section, even in an emergency under [Chapter Six §8 Emergency and Urgent Action](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#8-emergency-and-urgent-action). Any emergency power that the designating text gives the body is its own, used by its own decision.
- **Appointment by sortition:** its decision-makers are chosen by lot from the sentients who pass its competency gate, not by election and not by the choice of any official, so that no one who wants to steer the body can pick who sits on it:
  - **Competency gate:** for each decision seat, the body publishes role criteria and qualification standards under [Subcore 26-E §3 Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways](subcore_26.050_role_holders_interpretive_bodies.md#3-role-criteria-appointment-standards-and-removal-pathways). A candidate passes the gate by meeting the [Chapter Seventeen §3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](core_17.000_contribution_consequences.md#32-competency-bars-and-clearances) bar for that seat, applied after the standing-lock check that the same subsection requires. The gate tests readiness only, and may not screen out by sponsorship, prestige, or anything else that [Subcore 17-A §4.2 Guarding against Gatekeeping](subcore_17.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#42-guarding-against-gatekeeping) bars;
  - **Open entry:** any sentient who passes the gate enters the pool by applying. No one nominates, sponsors, vets, or approves a candidate, and a refusal of entry states its reason and is open to challenge;
  - **Conflict screen:** before the draw, a candidate is set aside for a published reason if they hold a [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) in the body's work, hold a seat that *No dual roles* below bars, or are inside a cooling-off period under [**Article XIII-C**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*). The screen is open to challenge;
  - **The draw:** the draw meets the sortition integrity requirement of [Chapter Twenty-Six §3.1 Mechanism Families, Auditability, and Pluralism](core_26.000_governance.md#31-mechanism-families-auditability-and-pluralism). Its method and random source are published before the pool closes, the draw is open to observers, anyone can rerun it and get the same result, and the pool size and the result are published together;
  - **Balance:** the draw may be stratified so that the body reflects the fields or places it serves, but only by criteria published before the pool opens;
  - **Thin pool:** where fewer candidates pass than a published minimum (by default, three for each open seat), the body widens and reopens the call. It may not lower the gate or appoint someone directly, and the seat is covered in the meantime under [Chapter Six §6 Published Placement, Vacancy, and Substitution](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-published-placement-vacancy-and-substitution);
  - **Terms:** terms are fixed, staggered so that only part of the body changes at once, and not renewable. A former holder may re-enter the pool only after one full term has passed;
  - **Vacancy:** a seat that falls vacant is filled by a fresh draw from the current pool, never by appointment;
  - **No veto:** neither the governing authority nor any body or party the body serves, funds, checks, measures, or decides about may veto, confirm, reorder, or delay a drawn candidate. Any appointment power that the designating text gives the governing authority is a duty to run the draw by the published rule and nothing more;
  - **Leadership:** where the body has a head or chair, the drawn members choose one of themselves by a published rule, or by lot among themselves;
  - **Other methods:** a designating text may use another method for a seat only where it publishes why sortition cannot fill that seat, such as a qualified pool too small to draw from, and the method it uses instead is published, contested, and rotatable appointment under [Chapter Twenty-Six §3.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums](core_26.000_governance.md#32-eligibility-contested-selection-and-democratic-minimums) that still meets every other requirement in this list.
- **Removal:** a decision-maker may be removed mid-term only for a published cause, through a removal pathway under [Subcore 26-E §3 Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways](subcore_26.050_role_holders_interpretive_bodies.md#3-role-criteria-appointment-standards-and-removal-pathways), and the removal is open to challenge. Removal, reassignment, or loss of pay because of a decision, finding, published figure, or dissent is barred.
- **Seats:** it publishes a seat map that places all four seats under [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement) and [Chapter Six §6 Published Placement, Vacancy, and Substitution](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-published-placement-vacancy-and-substitution) for each of its materially binding acts.
- **No dual roles:** no holder of a decision seat in the body may at the same time hold a decision seat in a body it funds, checks, or measures, or hold a [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) in a matter before it.
- **Protected funding:** its funding is adequate to its duties and is protected so that whoever pays it cannot steer it:
  - **Own track:** its funding floor is set on its own published track, by a published formula tied to its duties, separate from the budgets of the bodies it serves, funds, checks, or measures;
  - **Not set by those it checks:** no body it funds, checks, measures, or decides about may set, reduce, delay, or condition its funding, and where two Independent Bodies check or fund each other, neither sets the other's floor;
  - **Cycle protection:** its floor may not be reduced during a funding cycle except under a published shortfall rule that applies to every funded body alike, and a change to the formula takes effect no earlier than the next cycle;
  - **Reserve:** it has a reserve, held in an account in its name by the body itself or by the office that pays it, with a published target (by default, one quarter of its floor for a funding cycle). The reserve:
    - may be drawn only to keep the body's floor paid during a shortfall, a sudden rise in its workload, or a late budget;
    - may not be lent, pledged as security, used to reward or penalize anyone, or moved to any other purpose;
    - is held in safe and readily available form, never in assets issued by anyone the body funds, checks, measures, or decides about;
    - is restored after a draw before any increase above the floor; and
    - has its balance, target, and every draw published.
    Taking money from the reserve for any other purpose is interference under *Pattern evidence* below and an integrity trigger under [Subcore 4-B §3 Integrity Trigger Categories](subcore_04.060_integrity_triggers_gifts_funding.md#3-integrity-trigger-categories);
  - **Shortfalls:** where the money for its floor falls short, whoever pays it must:
    - publish the shortfall and its cause;
    - draw on the body's reserve before reducing its floor;
    - make any reduction by a rule published in advance that applies alike to every body paid from the same source, never case by case;
    - cut last the functions the designating text names as most urgent;
    - set an end date for each reduction; and
    - restore floors first, and refill reserves next, when money returns.
    A shortfall does not excuse the body's own deadlines, but it may cite the shortfall when it seeks an extension the governing rules allow;
  - **Funding integrity:** the funding-integrity floor of [Article XIII-D](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*) applies to whoever funds it, whether or not the body decides for others; and
  - **Self-funded bodies:** a body paid from its own operating income under a published budget meets this requirement where no one may seize, redirect, or condition that income except by a published rule set in advance. It builds its reserve from that income.
- **Information handling and privacy:** an Independent Body sees personal data that others cannot, so it publishes patterns, never individual sentients. It handles each data type under Subcore 4-A (*Data Types*), applies [**Article II-E**](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-ii-e-data-handling-and-network-dependency) (*Data Handling and Network Dependency*) and [**Article IX-B**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-ix-b-experiential-and-derived-data-rights) (*Experiential and Derived Data Rights*), and in addition:
  - **Only what its duties need:** it collects only the personal data its published duties need, keeps it no longer than a published retention period, and uses it only for the duty it was collected for;
  - **Not passed on:** it does not give personal data to the governing authority, to any body or party it serves, funds, checks, measures, or decides about, or to anyone else, except where its designating text or a published lawful exception requires it, and each such transfer is logged and its kind and number published;
  - **No identification:** it never publishes personal data in a form that identifies a sentient without that sentient's consent or a published lawful exception. What it publishes about its own acts, and about the bodies it funds, checks, measures, or decides about, is not personal data for this purpose;
  - **Not turned against the giver:** data a sentient gives the body for statistics, research, or measurement is never used as evidence against that sentient. Data gathered for its other duties may be used only within those duties and their published review routes;
  - **Withheld, but still checkable:** where data cannot be public, a qualified independent audit path still exists, and the body publishes that the data was withheld and why; and
  - **No shield:** privacy never hides the body's own acts. The trail rule in [Chapter Six §9 Act Records and Attributable Handoffs](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#9-act-records-and-attributable-handoffs) applies, and any unauthorized access to or release of personal data is reported to those affected and published.
- **Audit and challenge:** outsiders check its work, and anyone harmed by it can contest it, under [**Article XVII**](core_04.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*):
  - **Financial audit:** an independent financial auditor outside its Material Control Line audits its accounts each funding cycle, with a resource audit where shared resources are involved. Results go to the Integrity registrar under [CF-22.415 §8 Integrity Registrar](corpus_forum/cf_22.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-22415-8-integrity-registrar) and are published;
  - **Audit of its work:** an independent reviewer outside the team that did the work reviews the body's methods and its materially binding acts within each method-review interval (by default, five years), and the findings and the body's response are published;
  - **Challenge:** any sentient affected by a materially binding act of the body, or by its refusal or delay to act, may challenge it before an [Integrity Forum](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#35-integrity-forums), or before a [Technical Forum Domain](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#32-technical-forum-domains) where the dispute is purely technical. The designating text lists the challenge grounds special to the body, and may add challenge grounds but may not narrow this route;
  - **No self-judging:** where the forum that would hear a challenge asked for, is funded by, or is checked or measured by the act at issue, the matter routes to an independent backup forum under [Ways a Matter Moves Between Forums](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#ways-a-matter-moves-between-forums);
  - **Interim relief:** while a challenge is pending, the forum may pause the act, or restore what it took away, where the act foreseeably lets ongoing harm continue or would make redress useless;
  - **Remedy:** a successful challenge leads to a published correction, a redo of the act, or a new selection, as fits. The original stays visible beside its correction; and
  - **Scope of review:** the forum decides whether the body followed its rules. It does not put its own judgment in place of the body's within the discretion that the designating text gives.
- **Open decisions:** the body decides in the open:
  - **Calendar:** where it decides on a regular cycle, it publishes the calendar of decisions in advance and keeps to it, publishing any departure with reasons;
  - **Votes and reasons:** each materially binding decision is published when it is made, with its reasons and the vote of each decision-maker, and any dissent with its reasons;
  - **Minutes:** minutes of each decision meeting are published within the minutes window (by default, three weeks);
  - **Transcripts:** verbatim transcripts are published at the end of the transcript delay (by default, five years), with personal data removed under *Information handling and privacy* above; and
  - **Reports and hearings:** it publishes a report for each reporting year on its work, its accounts, and how it met its duties, and in each reporting year it appears at least once in an open public session of the governing authority to explain its decisions and answer questions.
- **Market-moving information:** no decision-maker, staff member, or contractor of the body may trade on, or pass to anyone else, non-public information from its work. Where its decisions or releases can move the price of assets, as the designating text states or as is plain from its duties, it also keeps:
  - **Quiet period:** its decision-makers do not speak in public about a pending decision or release for the pre-release quiet period before it (by default, one week) until it is published; and
  - **No holdings in what it moves:** its decision-makers and senior staff may not hold or trade individual assets whose price its decisions or releases directly move. They may hold broad funds cleared in advance, and their holdings and trades are disclosed and published.
- **Gifts and apparent conflicts:** its decision-makers and staff follow the gifts-and-benefits and apparent-conflict floors of [Article XIII-C](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*).

**Pattern evidence:** A removal, reassignment, pay change, or funding change that follows a decision, finding, or figure adverse to the party that made the change is evidence of interference. The party that made the change must show a lawful, published reason unrelated to that outcome.

**Scaling:** [Chapter Six §7 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#7-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting) applies. A small adopter may place an Independent Body's seats in fewer hands, share buildings, clerks, and systems with other bodies, or use another adopter's Independent Body, or its published work, by published agreement if that body meets this section. Even where hosting is merged or shared:

- the verify-or-authorize seat for the body's binding acts stays outside the body;
- its decision-makers stay separate from the bodies it serves, funds, checks, or measures, and from any Independent Body that funds, checks, or measures it;
- *Protected funding*, including the reserve, applies in full;
- *Appointment by sortition* applies. Where the adopter's own pool is too thin, it draws from a pool shared with other adopters, or opens its pool to candidates from outside who pass the same competency gate, before it may use *Other methods*;
- *Information handling and privacy* and *Audit and challenge* apply in full, and the auditors stay outside any shared host; and
- where an adopter uses another adopter's Independent Body, the published agreement keeps every sentient of the using adopter's challenge route and privacy protections, and says what the using adopter pays toward the body's floor and reserve.

<a id="4-the-bodies-this-constitution-creates"></a>
### 4. The Bodies This Constitution Creates

*In plain terms: this is the list of the Independent Bodies every adopter must have, with what each one promises. Each body's full operating rules are in its own subcore file.*

Each entry below names an Independent Body that this Constitution requires of every adopter. It states the body's purpose and, in one line each, every guarantee the body gives sentients directly, and it links the subcore file that holds the body's full operating rules. Each listed body meets [§3 What Makes a Body Independent](#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) in full.

- **Adding a body every adopter must have:** by amendment under [Chapter Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_amendment_ratification.md#chapter-twenty-eight-amendment-ratification-and-procedural-validity), with an entry here and a subcore file attached to this chapter.
- **A body one adopter creates for itself:** it follows [§3 What Makes a Body Independent](#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) when it is named as an Independent Body, and its operating rules are adopted implementation text.

No body is listed yet.
