<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: the Constitution asks whether its principles improve real lives. An independent office measures the results, shows how they connect to those principles, and produces official statistics. Its census counts the population without making the count a test of anyone's rights.*

**Purpose:** The Public Information Office measures whether real-world outcomes reflect this Constitution’s principles and gives sentients and public bodies reliable, accessible evidence for public and private decision-making, with uncertainty, limitations, and gaps visible.

**Role:** The Public Information Office is the Constitution’s **primary public measurer of constitutional results**. It maps evidence of real-world outcomes to the principles they serve and to the measurement categories in the [Preamble §2 Measurements Overview](core_00.000_preamble.md#2-measurements-overview). Subcore 7-D is the constitutional owner of this office’s **public constitutional measurement**, **official statistics**, **confidence ladder**, and **Office of the Census**. Where it sits:

- [Preamble §3.1 Using Measurements in Governance](core_00.000_preamble.md#31-using-measurements-in-governance) assigns shared technical standards to Technical Forum Domains and case decisions to the responsible forum. The office applies those standards where required; its public measurement does not decide a case or replace another body’s specific measurement duties.
- [Chapter Seven §3](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) (*What Makes a Body Independent*) protects the office from direction and sets its funding, privacy, and review safeguards.
- The [Subcore 7-B Public Policy Office](subcore_07.020_public_policy_office.md#subcore-7-b-public-policy-office--knowing-what-a-policy-will-do) evaluates policies, and the [Subcore 7-C Public Stewardship Office](subcore_07.030_public_stewardship_office.md#subcore-7-c-public-stewardship-office--looking-ahead-for-those-not-yet-here) looks ahead. Both may use this office's published statistics; neither directs its findings.
- The [Subcore 7-A Electoral Office](subcore_07.010_electoral_office.md#subcore-7-a-electoral-office) keeps the electorate roll. A census is a statistical count, not that roll.

**Chapter scope:** The office:

- measures constitutional results and maps them to the principles and measurement categories (§4.1 *Measuring Constitutional Results*);
- keeps a public statistical program and produces official statistics (§4 *Public Statistics and Methods*);
- maintains and applies a nine-rung confidence ladder (§4.2 *The Confidence Ladder*);
- publishes methods, limitations, uncertainty, releases, and corrections (§4 *Public Statistics and Methods*);
- protects information collected for its work (§5 *Information Handling and Privacy*);
- plans and conducts a recurring census through its Office of the Census (§6 *Office of the Census*); and
- reports on its work and provides routes to audit and challenge (§7 *Records, Audit, and Challenge*).

**Out of scope:** The office does not decide policy, voting entitlement, legal status, sentience status, standing, or a forum matter. It does not maintain the electorate roll, and the census is not a condition of rights or standing.

<a id="2-why-the-public-information-office-exists"></a>
### 2. Why the Public Information Office Exists

*In plain terms: numbers used to make public decisions must not depend on what those in power wish the numbers to say.*

The Constitution must be judged by what happens in the world, not only by what its text promises. The office gives sentients a public, independent account of those results and how they relate to constitutional principles. Public decisions and private choices need shared evidence that can be checked. Statistics can conceal as well as reveal: an average may hide groups left out, a changing definition may look like progress, and a delayed release may serve a decision-maker. The office publishes its methods, limits, and corrections independently, so that anyone can see what its numbers do and do not say. The census gives a picture of the population without making its categories a test of anyone's rights.

**Grounded in:**

- Preamble:
  - [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):
    - [Oversight](subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight): figures are produced outside the control of those whose work they describe;
    - [Accountability](subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability): methods and corrections can be checked;
    - [Participation](subcore_03.060_apex_participation_leg.md#participation): sentients have access to the same public evidence;
  - [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims): evidence about conditions and change helps sentients pursue flourishing and continuity;
- Chapter Four Articles:
  - [Article II-E](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-ii-e-data-handling-and-network-dependency) (*Data Handling and Network Dependency*);
  - [Article XVII](core_04.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*).

<a id="3-independence-and-placement"></a>
### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office is protected from direction, and its funding and decisions cannot be controlled by those who like or dislike its figures.*

The Public Information Office is an Independent Body and meets [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) in full, including appointment by lot, protected funding and reserve, information handling, audit, challenge, and scaling. Its published competency gate includes statistics, research methods, and data protection. Its Office of the Census operates within it under the same safeguards; it is not a separate Independent Body.

No governing authority, funder, or other body may direct the office's methods, findings, corrections, or release because of what a result shows. The office publishes its seat map and separates the production and independent checking of its work under [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement).

<a id="4-public-statistics-and-methods"></a>
### 4. Public Statistics and Methods

*In plain terms: the office tells everyone what it measures and how, releases results to everyone together, and does not quietly replace a wrong number.*

The office produces and publishes its statistics under these rules:

- **Program:** the office publishes the statistics it plans to produce, their purpose, the population or activity each covers, the expected frequency, and a release schedule. It states gaps where needed information is unavailable or a result would be unreliable.
- **Methods:** before a first release using a method, the office publishes its definitions, sources, collection and estimation methods, and known limits. A change states its reason and effective date, and compares old and new results where feasible, so a change in method is not mistaken for a change in conditions.
- **Findings:** each release identifies its reference period, sources, definitions, uncertainty, coverage, and material limitations. It distinguishes estimates from observed counts and describes uncertainty in a form suited to the figure. Where an aggregate could conceal meaningful differences, it publishes a breakdown where reliable and consistent with §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*); otherwise it says why it cannot.
- **Equal release:** results are released to everyone at the same published time. The office records a change to the release schedule and its reason. A result's content is not a reason to suppress it or favor a recipient.
- **Revisions and errors:** the office labels preliminary figures and revisions. It corrects errors openly, states their effect and reason, preserves the earlier version and correction history, and makes the correction accessible alongside the affected release.
- **Checkability:** the office makes its methods and supporting data available for checking to the extent consistent with §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*). Where protected data cannot be public, a qualified independent audit path remains available under Chapter Seven §3 (*Information handling and privacy*).

<a id="41-measuring-constitutional-results"></a>
#### 4.1 Measuring Constitutional Results

*In plain terms: the office shows what is happening, which constitutional principles the evidence speaks to, and what the evidence cannot yet tell us.*

The office maintains and publishes a constitutional measurement program. It measures actual conditions and outcomes, not merely whether a policy or institution claims to comply. It:

- **Maps the Constitution to evidence:** for each measure, identifies the relevant [Preamble §2 Measurements Overview](core_00.000_preamble.md#2-measurements-overview) category and subcategory, its Chapter Three measurement-family home, and the Chapter Two principle or principles it informs. It explains the connection between the evidence and the outcome the principle seeks.
- **Covers the full framework:** addresses Flourishing, Continuity, Participation, Oversight, Accountability, Timeliness, and Constitutional Performance. It states which outcomes are not yet measured or cannot reliably be measured, and why. Material stake guides the depth and priority of measurement.
- **Traces the results:** publishes the indicator or other evidence, source, population and period covered, method, uncertainty, and limitations alongside each mapping. It distinguishes observed outcomes from projections and from a proxy, explains what a proxy misses, and checks whether it still tracks the outcome it is used to represent. A convenient proxy cannot substitute for constitutional performance.
- **Makes differences visible:** shows changes over time and differences among affected groups where reliable and consistent with §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*). It identifies missing groups, inaccessible evidence, and other gaps rather than treating absence of evidence as success.
- **Keeps judgment open:** publishes the evidence and its interpretation so others can inspect and challenge them. A mapping does not itself decide whether a right was violated, confer or remove standing, or replace a forum decision. The office does not reduce the Rights Floor or trade a right away through an aggregate score.
- **Uses shared standards:** applies the standards Technical Forum Domains develop and maintain where the Constitution requires concrete measurement rules, under [Preamble §3.1 Using Measurements in Governance](core_00.000_preamble.md#31-using-measurements-in-governance). Other bodies retain the measurement and decision duties their own governing text assigns them.

The office includes the program's results, material gaps, and changes in its yearly report under §7 (*Records, Audit, and Challenge*), with links to the evidence and methods. It updates the mapping when evidence, methods, or constitutional text change, and preserves the history under §4 (*Public Statistics and Methods*).

<a id="42-the-confidence-ladder"></a>
#### 4.2 The Confidence Ladder

*In plain terms: a finding comes with a nine-step description of how well the evidence supports it. More independent confirmation, better methods, and stronger statistical evidence raise confidence; a number on its own cannot prove a claim.*

The office maintains and publishes a **confidence ladder**, numbered 1 (weakest) to 9 (strongest). It rates each substantive figure or finding it publishes against the specific claim, population, place, period, and conditions stated with it. The rating describes confidence in the evidence for that claim; it is not a probability that the claim is true, a measure of its importance, or a decision about a right. When a publication reports someone else's unrated claim, the office identifies the source and says that it is not rated; it does not present it as its own finding.

The ladder considers three dimensions together: **confirmation**, meaning the amount and independence of evidence; **quality**, meaning the suitability, execution, coverage, and review of the methods; and **statistical strength**, meaning precision and other evidence suited to the claim. Higher rungs call for stronger evidence across all three, assessed by the rules for the type of finding. An impressive result on one dimension cannot erase a material weakness in another.

| Rung | Name | What the evidence shows |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Anecdotal | An isolated account, judgment, or untested model; no systematic confirmation. |
| 2 | Indicative | An exploratory source or analysis suggests a possibility, but its methods, coverage, or uncertainty leave major questions. |
| 3 | Preliminary | One systematic source or study has a stated method and has been checked, but important limitations or uncertainty remain. |
| 4 | Provisional | A suitable method and useful evidence support the claim; independent confirmation or other material checks are still limited. |
| 5 | Moderate | At least two independent sources or studies support the claim, with material limitations identified and uncertainty appropriate to the conclusion. |
| 6 | Substantial | At least three independent sources or studies support the claim, sound methods predominate, and material alternatives and uncertainty have been examined. |
| 7 | Strong | Several independent sources or teams, including a meaningful replication or independent check, support the claim; a systematic assessment addresses contrary evidence and bias. |
| 8 | Robust | Multiple independent sources and methods or populations converge, with strong methods and a systematic assessment finding no unresolved material weakness in the claim. |
| 9 | Established | Several independent lines of evidence converge across methods and conditions, have held through repeated testing and review over time, and leave no unresolved material challenge to the stated claim. |

The office publishes criteria before applying them, including how the three dimensions are assessed for each type of evidence and how the rungs are distinguished. It applies shared technical standards where required under [Preamble §3.1 Using Measurements in Governance](core_00.000_preamble.md#31-using-measurements-in-governance). Where no suitable published criteria exist, it labels the finding **not yet rated**, explains the gap, and reports its uncertainty; it does not invent a rung. It may publish an early estimate when useful, clearly labeled as early and with the rating it has earned or the reason it is not yet rated.

<a id="43-applying-the-confidence-ladder"></a>
#### 4.3 Applying the Confidence Ladder

*In plain terms: the office checks whether sources really confirm one another, uses tests suited to the kind of evidence, and explains the rating so others can challenge it.*

The office applies the ladder as follows:

- **Independent confirmation:** a repeat analysis of the same data may check a method but is not an independent source. The office discloses shared data, funding, methods, and other dependencies; separate teams alone do not establish independence. Conflicts are disclosed and evaluated, rather than treated as proof that a result is wrong.
- **Quality and conflicting evidence:** the assessment considers design, coverage, bias, missing evidence, reproducibility, and whether results apply to the stated claim. It identifies material contrary findings and explains their effect on the rating; it may not ignore them to reach a higher rung.
- **Statistical strength:** report effect size and an uncertainty interval where applicable, with assumptions and relevant limits. Where significance tests are used, report their results and account for multiple tests and selective analysis. A threshold or a small p-value alone does not establish truth, importance, absence of bias, or a rung. A discovery made after looking through data is identified as exploratory until independently tested.
- **No effect:** a failure to find a statistically significant effect does not establish that there is no effect. A claim that an effect is absent or too small to matter states the relevant threshold and uses evidence precise enough to assess it.
- **Official statistics and census:** confirmation may come from an independent source or method; quality includes coverage, missingness, measurement error, and audit. Precision or an audited error estimate is assessed in the context of the figure. A full count is not automatically error-free, and repeated use of one source is not independent confirmation.
- **Forecasts:** a forecast states its assumptions and range. Its assessment considers a published record of past forecasts against outcomes, coverage of stated ranges, and comparison with a suitable simple benchmark. Repeating the same forecast is not independent confirmation; a new method without a track record is identified as such.
- **Nonexperimental evidence:** where experiments are impossible or unethical, suitable observational and other methods may support a high rung when their design, alternative explanations, uncertainty, and independent corroboration justify it. The office explains the basis rather than treating lack of an experiment as automatic disqualification.
- **Combined findings:** the office identifies material inputs and their dependencies. A weak input limits the result where it materially drives the conclusion; the office explains any reason the overall assessment differs from an input's rating.
- **Review and correction:** a qualified method reviewer in the independent check seat, outside the producing team, checks the rating and its reasons before release. The office publishes the evidence, reasons, material disagreements, and the criteria used, subject to §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*). It records changes to a rating and their reasons. A rating can be challenged under §7 (*Records, Audit, and Challenge*), and no one may steer it because of the result.

The rung accompanies the office's figure or finding when it is published, along with the uncertainty and limitations needed to interpret it. The ladder does not set a minimum rung for a decision; the governing rules for that decision control.

<a id="5-information-handling-and-privacy"></a>
### 5. Information Handling and Privacy

*In plain terms: collecting information for public statistics does not give anyone permission to expose it or use it against the sentient who gave it.*

The office applies [Subcore 4-A](subcore_04.050_data_types.md#subcore-4-a-data-types) (*Data Types*) to the data it handles, including the rule that the most protective applicable protections govern where more than one type fits. It also applies [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent), including *Information handling and privacy*, to the census and its other work. It collects only personal information its published duties need, uses it only for the duty for which it was collected, and keeps it no longer than a published retention period. It explains the purpose and handling of information when it collects it. Data a sentient gives for statistics, research, or measurement is never used as evidence against that sentient.

The office publishes no information that identifies a sentient directly or indirectly. Before releasing a breakdown or dataset, it checks for identification through small groups or combination with other available information, and withholds or adjusts the release where needed. It records transfers and reports unauthorized access or release as Chapter Seven §3 (*What Makes a Body Independent*) requires. The census is not the electorate roll, and census responses are not used to make or amend a roll entry.

<a id="6-office-of-the-census"></a>
### 6. Office of the Census

*In plain terms: the census seeks an accurate picture of the population, including sentients who are easily missed, while protecting the information they give.*

The Office of the Census plans and conducts the adopter's census on a published recurring schedule. It:

- **Design:** publishes in advance the population, reference date, questions, collection methods, accessibility arrangements, and timetable. It explains why the information is needed and tests questions and methods for clarity and accessibility.
- **Coverage:** takes reasonable steps to reach sentients who are hard to count and offers accessible ways to respond. Categories and methods are stated openly and do not exclude a sentient merely because of the kind of sentient it is. The office distinguishes the population intended to be counted from the responses it actually receives.
- **Quality:** reviews coverage after each census, assesses omissions and duplicate counts, and publishes aggregate results, coverage estimates, methods, uncertainty, and known limitations. It explains corrections and preserves their history under §4 (*Public Statistics and Methods*).
- **Protection:** collects only information needed for its stated statistical purposes and applies §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*). It publishes no identifiable responses.
- **No status decision:** being counted, omitted, or described in a category does not create, deny, or determine rights, legal status, or standing. The census does not decide sentience status or subject count.

<a id="7-records-audit-and-challenge"></a>
### 7. Records, Audit, and Challenge

*In plain terms: the office keeps a trail of its work, tells the public what went wrong, and can be checked or challenged by someone outside it.*

The office keeps its programs, methods, releases, revisions, and reasons for material decisions accessible as a public record, subject to §5 (*Information Handling and Privacy*). It reports each year on releases, census coverage work, material errors and corrections, and the results of independent audits. It identifies releases withheld or delayed and explains why without exposing protected information.

Independent audit and challenge follow [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) (*Audit and challenge*). The office publishes how to report an error or challenge its conduct, considers reports, and records the outcome. A challenge goes to a forum that is not the office and has no stake in the act challenged. A small adopter may share or use an office under Chapter Seven §3 (*Scaling*), without reducing its protections or a sentient's route to challenge.
