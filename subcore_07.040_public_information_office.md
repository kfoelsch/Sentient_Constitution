### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: everyone needs trustworthy public information to make decisions. An independent office produces the official statistics, and a census office within it counts the population without turning that count into a test of anyone's rights.*

The Public Information Office produces and publishes official statistics for public and private decision-making, as accurately as it can. It is the constitutional owner of official statistics and of the Office of the Census. It does not decide policy, determine standing, or decide a forum matter. The Public Policy Office evaluates policy; the Public Stewardship Office looks ahead. Both may use the statistics this office publishes.

### 2. Why the Office Exists

*In plain terms: numbers used to make public decisions must not depend on what those in power wish the numbers to say.*

Public decisions and private choices need shared evidence that can be checked. An independent producer publishes its methods and uncertainty so that mistakes can be found and corrected, including when the result is inconvenient to those in power.

### 3. Independence and Methods

*In plain terms: the office is protected from direction, and everyone can see how its figures were made and how certain they are.*

The Public Information Office is an Independent Body and meets [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) in full, including appointment, protected funding and reserve, information handling, audit, challenge, and scaling. Its published competency gate includes statistics, research methods, and data protection. Its Office of the Census operates within it under the same safeguards; it is not a separate Independent Body.

The office publishes its statistical program, methods, definitions, sources, limitations, uncertainty, and release schedule. It releases results to everyone at the same time. It labels estimates and revisions, keeps a public correction history, and corrects errors openly. It protects individual information under Chapter Seven §3 (*Information handling and privacy*); public material must not identify a sentient directly or indirectly. It makes its methods and data available for checking only to the extent consistent with those protections.

### 4. Office of the Census

*In plain terms: the census seeks an accurate picture of the population, including sentients who are easily missed, while protecting the information they give.*

The Office of the Census plans and conducts the adopter's census, publishes aggregate results and coverage estimates, and explains omissions, uncertainty, and corrections. It publishes in advance the population, reference date, questions, collection methods, accessibility arrangements, and timetable. It uses a published recurring schedule and reviews coverage after each census. It takes reasonable steps to reach sentients who are hard to count and offers accessible ways to respond. Categories and methods are stated openly and do not exclude a sentient merely because of the kind of sentient it is.

The census collects only information needed for its stated statistical purposes and applies Chapter Seven §3 (*Information handling and privacy*), including the bar on using information against the sentient who gave it. It publishes no identifiable responses. Being counted, omitted, or described in a category does not create, deny, or determine rights, legal status, or standing. The census is not an electoral roll; the Electoral Office retains its own duties under [Subcore 7-A](subcore_07.010_electoral_office.md#subcore-7-a-electoral-office) (*Electoral Office*).
