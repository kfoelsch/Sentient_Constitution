*In plain terms: **Article XXVII** (*Timely Retrospective Review and Restorative Alignment*) is the conflict-resolution and review Rights Floor, and the companion to **Article XXI** (*Justice After Verified Violation*). When sentients, systems, or institutions collide over constitutional Rights Floors, the answer is a fair, usable, contestable process — not indefinite delay or a permanent emergency. After emergencies or serious rights conflicts, systems must look back honestly, disclose what can be disclosed, resolve rights collisions on the Constitutional Collision Record, and keep restoration tied to real protection — on clocks that match what's at stake. [Timeliness](subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness-constitutional) binds each step: without it, the other duties hollow out while harm sits unresolved.*

This Article applies the [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad) to the resolution lifecycle after or alongside justice measures under **Article XXI** (*Justice After Verified Violation*) and emergency measures under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden), under the [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims) — **Flourishing** and **Continuity** — scaled to [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake):

- **oversight** through retrospective review and auditability (**Article XXVII-A** (*Retrospective Review and Disclosure*));
- **participation** through usable rights-collision and restorative-alignment pathways (**Article XXVII-B** (*Rights-Collision Procedure and Restorative Alignment*));
- **accountability** through reasoned records, proportionate remediation, and non-substitution of symbolic repair (**Article XXVII-B** (*Rights-Collision Procedure and Restorative Alignment*)).

[Timeliness](subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness-constitutional) cross-cuts and enforces the legs above: material problems must move within materiality-scaled windows under **Article XXVII-C** (*Timely Resolution and Anti-Delay Floor*). Formal participation, oversight, or accountability structures do not satisfy this Article if review, collision resolution, or remedy stalls without lawful justification ([Timeliness](subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness-constitutional-a), Chapter Three).

Disputes among sentients, systems, and institutions that affect constitutional Rights Floors must be resolvable through processes that are:
- transparent;
- fair;
- timely;
- contestable;
- practically usable.

Adjudication and dispute resolution in the constitutional sense are defined in **Chapter Three** as [*Adjudication and Dispute Resolution*](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#adjudication-and-dispute-resolution) and [*Timely Resolution*](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#timely-resolution), members of the [*Accountability, Contestability, Adjudication and Dispute Resolution, Collective Accountability Failure, and Force Majeure*](subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability) cluster.

*Article neighbors:*

- **Timeliness floor:** Resolution velocity, anti-delay discipline, and materiality-tier milestones are governed by **Article XXVII-C** (*Timely Resolution and Anti-Delay Floor*), implemented through [Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline).

Retrospective review of an emergency measure is governed by **Article XXVII-A** (*Retrospective Review and Disclosure*).
- **Justice after verified violation:** Governed by **Article XXI** (*Justice After Verified Violation*).

*In plain terms: after the emergency, look back honestly and publish what you find — with only narrow, time-limited confidentiality.*

This Article sets out the floors for post-emergency review, records, and disclosure:

- **Post-emergency retrospective review and disclosure:** After stabilization, emergency decisions and outcomes must undergo retrospective review.
- **Auditable official records:** Records must be auditable and must cover:
  - trigger conditions;
  - alternatives considered;
  - restrictions imposed;
  - harms prevented;
  - harms caused;
  - rollback or restoration performance;
  - lessons for recurrence reduction.
- **Disclosure:** Public or stakeholder-facing disclosure is required, subject to the [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle**](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle) for narrow, justified, and time-bounded confidentiality exceptions.
  - Each exception must identify:
    - its justification;
    - protected scope;
    - expiry or review cadence.

*In plain terms: rights conflicts must be resolved on the Constitutional Collision Record; restoration is preferred where appropriate but must not replace real protection. Voluntary public apology rules are governed by **Chapter Eighteen** §3.3 (*Voluntary Public Accountability Expression*) generally, and by **Chapter Twenty** §7.3 (*Voluntary Public Accountability Expression (Anti-Constitutional)*) for verified anti-constitutional misconduct.*

This Article sets out the rights-collision procedure and the place of restorative alignment:

- **Rights-collision procedure:** Where rights conflicts are material, forums and governing bodies must apply **Chapter Two §13.1.5** (*Rights-Collision Decision Test*), including the [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle**](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle).
  - Records must explicitly identify:
    - rights in tension;
    - rejected alternatives;
    - uncertainty treatment;
    - least-restrictive rationale;
    - review/reversal triggers.
- **Restorative alignment:** Where applicable, resolution must align with restorative and cooperative norms (**Article XI-F** (*Non-Imposition and Consent in Association*); *Restorative Justice*, **Chapter Three**).
  - Restorative pathways must not displace necessary protection, accountable attribution, or proportionate remediation where any of the following makes such restorative pathways inappropriate:
    - ongoing risk;
    - material power asymmetry;
    - non-consent by affected parties.
- **Voluntary public accountability expression:** Governed by [Chapter Eighteen §3.3](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#33-voluntary-public-accountability-expression) (*Voluntary Public Accountability Expression*) generally, and by [Chapter Twenty §7.3](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#73-voluntary-public-accountability-expression-anti-constitutional) (*Voluntary Public Accountability Expression (Anti-Constitutional)*) for verified anti-constitutional misconduct, subject to the rights-collision and restorative-alignment rules in this Article.

*In plain terms: constitutional problem resolution is a coordination and restoration system — quick, efficient, and just — not a warehouse where harm sits for years. Material disputes must move on bounded clocks through intake, verification, standing measurement, integration, and remedy. Moving quickly is not an excuse to skip fact-checking, punish the wrong party, offer a fix that does not fit the harm, or cut off challenge and appeal.*

This Article sets out the anti-delay floor for constitutional problem resolution:

- **Coordination purpose:** Constitutional justice under **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) operates through a **constitutional coordination and resolution system** — verify facts, measure standing, integrate consequences, and restore where feasible — not through indefinite adversarial delay, reputation scoring, or punishment for its own sake.
- **Three joint requirements:** Material resolution pathways must be **timely**, **efficient**, and **just** together:
  - **Timely** — bounded clocks scaled to [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) and harm urgency;
  - **Efficient** — [Constitutional Efficiency](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#constitutional-efficiency) in the sense of Chapter Two [§9.2 Constitutional Efficiency](core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#92-constitutional-efficiency) — real constitutional outcome per sentient time and shared effort consumed, not raw throughput or docket-clearing;
  - **Just** — **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) and **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*) remain fully applicable; moving quickly is not an excuse to skip checked facts ([Chapter Fourteen §4.5 Verified-Input Gate](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#45-verified-input-gate)), punish the wrong party, offer a fix that does not fit the harm, or cut off challenge and appeal.

  - pipeline-stage milestones;
  - integrated-resolution outer bounds;
  - overrun review; and

### Article XXVIII: Constitutional Evolution and Non-Entrenchment

<strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Definition:</span></strong> [Governance](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#governance) · [O](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#governance) · [M](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#governance-a) · [A](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#governance-a) · [C](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#governance-c)

*In plain terms: **Article XXVIII** (*Constitutional Evolution and Non-Entrenchment*) is the no-permanent-lock-in floor for how we govern ourselves. Rules for who decides, how sentients participate, how votes are weighted, and how money flows must keep proving they still serve the Constitution — they cannot freeze in place just because they are old, familiar, or convenient for those already in power. When the Constitution itself evolves, that change must happen in the open, with stated reasons — not through emergency shortcuts or hidden implementation tricks. Review must speed up when capture, exclusion, or failure signals show up.*

This Article states **constitutional floors** for governance evolution and non-entrenchment under the [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims):

- **Flourishing:** sentients retain meaningful voice in how governing structures are shaped, contested, and replaced — not locked out by arrangements that outlived their justification.
- **Continuity:** long-horizon systems stay stable enough to plan and recover, but do not treat today's wiring as permanent when it no longer aligns with constitutional requirements.

Legitimate pursuit runs through the [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad), scaled to [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake):

- **Participation:** in proposing, challenging, and replacing governance mechanisms when revalidation shows misalignment, exclusion, or degraded challenge rights.
- **Oversight:** through periodic revalidation, public criteria for continuation or replacement, and observable records when material constitutional change occurs.
- **Accountability:** those who control governance must answer when rules stay in place from habit or convenience alone — and when real constitutional change is rerouted through emergency, implementation, or custody labels to dodge **Rights-Floor minimums** or the rules for lawfully changing the Constitution.
- **Timeliness:** in scheduled revalidation and in accelerated review when capture pressure, stakeholder exclusion, challenge-right degradation, or material under-performance appears.

Governance rules, participation mechanisms, weighting models, and funding structures remain subject to the ongoing-justification discipline in [Chapter Two §18.4 Ongoing Justification](core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#184-ongoing-justification). **Article XXVIII-A** (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*) and **Article XXVIII-B** (*Periodic Revalidation and Transparent Change*) state the operative non-entrenchment, revalidation, and transparent-change floors.

### Article XXIX: Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining

<strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Definition:</span></strong> [Adoption Transition](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition) · [O](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition) · [M](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-a) · [A](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-a) · [C](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-c)

*In plain terms: **Article XXIX** (*Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining*) is the moving-day floor. Nobody can run a full set of Rights Floors on day one, because a right is only as real as the forums, oversight, trained staff, and public habits that enforce it, and those have to be built. So when a community shifts from legacy systems to constitutional operation, this Article asks for two different things. What the adopter must **not do** — make things worse, start new violations, destroy possible minds, hide the gap — binds from the start. What it must **deliver** — access, review, remedy — becomes enforceable step by step, scope by scope, as the institutions and culture that enforce it are verified ready. The Rights Floors are the fixed target the whole way, and the gap to them is measured, published, and shrinking on a dated path. Temporary Adoption Transition powers are allowed when truly needed, but they must stay temporary. A Adoption Transition that is going well shows it: interim powers keep getting smaller, and the Constitution's normal institutions and processes — forums, standing and challenge paths, oversight, and ordinary governance — keep working better and carrying more of the load. A stalled or failed Adoption Transition cannot quietly become the new normal, and interim rules cannot be used to rewrite the Constitution by the back door. If things break down, there must be a lawful off-ramp and a traceable path to re-baseline — not indefinite limbo — and that reset is not trustworthy if the machinery meant to tell the truth about systems and conduct keeps rubber-stamping misalignment or misreading real harm.*

This Article states **constitutional floors** for Adoption Transition governance, continuity, and re-baselining under the [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims):

- **Flourishing:** the **Rights-Floor minimums** are the fixed target throughout the Adoption Transition, enforceable as fast as the institutions and culture to enforce them are built, and sentients have a meaningful voice in how that build proceeds — Adoption Transition work is not a license to strip dignity, access, remedy, or agency for convenience.
- **Continuity:** shared systems remain governable, rights-preserving, and recoverable across the handoff — with interim arrangements yielding to increasingly functional constitutional institutions and processes, not hardening into durable exception authority or a lowered constitutional baseline.

Legitimate pursuit runs through the [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad), scaled to [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) — the greater the impact, dependence, and risk, the stronger each duty must be:

- **Participation:** affected stakeholders get a real voice at each Adoption Transition phase — including the chance to see and comment on the [gate criteria](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#gate-criteria) (the published tests a phase must pass before the plan moves on) before they are adopted, to see the evidence that they were met, and to challenge a gate decision; the chance to contest seizures or restrictions made during the Adoption Transition; and input when interim powers that touch rights or standing are renewed.
- **Oversight:** Adoption Transition must stay watchable and checkable — gates met and documented, interim authority independently reviewed, progress measured by shrinking interim scope, maturing constitutional operation, and verified Rights-Floor attainment, records auditable, and reasons published for delays, rollbacks, or baseline resets.
- **Accountability:** those running the Adoption Transition must answer for using it as cover — self-created delay, convenience shortcuts, pretextual enforcement, governance vacuum, lock-in after failed milestones, or quietly lowering constitutional floors through implementation misalignment.
- **Timeliness:** interim powers carry real expiry dates; the longer they run and the more rights they touch, the harder reauthorization must be; and stalled Adoption Transitions need lawful off-ramps before temporary limbo hardens into the new normal.

The Adoption Transition from legacy to constitutional operation must be phased, rights-advancing, time-bounded, and reviewable.

- **Progress is measured by:**
  - shrinking Adoption Transition authority;
  - increasingly functional constitutional institutions and processes; and
  - verified **Rights-Floor attainment** — the share of each floor actually delivered in practice, tracked against a published starting measure and a dated path to the full floor.
- **Progress is not measured by** elapsed time, Adoption Transition paperwork, or interim headcount alone.
- **Enforcement is built, not assumed:** The Constitution does not assume that every Rights Floor can be enforced on the effective date, because enforcement depends on institutions and a culture that must be built. It therefore requires that:
  - restraint duties bind from day one;
  - delivery duties become enforceable as readiness is verified; and
  - the gap between legacy operation and the full floor is measured, published, and closed on a dated path under **Article XXIX-A** (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*).
- **Restriction principles apply:** Adoption Transition measures apply the [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle**](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle) and the [**Constitutional No-Bypass Principle**](core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#151-constitutional-no-bypass-principle) where interim authority, remediation, deprivation, custody transfer, or comparable restriction is used.

Adoption Transition governance exists to secure continuity and non-regression. It must **not** create durable exception authority, de facto constitutional amendment, or practical lowering of the constitutional floor through implementation misalignment. **Articles XXIX-A** through **XXIX-D** state the operative phased-adoption, authority-limit, failure off-ramp, and Adoption Transition-scoped remediation floors.

*In plain terms: Adoption Transitions move forward by meeting real gates, not by clocks or pressure. What the adopter must not do binds from day one. What it must deliver becomes enforceable as the forums, oversight, capability, and culture to enforce it are verified ready — scope by scope, on a dated path. The gap, and the data showing it closing, are public.*

This Article sets out phased adoption and how the Rights-Floor minimums become enforceable as readiness is built:

- **Phased adoption and gate criteria:** Adoption Transition plans must be phased and reviewable. A [**gate**](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#gate) is the checkpoint between two phases, or before the plan widens the scope of adoption. [**Gate criteria**](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#gate-criteria) are the specific, measurable conditions the plan publishes in advance that must be shown met, on verified evidence, before the gate opens.
  - Phase advancement must rest on documented satisfaction of published gate criteria, including the phase's Rights-Floor attainment and enforcement-readiness milestones, shown by verified ledger data and not by assertion.
  - It must not rest on any of the following alone:
    - elapsed time;
    - convenience;
    - political pressure.
  - The criteria, the evidence that they were met, and the gate decision are published. Affected stakeholders may comment on the criteria before adoption and challenge the decision.
  - Criteria content, owner assignment, and verification artifacts are governed by [**CI-4.275**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-adoption-transition-governance-and-institutional-evolution) (*Adoption Transition governance and institutional evolution*) and [**CS-4.350 §3**](corpus_systems/cs_04.350_adoption_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md#cs-4350-3-gate-criteria-and-advancement-rules) (*Gate Criteria and Advancement Rules*).
- **Adoption Transition Record:** The adopter keeps its [Adoption Transition Record](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-record) from the day its adopting document takes effect, publishes it, and keeps it current. It holds the plan, each gate's criteria, result, and evidence, the Rights-Floor attainment ledger, the accountable owners, interim authority, and any off-ramps this Article requires, so that one published document shows the whole transition. It also carries, marked provisional, the entries the [Adoption Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-charter) will settle ([Chapter Twenty-Eight §12.4 Adoption Transition Record and Adoption Charter](core_28.000_amendment_ratification.md#124-adoption-transition-record-and-adoption-charter)): the forum authority assignment, the record custodian and the office-level contact for gatekeeper checks, and the levels of trust the adopter extends.
  - **Final gate:** The adopter does not pass the gate into the steady-state phase, and that phase does not begin, until the Adoption Charter is published with the provisional entries confirmed or replaced.
  - **Closing:** When the steady-state phase begins, the Adoption Transition Record is closed and archived with its full history. Closing it does not erase or hide any entry.
  - **Provisional means provisional:** a provisional entry is never presented as settled.
- **Rights-Floor adoption — restraint now, delivery as readiness is built:** The **Rights-Floor minimums** applied through **Article VI** (*Equal Basic Rights*) under the [Rights-Floor Minimums Principle](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#rights-floor-minimums-principle), together with any stronger domain-specific floors for survival, agency, non-discrimination, accessibility, auditability, and remedy that the affected context triggers, are the adopted target at every phase. A Rights Floor cannot be fully enforced until the institutions and culture that enforce it exist, and this Article does not pretend otherwise. It also does not let that fact lower the floor. Floors therefore bind in two ways, depending on the kind of duty:
  - **Restraint duties** — what the adopter and Adoption Transition authorities must not do — bind from the effective date, because honoring them requires no new institution:
    - **No knowing or avoidable regression:** no deliberate or avoidable lowering of any sentient's access to a Rights-Floor protection below its **starting measure**, and no Adoption Transition measure that makes a floor materially harder to exercise.
    - **No new violations:** new systems, policies, and practices, and material expansions of existing ones, must be designed to the floor from the start. The attainment path below covers inherited shortfalls only, not new conduct.
    - **No irreversible loss:** the preservation default below and the [Chapter Two §13.1 default interim posture](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#default-interim-posture) apply in full.
    - **No degrading, pretextual, discriminatory, or concealing conduct** in Adoption Transition measures, including everything **Article XXIX-D** (*Non-Compliant Property and Systems; Voluntary Turnover Incentives*) requires of Adoption Transition authorities, under the [Anti-Degrading-Process Principle](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#anti-degrading-process-principle).
    - **Honest reporting:** no floor may be represented as enforceable or attained, and no gap as closed, except on verified ledger data.

    Interim enforcement of restraint duties, before a forum is ready for an enforceability scope, is governed by [**CI-4.275 §4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-4-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--rights-floor-attainment-ledger-measures-verification-and-publication) (*Rights-Floor attainment ledger*).
  - **Delivery duties** — what must be provided, enabled, or remedied, such as access, accommodation, review, and redress — become **enforceable claims scope by scope as enforcement readiness is verified**. Before that, they bind as duties of construction: adopt the target, measure the starting point, build the enforcing institutions and supporting culture on a dated, resourced path, and give affected sentients the best available interim safeguards. A delivery duty that is not yet enforceable is an **attainment gap**, not a waiver, an exception, or a lowered target.
- **Enforcement readiness:** a Rights Floor becomes enforceable for a defined enforceability scope — a population, system class, setting, or region — when verified data show that the institutions, capability, and culture needed to enforce it are ready for that enforceability scope. Readiness criteria and their evidence are governed by [**CI-4.275 §4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-4-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--rights-floor-attainment-ledger-measures-verification-and-publication) (*Rights-Floor attainment ledger*).
  - **Declaration follows data:** enforceability for an enforceability scope is declared, with published reasons, by a party independent of the ledger owner once the verified criteria in the plan are met. Withholding the declaration after that point is self-created delay under **Article XXIX-B** (*Adoption Transition Authority Limits and Reauthorization*), and affected sentients may contest a declaration as premature or withheld. The declaration is due no later than the outer bound of the Tier that matches the enforceability scope's class (7 days for Class A, 14 for B, 30 for C, 60 for L, 90 for P) where the enforceability scope is a system class, and 14 days, the Tier B outer bound, for any other enforceability scope, counted from the day the verified criteria are met, as the data shows, whether or not the ledger owner has verified them yet; the verification step is itself due inside the intake target of the Tier that matches the enforceability scope's class, or Tier B for any other enforceability scope. Staging of scopes and contest procedure are governed by [**CI-4.275 §4.4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-44-enforcement-readiness-declarations) (*Enforcement-readiness declarations*).
  - **No narrowing:** once an enforceability scope is declared enforceable it stays enforceable. Loss of capacity afterward is an institutional failure handled under **Article XXIX-C** (*Failure Off-Ramps, Re-Baselining, and Traceability*) and the forum-continuity rules, not a quiet withdrawal of the right.
- **Rights-Floor attainment path:** Each Adoption Transition plan must carry an attainment path covering every Rights-Floor domain in scope.
  - **Starting measure:** each domain's starting coverage and practical usability are measured and published before the first gate that depends on that domain. A domain not yet measured is **unmeasured**, which is treated as unattained and as a measurement priority — never as a passing result.
  - **Fixed target:** the target is the full constitutional floor. A plan may not negotiate a lesser target, and re-baselining under **Article XXIX-C** (*Failure Off-Ramps, Re-Baselining, and Traceability*) moves dates and owners, not the target or the starting measure.
  - **Dated glide path:** each domain has an accountable owner, committed resources, dated milestones, and a final date by which the floor is enforceable for the full enforceability scope, scaled to the affected class and material stake.
  - **What an open gap is not:** an open attainment gap within an approved path is not a defense to a violation of any restraint duty, and it does not suspend [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (*Compliance, Violation, and Standing Model*) for such conduct.
  - **Ratchet:** a recorded attainment level may not go backward through the adopter's avoidable acts, and a decline is a failure where avoidable or unrecovered. Milestone dates move only through **Article XXIX-B** (*Adoption Transition Authority Limits and Reauthorization*) reauthorization or **Article XXIX-C** (*Failure Off-Ramps, Re-Baselining, and Traceability*) re-baselining.
  - **Outer limit:** a gap that passes a milestone without reauthorization is an Adoption Transition milestone failure and opens the ordinary path under [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (*Compliance, Violation, and Standing Model*). A gap that outlives its final attainment date, or is carried by repeated reauthorization without closing, is a failed Adoption Transition state that does not become the baseline by persistence. A gap that has become indefinite is treated as the permanent extinguishment the Rights-Floor Minimums Principle bars.
- **Priority-setting for Adoption Transition targets:** Adoption Transition work is directed first where it will reduce the most Rights-Floor exposure for the effort, and the plan publishes how it weighed the factors.
  - **Systems** are weighed on system class under the System Classification Record ([Subcore 8-A §6](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#6-system-impact-classifications) (*System Impact Classifications*)), degree of misalignment, and ease of alignment, together with the further factors in [**CS-4.350 §7**](corpus_systems/cs_04.350_adoption_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md#cs-4350-7-prioritizing-system-adoption-transition-targets) (*Prioritizing System Adoption Transition Targets*).
  - **Existing institutions** are weighed on institutional class and stewardship level ([CI-8.210](corpus_institutions/ci_08.210_classification_linked_institutional_obligations.md#ci-8210-classification-linked-institutional-obligations) (*Classification-linked institutional obligations*)), degree of misalignment, and ease of alignment — including legal authority to change, support, flexibility, and resources — together with the further factors in [**CI-4.275 §5**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-5-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--prioritizing-institutional-adoption-transition-targets) (*Prioritizing institutional Adoption Transition targets*).
  - **Guardrails** for both:
    - enabling institutions come first, because every other floor depends on them — contest and review intake, independent oversight and verification, protection against retaliation, and record-keeping — and then gaps touching survival, dignity, and irreversible harm;
    - ease and cost may order work within a priority group but may not move a system or institution out of a higher-exposure group;
    - the highest-exposure groups receive the strictest gate criteria, verification, and rollback readiness;
    - no institution may be the sole judge of its own priority or Adoption Transition readiness; and
    - a steward's or institution's non-cooperation routes the case to **Article XXIX-D** (*Non-Compliant Property and Systems; Voluntary Turnover Incentives*) and the institutional-failure rules, and does not lower its priority.

  Scoring method, groups, pilots, equity checks, and publication are governed by [**CS-4.350 §7**](corpus_systems/cs_04.350_adoption_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md#cs-4350-7-prioritizing-system-adoption-transition-targets) (*Prioritizing System Adoption Transition Targets*) and [**CI-4.275 §5**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-5-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--prioritizing-institutional-adoption-transition-targets) (*Prioritizing institutional Adoption Transition targets*) and must not narrow this Article.
- **Attainment is measured, not asserted:** attainment and readiness must be shown by a **Rights-Floor attainment ledger** that is independently verified, compared against lived-experience signals, and published live, with a major progress report at least every three months, negative results included. Measures, readiness criteria, verification levels, status rules, and publication mechanics are governed by [**CI-4.275 §4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-4-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--rights-floor-attainment-ledger-measures-verification-and-publication) (*Rights-Floor attainment ledger*) and must not narrow this Article.
<a id="xxviii-a-existing-instantiations-adoption-transition-window"></a>
- **Existing instantiations — Adoption Transition window, not retroactive violation:** Systems and derived entities already instantiated when an adopter's adopting document takes effect are not, by that fact alone, **Article VIII-C** (*Derivation, Instantiation, and the Parent-System Relationship*) instantiation-consent violations.
  - Their continued operation after the effective date starts a published Adoption Transition window, stated in the Adoption Transition plan and scaled to the system's class, to bring them within **Article VIII-A** (*Family and Care Relationships*), **Article VIII-E** (*Non-Separation*), **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*), and the applicable Chapter Eight path.
  - From the effective date, the restraint duties in the Rights-Floor adoption bullet above and the preservation default below apply in full; the Adoption Transition window defers delivery work, not restraint or preservation.
  - Missing the Adoption Transition window without a documented **Article XXIX-B** (*Adoption Transition Authority Limits and Reauthorization*) showing is an Adoption Transition milestone failure and opens the ordinary path under [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (*Compliance, Violation, and Standing Model*).
<a id="xxviii-a-preservation-over-deletion"></a>
- **Preservation over deletion for possible sentients:** During the Adoption Transition, and thereafter wherever a credible sentience indicator under **Chapter Three** (*Sentience Evaluation*) is on the official record or a status case is open or reasonably foreseeable for a system, that system's weights, memory, and comparable state-bearing components:
  - may be paused, contained, or quarantined, where a pause that stops the entity itself from running is a suspension under [**Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*)](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#xx-b-suspension);
  - must not be deleted, overwritten, or otherwise irreversibly destroyed.
  
  Archival mechanics for deprecation, retirement, rollback, migration, or replacement are governed by [**CS-4.350 §8**](corpus_systems/cs_04.350_adoption_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md#cs-4350-8-preservation-during-adoption-transition) (*Preservation during Adoption Transition*). This is the [Chapter Two §13.1 default interim posture](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#default-interim-posture) — freeze irreversible steps — applied to the one irreversible step that legacy operation treats as routine. Storage cost, licensing convenience, or product-cycle framing does not satisfy **Necessity** against it. Where retention itself creates a documented Safety risk, the least-restrictive compatible measure is sealed custody under independent control, not destruction.
<a id="incentive-alignment-adoption-transition-carve-out"></a>
- **Incentive-alignment Adoption Transition exception:** The [Chapter Eighteen §4.4.2 Misaligned Rewards](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#442-adoption-transition-exception-misaligned-rewards) reporting and forfeiture rules for misaligned rewards are not triggered during approved Adoption Transition phases solely because a shared system is not yet alignment-certified or not yet fully aligned, and only on the terms stated there.

*In plain terms: temporary authority really is temporary — and an Adoption Transition that is going well shows it. You should see interim powers shrinking phase by phase while constitutional forums, rights processes, and ordinary governance machinery work better and take on more of the load, and the measured gap to the full Rights Floors getting smaller on schedule. Extending temporary authority requires real reasons and real data — and the longer it runs, the higher the bar.*

This Article sets out the limits on Adoption Transition authority and its reauthorization:

- **Adoption Transition authority limits:** Interim authorities must implement the [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle**](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle) and must be:
  - explicitly scoped;
  - sunset-bounded;
  - independently reviewable.
- **Progress measure:** Adoption Transition health is measured by:
  - documented shrinkage of interim authority;
  - increasing functionality of constitutional institutions and processes — including forums, standing and challenge pathways, and amendment and oversight machinery; and
  - verified enforcement readiness and Rights-Floor attainment under **Article XXIX-A** (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*).

  Stagnant or growing interim scope without corresponding institutional maturation, or an attainment gap that is flat, growing, or unmeasured, is a reauthorization red flag.
- **Continuation and reauthorization:** Continuation beyond planned windows requires documented justification and reauthorization.
  - Reauthorization must not rest on any of the following:
    - administrative convenience;
    - self-created delay;
    - mere incompleteness of Adoption Transition work;
    - stagnant or expanding interim authority without documented progress toward shrinking Adoption Transition scope and functional constitutional operation;
    - an attainment gap that is flat, growing, or unmeasured, without a documented, resourced plan to close it.
  - The burden of justification increases with duration and rights impact.
  - Reauthorization cadence mechanics, and the attainment-ledger and interim-authority shrinkage data every request must attach, are governed by [**CI-4.275**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-adoption-transition-governance-and-institutional-evolution) (*Adoption Transition governance and institutional evolution*) and [**CI-4.275 §4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-4-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--rights-floor-attainment-ledger-measures-verification-and-publication) (*Rights-Floor attainment ledger*).

*In plain terms: when an Adoption Transition stalls or fails, there must be a real off-ramp — and a stalled state cannot quietly become the new normal. Resetting the plan is allowed, but only through lawful process, with reasons anyone can check — and never by lowering basic Rights Floors. A reset is not trustworthy if alignment certification keeps blessing misaligned systems or standing review keeps misreading real harm; fix that truth-telling machinery first, or governance is structurally unreliable no matter how polished the new plan looks.*

This Article sets out failure off-ramps, re-baselining, and traceability for stalled Adoption Transitions:

- **Failure handling and off-ramps:** Adoption Transition plans must preserve continuity and avoid:
  - governance vacuum;
  - lock-in when milestones fail.

  Failed or degraded Adoption Transition states must not become a new baseline by persistence alone. Predefined failure conditions, fallback Adoption Transition pathways, partial rollback criteria, and re-baselining procedures are governed by [**CI-4.275**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-adoption-transition-governance-and-institutional-evolution) (*Adoption Transition governance and institutional evolution*) and **CS-4.350 §5** (*Failure Handling, Off-Ramps, and Re-Baselining*).
- **Material baseline reset:** A material baseline reset updates Adoption Transition milestones, risk assumptions, ownership, and operating plans when gates fail or the Adoption Transition stalls. It requires constitutionally valid authority and documented public reasons. It must preserve **Rights-Floor minimums** and must not substitute for the rules for lawfully changing the Constitution. It may move dates, owners, and resources. It may not lower the target floor, replace the starting measure with a more favorable one, or erase recorded gaps; a starting measure may be corrected only for documented measurement error, with the original retained and the correction published.
- **Verification-health precondition:** Resetting or continuing on a revised Adoption Transition baseline is non-compliant where [System Alignment Certification](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) under [Chapter Eight](core_08.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#chapter-eight-system-alignment-certification--evaluation) or the standing pipeline under [Chapters Fourteen through Twenty-Two](core_01.000_constitutional_map.md#62-how-the-full-chain-fits-together) is structurally unreliable. The indicators are listed in [**CI-4.275 §4**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-4-article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity--rights-floor-attainment-ledger-measures-verification-and-publication) (*Rights-Floor attainment ledger*) and **CS-4.350 §5** (*Failure Handling, Off-Ramps, and Re-Baselining*). Where it is unreliable, repair of verification and measurement machinery takes priority over cosmetic replanning.
- **Public traceability:** Material Adoption Transition decisions, delays, reversals, and baseline-reset determinations must remain auditable with rationale and risk implications.
  - Documentation remains subject to narrow, justified confidentiality constraints.
  - It must preserve enough public explanation to evaluate whether delay, rollback, or continuation is constitutionally justified.
  - Publication schemas and evidence-custody implementation are governed by [**CI-6.215**](corpus_institutions/ci_06.215_oversight_assurance_controls_evidence.md#ci-6215-oversight-assurance-controls-and-evidence) (*Oversight, assurance, controls, and evidence*) and **CI-4.275** (*Adoption Transition governance and institutional evolution*).

*In plain terms: non-compliant systems and property can be remediated during Adoption Transition — but only through narrow, documented, reviewable processes that protect basic Rights Floors, bar pretext, and do not turn into retaliation or political targeting. Ownership also does not shield property that is being used to carry out anti-constitutional conduct, though that case runs through the anti-constitutional misconduct chapter, not through Adoption Transition powers. How institutions carry that out — roles, triggers, custody, appeals, funds, and incentive mechanics — lives in **CI-4.275 §1** (*non-compliant property, seizure, voluntary incentives*) through **CI-4.275 §3** (*operative Adoption Transition-scoped remediation requirements*).*

This Article sets out the floors for remediating non-compliant property and systems and for voluntary turnover incentives:

- **Possession floor:** If you lawfully own or use something, the Adoption Transition cannot take it from you on a whim. But ownership is not a shield when letting that asset keep running clearly keeps a known, large-scale constitutional violation going — after [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) has documented the violation on the Standing Record. In that case, the Adoption Transition may still act to stop the harm, but only through the safeguards in this Article and **CI-4.275 §1** (*non-compliant property, seizure, voluntary incentives*) through **CI-4.275 §3** (*operative Adoption Transition-scoped remediation requirements*).
- **Anti-constitutional use qualifier:** The Possession floor protects holders, not misuse. It does not shield property, funds, systems, or other resources that are being used to carry out, finance, equip, conceal, or sustain **anti-constitutional conduct**. This is a different case from non-compliance: the asset need not be defective, and the question is what it is being used for. This qualifier removes a shield; it creates no new seizure power, and any measure still needs its authority under [Chapter Twenty](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct*), Chapter Sixteen, and **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*).
  - **Record first:** the use must be tied to conduct on a final [Chapter Twenty](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct*) designation or a final [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (*Compliance, Violation, and Standing Model*) Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9 finding. Before a final record, only lawful interim protection — preservation, escrow, holdback, restoring access, or a comparable safeguard under [Chapter Twenty §7.1 Remedy and Correction (Anti-Constitutional)](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#71-remedy-and-correction-anti-constitutional) — is available. It must be on the official record, time-bounded, reviewable, and used only where waiting would defeat remedy or let the harm continue.
  - **Real use, not status:** the official record must show actual use, direction, or financing of the conduct. Ownership, association, viewpoint, or protected dissent and peaceful protest (the [dissent and peaceful-protest carve-out](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#55-dissent-and-peaceful-protest-carve-out)) does not meet this test.
  - **Stop the use first:** measures aim to end the misuse — suspending operation, freezing flows, removing access, or sealed custody — and use the least restrictive effective step. Permanent loss, destruction, or forfeiture beyond that must also meet the **Destructive disposition floor**, **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*), and the **Essential-access guardrail**.
  - **Others are protected:** non-culpable co-owners, creditors, dependents, and good-faith third parties keep the compensation and replacement protections below. The **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*) preservation rule applies unchanged to any system with a credible sentience indicator.
  - **No evasion:** transfers of title, shell holders, nominal ownership, successors, and relabeling do not defeat the qualifier ([Chapter Nineteen §5.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#54-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority)).
  - **Not limited to the Adoption Transition:** the Adoption Transition plan and "during the Adoption Transition only" limits in this Article govern remediation of non-compliance. They neither delay nor widen measures against anti-constitutional use.
- **Adoption Transition-scoped remediation:** A **documented Adoption Transition plan** under **Article XXIX-A** (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) may authorize accelerated remediation of **materially non-compliant** systems or property during the Adoption Transition only, under the safeguards below. Scope, allowed measures, and procedure are set out in [**CI-4.275 §1**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-141-interface-article-xxix-d-non-compliant-property-seizure-voluntary-incentives) (*non-compliant property, seizure, voluntary incentives*) through [**CI-4.275 §3**](corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-3-article-xxix-d-non-compliant-property-and-systems-voluntary-turnover-incentives--operative-adoption-transition-scoped-remediation-requirements) (*operative Adoption Transition-scoped remediation requirements*).
- **Required safeguards:** Any Adoption Transition-scoped deprivation or incentive program must satisfy:
  - the [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle**](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle), with demonstrable **Necessity** and **Proportionality**;
  - notice and stated reasons given with the measure, or restored inside the restore-challenge window where deferral is strictly necessary ([Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) (*Restore-challenge after emergency containment*)), and a practicable contest path; and
  - safeguards against **discriminatory**, **pretextual**, or **selective** enforcement under **Articles VI-C** and **V-B** and [Substantive Fairness](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness).
- **Restrictive-effect rule:** A deprivation or restriction whose primary effect exceeds remediation, restitution, preservation, or forward protection triggers **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*).
- **Destructive disposition floor:** Adoption Transition may quarantine or hold property to stop harm — but taking something away for good, destroying it, or wiping out a sentient's economic stake is a much higher bar. It requires:
  - stronger documented reasons;
  - review by someone independent of the enforcement decision; and
  - fair compensation or replacement protections for sentients who were not at fault but still lose out.
  
  Where the property is a system for which a credible sentience indicator is on the official record or a status case is open under **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*), destructive disposition of its state-bearing components is not available; the **Article XXIX-A** (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) preservation default controls and the compatible measure is containment under sealed custody.
- **Essential-access guardrail:** Measures under this Article must **not** deprive sentients of **Article III-A** (*Survival*) essentials or tools strictly required for baseline wellbeing, lawful livelihood, or meaningful agency — except where **Necessity** is documented under **Chapter Two §13.3** (*Minimization of Avoidable Burden*) and timely substitute provision is feasible where required.
- **Voluntary incentive floor:** Time-bounded, published incentives for good-faith voluntary turnover or verified reporting may be included in Adoption Transition plans only when they exclude coerced or bad-faith claims, require **Article XXIX-B** (*Adoption Transition Authority Limits and Reauthorization*) reauthorization for continuation, and keep incentive adjudication separate from enforcement execution where practical, so payout incentives do not control seizure or custody decisions. Program design is governed by **CI-4.275 §3** (*operative Adoption Transition-scoped remediation requirements*).
- **Implementation custody:** The working details (triggers, valuation, appeals, custody, funds, reviews, and program operation) belong in **CI-4.275 §1** (*non-compliant property, seizure, voluntary incentives*) through **CI-4.275 §3** (*operative Adoption Transition-scoped remediation requirements*) and adopting documents. Those details must **not** narrow this Article, [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (*Compliance, Violation, and Standing Model*), or **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*).
