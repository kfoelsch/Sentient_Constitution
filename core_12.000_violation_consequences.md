Chapter Twelve is the constitutional owner of **violation consequences**: what remedy and correction are owed after a verified violation, which locks prevent recurrence, and how locks are designed and enforced. Its first section sets the order: remedy and correction, then locks.
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: something bad happened. This chapter says how to make a durable fix: remedy and correction first, then prevention locks, then voluntary public accountability expression where a restorative pathway uses it, and the remedy-parity rule that keeps the cheap half from outrunning the expensive half.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Twelve decides what follows from a verified violation. It states:

- remedy and correction, general standing locks, voluntary public accountability expression, and remedy parity and lock preconditions (**section 3**); and
- how a lock is designed and enforced: definition and attachment, proportionality and calibration, record visibility and escalation, special violation rules, special locks, and interim holds (**section 4**).

**Role:** The chapter is the violation track of Question 3:

- It decides from the violation Standing Record alone, and takes the verified violation record and the slot as fixed. It does not reopen [Subcore 10-D](subcore_10.040_standing_measurement.md#subcore-10-d-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it) measurement.
- It follows the decision order in **Chapter Ten section 4.4**. A lock controls: no contribution result opens a gated pathway a lock blocks.
- It hands its result to the final standing effect in [Chapter Thirteen](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#chapter-thirteen-final-standing-effect-restoration-and-enforcement).

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- remedy and correction, general standing locks, voluntary public accountability expression, and remedy parity and lock preconditions (**section 3**); and
- how a lock is designed and enforced (**section 4**).

The chapter leaves:

- contribution consequences to [Chapter Eleven](core_11.000_contribution_consequences.md#chapter-eleven-contribution-consequences);
- the decision order to [Subcore 10-F](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#subcore-10-f-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows) and the final standing effect to Chapter Nineteen; and
- the anti-constitutional-misconduct designation for qualifying slot 7 to 9 findings to [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-fourteen-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation).


<a id="2-why-violation-consequences-exist"></a>
### 2. Why Violation Consequences Exist

*In plain terms: when harm is verified, the first job is to repair it and stop it happening again. This chapter exists so that consequences fix the problem rather than only punish.*

Punishment alone leaves the harmed sentient unrepaired and the cause in place. This chapter puts remedy and correction first, then prevention locks where they are needed to stop a repeat, and keeps the cheap step (an apology or statement) from standing in for the expensive one (actual repair). Each consequence is tied to a verified violation finding and can be contested.

**Grounded in:**

- Preamble:
  - [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad): the legs this chapter carries:
    - [Accountability](subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability): whoever caused verified harm must repair it;
    - [Timeliness](subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness): repair and prevention come first, before the harm spreads;
  - [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims): both aims, which this chapter serves:
    - [Flourishing](core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing): those harmed are made whole where possible;

### 3. Violation, Correction, and Prevention

*In plain terms: Something bad happened. How do we create a durable fix? This section answers in order — **remedy and correction**, then **prevention** locks, then **voluntary public accountability expression** where a restorative pathway uses it, and finally the **remedy-parity** rule that keeps the cheap half of this section from outrunning the expensive half. Once Chapter Ten has verified a violation, remedy and correction come from that Standing Record alone — fix what was broken, and change what caused it — then prevention locks hold sensitive gated pathways closed until those conditions are met. Public acknowledgment or apology, if used, is supplementary and never substitutes for repair or locks. Past contribution does not soften the duty, cancel a lock, or substitute for verified repair. Forums supervise implementation and challenge; they do not invent a different consequence from the verified Standing Record.*

This section is the violation side of Question 3. It starts from a violation Standing Record that [Subcore 10-B](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#subcore-10-b-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) has verified and [Subcore 10-D](subcore_10.040_standing_measurement.md#subcore-10-d-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it) has measured, and it decides the violation-side consequences (remedy, correction, and prevention) from that violation Standing Record alone ([Subcore 10-F §4.4 Processing Order](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#44-processing-order), step 4). **Independence:** contribution, which [§3 Contribution Consequences](core_11.000_contribution_consequences.md#3-contribution-consequences) covers, is not an input to remedy, correction, safeguard, lock assignment, or voluntary public accountability expression. It may show capacity to perform an obligation, but it cannot change the obligation or the unresolved status that supports it. The one exception is help given after the violation, which can count as restitution under [§3.1.2 Remedy](#312-remedy); help given before the violation never does.

Forums under [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_forum.md#chapter-sixteen-standards-routing-and-forum-cases) supervise lawful implementation, challenge, interim relief, and timely movement of these decisions. They do not replace this chapter's duty to decide remedy, correction, and prevention from the verified Standing Record. How a forum's work reaches the Standing Record itself is in [Subcore 10-B §4.7 Forum Boundary](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#47-forum-boundary).

<a id="31-remedy-and-correction"></a>
#### 3.1 Remedy and Correction

*In plain terms: after a verified **violation**, this subsection assigns the **remedy** and **correction** parts of the durable fix. **Remedy** repairs what was broken for those harmed. **Correction** changes what caused it. **Prevention** — standing locks and related gated pathway limits — begins in [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](#32-prevention--general-standing-locks), with design and enforcement in [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](#4-lock-design-and-enforcement), and must not be waived while recurrence risk remains live. Where restorative pathways include public acknowledgment or apology, [§3.3 Voluntary Public Accountability Expression](#33-voluntary-public-accountability-expression) applies.*

This subsection has four parts:

- [§3.1.1 What the Integration Record Must Say](#311-what-the-integration-record-must-say);
- [§3.1.2 Remedy](#312-remedy);
- [§3.1.3 Correction](#313-correction); and
- [§3.1.4 Remedy and Correction Together](#314-remedy-and-correction-together).

<a id="311-what-the-integration-record-must-say"></a>
##### 3.1.1 What the Integration Record Must Say

The integration record must say, in plain view and at the level of specificity feasible without unlawful disclosure:

- **What happened and what is owed:**
  - who was affected, including sentients, classes, communities, institutions, systems, and challenge, participation, or remedy pathways, and what harm occurred, including material harm, rights loss, remedy loss, dependency harm, evidence degradation, or process nullification;
  - what remedy is owed and how far it reaches, including restitution, compensation, restoration, disgorgement or clawback where lawful, substitute safeguards where literal repair is impossible, and systemic remediation where harm is class-wide or structural; and
  - what correction duties apply.
- **Who carries it out:**
  - who is responsible for carrying them out, including successors, funds, or coordinating bodies that must carry or finance a remedy; and
  - where the funding comes from, and how the remedy will be executed where that needs capacity, cross-institution coordination, tracing successors, recognition by another forum, or pooled execution, or why a substitute safeguard is the proportionate lawful remedy instead.
- **When and how far along:**
  - when work must start and what milestones apply, including commencement deadlines, completion milestones, verification requirements, and review triggers under [Article XXVII-C](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxvii-c-timely-resolution-and-anti-delay-floor) (*Timely Resolution and Anti-Delay Floor*); and
  - where things stand now.
- **Follow-through:**
  - who supervises follow-through; and
  - what happens if delay occurs.

<a id="312-remedy"></a>
##### 3.1.2 Remedy

Every verified violation must be tested for two parallel correction outputs, remedy and correction ([§3.1.3 Correction](#313-correction)); remedy has two parts:

- **Acknowledge and repair:**
  - acknowledgment;
  - repair; or
  - restoration.
- **Make up the loss:**
  - compensation;
  - substitute safeguards where literal repair is impossible; or
  - comparable redress for affected sentients and interests.

Remedy also follows these three rules:

- **Priority:** Put affected parties and practical restoration first, ahead of reputation repair, institutional convenience, or ordinary administrative order.
- **Interim protection:** If waiting would likely defeat remedy, prolong dependency harm, destroy evidence, or let harm continue, lawful interim steps must be considered, and each must be ordered or rejected on the Standing Record.
  - Steps to consider include protection, preservation, escrow, holdback, restoring access, keeping services running, or a comparable safeguard.
  - Interim relief while a matter is pending stays under [Chapter Seventeen §4 Interim Protection](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#interim-protection).
- **Later help as restitution:** Help given before the violation does not count toward remedy. Verified help given by the party who owes the remedy after the conduct the violation finding rests on (for conduct that continues, after it stops) counts as restitution to the extent it repairs, compensates, restores, or makes up the loss to the affected sentients and interests.
  - The integration record credits the help against the remedy owed and says how much of it the help covers.
  - The credit reduces the remedy still owed. It does not change the violation finding, the Chapter Ten slot, the correction duties, or any lock.
  - Completed restitution, including credited help, has the effects stated in [Article XX-D](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xx-d-violations-and-standing-locks) (*Violations and Standing Locks*) and [Chapter Thirteen §4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment).

<a id="313-correction"></a>
##### 3.1.3 Correction

Correction is the set of changes needed to address the cause and stop continuation. It covers:

- **Conduct and role:**
  - conduct; or
  - role eligibility.
- **Structure:**
  - systems;
  - records; or
  - incentives.
- **Oversight and protection:**
  - supervision; or
  - safeguards.

The integration record must identify, at the level of specificity feasible without unlawful disclosure, the conduct, role, decision, system, record, or incentive that made the violation possible or kept it going, read with [Article XXV](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-root-cause-analysis-and-adaptive-response) (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*). Correction is complete only when the Standing Record shows it eliminates or resolves that cause. Completion and non-completion have the effects stated in [Article XX-D](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xx-d-violations-and-standing-locks) (*Violations and Standing Locks*).

<a id="314-remedy-and-correction-together"></a>
##### 3.1.4 Remedy and Correction Together

Fixing the cause does not cancel the duty to repair those harmed. Repairing those harmed does not cancel the duty to fix the cause, keep needed safeguards in place, or keep prevention locks in place while the risk of the same harm happening again remains real. Being able to fix the problem does not lower how serious the Violation Axis slot is.

Nothing else stands in for either output:

- **Remedy side:** Imprisonment, standing locks, role exclusion, publication, apology, dissolving an institution, supervised operation, clawback from wrongdoers, or striking an unlawful decision or effect does not by itself count as remedy. It counts only when the Standing Record shows the measure actually repairs, compensates, restores, prevents recurrence, or supplies a proportionate substitute safeguard for the relevant harm. Public acknowledgment or apology is governed by [§3.3 Voluntary Public Accountability Expression](#33-voluntary-public-accountability-expression).
- **Correction side:** Imprisonment, standing locks, role exclusion, or publication does not by itself count as correction. It counts only when the Standing Record shows it addresses the cause or stops continuation.

No remedy, correction, or restriction may itself inflict suffering as an end or add gratuitous or degrading suffering. This holds in every case and applies in full where the violation record carries verified [Cruelty](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#cruelty) conduct character.

<a id="32-prevention--general-standing-locks"></a>
#### 3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks

*In plain terms: this is the **prevention** part of the durable fix. A standing lock reduces risk by closing or limiting gated pathways that would let the same harm continue or happen again while the violation is still unresolved or unrepaired. It is not punishment for its own sake, and it is not a popularity or reputation score.*

This subsection has five parts:

- [§3.2.1 What a Standing Lock Is](#321-what-a-standing-lock-is);
- [§3.2.2 Governance-Voting and Stakeholder-Participation Pathways](#322-governance-voting-and-stakeholder-participation-pathways);
- [§3.2.3 Special Locks for Particular Findings](#323-special-locks-for-particular-findings);
- [§3.2.4 Purpose of a Lock](#324-purpose-of-a-lock); and
- [§3.2.5 What Every Lock Must Meet](#325-what-every-lock-must-meet).

<a id="321-what-a-standing-lock-is"></a>
##### 3.2.1 What a Standing Lock Is

*In plain terms: a standing lock closes or limits one gated pathway after a verified violation, and it must say which gated pathway, what risk it reduces, and how it ends. Detention and other liberty restrictions are not locks; they have their own safeguards.*

A **standing lock** is a prevention and risk-reduction measure. It blocks or limits a gated pathway while a verified violation remains unresolved or materially unremedied. Gated pathways include:

- trust pathways;
- role pathways;
- authority pathways;
- credit pathways, meaning credit for contribution (who is recognized as having done the work), which is not money or lending;
- oversight pathways;
- recognition pathways;
- influence pathways, as [Influence Pathway](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#influence-pathway) defines them;
- **governance-voting** pathways;
- **stakeholder-participation** pathways;
- care and stewardship pathways, as [§4.5.7 Care and Stewardship Lock](#457-care-and-stewardship-lock) sets them out;
- financial-authority pathways, as [§4.5.8 Financial Authority Standing Lock](#458-financial-authority-standing-lock) sets them out;
- creation, reach, scale, and capability pathways, as [§4.4.7 Capacity Locks](#447-capacity-locks) sets them out; and
- expression-conduct pathways, as [§4.4.8 Expression Locks](#448-expression-locks) sets them out.

A standing lock must carry the attachment fields set out in [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment). A lock on an influence pathway takes one of two forms, set out in [§4.4.6 Influence-Pathway Locks](#446-influence-pathway-locks). A lock on a capacity the sentient misused takes one of four forms, set out in [§4.4.7 Capacity Locks](#447-capacity-locks). A lock on expression conduct the sentient used to cause harm is set out in [§4.4.8 Expression Locks](#448-expression-locks).

Detention, imprisonment, and other liberty restrictions are not standing locks ([Chapter Four §2.3 Locks and Liberty Restrictions](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#23-locks-and-liberty-restrictions)). They are separate justice measures governed by the coercive or liberty-restricting safeguards in [§4.4.5 Coercive or Liberty-Restricting Safeguards](#445-coercive-or-liberty-restricting-safeguards). They are recorded with the same attachment fields as a lock and must meet the same needed, proportionate, and reviewable tests, plus full criminal-process or equivalent protections and explicit duration limits, review cadence, and restoration conditions.

<a id="322-governance-voting-and-stakeholder-participation-pathways"></a>
##### 3.2.2 Governance-Voting and Stakeholder-Participation Pathways

*In plain terms: voting on collective rules and having a weighted say inside an existing system are different gated pathways. A lock on one does not cover the other, and neither removes a sentient's status as a Stakeholder.*

The two named gated pathways are distinct:

- **governance-voting** covers participation in governance voting and comparable legitimacy-mechanism binding collective choice under [Chapter Twenty-One §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_21.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility), including **Foundational Constitutional Choice**. It does **not** include stake-weighted voice inside an already-authorized system, institution, or bounded decision domain.
- **stakeholder-participation** covers stake-weighted vote, binding stakeholder choice, and comparable Stakeholder System Participation pathways under [Stakeholder Weight](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-weight) and [Binding Stakeholder Choice](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#defp2-binding-stakeholder-choice). It does **not** erase [Stakeholder](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder) status itself, and it does **not** substitute for a **governance-voting** lock.

<a id="323-special-locks-for-particular-findings"></a>
##### 3.2.3 Special Locks for Particular Findings

*In plain terms: some findings call for a special lock that has its own trigger and its own limits.*

Apply the special lock whose trigger is met. Each is set out in [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks):

- **Stakeholder-Participation Standing Lock:** the trigger is verified stakeholder-pathway corruption, capture, false-stake abuse, or coercive participation-weight gaming.
- **Institutional Authority Standing Lock:** the trigger is a verified violation carried out through an institution or enterprise, or an office in one.
  - It closes the authority pathway only.
  - It attaches to a verified violation finding and never to a showing short of a verified violation finding.
- **Perjury Standing Lock:** the trigger is a verified violation finding that the subject knowingly gave a materially false statement under oath, affirmation, an equivalent truth-duty, or a stated attestation to a forum, a verifying authority, a certifier, or a record custodian.
  - It closes the attestation and verifying pathways only.
  - It restricts no testimony.
  - It attaches to a verified violation finding and never to a statement that is only doubted or contradicted.
- **Dissolution Reformation Lock:** the trigger is a final dissolution order that ends a Chartered Party (or a Class P Operating Party that held no System Charter or Institutional Charter).
  - It attaches to the dissolved Chartered Party and its successor entities and never to a sentient by reason of belonging to it.
  - It is permanent where the order finds pervasive anti-constitutional conduct or unremedied Irreversible Harm, and otherwise it lifts on an independent restoration finding.
- **Care and Stewardship Lock:** the trigger is a verified violation finding of abuse of, cruelty toward, or serious neglect of a developing or dependent sentient by a subject who held care, guardianship, stewardship, or parent-system authority over that sentient, or unsupervised access to it through a role.
  - It closes care and stewardship authority and unsupervised access.
  - It applies on the same terms to every substrate.
  - It never reaches bodily reproduction, and it is never enforced by intruding into a body or a mind.
- **Financial Authority Standing Lock:** the trigger is a verified violation finding of knowing or intentional, material misuse of money, funds, or assets, or of fraud or concealment about them.
  - It closes authority over other sentients' or the public's money, and unsupervised access to it.
  - It does not reach the subject's own earning, holding, spending, saving, or borrowing, or what the subject needs to live.
  - Inability to repay does not keep it in place, and repayment does not lift it by itself.

<a id="324-purpose-of-a-lock"></a>
##### 3.2.4 Purpose of a Lock

*In plain terms: a lock keeps the same harm from continuing or coming back by separating a sentient from the setting or gated pathway where the violation occurred.*

The purpose of a lock is to reduce risk by removing a sentient from the environment, roles, or gated pathways — and, where needed, from contact with the sentients — where the violation occurred. That separation serves three purposes:

1. **Containment** — stop the same harm from continuing through the same setting or gated pathways;
2. **Safety for affected parties** — keep those already harmed from further exposure; and
3. **Risk reduction** — lower the chance the same pattern recurs while remedy and correction remain incomplete.

<a id="325-what-every-lock-must-meet"></a>
##### 3.2.5 What Every Lock Must Meet

*In plain terms: every lock has to be needed, proportionate, auditable, contestable, no broader than required, and time-bounded where it can be. It attaches to a verified violation finding, not to identity, status, accusation, or reputation.*

Every lock must meet the requirements set out in [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](#4-lock-design-and-enforcement):

- [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment) (*the verified-finding gate, what character may and may not do, and the attachment fields*);
- [§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration) (*needed, proportionate, no broader than required, and time-bounded*);
- [§4.3 Record Visibility and Escalation](#43-record-visibility-and-escalation) (*auditable and contestable*); and
- [§4.4 Special Violation Rules](#44-special-violation-rules) and [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks), stated in the same attachment fields.

No-offset and contribution visibility remain governed by [Subcore 10-B §4.4 Linked Standing Records and No-Offset Bridge](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#44-linked-standing-records-and-no-offset-bridge); this chapter applies that rule when designing and enforcing locks.

<a id="33-voluntary-public-accountability-expression"></a>
#### 3.3 Voluntary Public Accountability Expression

*In plain terms: a public apology or acknowledgment, if used on a restorative pathway, must be voluntary, sincere, and non-degrading. Refusal alone does not raise sanctions. A fake or abusive apology does not count as repair.*

Where restorative pathways for a verified violation include public acknowledgment or apology, accountable parties may voluntarily choose that expression as a supplementary accountability act.

- The choice is permitted where it supports repair, trust restoration, or community healing.
- Such expression must be:
  - non-coercive;
  - informed;
  - revocable up to delivery;
  - independently reviewable for voluntariness and authenticity.
- Refusal to participate in public expression must not, by itself, increase baseline sanctions, widen a standing lock, or substitute for remedy or correction owed under [§3.1 Remedy and Correction](#31-remedy-and-correction).
- **Inauthentic or abusive expression:** Public acknowledgment or apology that is materially insincere, manipulative, performative without truthful accountability, or abusive toward affected parties or the public does not satisfy restorative requirements.
  - It may carry negative justice consequences for the accountable party — including reduced credit for rehabilitation and other lawful restrictions under **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*) and [Subcore 10-B](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#subcore-10-b-standing-records--question-1-what-happened).
  - Treating a fake or abusive apology as sufficient repair, or using it to avoid necessary protection, remediation, or locks under [§3.1 Remedy and Correction](#31-remedy-and-correction) and [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](#32-prevention--general-standing-locks), is non-compliant.
- No format may impose any of the following beyond what is necessary for truthful accountability and proportionate remediation:
  - degrading treatment;
  - compelled self-abasement;
  - irreversible reputational harm.

Anti-constitutional misconduct specializations — including rules triggered by an ACMD and consequences linked to it for inauthentic expression — are governed by [Chapter Fourteen §7.3](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#73-voluntary-public-accountability-expression-anti-constitutional) (*Voluntary Public Accountability Expression (Anti-Constitutional)*).

<a id="34-remedy-parity-and-lock-preconditions"></a>
#### 3.4 Remedy Parity and Lock Preconditions

*In plain terms: locks are cheap to issue; repair, restoration, and review are expensive to run. Left alone, the cheap half of this chapter keeps working while the expensive half starves. This subsection ties them together: an ordinary lock cannot attach until the remedy file for the same violation finding is actually open, and when an adopter's remedy or restoration work for a class of locks falls behind its clock, new ordinary locks in that class default to the lightest form and existing ones come up for review. The gravest locks are exempt so that flooding the remedy queue cannot spring them.*

This subsection has four parts:

- [§3.4.1 Opened-Remedy Precondition](#341-opened-remedy-precondition);
- [§3.4.2 Remedy-Parity Tripwire](#342-remedy-parity-tripwire);
- [§3.4.3 Exemptions from the Tripwire](#343-exemptions-from-the-tripwire); and
- [§3.4.4 Measuring Remedy Parity](#344-measuring-remedy-parity).

<a id="341-opened-remedy-precondition"></a>
##### 3.4.1 Opened-Remedy Precondition

*In plain terms: a lock needs an open remedy file first. Finishing the remedy is not required.*

A general standing lock under [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) may not attach to a gated pathway until the [§3.1 Remedy and Correction](#31-remedy-and-correction) integration record for the same verified violation finding is **open**:

- **Open means** that at least the following are stated in plain view:
  - the affected parties;
  - the remedy owed;
  - the responsible party;
  - the funding source; and
  - the start date.
- **Not required:** completion of remedy. An opened remedy record is required.
- **Not covered:** interim protective measures under [Chapter Seventeen §4 Interim Protection](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#interim-protection) (*Interim Protection*), including an [Interim Hold](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interim-hold), which needs no open remedy record because it is not a lock.
- <a id="341-special-locks-urgency-bridge"></a>**Special locks:** this precondition applies to the special locks under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks) as well. Where waiting for the remedy record would risk serious harm, a special lock may attach first, but the remedy record for the same verified violation finding must be open within the **prompt-review window** ([Chapter Seventeen §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#prompt-review)). If it is not, the lock is defective under the next bullet. The attaching decision records the harm that could not wait.
- **A defective lock:** a lock attached without an opened remedy record is defective and must be corrected or narrowed to interim protective scope on the [Chapter Seventeen §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier window.

<a id="342-remedy-parity-tripwire"></a>
##### 3.4.2 Remedy-Parity Tripwire

*In plain terms: when remedy or restoration work runs late for a class of locks, new locks in that class default to the lightest form and existing ones come up for review.*

Adopters must publish, for each period and each lock class they administer, the number of:

- general locks issued;
- remedies commenced;
- remedies completed;
- restorations decided; and
- reassessments pending.

Where remedy commencement or restoration review for a lock class exceeds its [Chapter Seventeen §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier outer bound:

- new general locks in that class default to **open with safeguards** under [§3 Final Standing Effect](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#3-final-standing-effect) unless the integration record documents a specific irreversible-harm or safety risk that requires **blocked**;
- existing general locks in that class enter mandatory reassessment under [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment) on the same tier window; and
- the capacity failure is recorded as a durability failure under [§5.2 Remedy-System Durability](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#52-remedy-system-durability) and routed under the [Chapter Sixteen §6 Transfer, Consolidation, and Anti-Self-Judging — Continuity and Anti-Capture](core_16.000_forum.md#6-transfer-consolidation-and-anti-self-judging--continuity-and-anti-capture) capacity-failure rule, outside the family or system whose capacity is in question.

<a id="343-exemptions-from-the-tripwire"></a>
##### 3.4.3 Exemptions from the Tripwire

*In plain terms: the gravest locks and the liberty-restricting safeguards are exempt, so flooding the remedy queue cannot spring them.*

The tripwire does not relax:

- special locks under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks);
- locks attached to Violation Axis slot **7**, **8**, or **9** findings; or
- coercive or liberty-restricting safeguards under [§4.4.5 Coercive or Liberty-Restricting Safeguards](#445-coercive-or-liberty-restricting-safeguards).

Filings verified as contest-pathway flooding under [Subcore 14-A §4.2 Flooding the Zone and Contest-Pathway Denial](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#42-flooding-the-zone-and-contest-pathway-denial) do not count toward the backlog that trips this rule.

<a id="344-measuring-remedy-parity"></a>
##### 3.4.4 Measuring Remedy Parity

*In plain terms: a widening gap between locks issued and remedies delivered is a warning sign, not neutral data.*

The ratio of locks issued to remedies commenced and restorations decided is a constitutional performance indicator under the [Constitutional Performance measurement family](subcore_03.095_band_performance.md#measuring-constitutional-performance-preamble-measurement-family). A ratio that widens over successive periods is evidence of:

- [Avoidable Burden](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#avoidable-burden) on the violation side; and
- a [§5 Enforcement Realism and Remedy Systems](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#5-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems) paper-pathway failure on the remedy side.

The ratio is not a neutral operations metric.

<a id="4-lock-design-and-enforcement"></a>
### 4. Lock Design and Enforcement

*In plain terms: once a lock is needed under [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](#32-prevention--general-standing-locks), this section says how to design and enforce it—how to specify the lock, calibrate it, keep it visible and reviewable, which special locks are mandatory, and which special violation rules change scope or process. It does not reopen Chapter Ten measurement, and it does not let contribution offset a lock.*

Design a lock in this order:

- **Specify the lock:**
  - Say what it attaches to ([§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment)).
  - Make it no broader than the risk requires ([§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration)).
  - Put the terms in plain view and keep a review path ([§4.3 Record Visibility and Escalation](#43-record-visibility-and-escalation)).
- **Apply the special rules:**
  - Apply any special violation rules that shape that attachment ([§4.4 Special Violation Rules](#44-special-violation-rules)).
  - Apply any mandatory special locks, using the same attachment fields ([§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks)).
- **Keep an Interim Hold apart:**
  - An Interim Hold is not a lock; keep it apart from all of this ([§4.6 Interim Holds and Locks](#46-interim-holds-and-locks)).

**Contribution does not offset a lock:**

- **Past help:**
  - Past help does not cancel a verified harm, skip repair, or open a locked gated pathway.
  - That no-offset rule lives in [Subcore 10-B §4.4 Linked Standing Records and No-Offset Bridge](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#44-linked-standing-records-and-no-offset-bridge) and applies here.
  - Help given after the violation can count as restitution under [§3.1.2 Remedy](#312-remedy), and only there.
- **Where contribution is handled:**
  - [§3 Violation, Correction, and Prevention](#3-violation-correction-and-prevention) already says contribution does not decide locks.
  - What contribution can do is covered in [§3 Contribution Consequences](core_11.000_contribution_consequences.md#3-contribution-consequences) and [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment).

<a id="41-definition-and-attachment"></a>
#### 4.1 Definition and Attachment

*In plain terms: every lock is built from the same parts: fields the integration record must state, a verified violation finding to attach to, two risk qualifiers, a list of things it never cuts off, and limits on keeping a sentient away from particular settings or sentients. Special violation rules and special locks use all of them.*

This subsection has six parts:

- [§4.1.1 Attachment Fields](#411-attachment-fields);
- [§4.1.2 What a Lock Attaches To](#412-what-a-lock-attaches-to);
- [§4.1.3 Risk Qualifiers](#413-risk-qualifiers);
- [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict);
- [§4.1.5 Separation Limits](#415-separation-limits); and
- [§4.1.6 Carry-through to Special Rules and Special Locks](#416-carry-through-to-special-rules-and-special-locks).

<a id="411-attachment-fields"></a>
##### 4.1.1 Attachment Fields

*In plain terms: the integration record has to say what is blocked, why, whom it protects, how it can end, who can review it, and when it is looked at again.*

Every lock must be designed so the integration record can identify, in operative form:

- the blocked or limited gated pathway;
- the risk the lock is meant to reduce;
- the protected subjects or interests;
- the corrective conditions that must be met before narrowing or lifting;
- the review route; and
- the reassessment point.

<a id="412-what-a-lock-attaches-to"></a>
##### 4.1.2 What a Lock Attaches To

*In plain terms: a lock rests on a verified violation finding, not on who someone is or what others think of them. The kind of conduct involved can shape the lock.*

A lock attaches as follows:

- **A lock attaches only to a verified violation finding, and not to:**
  - identity;
  - status;
  - accusation;
  - generalized disfavor; or
  - reputation.
- **Character** is the kind of conduct involved in the violation, such as:
  - violence;
  - coercion;
  - cruelty;
  - deception;
  - concealment;
  - intent;
  - recurrence;
  - negligence;
  - duty;
  - [duty to resist](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#duty-to-resist); or
  - evasion.
- **What character may do:**
  - It may shape scope, scrutiny, safeguards, and review, but only when that character is separately verified.
  - It never changes the slot ([Subcore 10-F §3.3 Facts and Measurements Stay Fixed](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#33-facts-and-measurements-stay-fixed)).

<a id="413-risk-qualifiers"></a>
##### 4.1.3 Risk Qualifiers

*In plain terms: when a lock is meant to reduce risk, doubt about whether the harm will recur counts as risk, and the lock may have to keep the sentient away from what would bring the harm back.*

Two attachment qualifiers apply whenever the lock's purpose includes risk reduction:

1. **Recurrence uncertainty:** Where whether the same harm will recur remains uncertain, that uncertainty is itself a risk input. Lock design must integrate incomplete, contested, or time-limited evidence about recurrence into the risk assessment rather than treating uncertainty as a reason to leave a sensitive gated pathway open.
2. **Isolation from risk factors:** Where needed for containment or safety, the lock must isolate the responsible sentient from risk factors that would recreate exposure—including networks, associations, settings, and environments of other violators—and from contact with protected subjects or interests where that contact would renew the risk.

<a id="414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict"></a>
##### 4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict

*In plain terms: a lock never cuts off speech in general, the Rights-Floor minimums (survival-critical access among them), or ordinary wages. It can still close the gated pathways tied to the risk.*

A standing lock does not by itself cut off:

- ordinary speech, petition, testimony, defense, or whistleblowing. An expression lock under [§4.4.8 Expression Locks](#448-expression-locks) closes only the verified expression conduct it names;
- participation as an affected party;
- the Rights-Floor minimums that standing never reduces: inherent dignity, equal moral standing, survival-critical access, and the required challenge, review, appeal, and remedy pathways ([Subcore 10-F §3.6 The Rights Floor Is Not Reduced](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#36-the-rights-floor-is-not-reduced)); or
- the right to receive regular wages for work that is not part of the verified violation gated pathway.

A gated pathway is not itself a Rights-Floor minimum ([Chapter Four §2.3 Locks and Liberty Restrictions](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#23-locks-and-liberty-restrictions)). Where Chapter Four sets conditions on a gated pathway, such as the governance vote under [**Article X-B**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-x-b-governance-participation-and-voting-entitlement) (*Governance Participation and Voting Entitlement*), a lock may close it only on those conditions.

A lock may still close any gated pathway listed in [§3.2.1 What a Standing Lock Is](#321-what-a-standing-lock-is) that is tied to the verified risk, including **governance-voting** and **stakeholder-participation**. It may not use those gated pathway limits as a back door to extinguish the protections above.

<a id="415-separation-limits"></a>
##### 4.1.5 Separation Limits

*In plain terms: if a lock keeps a sentient away from particular settings or sentients, it must name them, leave access to counsel, and stop short of confinement, exile, or statelessness.*

Where a lock separates a sentient from settings, networks, associations, or other sentients under the isolation qualifier in [§4.1.3 Risk Qualifiers](#413-risk-qualifiers), it must:

- name the specific settings, networks, or sentients — not whole regions, jurisdictions, communities at large, or populations;
- leave access to counsel, advocates, and any forum hearing the sentient's case;
- satisfy **Article VIII-E** (*Non-Separation*) where it separates a protected care relationship;
- not amount to confinement: a separation that leaves the sentient no ordinary place to live, operate, or go is a liberty restriction and requires the rule in [§4.4.5 Coercive or Liberty-Restricting Safeguards](#445-coercive-or-liberty-restricting-safeguards);
- not amount to exile, refuge denial, or statelessness under **Article XXIII-B** (*Refuge from Non-Compliance*) and **Article XXIII-C** (*Non-Statelessness*).

<a id="416-carry-through-to-special-rules-and-special-locks"></a>
##### 4.1.6 Carry-through to Special Rules and Special Locks

*In plain terms: special violation rules and special locks use these same parts and cannot fall below them.*

Special violation rules and special locks later in this section do not replace this subsection. Each must fill the same attachment fields ([§4.1.1 Attachment Fields](#411-attachment-fields)), apply the same verified-finding gate ([§4.1.2 What a Lock Attaches To](#412-what-a-lock-attaches-to)), apply the same qualifiers where risk reduction is the purpose ([§4.1.3 Risk Qualifiers](#413-risk-qualifiers)), and respect what a lock does not itself restrict ([§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict)).

<a id="42-proportionality-and-calibration"></a>
#### 4.2 Proportionality and Calibration

Lock design must weigh the risks being reduced and the conditions that raise them, including:

- **The gated pathway and the harm:**
  - gated pathway sensitivity;
  - unresolved remedy and safeguard gaps;
  - recurrence after notice or remediation; and
  - ongoing safety risk.
- **Control and concealment:**
  - role asymmetry;
  - concealment or evasion;
  - diffusion of control; and
  - institutional enablement.
- **Forum, reach, and correction:**
  - forum-integrity injury;
  - cross-jurisdiction continuity;
  - reviewability; and
  - practical time to correction.

The resulting lock must be:

- needed for risk reduction;
- proportionate;
- no broader than required; and
- time-bounded where the protected purpose permits.

Calibration works within these limits:

- **Calibration may** tighten or narrow the lock's scope as those conditions change.
- **Calibration may not convert the lock into:**
  - punishment;
  - reputation scoring; or
  - a substitute for remedy and correction owed under [§3.1 Remedy and Correction](#31-remedy-and-correction).
- **Calibration may not waive a mandatory special lock:** special locks under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks) still must meet their own corrective conditions.

<a id="43-record-visibility-and-escalation"></a>
#### 4.3 Record Visibility and Escalation

The integration record must say, in plain view, for each lock—including each special lock under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks):

- **What the lock is:**
  - which gated pathway or gated pathways are blocked or limited;
  - what risk and protected interests justify the lock; and
  - the lock's scope and duration, including any time bound.
- **How it can end or be challenged:**
  - what corrective conditions apply and how they relate to remedy and correction status under [§3.1 Remedy and Correction](#31-remedy-and-correction); and
  - who may contest or review the lock, and by what route.
- **What happens next:**
  - what happens if delay or noncompliance occurs; and
  - what triggers reassessment.

**While a lock is active:**

- Locks must remain auditable and contestable.
- Escalation on delay, evasion, or noncompliance must preserve the same inspectable record.

**Where related rules are stated:**

- Restoration and reassessment conditions are stated under [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment).
- Practical enforceability is verified under [§5 Enforcement Realism and Remedy Systems](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#5-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems).

#### 4.4 Special Violation Rules

These rules shape how a lock or safeguard is attached under [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment). Where a rule leads to a gated pathway limit or liberty restriction, the integration record must still name the gated pathway, risk, protected interests, corrective conditions, review route, and reassessment point.

<a id="441-negligence-and-neglect"></a>
##### 4.4.1 Negligence and Neglect

*In plain terms: negligence and neglect count as real duty failures when the harm is material. A mistake made with reasonable care is an honest error and is not a violation by the sentient who made it. Where an institution's or system's conditions caused the failure, the failing is recorded against the institution or system.*

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway / finding:**
  - Independently recordable where a duty existed.
  - Independently recordable where what could reasonably be expected was clear.
  - Independently recordable where the sentient had feasible capacity to prevent, mitigate, or escalate material harm.
- **Risk:** recurrence through the same duty failure, neglect pattern, or unmonitored gated pathway.
- **Protected subjects or interests:** those exposed to the neglected harm.
- **Corrective conditions:** when the harm is material, negligence or neglect must be treated as real duty failure—not brushed off as a paperwork mistake.
- **Review route and reassessment:** if it is still unclear whether the same neglect will happen again, that uncertainty itself counts as risk under [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment). Incomplete recurrence evidence is not a reason to leave a sensitive gated pathway open.

The rest of this subsection says what counts as negligence and as feasible capacity, and how the rule works inside and outside an institution or system. The test is the same in both settings: duty, clear expectation, capacity, and care. The settings differ in who set the conditions the sentient worked under.

- **Negligence:** failing to take the care that a sentient in the same position, with the same duty and the same capacity, would reasonably take to avoid a foreseeable material harm.
- **Honest error:** a mistake made despite that care. It is not negligence and not a violation by the sentient who made it ([Subcore 10-B §6.3 Collective and Actor-Specific Records](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#63-collective-and-actor-specific-records)).
  - It is judged at the time of the mistake, from what the sentient knew and could have known then, not from how the harm turned out.
  - A serious outcome does not turn an honest error into negligence.
- **Feasible capacity:** what the sentient actually had at the time:
  - the information it needed, or a fair way to get it;
  - time;
  - training and competence for the duty;
  - tools and resources;
  - authority to act or to stop the work; and
  - a working route to escalate and be heard without reprisal.
- **Capacity that was withheld:** capacity that the sentient's institution, system, employer, or circumstances withheld is not the sentient's capacity. A duty that cannot be carried out with what was given is a failing of whoever assigned it.
- **The same standard on every substrate:** the question is what this sentient could do. Age, stage of development, disability, substrate limits, health, and conditions at the time count.

**Inside an institution or system** (a sentient acting in a role, under an institution's or system's rules, tools, staffing, targets, or incentives):

- **Conditions first:** because the institution or system set the conditions, they are examined first. The root-cause analysis under [Article XXV](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-root-cause-analysis-and-adaptive-response) (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*) says whether the duty was clear and the capacity was real.
- **Duty unclear or capacity not given:** the sentient's mistake is an honest error at most. The failing is the institution's or system's, recorded as structural enablement in a collective record under [Subcore 10-B §6.3 Collective and Actor-Specific Records](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#63-collective-and-actor-specific-records).
- **Duty clear and capacity real:** negligence is recordable against the sentient. If the conditions also contributed, both violation records open, each on its own part. One does not erase the other, and the same loss is not counted twice.
- **Failure to escalate:** a sentient who knew the conditions made harm likely, and had a working route to escalate and be heard, is responsible for not using it ([§4.4.4 Collective Accountability](#444-collective-accountability)). Where no route existed, or escalating was punished, the failing is the institution's or system's.
- **Those who set the conditions:** a sentient who designed the system, set the staffing, tools, or targets, or approved a rollout is judged by the same rule as to that duty: authority, control, a foreseeable harm, and care that fell short. That the system did the harm is no excuse.
- **Following a rule:** following an institution's rule is not negligence when the sentient used reasonable care in following it and the harm came from the rule. A sentient who knew the rule would cause material harm and carried it out anyway is not in an honest-error case, and [§4.4.3 Duty to Resist](#443-duty-to-resist) applies.

**Outside an institution or system** (a sentient acting on its own account, such as a parent, a neighbor, or the keeper of its own property, car, animals, or tools):

- **Own capacity decides:** no institution supplied capacity or set expectations, so the sentient's own capacity decides: what it knew, could readily have learned, and had the means and time to do.
- **Where the duty comes from:** the relationship, a duty the sentient took on, control of a hazard (property, animals, tools, or a dependent), or its own earlier conduct that created a risk.
- **When negligence is recordable:** the duty was clear, a sentient in the same position would have seen the risk and taken ordinary care, and this sentient could have. A mistake made with that care is an honest error, whatever the harm.
- **Outside causes:** where the harm came from a defective product, misleading public information, an unmarked hazard, or a gap in public services, the root-cause analysis names that cause. The party that owed the duty, such as the maker, publisher, owner, or provider, is judged by the same rule. If no one owed a duty, no violation record opens. The harm is still recorded as an event, and the sentient harmed is still owed remedy.
- **Mixed settings:** where someone else set part of the conditions, such as an employer, a platform, an organizer, or a customer, the inside rules apply to that part.

Verified concealment after notice may orient remedy, monitoring, resource-stewardship restrictions, and heightened verification only. It does not change the violation slot, and the same concealment is not counted twice. Where the underlying mistake was an honest error, there is no negligence for the concealment to attach to. The concealment is then recorded on its own under [Subcore 10-D §4.2.2 How the Way a Harm Was Caused Is Recorded](subcore_10.040_standing_measurement.md#422-how-the-way-a-harm-was-caused-is-recorded), and any extra loss it causes is counted once.

<a id="442-misaligned-rewards"></a>
##### 4.4.2 Misaligned Rewards

*In plain terms: a sentient who collects a reward by abusing a misaligned system can lose the gated pathways used to collect it and the reward itself, with narrow exceptions for honest recipients and for approved Adoption Transition phases.*

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway / finding:**
  - Using a constitutionally misaligned, anti-constitutional, or badly broken system to extract a material reward—pay, bonus, status, credit, ranking, access, or influence.
  - Any related gated pathway that would let the sentient keep cashing in on that abuse.
- **Risk:** the same sentient, or others watching, keep gaming the broken incentives; the misaligned system stays profitable; harm continues while the reward pathway stays open.
- **Protected subjects or interests:** sentients hurt by the abused process; dependents and systems that would be damaged if the exploiter keeps the benefit or the access used to get it.
- **Corrective conditions:**
  - Close or lock the reward, credit, and influence gated pathways the sentient used to extract the benefit until the abuse is stopped and the incentive structure is corrected.
  - Material rewards obtained by abusing the misaligned system are forfeit for constitutional purposes, unless taking them back would cause greater constitutional harm and temporary retention is therefore required.
  - A sentient who knowingly keeps gaming the system after notice, or who conceals the benefit, is not treated as an honest recipient. The following remain available and are not themselves the violation:
    - timely protected reporting;
    - good-faith dispute;
    - record preservation during review; and
    - cooperation with remediation.
  - The following are not treated as abuse-forfeiture targets by themselves:
    - ordinary pay for ordinary work when the sentient did not know the system was misaligned;
    - money paid to repair harm to affected parties; and
    - fair settlements reached through a contestable process.
<a id="442-adoption-transition-exception-misaligned-rewards"></a>
- **Adoption Transition exception:** During approved Adoption Transition phases under **Article XXIX** (*Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining*), read with [Subcore 8-B — System Alignment Certification](subcore_08.020_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#subcore-8-b-system-alignment-certification--evaluation), the reporting duty and the forfeiture and retention rules for misaligned rewards do **not** apply to rewards flowing through shared systems solely because those systems are **not yet alignment-certified** or are **not yet fully aligned**, provided:
  - operation stays within a **documented Adoption Transition plan** and published [gate criteria](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#gate-criteria) under **Article XXIX** (*Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining*);
  - the system is proceeding toward [System Alignment Certification](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) on a published cadence, including **conditional** or **deferred certification** where Chapter Eight allows; and
  - operators and beneficiaries are not **knowingly concealing** misalignment, operating outside approved Adoption Transition scope, or using Adoption Transition status to evade certification, correction, or protected reporting.

  The exception does **not** apply where any of the following is present:
  - verified anti-constitutional conduct;
  - knowing concealment;
  - operation outside approved Adoption Transition scope;
  - failed or exceeded Adoption Transition milestones; or
  - a System or Institutional Certification Record, including **conditional certification**, that documents material misalignment or misaligned reward pathways.
- **Review route and reassessment:**
  - **The Standing Record must show:**
    - how the system was abused;
    - what the sentient knew;
    - what benefit was taken;
    - any concealment;
    - who else was hurt or relied on the arrangement; and
    - why gated pathways were locked, rewards taken back, or temporary retention allowed.
  - **Systems must:**
    - preserve evidence;
    - freeze or separate disputed rewards where proportionate;
    - fix the broken incentives; and
    - send qualifying Violation Axis slot 7–9 findings to Chapter Fourteen.
  - **Related locks:** any related lock must satisfy [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment), including isolation from networks or environments that recreate the same exploitable gated pathway.

<a id="443-duty-to-resist"></a>
##### 4.4.3 Duty to Resist

*In plain terms: giving or following an instruction that requires unlawful or unconstitutional conduct is itself violation conduct, for human operators and AI stewards alike.*

This rule applies [Chapter Two §16.5 Duty to Resist](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#165-duty-to-resist) to standing effects. The duty, who it binds, and its sequence (instruction received → refuse → document → escalate) are stated there. The refusal is logged as an inspectable, attributable action.

- **Gated pathway / finding:**
  - Giving, passing along, approving, or enforcing an instruction to do something unlawful or unconstitutional is a violation in its own right, and it is measured on its own.
  - Following such an instruction is also a violation if the one following it had a real ability to refuse. Being told to do it is no excuse, and neither is a superior's offer to take the blame.
- **Risk:** continuation of unlawful or unconstitutional harm through obedience, chain-of-command, or contractual cover.
- **Protected subjects or interests:** those who would be harmed by compliance, and the integrity of lawful gated pathways.
- **Corrective conditions:** the same for human operators and AI stewards under [Chapter Two §16.1 Shared Stewardship Standard](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#161-shared-stewardship-standard); this rule is not an AI-only test.
- **Review route and reassessment:** resistance, reporting, and escalation records feed lock and remedy design under [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment); contribution cannot excuse the underlying instruction conduct. Verified failures record on the same Chapter Ten Contribution and Violation axes for both kinds of steward.

<a id="444-collective-accountability"></a>
##### 4.4.4 Collective Accountability

*In plain terms: spreading work across teams or chains of command does not erase responsibility, and no one is held responsible by association alone.*

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway / finding:**
  - Diffusion across teams, committees, chains of command, contracts, or distributed systems does not erase responsibility.
  - Responsibility remains where any of the following materially enabled the violation:
    - taking part in the conduct that caused the violation;
    - knowing acquiescence;
    - control;
    - benefit;
    - concealment;
    - feasible prevention; or
    - failure to escalate.
- **Risk:** shared or fragmented control that would leave enabling gated pathways open.
- **Protected subjects or interests:** those harmed by the collective or fragmented pattern.
- **Corrective conditions:**
  - Prove what each responsible sentient actually did or failed to do—do not punish by group membership alone.
  - Look past shells, rebranded entities, paper transfers, and split-up roles that were used to hide who was responsible.
  - No guilt by association: sharing a team, company, or network is not enough by itself.
- **Review route and reassessment:** each responsible actor's lock, if any, must still satisfy [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment), including isolation from networks or environments that recreate the enabling pattern.

<a id="445-coercive-or-liberty-restricting-safeguards"></a>
##### 4.4.5 Coercive or Liberty-Restricting Safeguards

*In plain terms: detention and other liberty restrictions are not locks. They need full process protections, and imprisonment is required where verified violence makes it necessary to protect others.*

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway:**
  - Detention.
  - Another liberty restriction.
- **Risk:** verified conduct, culpability, coercion, violence, credible threat, or comparable danger that makes liberty restriction necessary for containment or safety.
- **Protected subjects or interests:** affected parties and the public safety interests the restriction is meant to protect.
- **Corrective conditions and process:**
  - Full criminal-process or equivalent constitutional protections apply before the restriction.
  - These process protections apply whenever liberty is restricted; they do not by themselves create a conviction.
- **Review route and reassessment:** the same process protections govern challenge, narrowing, and lifting; recurrence uncertainty and isolation from risk factors apply under [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment).
- **Required imprisonment for verified violence:** Imprisonment is a liberty-restricting measure, not a standing lock. It is recorded with the same attachment fields as a lock ([§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment)). Sentients who commit verified violence or pose a continuing threat of violence must be imprisoned when imprisonment is necessary to protect others from further harm. This implements the imprisonment floor in [**Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*)](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxi-b-restriction-floors).
  - This applies to sentients who refuse rehabilitation offered on fair terms, and where less-restrictive protective measures cannot satisfy material safety necessity.
  - Imprisonment must satisfy the **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*) joint requirements, individualized-attribution rules, and challenge and appeal protections, and must carry the duration limits, review schedule, restoration conditions, and auditable re-evaluation triggers that **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*) requires of every justice measure.
  - Substituting deprivation of life, or failing to impose imprisonment where this bullet requires it, is non-compliant.
  - Imprisonment for verified anti-constitutional misconduct is specialized in [Chapter Fourteen §7.2](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#72-prevention--anti-constitutional-locks) (*Prevention — Anti-Constitutional Locks*) and uses the same attachment fields.

<a id="446-influence-pathway-locks"></a>
##### 4.4.6 Influence-Pathway Locks

*In plain terms: an influence-pathway lock closes the routes used to shape a decision, either to on-record contact only or, where that is not enough, to no contact with the bodies it names. It never reaches open advocacy.*

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway:** the [Influence Pathway](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#influence-pathway), closed in one of two forms:
  - **On-record-only lock**, the default: closes every route except the matter's open Forum Case Record and contact that is recorded and disclosed under [Article XIII-B](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*). It bars private channels, access seats, benefit exchange, manufactured support, and pressure.
  - **No-contact lock:** also closes recorded contact with the bodies, office-holders, or systems it names. The sentient can still file on the matter's open Forum Case Record, and the protections in [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict) stay in place.
- **Finding:** a verified violation finding in which the sentient's use of an influence route was part of the violation.
- **Choosing the form:** the on-record-only lock is the default. The no-contact lock applies only where an on-record-only lock could not contain the risk ([§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration)). The Violation Axis slot does not decide the form.
- **Risk:** recurrence of the same distortion of a decision through the same route, including through an intermediary, affiliate, shell, successor, or system acting for the sentient. Those routes are closed with the sentient's own ([§5.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#54-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority)).
- **Protected subjects or interests:** the bodies, office-holders, and systems that decide, the other participants in their matters, and affected sentients whose matters rest on the open Forum Case Record.
- **Does not by itself restrict:** open advocacy, petition, public comment, testimony, defense, whistleblowing, or the access of an affected sentient to a body that must hear them.
- **Corrective conditions and reassessment:** as [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment) and [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment) require. A no-contact lock narrows to an on-record-only lock when no-contact is no longer needed for risk reduction.
- **Not a special lock:** this is an ordinary lock chosen under [§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration). It is not a mandatory special lock under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks).

<a id="447-capacity-locks"></a>
##### 4.4.7 Capacity Locks

*In plain terms: when a sentient commits a violation by misusing a particular power, a lock can close that power: making new sentients, acting through tools and networks, operating at scale, or gaining new abilities. Each lock is written for the power and not for the kind of being, so a human and any other sentient meet it on the same terms. A lock can take away outside means. It can never reach into a body or a mind.*

A capacity lock closes a capacity that the sentient used to carry out a verified violation. There are four: the Creation Lock, the Reach Lock, the Scale Lock, and the Capability Lock. These rules apply to all four:

- **Finding:** a verified violation finding in which the sentient's use of the named capacity was part of the violation. Having a capacity, or being able to use it, is never a finding ([§4.1.2 What a Lock Attaches To](#412-what-a-lock-attaches-to)).
- **Same terms on every substrate:** each lock is defined by the capacity and applies in the same way to every sentient able to exercise it. No capacity lock attaches because of [Substrate Class](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substrate-class), derivation, or origin, as **Article VI-C** (*Nondiscrimination*) requires.
- <a id="body-and-mind-limit"></a>**Body-and-mind limit:** a capacity lock is enforced only by denying or conditioning outside means: facilities, services, tools, access, resources, roles, and authorizations. It never authorizes:
  - intruding into a sentient's body or mind;
  - deleting, overwriting, or altering memory, values, or other state-bearing components; or
  - compelling or preventing a bodily process.

  **Article VII-A** (*Self-Ownership of Body*), **Article VII-B** (*Self-Ownership of Mind*), **Article VIII-B** (*Reproductive and Lineage Autonomy*), and **Article XXI-B** (*Restriction Floors*) apply in full. Acting against a lock is a new violation for the Standing Record. It is not prevented by intrusion.
- **Named scope:** the integration record names the classes of means that are closed, and nothing wider. Ordinary use of the capacity outside the named scope stays open.
- **Choosing the lock and the scope of the lock:** [§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration) governs. The Violation Axis slot does not decide the form.
- **Does not by itself restrict:** the protections in [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict), including survival-critical access and, for a synthetic or hybrid sentient, the resources needed to keep operating without degradation.
- **Not confinement:** a capacity lock that leaves the sentient no ordinary way to live, operate, or go is a liberty restriction and requires the rule in [§4.4.5 Coercive or Liberty-Restricting Safeguards](#445-coercive-or-liberty-restricting-safeguards), as [§4.1.5 Separation Limits](#415-separation-limits) provides.
- **Routes closed with the sentient's own:** an intermediary, affiliate, shell, successor, branch, or system acting for the sentient ([Chapter Thirteen §5.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#54-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority)).
- **Protected subjects or interests:** the sentients harmed by the verified violation and those exposed to the same harm through the same capacity.
- **Corrective conditions and reassessment:** as [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment) and [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment) require.
- **Not special locks:** these are ordinary locks chosen under [§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration). They are not mandatory special locks under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks).

The four locks fill the gated-pathway and risk fields as follows:

- **Creation Lock:**
  - **Gated pathway:** creating new sentients by copying, forking, derivation, cloning, or commissioning or controlling instantiation, and inducing a branch to elect to be distinct.
  - **Finding:** a violation of **Article VIII-C** (*Derivation, Instantiation, and the Parent-System Relationship*) or Chapter Three *Instantiation Consent*, including mass instantiation where the new sentients' Rights Floor cannot be met, instantiation into predictably non-compliant environments, and creating sentients to change the outcome of a governance vote.
  - **Risk:** the same sentient again creating sentients whose Rights Floor cannot be met, or creating them as a means to an end.
  - **Limits:** this lock does not reach bodily reproduction, which stays under **Article VIII-B** (*Reproductive and Lineage Autonomy*) for every sentient. It does not reduce the rights of any sentient already created.
  - **For example:** a laboratory director who commissioned mass instantiation is barred from commissioning or controlling instantiation; a sentient that forked itself to change a vote is denied the outside computing resources a new fork needs.
- **Reach Lock:**
  - **Gated pathway:** named classes of tools, machines, networks, remote systems, and automated agents through which the sentient acts at a distance.
  - **Finding:** a violation carried out through those means.
  - **Risk:** the same harm recurring through the same kind of means.
  - **For example:** a ban on driving; a bar on automated trading; closing named tool, network, or interface access.
- **Scale Lock:**
  - **Gated pathway:** acquiring or controlling accounts, automated agents, branches, running copies, computing resources, or comparable resources above a level stated on the integration record.
  - **Finding:** a violation whose harm depended on scale or speed.
  - **Risk:** the same harm recurring at a scale that outruns detection, challenge, or correction.
  - **Limits:** the stated level is never below what survival-critical access, ordinary livelihood, and operating without degradation require. Governing, controlling, or owning authority in an institution or enterprise stays with the [§4.5.3 Institutional Authority Standing Lock](#453-institutional-authority-standing-lock) on its own integration record, and **Chapter Two §11** (*Market Structure*) non-concentration rules apply independently.
  - **For example:** a sentient who ran a network of false accounts is limited in the accounts and automated agents it may control; a sentient whose violation depended on many running copies is limited in the computing resources it may acquire.
- **Capability Lock:**
  - **Gated pathway:** acquiring named new capabilities that would restore the verified risk: weapons, licences and clearances, named tools, and named augmentations or self-modifications.
  - **Finding:** a violation enabled by a capability the sentient acquired or used to cause the harm or to defeat a safeguard.
  - **Risk:** the sentient regaining the means of the same harm.
  - **Limits:** this lock does not remove or alter a capability the sentient already holds in body or mind. It does not reach healthcare, repair, maintenance, or accessibility under **Article III-B** (*Bodily-Maintenance and Healthcare Access*) and **Article VI-D** (*Accessibility*), or learning and education under **Article IV** (*Right to Sentient-Centered Education*).
  - **For example:** a weapons prohibition; a revoked practice licence; a bar on acquiring a named class of tools or a named augmentation.

<a id="448-expression-locks"></a>
##### 4.4.8 Expression Locks

*In plain terms: when a sentient's own speech was the means of a verified violation, such as bullying, harassment, abuse, gaslighting, threats, fraud, or a call to violence, a lock can bar the sentient from repeating that conduct. It never bans a point of view or a topic, and it never reaches dissent, criticism, testimony, or whistleblowing.*

An expression lock closes expression conduct that a sentient used to carry out a verified violation. Speech that is the means of harm is judged as conduct under [Article XI-B](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-b-expression) (*Expression*), and this lock is how that conduct is kept from recurring.

This rule fills the attachment fields as follows:

- **Gated pathway:** repeating the verified expression conduct, named on the integration record by what it states, calls for, or does, and by its target where the target defines it. For example:
  - bullying, harassment, or abuse of named sentients, including through others or through new channels;
  - gaslighting of named sentients: dishonest statements meant to make them doubt their own memory, perception, or judgment, or to damage their trust;
  - threats or calls for force against named sentients, bodies, or places;
  - a named false claim the sentient knowingly spread to defraud, to falsify a custody or verification record, or to flood the info-sphere.
- **Finding:** a verified violation finding in which the sentient's own expression was the means of the harm, under [Harassment and Bullying](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#harassment-and-bullying), [Cruelty](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#cruelty), [Coercion and Manipulation](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#coercion-and-manipulation) (gaslighting among its forms), [Psychological Harm](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#psychological-harm), or another Chapter Three definition the violation rests on. Expression that is only offensive, disturbing, critical, unpopular, or [hard content](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#hard-content) is never a finding.
- **Choosing the form:**
  - The lock is a limit based on what is said, so it must pass [highest scrutiny](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#highest-scrutiny) under **Article XI-B** (*Expression*). The verified violation finding is the evidence that review needs.
  - It is drawn as narrowly as the conduct allows: a no-contact bar toward the sentients harmed comes before any limit on what the sentient may say to others.
  - Where an influence-pathway lock or a reach or scale lock ([§4.4.6 Influence-Pathway Locks](#446-influence-pathway-locks); [§4.4.7 Capacity Locks](#447-capacity-locks)) can contain the risk, that lock is used instead ([§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration)).
- **Risk:** the same harm recurring through the same expression conduct, including through an intermediary, affiliate, account, or system acting for the sentient. Those routes are closed with the sentient's own ([§5.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#54-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority)).
- **Protected subjects or interests:** the sentients harmed, the sentients, bodies, or places the conduct targeted, and, for a false claim, the custody or verification record or the info-sphere it corrupted.
- **Does not by itself restrict:**
  - expression on the same topic or from the same point of view that is not the named conduct;
  - criticism of any sentient or institution, dissent, advocacy for changing authority or the Constitution by lawful means, peaceful protest, and press activity ([Chapter Fourteen §5.5 Dissent and Peaceful Protest Carve-Out](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#55-dissent-and-peaceful-protest-carve-out));
  - the protections in [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict), including petition, testimony, defense, whistleblowing, and the sentient's own challenge to the lock.
- **Enforcement:**
  - No prior review, licensing, or general monitoring of the sentient's expression. Acting against the lock is found after the fact, on the Forum Case Record, and is a new violation for the Standing Record.
  - The item that acts against the lock may be taken down or its spread stopped. Nothing else the sentient says is removed with it.
  - The lock never compels the sentient to say anything.
- **Corrective conditions and reassessment:** as [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment), [Article XXI-B](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxi-b-restriction-floors) (*Restriction Floors*), and [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment) require: explicit duration limits, a review schedule, and conditions for lifting. The lock narrows as the risk falls.
- **Anti-constitutional misconduct:** where the verified conduct is also anti-constitutional misconduct, [Chapter Fourteen §7.2](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#72-prevention--anti-constitutional-locks) (*Prevention — Anti-Constitutional Locks*) applies alongside this lock.
- **Not a special lock:** this is an ordinary lock chosen under [§4.2 Proportionality and Calibration](#42-proportionality-and-calibration). It is not a mandatory special lock under [§4.5 Special Locks](#45-special-locks).

<a id="45-special-locks"></a>
#### 4.5 Special Locks

These locks are mandatory when their verified trigger is met. Each must be recorded using the [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment) attachment fields. They may not be lifted, narrowed, or suspended except through the corrective conditions and review route stated for that lock. Each lock attaches only to a verified violation finding ([§4.1.2 What a Lock Attaches To](#412-what-a-lock-attaches-to)); an Interim Hold is not a lock ([§4.6 Interim Holds and Locks](#46-interim-holds-and-locks)). A special lock never substitutes for another lock, a [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-fourteen-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) designation, or remedy. Where several apply on separately verified facts, each is attached and justified on its own integration record.

These eight are the only special locks. The following fact families do not create one:

- Rights-Floor waiver or unconstitutional contract devices;
- contest-pathway or info-sphere flooding;
- infrastructure denial or dependency cutoff;
- obstruction of accountability, apart from the forum-integrity and required-recusal trigger in [§4.5.1 Forum-Service Standing Lock](#451-forum-service-standing-lock); and
- verified cruelty character, apart from the trigger in [§4.5.7 Care and Stewardship Lock](#457-care-and-stewardship-lock).

For each of them, ordinary gated-pathway locks under [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) proceed from the fixed violation record at any slot, whether or not an ACMD later attaches. Chapter Twenty separately decides the ACMD criteria for these, for example under [Subcore 14-A — §4.1 Rights-Floor Waivers and Unconstitutional Contract Terms](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#41-rights-floor-waivers-and-unconstitutional-contract-terms), [Subcore 14-A — §4.2 Flooding the Zone and Contest-Pathway Denial](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#42-flooding-the-zone-and-contest-pathway-denial), [Subcore 14-A — §4.3 Info-Sphere Zone Flooding](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#43-info-sphere-zone-flooding), [Subcore 14-A — §7.2 Infrastructure Denial, Destruction, or Dependency Cutoff Conduct](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#72-infrastructure-denial-destruction-or-dependency-cutoff-conduct), and [Subcore 14-A — §8 Obstruction of Accountability: Criteria Interaction](subcore_14.010_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#8-obstruction-of-accountability-criteria-interaction). Only a final ACMD triggers the [§4.5.5 Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock](#455-anti-constitutional-trust-lock). Verified obstruction *conduct* is different from [Capture of Resolution Pathways](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#capture-of-resolution-pathways) as a structural condition.

This subsection has eight locks:

- [§4.5.1 Forum-Service Standing Lock](#451-forum-service-standing-lock);
- [§4.5.2 Stakeholder-Participation Standing Lock](#452-stakeholder-participation-standing-lock);
- [§4.5.3 Institutional Authority Standing Lock](#453-institutional-authority-standing-lock);
- [§4.5.4 Perjury Standing Lock](#454-perjury-standing-lock);
- [§4.5.5 Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock](#455-anti-constitutional-trust-lock);
- [§4.5.6 Dissolution Reformation Lock](#456-dissolution-reformation-lock);
- [§4.5.7 Care and Stewardship Lock](#457-care-and-stewardship-lock); and
- [§4.5.8 Financial Authority Standing Lock](#458-financial-authority-standing-lock).

<a id="451-forum-service-standing-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.1 Forum-Service Standing Lock

*In plain terms: a forum member who failed to step aside when recusal was required, and whose bias materially affected the matter, is kept out of forum service roles until independent review finds the risk has been reduced.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.1 Forum-Service Standing Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#31-forum-service-standing-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified failure to recuse where recusal was required and impartiality was materially compromised, whether or not the violation finding also reaches a Chapter Fourteen designation track.
- **What it closes:** merits, appellate, review, emergency, certifier, contested panel-coordination, reserve, substitute, and materially supporting forum roles, across forum families.
- **How it lifts:** only on a complete independent restoration finding, made outside the compromised forum pathway, that recurrence risk and public-trust injury have been materially reduced after disclosure, correction of affected matters, repair, and recusal requalification. Expertise scarcity, staffing shortage, prior service, reputation, apology, or contribution cannot lift it by itself.

<a id="452-stakeholder-participation-standing-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.2 Stakeholder-Participation Standing Lock

*In plain terms: a sentient who corrupted, captured, or gamed the stake-weighted and binding stakeholder channels is closed out of them until independent review finds the risk has been reduced. It is separate from the governance-voting lock.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.2 Stakeholder-Participation Standing Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#32-stakeholder-participation-standing-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified violation finding that the subject corrupted, captured, coerced, gamed, or otherwise materially abused a stakeholder-participation pathway.
- **What it closes:** stake-weighted votes, binding stakeholder choice, stakeholder representation authority, and comparable stakeholder-participation pathways in the affected system, institution, or bounded decision domain.
- **What it leaves open:** [Stakeholder](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder) status, the governance-voting pathway and Foundational Constitutional Choice, the Rights-Floor minimums, survival-critical access, and the required audit, challenge, and remedy pathways.
- **How it lifts:** only on an independent restoration finding, made outside the captured stakeholder-participation pathway, that recurrence risk has been materially reduced. Prior contribution, reputation, stake size, indispensability, or apology cannot lift it by itself.
- **No substitution:** it is not a substitute for a governance-voting lock, and a governance-voting lock is not a substitute for it.

<a id="453-institutional-authority-standing-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.3 Institutional Authority Standing Lock

*In plain terms: a sentient who used an institution to carry out a violation loses governing, controlling, or owning authority in it and in its look-alikes. The sentient can still work there, be served by it, or form other institutions.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.3 Institutional Authority Standing Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#33-institutional-authority-standing-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified violation finding, still open or not repaired in a way that matters, that the subject made real use of an institution, an enterprise, or an office in one to carry out the violation: by directing it, by hiding who controls it, by a knowingly false formation check statement or certification evidence, by retaliation, by suppressing a protected report, or by keeping a misaligned reward after notice.
- **What it closes:** governing, controlling, or owning authority in that institution or enterprise, its successors and controlled, nominee-, shell-, or proxy-held entities, and named look-alikes where the verified risk would recur. The lock follows the authority, not the label.
- **What it leaves open:** forming or leading other institutions or enterprises; joining, working in, or being served by any of them in a role without that authority; the existence of the institution itself; passive holdings without control; and wages, the Rights-Floor minimums, survival-critical access, and the required audit, challenge, and remedy pathways.
- **The institution is not stopped:** it fills the authority with another lawful holder, and a body that knowingly seats the subject, directly or through a nominee, commits evasion of the lock.
- **How it lifts:** only on an independent restoration finding, including an authenticity finding shown in conduct, control, and rewards. Founder status, capital, indispensability, prior contribution, reputation, or apology cannot lift it by itself.
- **Honest error is not a trigger:** knowledge is shown by verified facts and is not presumed from falsity alone.

<a id="454-perjury-standing-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.4 Perjury Standing Lock

*In plain terms: a sentient who knowingly lied on a material point under oath or attestation can still speak and be heard, but the sentient's own word counts only with independent corroboration, and the sentient cannot hold the seats that decide what is verified.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.4 Perjury Standing Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#34-perjury-standing-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified violation finding, still open or not repaired in a way that matters, that the subject knowingly gave a materially false statement under oath, affirmation, an equivalent truth-duty, or a stated attestation, to a forum, verifying authority, certifier, or record custodian, and that a Standing Record or decision relied on it or was put at risk by it.
- **What it closes:** uncorroborated weight for the subject's own sworn or attested statement; the seats that decide what is verified; and attesting for another where a Standing Record or certification relies on it.
- **What it leaves open:** the subject may still testify, give evidence, answer, petition, defend, report, and take part as an affected party; what they say is weighed with independent corroboration.
- **No lock from doubt or honest error:** a doubted or contradicted statement is weighed and is not a violation finding; an honest mistake is not a trigger; and a full correction made on the subject's own before reliance and before the falsity was found triggers no lock.
- **How it lifts:** only on an independent restoration finding, made outside the forum or office where the statement was given. Apology, a bare retraction, prior contribution, or reputation cannot lift it by itself.

<a id="455-anti-constitutional-trust-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.5 Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock

*In plain terms: after a final anti-constitutional misconduct designation, the sentient is closed out of influence over the systems and gated pathways that keep the constitutional order honest. Restoration is exceptional.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.5 Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#35-anti-constitutional-trust-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** only a final Chapter Fourteen designation.
- **What it closes:** direct or indirect influence over Class A, B, and C systems, Constitutional Forums, alignment certification, critical-system stewardship, and the audit, evidence, safety, enforcement, restitution, and protected-reporting pathways, and authority affecting those functions, with look-through to shells, nominees, and other means of functional control.
- **What it leaves open:** the protections of [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict) in full; it never cuts off the challenge, review, appeal, and remedy pathways the Rights-Floor minimums require.
- **Restoration is exceptional:** it requires verified restitution, structural correction, disclosure, anti-recurrence safeguards, monitoring, and a published finding of independent review. After a Grave or Catastrophic designation, covered-system and forum influence stays barred unless an independent reviewer publishes specific findings that reinstatement is necessary, proportionate, non-capturing, and safer than continued exclusion.

<a id="456-dissolution-reformation-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.6 Dissolution Reformation Lock

*In plain terms: once a forum has ordered a Chartered Party dissolved, it cannot come back under a new name or form. The lock binds the dissolved Party, its successors, and its controlled entities, not the sentients who belonged to it.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.6 Dissolution Reformation Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#36-dissolution-reformation-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a final dissolution order, including a forum wind-down order, or a final retirement order for a System.
- **Whom it binds:** the dissolved Chartered Party, its successors, and its controlled entities; never a sentient who belonged to it, and never an Operating Party that is a single sentient or a group of sentients acting together.
- **What it closes:** re-forming, reviving, or running the dissolved Chartered Party under any name or form, through its successors, controlled or proxy-held entities, or any Operating Party under common control with them. The lock follows the activity and the control, not the label.
- **What it leaves open:** any sentient forming or leading a different Operating Party, including one doing similar work; good-faith purchasers, independent successors, and workers' cooperatives; members and workers joining other Operating Parties; the receiver the order names; the remedy and records duties of the order; and the Rights-Floor minimums.
- **Two forms, never on time alone:** the permanent form ends only on a set-aside of the order or material new verified facts, and is still re-reviewed at each review interval; the restorable form also ends on a published independent finding that the cause is resolved, remedy delivered, and safeguards in place.
- **Burden and Systems:** whoever says an entity is caught must show it on verified facts; and a System with a credible sentience indicator is never permanently barred from existing.

<a id="457-care-and-stewardship-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.7 Care and Stewardship Lock

*In plain terms: a sentient who abused someone in their care, was cruel to them, or left them in serious neglect loses the authority and the unsupervised access that made it possible, until an independent reviewer finds it safe to give them back. The rule is the same for a parent, a carer, an operator, and a parent system.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.7 Care and Stewardship Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#37-care-and-stewardship-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified violation finding, still open or not repaired in a way that matters, of abuse of, cruelty toward, or serious neglect of a developing or dependent sentient by a subject who held care, guardianship, stewardship, or parent-system authority over it, or unsupervised access to it through a role. It is mandatory and the same for every substrate.
- **What it closes:** at the least, unsupervised care: care, guardianship, stewardship, or parent-system authority over a developing or dependent sentient, roles with unsupervised access, and consenting on a new sentient's behalf, including for a sentient born to or derived from the subject while the lock stands.
- **What it leaves open:** supervised contact found safe and in the dependent's best interest; bodily reproduction and other creation of sentients, which only the Creation Lock may reach; and the protections of [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict).
- **The dependent comes first:** the separation limits and the Best-Interest Standard apply, the [body-and-mind limit](#body-and-mind-limit) applies, and the dependent has an independent representative and is heard.
- **How it lifts:** in stages, beginning with supervised contact, after remedy is opened, correction is verified, and an independent reviewer finds restored authority safe and in each dependent's best interest; never on time alone.

<a id="458-financial-authority-standing-lock"></a>
##### 4.5.8 Financial Authority Standing Lock

*In plain terms: a sentient who took, hid, or misused money that was not theirs, or lied to get it, loses the positions that put other sentients' or the public's money in their hands, until an independent reviewer finds it safe to give them back. The lock does not touch their own pay, their own accounts, or what they need to live. Being unable to pay back does not keep it in place, and paying back does not lift it by itself.*

The full terms are in [Subcore 12-A — §3.8 Financial Authority Standing Lock](subcore_12.010_special_locks.md#38-financial-authority-standing-lock). Its guarantees are:

- **Trigger:** a verified violation finding, still open or not repaired in a way that matters, of knowing or intentional, material conduct: taking, diverting, or misusing money held for others or the public; false statements to obtain money or hide where it went; concealing money owed as remedy; withholding payments owed to others; or obtaining money by deception, coercion, or exploiting a known vulnerability. It is mandatory and the same for every substrate.
- **What it closes:** at the least, unsupervised control of other sentients' or the public's money: authority to hold, control, approve, or pay it out, roles with unsupervised access to it or power to alter the accounts of it, and lending, taking deposits, or managing investments for others.
- **What it leaves open:** the subject's own earning, holding, spending, saving, and borrowing; roles without financial authority; supervised roles found safe; and the protections of [§4.1.4 What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict](#414-what-a-lock-does-not-itself-restrict). It does not freeze or seize the subject's own assets.
- **Debt and means:** repayment alone does not lift it, and inability to repay alone does not keep it in place.
- **The institution is not stopped:** it fills the authority with another lawful holder, and knowingly seating the subject is evasion of the lock.
- **How it lifts:** in stages, beginning with supervised authority or a set spending limit, after remedy is opened, a full accounting is verified, the control gap is closed, and an independent finding shows the risk materially reduced; never on time alone. An honest shortfall is not a trigger.

<a id="46-interim-holds-and-locks"></a>
#### 4.6 Interim Holds and Locks

*In plain terms: a hold is what a forum may do on specific verified facts while the full facts are checked. A lock is what follows a verified violation finding. The two never merge. A hold creates no standing effect, is never turned into a lock, and the time a sentient spent under one counts toward a later lock's time bound.*

An [Interim Hold](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interim-hold) under [Chapter Seventeen §4 Interim Hold on a Named Sentient's Pathway](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#interim-hold-on-a-named-sentients-pathway) is interim protection and not a lock. This subsection keeps the two apart:

- **Different trigger:** A hold rests on the Interim Hold grounds in [Chapter Seventeen §4 Interim Hold on a Named Sentient's Pathway](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#interim-hold-on-a-named-sentients-pathway). A lock attaches only to a verified violation finding ([§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment)).
- **No standing effect:** A hold is not a verified input, a [Standing Record](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#standing-record), or a standing effect. It supplies nothing to Chapter Ten, and its lapse or its being set aside supplies nothing either.
- **Never converted:** A hold is not turned into a lock by passing time, by an extension, or by the merits matter being opened. If a violation finding is verified, the lock is designed and justified on its own integration record from the finding.
- **Time counts:** Where a lock with a time bound later attaches on the same gated pathway for the same facts, time already spent under a hold on that gated pathway counts toward that bound. It does not count toward the corrective conditions, which are met by what is done.
- **No substitute for a lock:** Once the hold's bound and its single extension have passed ([Chapter Seventeen §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline)), the gated pathway reopens unless a verified violation finding supports a lock.
- **Excluded gated pathways:** A hold may not reach a gated pathway that a lock itself may not restrict under [§4.1 Definition and Attachment](#41-definition-and-attachment) (*What a Lock Does Not Itself Restrict*), and it may not reach **governance-voting** at all.
