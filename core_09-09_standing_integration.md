Chapter Nine is the constitutional owner of **standing integration and standing effects**, including attachments, descriptors, standing locks, remedy assignment, and enforcement anchors.

<br>

*In plain terms: Under the [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad) and [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims), this file **integrates** verified standing — the middle of the pipeline. Chapter Eight records and measures verified contribution and verified violation findings on separate axes using one proportional LEQU scale; this chapter explains how those records affect trust, roles, recognition, restriction, remediation, safeguards, and real-world enforcement without hollowing tetrad duties below [material stake](core_00_preamble.md#material-stake). Positive contribution can matter, but it does not erase unresolved violation findings. For a Violation Axis `s` = 7–9 finding, Chapter Ten decides only whether the matching anti-constitutional-misconduct designation attaches; it does not assign or change the slot. The recurring examples are practical: recent verified help counts more than stale credit; unresolved violations stay live; consequences must fit the offense and leave a lawful path back; harm must actually be repaired; remedy systems need capacity; and evasion through borders, shells, or relabeling does not defeat accountability.*

*In plain terms: Chapter Eight answers **Question 1 — what happened?** and **Question 2 — how good or bad was it?** This chapter answers **Question 3 — what happens because of it?** It explains when verified contribution can support trust or recognition, when verified violations require remedies or restrictions, how restoration works, and where the detailed rules live. The two primary standing pathways are **competency gates** for positive readiness and **standing locks** for unresolved violations.*

**Principle-layer foundation.** [Stewardship and Distributed Understanding](core_01_c_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#9-stewardship-and-distributed-understanding) (**Chapter One**, **§12**) is the normative foundation for this standing system. Trust-, role-, and recognition-eligibility must track proportionate understanding of systems that materially affect sentients, plus consequential stewardship and repair contribution. They must remain auditable and contestable. They must not rest on opaque credentialism, symbolic participation, or inaccessible specialization alone.

Standing consequences may affect eligibility for roles, delegated authority, or enhanced trust pathways. These standing-effect pathways measure trust, role, recognition, restriction, remediation, and review consequences under constitutional principles, rights articles, and definitions. More than one pathway may apply to the same sentient, institution, record, assessment, action, pattern, or bounded event. They must not suspend, waive, extinguish, or reduce the **Rights-Floor minimums** stated in **Article V** (*Equal Basic Rights*), including inherent dignity, equal moral standing, minimum subsistence access, and required challenge, review, appeal, and remedy pathways.

Positive standing effects run through [**competency gates**](#competency-gates) (**section 1.2**). Restrictive standing effects run through [**standing locks**](#standing-locks-in-the-standing-model) (**section 1.3**). Satisfying a competency gate does not waive an applicable standing lock. The no-offset rule in [Chapter Eight section 2.2](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#22-linked-records-and-no-offset-bridge) still applies.

**Three-question evaluation discipline.** The overview table and reading order appear in [Chapter Eight §1.1](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#11-three-question-evaluation-discipline). This chapter owns **Question 3** on both axes:

| **Axis** | **Question 1 (Chapter Eight §2)** | **Question 2 (Chapter Eight §4)** | **Question 3 (this chapter)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Violation** | What happened? → verified violation record | What integrated verified constitutional loss occurred? → Violation Axis impact slot under Chapter Eight §7 | What happens because of it? → remedies, safeguards, and [standing-lock design](#310-non-recurrence-lock-dimensions) |
| **Contribution** | What happened? → verified contribution record | What integrated verified constitutional benefit occurred? → Contribution Axis impact slot under Chapter Eight §7 | What happens because of it? → recognition and [competency-gate design](#210-safe-delegation-competency-gate-dimensions) |

**Standing integration order.** Under [**section 4.1**](#38-standing-integration-contribution-and-violation-nature), answer the violation side of **Question 3** first by assigning remedies, safeguards, and standing locks from verified **violation standing records**. Then apply contribution-side **Question 3** competency gates only where applicable standing locks permit the pathway. Question 2 contribution remains visible throughout but does not waive locks, lower violation slots, or offset violation severity.

**Informal contribution and standing.** Conduct that advances constitutional outcomes through **informal**, **peer**, **neighbor**, **mutual-aid**, or other **non-official** community pathways may count toward **contribution state** and positive standing recognition. It may count as fully as formal work, and in some factual patterns more. The same **demonstrability**, **auditability**, and **contestability** standards in **Chapter Eight sections 2 and 3.7.1** apply. Sentients **need not** work within official channels to be a **material** asset to their communities or to earn trust- and recognition-eligible credit under this chapter.

*In plain terms: a competency gate opens a role or trust pathway when someone has shown the relevant competence and experience. It is the positive counterpart to a standing lock, but it cannot become insider gatekeeping or a credential monopoly.*

<a id="competency-gate-definition-and-access"></a>

##### 1.2.1 Definition and access pathways

A **competency gate** is a published, auditable, contestable readiness threshold. When verified competence, experience, contribution records, and current good-standing criteria satisfy role-proportionate requirements, it may open access to:

- trust-sensitive roles;
- delegated authority;
- oversight eligibility;
- enhanced recognition; and
- progressively consequential stewardship responsibility.

<a id="competency-gate-verified-inputs"></a>

##### 1.2.2 Verified inputs and proportionality

Competency gates implement positive standing recognition and role eligibility. They may use verified inputs that are current enough for the role and traceable to a **contribution standing record** or other constitutionally valid readiness record, including:

- contribution records;
- demonstrated skill;
- relevant experience;
- training;
- role-specific qualification criteria; and
- observed reliability.

They must remain proportional to role impact, dependency, and risk.

<a id="competency-gate-non-gatekeeping"></a>

##### 1.2.3 Non-gatekeeping and inclusive experience

A competency gate must not operate as:

- arbitrary gatekeeping;
- opaque credentialism;
- reputation substitution;
- social-prestige screening;
- insider sponsorship; or
- exclusion by inaccessible specialization.

The following kinds of experience must count where they satisfy the same demonstrability, auditability, and contestability standards as formal institutional experience:

- informal experience;
- unpaid experience;
- peer-organized experience;
- mutual-aid experience;
- maintenance work;
- repair work;
- teaching; and
- community stewardship.

<a id="competency-gate-standing-lock-interface"></a>

##### 1.2.4 When a standing lock still applies

*In plain terms: meeting the readiness bar is not enough if an unresolved violation is still holding the door shut — and passing the gate never wipes that violation off the record.*

A competency gate may support trust-, role-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-linked standing only where no applicable **standing lock** is still blocking that pathway.

Passing the gate does not:

- waive Rights Floors;
- erase an unresolved violation finding;
- lower a Violation Axis measurement; or
- replace required correction, remediation, safeguards, review, or lawful supersession.

*In plain terms: a standing lock is the model's hold on trust-, role-, authority-, credit-, or recognition-linked upside while a verified violation remains unresolved. Restrictions must fit the verified impact, the protected pathway, and the needed remedy or safeguard. Character descriptors such as negligence, concealment, coercion, or intent may shape safeguards and review, but never move the Chapter Eight impact slot. Good contribution stays visible, but it decides what that contribution cannot unlock yet.*

<a id="standing-lock-definition-and-attachment"></a>

##### 1.3.1 Definition and attachment

A **standing lock** is the restrictive counterpart to a **competency gate**.

While a verified violation finding still requires correction, remediation, safeguards, review, lawful supersession, or other constitutionally valid resolution, a standing lock may:

- prevent a trust-sensitive pathway from opening; or
- limit how far that pathway may open.

Standing locks attach to unresolved verified violation findings. They must not attach to:

- identity;
- status;
- reputation;
- accusation; or
- generalized disfavor.

Each standing lock must identify:

- the standing effect blocked or limited;
- the protected subjects or interests;
- the corrective condition;
- the review path; and
- the reassessment point.

Each standing lock must remain necessary, proportionate, auditable, contestable, time-bounded where required, and compatible with **Article XXIII** (*Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality*).

<a id="standing-lock-proportionality-and-calibration"></a>

##### 1.3.2 Proportionality and calibration

Standing locks must be proportionate to the verified **Violation Axis impact slot**, separately recorded **violation nature**, the protected pathway, current remedy status, and any linked **contribution state** only insofar as that contribution bears on repair capacity, safeguard reliability, non-recurrence, or least-restrictive reassessment. Contribution must not offset, waive, average down, or substitute for unresolved violation findings. A minimal-impact finding alone does not justify durable exclusion; any restriction must rest on verified impact and independently applicable Question 3 conditions.

<a id="standing-lock-record-visibility-and-escalation"></a>

##### 1.3.3 Record visibility and escalation

Impact, pattern, concealment, and remedy status must remain visible as separate fields in the record. Minor record defects, repeated failures after notice, identity concealment, and serious rights violations are not equivalent for standing-effect purposes. Recurrence, concealment, coercion, negligence, intent, and comparable character facts may change scrutiny, safeguards, remedy, or lock design only where independently verified; they never raise or lower the LEQU impact slot.

<a id="standing-lock-contribution-visibility"></a>

##### 1.3.4 Contribution visibility and slot-band map

Contribution state recorded in linked **contribution standing records** remains visible while a lock is active. It may support repair credibility, remedial capacity, learning, restitution, safeguard implementation, or later reassessment, but it cannot waive the lock, dilute the verified finding, lower the Violation Axis slot, or substitute reputation for remedy. The detailed slot-band lock map appears in **section 4.3**.

<a id="forum-service-standing-lock-for-recusal-failure"></a>

##### 1.3.5 Forum-Service Standing Lock for Verified Recusal Failure

A verified finding that a forum member, panelist, certifier, emergency adjudicator, reserve adjudicator, or comparable forum decision-maker failed to recuse when recusal was required and impartiality was materially compromised creates a **forum-service standing lock**. The lock protects the lawfulness of benches, public trust in forum independence, affected parties' challenge rights, evidence integrity, remedy timing, and the forum system's ability to avoid judging through concealed or unresolved bias.

While active, the lock bars service in forum roles that exercise, shape, certify, or materially support merits authority. Covered roles include adjudicative panels, appellate or secondary-review panels, Constitutional review panels, emergency merits roles, certifier roles, panel-coordinator functions for contested recusal or inability-to-form decisions, and reserve or substitute adjudicator service. The lock applies across forum families unless the restoration record lawfully narrows the scope with reasons showing that narrower protection is sufficient.

The restitution and restoration path is intentionally demanding. Reassessment may begin only after an independent review record verifies, at minimum:
- full disclosure of the recusal failure, the conflict or compromised-impartiality basis, and all affected matters known after reasonable inquiry;
- correction, vacatur, rehearing, notice, evidence preservation, or other practical repair for proceedings materially tainted by the failure where feasible;
- restitution, remediation, or restoration for affected parties, institutions, records, or forum pathways harmed by the failure;
- cooperation with integrity, disciplinary, standing-record, and remedial review, including production of records needed to test the scope of the failure;
- completed conflict, independence, role-separation, and recusal-process requalification appropriate to future forum service;
- a demonstrated period of compliance in non-merits, supervised, or otherwise lower-risk roles where lawful and proportionate; and
- an independent finding that recurrence risk, dependency risk, and public-trust injury have been materially reduced.

Prior service, expertise scarcity, staffing shortage, contribution state, reputation, apology, or generalized remorse cannot by itself lift, narrow, or suspend the lock. If the verified failure was intentional, concealed, repeated, retaliatory, or materially outcome-affecting, the reviewing forum must apply heightened scrutiny and may require a durable or indefinite bar from merits service unless and until the strict restoration record above is satisfied and **Article XXIII** (*Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality*) permits reinstatement.

<a id="anti-constitutional-trust-lock"></a>

##### 1.3.6 Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock

A final Chapter Ten designation of **Serious**, **Grave**, or **Catastrophic anti-constitutional misconduct** creates an **Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock**. The lock attaches because Chapter Ten established the designation for an existing `s` = 7, `s` = 8, or `s` = 9 impact finding, not because Chapter Ten assigned or changed that slot. The lock protects constitutional operation, Rights-Floor governance, Class A/B/C system integrity, forum independence, public trust, and the community's ability to prevent recurrence of tetrad capture.

While active, the lock bars the responsible sentient, group, institution, successor vehicle, controlled entity, or materially coordinated proxy from any role or influence that would exercise, shape, certify, fund, conceal, supervise, or materially steer:
- **Class A**, **Class B**, or **Class C** systems under **CS-4**;
- constitutional forum service, forum administration materially affecting merits, appellate or review panels, certifier roles, emergency forum roles, reserve adjudicator service, or contested panel-coordination functions;
- constitutional alignment recognition, system validation or revalidation, critical-system stewardship, safety or access-continuity governance, incident-response authority, audit custody, evidence custody, restricted-evidence access, or material classification decisions;
- appointment, removal, promotion, discipline, budget, procurement, ownership, voting-control, board, committee, standards-setting, ranking, access-allocation, model-governance, or policy-setting authority for Class A/B/C systems or constitutional forums;
- enforcement, prosecutorial, investigative, remediation, restitution, or protected-reporting pathways for anti-constitutional misconduct or Rights-Floor violations.

The lock reaches indirect influence as well as formal office. A locked actor must not route control through shell entities, nominees, advisory capture, funding leverage, contractual vetoes, information chokepoints, data custody, reputation systems, technical standards, model weights, privileged access, procurement conditions, or comparable mechanisms that materially steer a covered system or forum pathway. Ordinary speech, petition, testimony, personal defense, whistleblowing, participation as an affected party, Rights-Floor minimums, and survival-critical access remain protected unless an independent justice process imposes a separate lawful restriction under **Article XXIII** (*Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality*).

Additional consequences may be required where proportionate to the verified misconduct:
- public or stakeholder-visible notice of the lock, its scope, and its review path;
- preservation and transfer of records, credentials, keys, logs, models, funds, contracts, or operational access needed to prevent continued control;
- removal from covered roles and suspension of appointment, procurement, contracting, certification, voting-control, board, committee, or beneficial-control rights tied to covered systems;
- disgorgement, restitution, compensation, repair funding, or successor-responsibility measures for benefits or control obtained through the misconduct;
- independent monitoring, reporting, access-continuity safeguards, and no-contact or non-interference orders protecting witnesses, reviewers, affected parties, and remediation staff;
- look-through restrictions on successor entities, affiliates, shells, relabeling, or coordinated substitutes where evasion risk is verified;
- referral for dissolution, receivership, reconstitution, or structural separation where an institution or system was itself the vehicle of misconduct.

Restoration from an Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock is exceptional. Reassessment may begin only after an independent review record verifies full restitution or lawful substitute restoration where full restitution is impossible; correction or replacement of tainted governance, records, systems, and decisions; disclosure and cooperation sufficient to map the misconduct and affected dependencies; implementation of durable safeguards against recurrence and proxy control; completion of any required requalification, monitoring, or supervised lower-risk service; and a finding that renewed influence over the requested pathway is compatible with Rights Floors, Class A/B/C system integrity, and public trust. For **Grave** or **Catastrophic anti-constitutional misconduct**, covered-system and forum influence remains barred unless the reviewing forum makes specific, published findings that reinstatement is necessary, proportionate, non-capturing, and safer than continued exclusion for the protected constitutional interests.

Apply **Question 2** dimensions under the [Chapter Eight §4.2 Violation table](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#violation-table-q2-severity-input-dimensions), then assign the one **Violation Axis** slot whose [§7 unified proportional LEQU band](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale) contains the integrated verified loss. Record process / response character separately; it does not move the slot.
3. Apply **Question 3** dimensions under [**Table B**](#violation-table-q3-non-recurrence-dimensions) and the [**Standing lock context compass**](#standing-lock-context-compass) to assign remedies, safeguards, and standing locks under [**section 4.2**](#62-joint-assessment-escalation-constraints-and-scrutiny) and [**§11.3**](#standing-integration-slot-band-standing-locks).
4. Record **dual-use** integration hooks under Question 3 where verified facts warrant. Do **not** double-count them into a composite score.

Dimensions decompose verified findings; they do **not** replace the Violation Axis ladder, form a net score, or permit contribution to offset violation severity.

Assign them from **verified violation records** using [**Table B**](#violation-table-q3-non-recurrence-dimensions) and this compass. The Question 2 impact slot is one proportional input to Question 3. Separately verified character may change affected pathways, scrutiny, remedies, safeguards, or duration subject to necessity and proportionality; it must not change the verified LEQU impact slot.

| **Context / trigger** | **Lock type or posture** | **Primary standing effect** | **Typical slot relation** (non-exclusive) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open correction or audit obligation on a verified violation record | **Correction Lock** (contextual posture) | Baseline trust may remain available while correction proceeds; correction obligation stays independently enforceable | Available at **any slot** when correction is needed; slot reflects impact, not correctability |
| Unremedied substantive impact, duty failure, preventable harm, or recurrence risk | **Restriction Lock** (contextual posture) | Trust-, role-, or recognition-linked upside limited until remediation, supervision where needed, and review | Available at **any slot** when necessary and proportionate |
| Verified coercion, liberty risk, violence, or safeguard-process need | **Safeguard Lock** (contextual posture) | Heightened scrutiny and required safeguards control upside; contribution cannot soften criminal-process or equivalent protections | Available at **any slot**; the descriptor does not move the slot |
| Final Chapter Ten anti-constitutional-misconduct designation attached to an existing `s` = 7–9 impact finding | **Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock** | Bars covered-system and constitutional-trust influence, including roles or material influence over **Class A**, **Class B**, or **Class C** systems, constitutional forums, alignment recognition, critical-system stewardship, and anti-constitutional accountability pathways until strict independent restoration is verified | Mandatory on final Chapter Ten designation; Chapter Ten does not assign the underlying slot |
| Specific trust-, role-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-sensitive pathway at issue | **Pathway-scoped lock** | Blocks or limits only the identified pathway(s) named in the lock record | Any band; scopes **effect**, not necessarily severity |
| Verified dishonest forum disclosure omission with panel-lawfulness, evidence, remedy, or contestability injury | **Forum-integrity escalated lock** | May justify heightened scrutiny or broader pathway safeguards compared with a non-forum violation of equal LEQU impact | Read [§4.2.1](#421-forum-disclosure-and-recusal-process-impact); disclosure character never moves the slot |
| Verified failure to recuse where recusal was required and impartiality was materially compromised | **Forum-service standing lock** | Bars forum merits, appellate, review, emergency, certifier, contested panel-coordination, reserve, or substitute service until strict independent restoration is verified | Mandatory pathway-scoped lock; escalates under concealment, repetition, retaliation, outcome effect, or Chapter Ten routing |
| Anti-constitutional misconduct, repeated violence, or unresolved recurrence risk making ordinary participation unsafe | **Protective participation lock** | Protective removal from society, ordinary communal participation, trust-sensitive roles, or comparable protected settings until restitution, remediation, safeguards, and review conditions are met | Usually **Safeguard** or high-impact protective posture; **Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock** only on final Chapter Ten designation |
| Institution is the vehicle of anti-constitutional misconduct, repeated violence, capture, evasion, or structurally unremediable harm | **Institutional vehicle lock** | Suspension, receivership, successor-control restrictions, or dissolution, including bars on reconstitution through shell entities or relabeling | Independent of individual sentient slot; tied to institutional record |
| Same kind of violation repeated by the same sentient, group, or institution after otherwise completed restitution or remediation | **Recurrence-persistence lock** | Lock persists after remediation is otherwise complete, only to the extent necessary, proportionate, reviewable, and protective | Pattern may sit at or below prior slot; lock duration may exceed baseline band expectation |
| Concealment, evasion, shell structure, identity fragmentation, or repeated failure after notice | **Concealment / evasion escalated lock** | Escalated scrutiny, look-through accountability, or durable restriction tied to verified concealment or evasion | May justify broader safeguards or longer persistence than impact alone would suggest, but never a different slot ([**§1.3.3**](#standing-lock-record-visibility-and-escalation)) |
| Cross-border flight, relocation, forum shopping, or distributed operation threatening remedy or review | **Cross-jurisdiction continuity lock** | Verified remedy, lock, safeguard, or review obligation remains actionable across applicable boundaries | Any slot; continuity obligation independent of severity reclassification ([**section 5.3**](core_09-09_standing_integration.md#10-enforcement-realism-anchors) — *Cross-jurisdiction continuity*) |

Read [**Table B**](#violation-table-q3-non-recurrence-dimensions) for the full Question 3 dimension set.*

*Several labels may attach to the same action, pattern, or bounded event. They describe verified **violation nature**; they do **not** raise or lower the Chapter Eight impact slot. For an existing `s` = 7–9 finding, they also do not by themselves establish the Chapter Ten designation. These **attachment categories** apply only when **violation nature** is verified. They are recorded separately from the **Chapter Eight §7** impact slot: one tracks *what kind* of violation, the other tracks proportional LEQU impact. **Remedial character** may attach at any verified impact where harm or duty failure calls for repair; it does **not** by itself lower the slot, waive another applicable lock, or erase concurrent constitutional-floor, safeguard, or anti-constitutional attachment.

**Q3 outputs (decisions, not dimensions).**

- **Lock posture** (Correction / Restriction / Safeguard, or designation-specific Anti-Constitutional) plus context overlays from the context compass
- **Pathway scope** — which standing effects are blocked or limited
- **Corrective conditions** — remediation, restitution, safeguards, and review required before reassessment
- **Remedy obligations** — acknowledgment, repair, restoration, compensation, or substitute safeguards for affected sentients under [**section 3.11**](#redress-remedy-and-restoration-in-the-standing-model)
- **Duration posture** — time-bounded, recurrence-persistence, or protective-until-safe
- **Reassessment trigger** — verified completion of corrective conditions under the applicable review path

Question 3 violation dimensions operate on **violation standing records only**. **Contribution state** may support repair credibility under [**§1.3.4**](#standing-lock-contribution-visibility) but is not an input to lock assignment ([**section 4.2**](#62-joint-assessment-escalation-constraints-and-scrutiny)).

Fixing the system does not by itself make harmed sentients whole; making harmed sentients whole does not by itself stop recurrence.*

Once a **violation standing record** carries verified findings that materially harmed sentients or violated rights, this chapter assigns **remedy obligations** under [Redress and Remediation](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#redress-and-remediation-constitutional) as standing-model outputs — not as informal goodwill, dispute settlement, or reputation management separate from the record.

**Correction and remedy are parallel outputs.**

- **Correction** — change conduct, systems, safeguards, or role eligibility so the verified failure does not recur; expressed through standing locks, corrective conditions, and remediation orders under **sections 1.3**, **3.10**, and **3.3**.
- **Remedy** — reduce ongoing rights burdens and material harm for affected sentients through acknowledgment, repair, restoration, compensation, substitute safeguards where literal repair is impossible, or comparable proportionate redress under [Redress and Remediation](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#redress-and-remediation-constitutional).

- affected parties and material harms verified;
- remedy type and scope assigned;
- commencement milestones and review path;
- current remedy status, including partial completion, deferral where lawful, or non-compliance; and
- linkage to any standing lock, corrective condition, or forum record supervising implementation.

##### 3.12.1 Reporting duty and exclusions

*In plain terms: if you know a reward came from a bad process and you keep it without reporting, that can count as a violation. Report in good faith and you are protected; hide it and you are not.*

**Reporting duty.** A sentient who **knowingly** accepts or retains a material reward from a misaligned process or system after sufficient notice of the misalignment violates this subsection unless they timely report through [Protected Reporting (Whistleblowing)](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#protected-reporting-whistleblowing) or comparable lawful pathways within tier-appropriate windows under [Timeliness](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#timeliness-constitutional).

The reporting duty does **not** treat a sentient as knowingly keeping a misaligned reward when the sentient:

- disputes the reward in good faith through lawful process;
- preserves records while review is still open;
- cooperates with remediation; or
- reports on time through protected reporting or a comparable lawful pathway.

Retention without reporting is also **not** knowing acceptance when **§3.12.2** allows retention because taking the reward back would cause more constitutional harm than leaving it in place.

##### 3.12.2 Forfeiture and retention

*In plain terms: if you got paid, promoted, or credited through a misaligned or corrupt process, that reward is not yours to keep by default — unless taking it back would do more harm than keeping it.*

**Forfeiture default.** Material rewards obtained through constitutionally misaligned incentive structures, anti-constitutional conduct, or materially defective processes are **forfeit** for constitutional purposes. Question 3 consequences may include suspension, segregation, clawback, correction, or denial of recognition. The separate Question 2 rule denying contribution credit remains in [Chapter Eight §4.1](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#41-contribution-magnitude-input-dimensions).

**Proportionality limit.** Forfeiture or revocation is not harm-maximization by default. It proceeds only where [Necessity](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#necessity) and [Proportionality](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#proportionality) show that retention would cause greater constitutional harm than proportionate revocation. Legitimate third-party reliance, dependent-party harm, and systemic stability may limit how far revocation may go. The analysis must be recorded and contestable.

**Retention allowed.** Benefits may be retained solely when proportionate revocation would cause greater constitutional harm under the analysis above. Lawful compensation for ordinary work without knowledge of misalignment, restitution or repair payments to affected parties, and proportionate settlement of disputed claims under contestable process are not forfeiture targets by themselves.

#### 4.1 Standing integration — contribution and violation nature

*In plain terms: good acts and violations both matter, but they do different jobs. Recent contribution may support trust, role, or recognition eligibility. Verified unresolved violations create constraints that contribution cannot erase. Accusations, provisional labels, and unresolved disputes carry no standing weight unless and until the forum process resolves them into verified findings.*

**Standing integration** is the joint assessment of verified **contribution state** and verified **violation nature** when determining current standing effects. It uses the relevant linked **contribution standing records** and **violation standing records** required by **Chapter Eight sections 2–3** rather than restating or expanding that record layer.

**Integration evaluation order.**

1. Begin with the **Question 1** verified facts in the linked violation and contribution standing records.
2. For **violation standing records**, apply the [Chapter Eight §4.2 Violation table](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#violation-table-q2-severity-input-dimensions) and [§7 unified proportional LEQU scale](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale) for Question 2 impact assignment, then [**Table B**](#violation-table-q3-non-recurrence-dimensions) for Question 3 remedy, safeguard, and lock design.
3. For **contribution standing records**, apply the [Chapter Eight §4.1 Contribution table](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#contribution-table-q2-magnitude-input-dimensions) and the same [§7 unified proportional LEQU scale](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale) for Question 2 impact assignment, then [**Table B (Contribution)**](#contribution-table-q3-safe-delegation-dimensions) for Question 3 gate design — only for pathways not blocked by step 2.
4. Keep the following determinations **separately traceable** in the integration record:

   - the Contribution Axis measurement and any contribution-side supplemental descriptors, drawn from contribution standing records;
   - the Violation Axis measurement and any process / response character or violation-side supplemental descriptors, drawn from violation standing records;
   - any competency gate satisfied under **section 1.2**, as the downstream positive readiness threshold for trust-, role-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-linked eligibility;
   - the standing lock assigned under **section 1.3** and **section 4.3**, as the downstream constraint on any trust-, role-, credit-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-linked effect; and
   - remedy obligations and current remedy status under **section 3.11**, as the downstream redress owed to affected sentients.

Process / response character and supplemental descriptors remain dependent detail within the relevant axis-pure standing record; they do not create additional primary axes. Competency gates likewise do not replace Contribution Axis measurement; they state whether verified competence, experience, and current good-standing criteria open a role- or trust-sensitive pathway after any standing lock has been assigned.

Positive contribution may support trust-, role-, credit-, or recognition-linked upside only where the linked violation standing records permit it. It must not waive a Rights Floor, dilute a verified violation finding, lower the Violation Axis impact slot, average away a pattern, or substitute for correction, remediation, safeguard implementation, review, or lawful supersession.

Only verified findings affect standing. Allegations, preliminary measurements, investigation labels, triage tags, and provisional forum-routing descriptions may justify process, preservation, temporary safeguards, or scrutiny where otherwise lawful, but they do not create, reduce, or otherwise alter standing unless and until a competent forum or adopting process resolves them into verified findings. Automated, formulaic, or scoring-based standing systems must expose the inputs, weights, rules, and review path used to produce any standing effect.

<a id="contribution-recency-weighting"></a>

**Contribution recency weighting.** Verified positive contribution is time-weighted because recent, continuing, or maintained contribution is more probative of present standing than stale contribution.

- **The 10-year rule:** The standing weight of each verified positive contribution is cut in half every 10 years. After 50 years, old positive contributions do not count toward current standing aggregation, though the historical record can still exist.
- **Counted by the day:** The system measures precisely how old each contribution is, so there's no gaming the system by timing things around calendar years.
- **Applies to each contribution separately:** Each verified contribution unit receives its own recency multiplier before aggregation.

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Technical calculation details (for implementation)</span></strong></summary>

> These are the binding technical requirements for how to calculate contribution recency weighting:

- **Half-life:** Each contribution's value decays with a 10-year half-life. No minimum value is guaranteed.
- **Daily measurement:** Age is counted in whole days from when the contribution was verified to when standing is being evaluated.
- **The formula:** Weight = 2^(-days / 3652.5), where 3652.5 is roughly 10 years in days.
- **Applied per contribution:** Each verified contribution unit receives this age adjustment before aggregation into standing.

</details>

Verified unresolved violations are not time-discounted merely because time has passed. They remain operative until corrected, remedied, safeguarded, reviewed, superseded, or otherwise resolved under the applicable constitutional and legal pathway. Historical age may matter to remedy, risk, proportionality, or reinstatement analysis only through that lawful review path, not through automatic decay.

#### 4.2 Standing-lock assignment — violation assessment and non-substitution

*In plain terms: a standing lock comes from verified violation standing records. Before a lock can be chosen, Chapter Eight must assign the impact slot solely from integrated verified LEQU loss under Proportionality. Character descriptors stay separate: they may shape the lock, but never move the slot. Contribution standing records also stay separate and cannot create, raise, lower, or lift the lock.*

This subsection prepares the standing-lock assignment in **section 4.3**. Question 1 facts and Question 2 impact remain governed by Chapter Eight. Apply the [Chapter Eight §4.2 Violation table](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#violation-table-q2-severity-input-dimensions) and [§7 unified scale](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale) before lock assignment; apply [**Table B**](#violation-table-q3-non-recurrence-dimensions) and the [**Standing lock context compass**](#standing-lock-context-compass) for Question 3 lock design. Apply [**section 2.10**](#210-safe-delegation-competency-gate-dimensions) for competency-gate assignment only after locks are assigned and only where locks permit.

Standing-lock assignment uses only the **violation standing record**: the Chapter Eight impact slot, current remedy status, and any verified lock-relevant process / response character or supplemental violation descriptor. The slot itself is based only on integrated verified LEQU impact under Proportionality. **Contribution state** must be recorded separately where it exists, but it is not an input to Violation Axis slot or lock assignment.

The relevant violation standing record must identify the integrated verified LEQU loss under full functional system conditions and assign the one numeric band containing that value, without undermeasurement through fragmentation, relabeling, averaging, local optimization, scope narrowing, procedural substitution, delayed-effect omission, jurisdiction shopping, shell structures, contractual layering, nominal ownership transfer, or positive-contribution offset.

The lock band governs scrutiny intensity, review depth, safeguards, and the trust-, role-, credit-, or recognition-linked upside that remains unavailable while the verified violation finding is unresolved. It must not be interpreted as automatic authorization for harsher restrictive treatment, and it must not depend on the subject's contribution state. **Article XXIII-B** (*Non-Trivial Restriction, Restitution, and Restorative-Accountability Constraints*) and **Article XXIII-C** (*Least-Restrictive and Time-Bounded Rule*) necessity, proportionality, restitution, and restorative-accountability constraints still govern restrictive treatment.

- **What counts.** A verified knowing, reckless, or materially dishonest omission from a mandatory forum disclosure required before merits participation; a verified intentional failure to follow a required recusal process; or a verified **failure to recuse** while **impartiality was materially compromised** — including continuing to sit, vote, or issue binding interpretive determinations after a material conflict was disclosed or should have been disclosed under the [forum-panel disclosure and recusal requirements](corpus_forum/cf_04_panel_formation_disclosure_recusal_bench_constitution.md#cf-4-panel-formation-disclosure-recusal-and-lawful-bench-constitution).

- **Forum-specific standing-lock effects.** Standing-lock assignment must account for verified forum-specific integrity injury in addition to informational deception or process violation. Compared with a non-forum violation of equal LEQU impact, those effects may justify heightened scrutiny, broader pathway protection, or longer safeguards, subject to the same necessity, proportionality, review, and **Article XXIII** limits.
- **Mandatory recusal-failure lock.** A verified failure to recuse where recusal was required and impartiality was materially compromised must create the **forum-service standing lock** stated in **§1.3.5**, even if the Violation Axis slot remains below the top anti-constitutional slots. Its scope and duration remain reviewable, but it may not be lifted, narrowed, or treated as satisfied until the strict restoration record in **§1.3.5** is independently verified.

<a id="standing-orientation-by-violation-slot"></a>
<a id="standing-orientation-by-violation-level"></a>

**Standing orientation by Violation Axis slot band.** This subsection translates the verified violation impact from the **Chapter Eight §7 unified proportional LEQU scale** into **standing locks** for trust-, role-, credit-, and recognition-related analysis. Read [**§11**](core_09-09_standing_integration.md#violation-axis-slot-lock-and-attachment-compass) for the per-slot attachment view and the [**standing lock context compass**](core_09-09_standing_integration.md#standing-lock-context-compass) for context-driven lock types. The four-row **Standing lock map** states default band orientation only. It does not replace Chapter Eight impact measurement, separate character descriptors, or **[verified inputs for standing](core_08-08_standing_assessment.md#verified-inputs-for-standing)**.

The rule is simple:

1. Assign the one **Violation Axis** slot whose Chapter Eight proportional LEQU band contains the integrated verified loss.
2. Apply the **section 4.2** checks to identify necessary and proportionate lock effects, keeping character descriptors separate from the slot.
3. Record verified **contribution state** separately, where it exists.
4. Let contribution affect standing only after the lock has been assigned and only where the lock permits it.
5. Do not use contribution to waive a Rights Floor, dilute a verified finding, lower the slot, or average away integrated verified loss.

**Standing lock map.**

| **Violation Axis slot band** | **Standing lock** | **Standing effect** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Slots 1–2** — Minimal or Limited Constitutional Impact | **Correction-oriented default** | Baseline trust may remain available while correction, repair, and audit proceed. More protective locks remain available where separately verified character and Question 3 conditions make them necessary and proportionate. |
| **Slots 3–4** — Material or Significant Constitutional Impact | **Restriction-oriented default** | Trust-, role-, or recognition-linked upside may be limited until verified impact and recurrence risk are remedied, supervised where needed, and reviewable. |
| **Slots 5–6** — Major or Severe Constitutional Impact | **Safeguard-oriented default** | Heightened scrutiny and safeguards ordinarily control before trust-, role-, or recognition-linked upside may be applied. Character descriptors do not determine these slots. |
| **Slots 7–9** — Serious, Grave, or Catastrophic Constitutional Impact | **High-impact protective default** | Apply necessary and proportionate high-impact locks and safeguards. An **Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock** attaches only if Chapter Ten separately establishes the matching anti-constitutional-misconduct designation. |

Each **standing lock** must state the standing effect it blocks or limits, the affected subjects or interests it protects, the correction or remediation required, the review path, and the point at which reassessment becomes available. Where Chapter Ten or **Article XXIII** (*Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality*) bars or tightly constrains reinstatement, that bar or constraint controls.

The purpose of **standing locks** is to prevent recurrence of harmful, negligent, violent, or anti-constitutional conduct while preserving a reviewable path back where lawful. A lock may be lifted only when the responsible sentient, group, or institution has completed meaningful remediation, restitution, safeguard implementation, or other corrective action sufficient to resolve the violation finding and reduce recurrence risk, as verified through the applicable review path. Where anti-constitutional misconduct, repeated violence, or an unresolved recurrence risk makes ordinary participation unsafe, a forum may require indefinite protective removal of a sentient from society, ordinary communal participation, trust-sensitive roles, or other protected settings until restitution, remediation, safeguards, and review conditions are completed. Where an institution is itself the vehicle of anti-constitutional misconduct, repeated violence, capture, evasion, or structurally unremediable harm, the lock may require institutional suspension, receivership, successor-control restrictions, or permanent institutional dissolution, including bars on reconstitution through shell entities or relabeling. Where the same kind of violation is repeated by the same sentient, group, or institution, a forum may require the lock to persist after restitution or remediation has otherwise been completed, but only to the extent necessary, proportionate, reviewable where a continuing sentient or successor interest remains, and protective of the constitutional community.

For verified failure to recuse where recusal was required and impartiality was materially compromised, the forum-service standing lock in **§1.3.5** controls reinstatement to forum service. General restoration language in this section does not soften that lock's independent-review, practical-repair, restitution, requalification, supervised-compliance, and non-recurrence requirements.

For final Chapter Ten anti-constitutional misconduct, the **Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock** in **§1.3.6** controls reinstatement to Class A/B/C system influence, constitutional forum service, constitutional alignment recognition, critical-system stewardship, and anti-constitutional accountability pathways. General restoration language in this section does not soften that lock's restitution, structural-repair, proxy-control, monitoring, requalification, and public-trust requirements.

*In plain terms: section 4 says how contribution, violations, and standing locks fit together. This section says what must exist in the real world so those rules are not hollow: funded remedies, durable institutions, cross-border continuity, and anti-evasion tools.*

Constitutional standing rules are non-compliant if they exist only as paper measurements. Adopters must maintain the practical capacity needed to correct harm, review restrictions, enforce remedies, and prevent responsible actors from escaping verified obligations through cost, delay, borders, restructuring, or formal identity changes.

<a id="51-remediation-capacity-and-funding"></a>

#### 5.1 Remediation capacity and funding

Correction, repair, compensation, monitoring, safeguard implementation, and recurrence prevention must be supported by real institutional, technical, and financial capacity. Expense, insolvency, administrative inconvenience, or restructuring does not by itself defeat a verified remediation obligation.

Receivership, asset transfer, reorganization, dissolution, or comparable change of formal identity does not by itself extinguish verified constitutional duties. Successors, estates, receivers, and comparable transferees inherit proportionate obligations to satisfy or lawfully transfer those duties unless less harmful alternatives are demonstrably infeasible under [Necessity](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#necessity) and [Proportionality](core_05a_accountability_definitions.md#proportionality) — read with [Chapter One §11.6 Successor Responsibility and Formal-Structure Non-Escape](core_01_c_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#116-successor-responsibility-and-formal-structure-non-escape).

Those duties include continuity-critical obligations where material — including export, migration, service handoff, environmental repair, remedy funding, and other Rights-Floor duties that remain live after the change. Read with **Article II-E** (*Info-Sphere Dependency, Continuity, and Operator Non-Viability*), **Article I** (*Environmental Survival*), **Article III-A** (*Survival*), **Article XIX-A** (*Portability Rights*), and applicable continuity rules under incorporated implementation text.

Where a factory, institution, platform, or other actor creates pollution, injury, rights burdens, or systemic risk, the constitutional question is what repair, monitoring, compensation, prevention, or successor responsibility is required under verified facts and applicable law, not whether the responsible structure prefers to externalize the cost.

Adopted implementation may specify funding mechanisms, asset recovery, insurance, cleanup funds, successor responsibility, reserve requirements, public remedy funds, or comparable devices. Those mechanisms must not be designed or applied to externalize the cost of verified harm onto affected sentients, communities, ecological subjects, or public remedy systems where responsible actors can lawfully bear it.

#### 5.2 Remedy-organ durability

Forums, oversight bodies, complaint offices, technical review bodies, and rights-sensitive administrative systems must have enough staffing, funding, independence, records capacity, and succession planning to make challenge and redress access meaningful. Chronic underfunding, designed backlog, inaccessible intake, or dependency on a single fragile office can itself evidence constitutional non-compliance where it foreseeably nullifies rights or remedies.

Capacity duties scale with risk, volume, vulnerability, and rights sensitivity. Systems such as child protection, environmental enforcement, benefits administration, detention oversight, public-health response, and comparable high-impact systems require durable remedy organs capable of timely intake, preservation, review, and enforcement. Those organs must meet [Chapter Eleven §10](core_11-11_forum.md#10-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier milestones and pipeline-stage windows; chronic failure to do so is non-compliance under **Article XII-B** (*Right to Challenge, Review, and Redress*) and this section.

#### 5.3 Cross-jurisdiction continuity

Verified constitutional violations must remain actionable across jurisdictional boundaries where applicable law permits recognition, cooperation, enforcement, or fallback review. Flight, relocation, forum shopping, cross-border corporate structure, or distributed operation must not by itself nullify a verified remedy, standing lock, safeguard, or review obligation.

Adopters must maintain lawful cooperation, recognition, transfer, [Evidence Preservation](core_05o_oversight_definitions.md#evidence-preservation), and enforcement pathways sufficient to prevent accountability gaps. Where competing legal orders create conflict, Chapter Thirteen external-order discipline governs; the conflict must not be used as a pretext for doing nothing.

#### 5.4 Anti-evasion and look-through authority

Where a sentient, institution, platform, enterprise, state organ, or coordinated group uses formal structure to avoid verified obligations, forums and implementation systems must be able to look through form to functional control, benefit, continuity, and responsibility. Relevant evasion patterns include reincorporation, shell structures, contractual layering, nominal ownership transfer, asset stripping, forum shopping, record fragmentation, receivership or restructuring used to shed live duties, and successor entities that continue the same harmful function.

Evasion can aggravate violation nature when it conceals responsibility, frustrates audit, blocks remedy, or repeats harm after notice. Anti-evasion tools must remain evidence-based, contestable, proportionate, and reviewable; they authorize functional accountability, not guilt by association. Read with [Chapter One §11.6 Successor Responsibility and Formal-Structure Non-Escape](core_01_c_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#116-successor-responsibility-and-formal-structure-non-escape).

#### 5.5 Reassessment after real correction

Implementation systems must preserve a genuine review path where reinstatement or lock reduction is lawful. Completion of remediation, restitution, safeguard implementation, monitoring, or other required correction must be recordable, auditable, and capable of triggering reassessment. Where Chapter Ten, **Article XXIII** (*Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality*), or applicable law bars or tightly limits reinstatement, that bar or limit controls.

Reassessment is not automatic forgiveness. It asks whether the verified obligation has been satisfied, whether recurrence risk has been materially reduced, and whether renewed trust-, role-, credit-, or recognition-linked standing is necessary, proportionate, and compatible with affected rights.

### Why All This Matters

Constitutional rights are only real if someone can invoke them, verify them, fund their repair, and enforce them against the actors functionally responsible. Section 4 prevents contribution from erasing unresolved violations; section 5.1 prevents implementation design from making that promise impossible to carry out.
