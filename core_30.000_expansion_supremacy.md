Chapter Thirty is the constitutional owner of **how this Constitution fits with other rules and outside law**: it lets protections grow, works alongside outside law and external tribunals without displacing that law or giving up its own review routes.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: forward is fine, and new rights, stronger safeguards, and broader inclusion are welcome when they increase protection. This chapter also says how this Constitution relates to other binding norms and to outside law: it is not a secret override, and it cannot be turned off during amendment, so audits and challenges stay available throughout. Stricter outside law is respected when it fits, and outside forums cannot be used as an excuse to destroy records or dodge rights.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Thirty states what is permitted and what holds when this Constitution meets other rules:

- additive expansion, which is permitted (**section 3**);
- continuity of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment (now in [Chapter Twenty-Nine §10.1 *Continuity of Supremacy and Challenge Rights during Amendment*](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#101-continuity-of-supremacy-and-challenge-rights-during-amendment));
- supremacy relative to other binding norms, stricter external protections, and conflict disclosure (**section 5**);
- disputes involving external legal orders (**section 6**); and
- how this Constitution relates to applicable external law, including non-displacement and limited binding effect (**section 7**).

**Role:** The chapter works with its two neighbors on change and adoption:

- The guardrail on expansion is Chapter Twenty-Eight.
- A body can bind itself only by adopting through Chapter Twenty-Nine, and only as far as it already has lawful authority to bind itself.
- **Chapter Thirty-One section 5** applies where partial adoption could shrink duties an entity already bears.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- additive expansion (**section 3**);
- a pointer to continuity of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment (**section 4**);
- supremacy relative to other binding norms (**section 5**);
- disputes involving external legal orders (**section 6**); and
- the relation to applicable external law (**section 7**).

The chapter leaves:

- the tests for whether a change is valid (Substantive Test 1 and Procedural Tests 2 to 4) to Chapters [Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-eight-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity) and [Twenty-Nine](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#chapter-twenty-nine-amendment-ratification-and-procedural-validity); and
- the incorporation bridge, custody, and operative effect of adopted text to [Chapter Thirty-One](core_31.000_incorporation.md#chapter-thirty-one-incorporation-bridge).

### 2. Why Expansion and Supremacy Rules Exist

*In plain terms: this Constitution must be able to grow, and it must sit alongside other law without hiding behind it or overriding it in secret. This chapter exists to say how.*

New rights and stronger safeguards need an easy way in, so the text can improve as understanding grows. The Constitution also has to work beside other law: where outside law protects more, that protection stands; where outside law is used as an excuse to destroy records or dodge rights, the excuse fails. This chapter sets both rules.

*In plain terms: if the law of the land (or a treaty) is tougher than this **Constitution** on protecting sentients, safety, or accountability, follow the tougher rule — unless it would force a violation of Safety or Truth, in which case interpret to maximize protection within those two hard limits.*

Where valid external law, regulation, or treaty obligation applicable to the adopter imposes stricter protections, the stricter requirement governs. Stricter protections may address sentients, safety, epistemic integrity, transparency, or accountability. That rule applies where the stricter requirement can be honored without forcing violation of Chapter Two non-negotiable principle constraints (Safety and Truth). Where multiple interpretations are reasonably available, interpretation must favor the maximal protection consistent with those constraints.

#### 5.3 Conflict Disclosure and Mitigation
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§5.1](#51-internal-hierarchy-for-adopters)–[§5.2](#52-stricter-external-protections) hierarchy and stricter-external rule.
- Downstream: [§6](#6-disputes-involving-external-legal-orders) cooperation and preserved internal review pathways; **CS-4.320** (*Justice safeguards, restitution, and rehabilitation*) and [Chapter Nineteen §5 — *Enforcement realism*](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#5-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems) (19.000 §5) as cited in operative bullets.
- Read with: **[CS-4.320](corpus_systems/cs_04.320_justice_safeguards_restitution_rehabilitation.md#cs-4320-justice-safeguards-restitution-and-rehabilitation)** (*Justice safeguards, restitution, and rehabilitation*) cross-jurisdiction and anti-evasion controls referenced in operative text.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: when outside requirements and constitutional floors seem impossible to square, say so on the official record, tell **affected** **stakeholders** where it makes sense, try to sequence harm down — and never use the conflict as cover to comply only where it is easy or to dodge rights. If outside law targets an identity or conduct this Constitution protects, do not voluntarily help enforce it; challenge and narrow a conflicting binding demand through available lawful means.*

Where external requirements appear irreconcilable with constitutional Rights Floors or non-negotiable principle constraints, adopters must document the conflict. They must:

- notify materially affected stakeholders where appropriate.
- pursue feasible harmonization and least-harm sequencing.
- **not** treat external norms as a pretext for selective compliance, forum shopping, reincorporation arbitrage, or evasion of Rights-Floor obligations.

**External prohibitions on protected characteristics or conduct.** An external prohibition or penalty does not by itself make a [Protected Characteristic](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#protected-characteristics) or conduct protected by this Constitution a constitutional violation. This includes, for example, sexual orientation and consensual same-sex relationships where protected by this Constitution.

An adopter must not voluntarily discriminate against, punish, report, or assist external enforcement against a covered sentient on the ground of such a characteristic or protected conduct. Where a binding external demand requires action that conflicts with these protections, the adopter must document and review the demand under this section, exhaust available lawful challenges and means of narrowing it, protect sensitive information, and pursue the least harmful available course. Constitutional review and remedy pathways remain available under [§6.2 Preserved Internal Review Pathways](#62-preserved-internal-review-pathways). This rule does not itself displace applicable external law or guarantee protection from action by external authorities, as provided in [§7.1 Non-Displacement and Limited Binding Effect](#71-non-displacement-and-limited-binding-effect).

Cross-jurisdiction coordination and anti-evasion execution follow **[CS-4.320](corpus_systems/cs_04.320_justice_safeguards_restitution_rehabilitation.md#cs-4320-justice-safeguards-restitution-and-rehabilitation)** (*Justice safeguards, restitution, and rehabilitation*), including [§9 Cross-Jurisdiction Execution and Anti-Evasion Controls](corpus_systems/cs_04.320_justice_safeguards_restitution_rehabilitation.md#cs-4320-9-cross-jurisdiction-execution-and-anti-evasion-controls). Execution also follows [Chapter Nineteen §5 — *Enforcement realism*](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#5-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems), where applicable.

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§7](#7-relation-to-applicable-external-law) relation-to-external-law frame; [Chapter Thirty-One §5](core_31.000_incorporation.md#5-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority) (31.000 §5) (*enforceable only after valid adoption*).
- Downstream: [§7.2](#72-alignment-when-used-within-external-legal-frameworks) alignment when used inside an external framework; [§5.1](#51-internal-hierarchy-for-adopters) internal hierarchy for adopters within that limited effect.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Nine §12.1](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#121-deliberate-adoption) (29.000 §12.1)–[§12.2](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#122-adoption-document) (*deliberate, documented, auditable document stating entity, scope, and effective date*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: the document does not repeal other law just by existing. Adoption makes it binding for the adopting body, inside that body’s lawful reach and the recorded scope — not a claim that every other legal order has been replaced.*

This Constitution does not, of its own force, repeal, displace, override, or render inoperative applicable local, national, or international law, including treaty and other international obligations where they apply to the entity.

Publication, distribution, citation, forking, operations-guide use, or any statement that a body “follows” this Constitution is not adoption and does not create supremacy over applicable external law.

This Constitution becomes binding for a particular entity only upon valid adoption under **Chapter Twenty-Nine §12** (*Ratification and Adoption*) and **Chapter Thirty-One §5** (*Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority*). Binding effect is limited to:

- the adopting entity;
- the systems under that entity's authority; and
- the stated scope of the [adoption document](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#122-adoption-document).

Valid adoption does not, by itself, repeal, displace, or render inoperative otherwise applicable local, national, or international law, except to the extent that:

- the adopting entity has lawful authority under that external law to bind itself and the systems under its authority to this Constitution; and
- the adoption document records that commitment within that authority.

Within that limited binding effect, [§5.1](#51-internal-hierarchy-for-adopters) (*Internal Hierarchy for Adopters*), [§5.2](#52-stricter-external-protections) (*Stricter External Protections*), and [§5.3 Conflict Disclosure and Mitigation](#53-conflict-disclosure-and-mitigation) continue to apply.

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

<br>

*In plain terms: if you are using this Constitution inside a company, university, protocol, city, or other body that already lives under other law, the certification, standing, contest, and participation processes here have to be ones you can actually run without violating that law — and you still may not use the outside rulebook as cover to skip floors this Constitution requires where they can be honored together.*

Where this Constitution is used, invoked, or applied inside an external legal framework — including operations-guide use, incorporation into policy or contract, or adoption by an entity that remains subject to that framework — processes, participation, records, forums, and remedies under this Constitution must be capable of being carried out consistently with applicable requirements of that framework.

Alignment with an external legal framework:

- does not convert operations-guide use, citation, or “we follow it” practice into adoption;
- does not authorize shrinking **Chapter Twenty-Eight** non-regression floors, **Chapter Four** Rights Floors, or **Chapter Two** Safety and Truth constraints by pointing at weaker external norms, as already forbidden by [§5.2 Stricter External Protections](#52-stricter-external-protections) and [§5.3 Conflict Disclosure and Mitigation](#53-conflict-disclosure-and-mitigation);
- does not excuse [§6.1 Good-Faith Cooperation](#61-good-faith-cooperation) duties where competent external jurisdiction is lawfully asserted; and
- does not permit using an asserted conflict as a pretext for selective compliance, forum shopping, or evasion of Rights-Floor obligations.
