### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 4-B is the constitutional owner of the **integrity trigger categories** every institution uses, and of the **gift register, gift threshold, apparent-conflict clearance, and funding-change log** that carry [Article XIII-C](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*). Each institution's control map, named owners, and escalation routes stay in the institutions corpus.

*In plain terms: this file names the kinds of corruption and capture every institution must watch for, and sets the gift register and funding-change log that make the gift and funding rules checkable. These are the same for every adopter.*

### 2. Why Integrity Rules Are the Same Everywhere

*In plain terms: if each adopter could define corruption its own way, or keep gift records its own way, the rules against buying influence would be only as strong as the weakest adopter.*

Capture rarely announces itself. It arrives as a gift, a funding cut after an unwelcome ruling, or a conflict nobody recorded. Shared trigger categories mean the same failure is recognized and routed the same way everywhere, and shared registers and logs mean anyone can see whether a decision-maker was offered something or a body was punished through its budget.

### 3. Integrity Trigger Categories

*In plain terms: institutions use a common set of trigger labels — corruption, fraud, self-dealing, procurement capture, and the rest — so training, control maps, and routing stay consistent. Shared cross-layer routing lives in **CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*); Standing Records and measurement remain in **Chapter Fourteen** and **Chapter Fifteen**; **Chapter Twenty** owns the separate misconduct designation.*

These are the integrity trigger categories every institution uses in its control maps and training under [CI-6.210](corpus_institutions/ci_06.210_conflict_integrity_anti_capture_anti_corruption.md#ci-6210-conflict-integrity-anti-capture-and-anti-corruption) (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*). Apply **CJS-3.110 §6** (*Oversight: Integrity Assurance and Resilience Operations*) **Integrity-trigger routing chain** for shared cross-layer traceability.

**Trigger categories:**

- corruption;
- fraud;
- self-dealing;
- procurement capture;
- assurance capture;
- reporting suppression;
- conflict concealment;
- influence-contact concealment; and
- selection-funding circumvention.

When a trigger fires, route it to the correct owner section — not every problem stays inside the institution that received it. Assign a **Notification Urgency** (**NU**) category under **CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*) and publish notice through **CI-6.215 §1** (*Controls Declaration*) without waiting for routine attestation:

- **CI-6.210** — receiving and opening reports;
- **CI-6.215** — independent assurance checks and urgency-scaled incident notification (**CI-6.215 §1** (*Controls Declaration*));
- **CI-22.210** — escalation when local review fails;
- **CI-6.205** — structural independence and lane safeguards when capture is suspected;
- **CI-22.205** — fair procedure and contest rights;
- applicable **CF** sections — forum-side support; and
- **CI-4.215** (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*), **CI-24.205** (*Institutional accountability and sanctions*) for sanctions where misconduct is confirmed.

Standing Records remain under [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened), and contribution and violation measurement, including the Violation Axis, remains under [Chapter Fifteen](core_15.000_standing_measurement.md#chapter-fifteen-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it). [Chapter Twenty](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) owns the separate anti-constitutional-misconduct designation.

<a id="4-gifts-apparent-conflicts-and-funding-integrity"></a>

### 4. Gifts, Apparent Conflicts, and Funding Integrity

*In plain terms: **Article XIII-C** (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and **Article XIII-D** (*Funding Integrity*) say decision-makers turn down gifts that could buy influence, step aside when a fair-minded observer would doubt them, and are never steered by money. This subsection says how an institution keeps the registers and logs that make those rules checkable.*

**Gift register:** Each covered body, **appointing authority**, and **adopting institution** keeps a public gift register. For each benefit offered to a member, panelist, staff member, or officer above a nominal value, it records:

- who received the offer, who made it, and when;
- what was offered and its estimated value;
- whether the source had a pending or foreseeable matter before the recipient; and
- whether the benefit was accepted, declined, returned, or surrendered to the public.

Entries are made within the 30-day **gift-register entry window**, counted from the offer.

**Gift threshold:** The adopting institution publishes a gift threshold in its own currency and reviews it at least once in each three-year **gift-threshold review period**.

- **Default:** until a threshold is published, it is the median local daily wage.
- **Adding up:** benefits from one source, and from sources acting together or through intermediaries, are added up over the twelve-month **gift-aggregation period** set by **Article XII** (*Stakeholder System Participation, Representation, and Due Process*)-D (*Non-Capture Safeguards*).
- **Nominal value:** the adopting institution also publishes a nominal value below which no register entry is needed. It must be well below the gift threshold.

**What is not a gift:** The following are not gifts for **Article XIII-C** (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*), though each is still recorded where this subsection or another rule requires it:

- ordinary pay, expenses, and benefits from the body itself;
- gifts from family or close personal friends, given for personal reasons and not connected to any matter; such gifts are still barred where the giver is an interested party with a pending matter;
- refreshments and materials offered on equal terms to every participant in a meeting or event; and
- public awards made openly by a body with no matter before the recipient.

**Apparent-conflict clearance:** A member or panelist who discloses an apparent conflict under **Article XIII-C** (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) recuses unless an independent seat clears the matter. The clearance decision is published, names the facts disclosed, and gives reasons. A cleared member may be challenged by any party, and the challenge is decided by a seat other than the one that cleared the member.

**Funding-change log:** Each funder, budget authority, or paying body of a covered body keeps a public funding-change log of every change to that body's funding, with the date, the amount, and the published reason. The log must let a reader see whether a change followed a decision adverse to the funder.

**Routing:** A violation of these rules is an integrity trigger under [§3 Integrity Trigger Categories](#3-integrity-trigger-categories). Offering, accepting, or routing a benefit tied to a matter may also be alleged as bribery or improper benefit exchange under [Chapter Twenty-One §5.1](core_21.000_misconduct_pattern_applications.md#51-bribery-and-improper-benefit-exchange) (*Bribery and Improper Benefit Exchange*).
