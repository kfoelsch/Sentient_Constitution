Subcore 3-C is the constitutional owner of **burden allocation, compliance evidence, definition traceability, observability, and security-constrained verification**.

*In plain terms: if you say you're following the rules, you have to prove it — and show a clear, checkable path from what the Constitution defines to what sentients can actually observe.*
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: if you claim compliance, you prove it, through one five-step check. The proof has to be real, open to inspection in practice, and checkable by others, even where security narrows how or when it is shown.*

**Purpose:** Subcore 3-C answers who must prove compliance and how the proof must look:

- the party claiming compliance carries the burden through one five-step check (**section 3**);
- it must show a map from each applicable definition's parts, Ontological, Measurement, Assessment, and Compliance, to what the system actually does (**section 4**), and must expose enough of itself for that map to be checked in practice (**section 5**);
- security can narrow how, when, and to whom disclosure happens but cannot erase the duty to trace and verify (**section 6**); and
- evidence must be tied to the map, independent where possible, complete, and sustained (**section 7**).

**Role:** [Subcores 3-A to 3-C](subcore_03.010_definition_structure.md#subcore-3-a-definition-structure-and-component-requirements) together set the rules every compliance claim must meet:

- It takes definition mechanics from Subcores 3-A and [3-B](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#subcore-3-b-definition-integrity-evasion-and-non-compliance), and definitions from [Chapter Three](core_03.000_definitions_home.md#chapter-three-foundational-definitions), as fixed.
- It hands those rules to Chapter Eight, where System Alignment Certification applies them, and to Chapter Fourteen, where Standing Records rest on verified facts.
- A current certification is not a way around Subcores 3-A to 3-C (**section 7**).

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- burden allocation (**section 3**);
- traceability (**section 4**);
- observability (**section 5**);
- security-constrained verification (**section 6**);
- the compliance evidence standard (**section 7**); and
- the relationship to System Alignment Certification (**section 8**).

The chapter leaves:

- the certification process itself to [Chapters Eight through Thirteen](core_08.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#chapter-eight-system-alignment-certification--evaluation);
- the meaning of each definition to Chapter Three; and
- Standing Records and their effects to [Chapters Fourteen through Nineteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened).


### 2. Why the Burden of Proof Exists

*In plain terms: the one who claims to comply is the one who must show it. This file exists because a sentient harmed by a system rarely has the evidence to prove what went wrong.*

The sentient affected by a system usually knows least about how it works, and the operator knows most. If the burden sat with the affected sentient, most failures could never be shown. This file places the burden on whoever claims compliance, and requires the proof to be traceable, open to inspection, and checkable by others, even where security limits how or when it is shown. That keeps a compliance claim from being a promise no one can test.

*In plain terms: show a checkable map from each applicable definition's parts — O, M, A, and C — to what the system actually does, before **sections 5** through **6** judge whether anyone can verify it and whether the proof holds.*

This section requires a **structured trace map**. For every definition that materially applies to system behavior — including every materially required **Chapter Three** definition — the map must link each part to **observable** behavior and real-world effects:

- **what it is (O)** — what the term is about
- **how it is measured (M)** — which measure applies at each layer
- **how it is judged (A)** — how the term must be assessed
- **what must hold (C)** — what must be true in practice

The trace map must also:
- be **maintained** across system updates, version changes, and deployment contexts; failure to maintain tracing over time is non-compliant
- be **verified** under the operational and assessment conditions Subcores 3-A and 3-B require
- be stated in terms that can be observed and independently checked — the standard **sections 5** through **6** apply when deciding whether the system is open enough to verify under security limits and whether the evidence is sufficient
- be bidirectional, such that:
  - each definition component (O/M/A/C) maps to observable system behavior and effects, including direct, indirect, delayed, and aggregated effects where materially relevant
  - all materially relevant system behaviors must be mapped completely and representatively, without omission of materially relevant behaviors or conditions

Failure to invoke or correctly apply any materially required **Chapter Three** definition invalidates the associated evaluation, evidence, and compliance determination in full. Partial, selective, or non-representative tracing is non-compliant.

<strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Definition:</span></strong> [Foreseeability](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [O](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [M](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [A](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [C](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-c)

*In plain terms: section 4 requires a trace map — this section requires the system to expose enough of itself that authorized or affected parties can check the map in practice, not just in theory. Opaque architecture, outside dependencies, and black-box components are not excused.*

Section 4 requires a trace map; this section requires the system to expose enough of itself for that map to be checked in practice.

**Systems must:**
- provide sufficient **observability, instrumentation, and access** to components, data, and interactions to fully satisfy **section 4** — for appropriately authorized or affected parties, with proportionate methods, resources, and access conditions
- provide access within timeframes that preserve the ability to detect, challenge, and respond to non-compliance
- structure information so it is complete and usable for verification — not obscured through fragmentation, aggregation, or noise
- treat failure to observe, measure, or verify any materially relevant O/M/A/C part under this chapter's evaluation conditions as **non-compliant**
- treat limitations from system design, architectural opacity, external dependencies, or third-party components as **design failures** to be addressed through modification, constraint, or exclusion
- treat affected definition parts and any dependent compliance claims as **non-compliant** where full tracing cannot be achieved

**Systems must not:**
- rely on components or dependencies that prevent satisfaction of this requirement under **reasonably foreseeable** conditions
- treat design limitations, architectural opacity, external dependencies, or third-party components as exemptions from this requirement
- impose cost, complexity, delay, access restrictions, or informational conditions that materially impair independent verification
- rely on **theoretical** verifiability where **practical** verification is not achievable under reasonably foreseeable conditions
- require dependence on the system or actor asserting compliance beyond what is strictly necessary

Where verification is technically possible but not practically achievable, treat compliance claims as **non-compliant**.

*In plain terms: security can narrow how, when, and to whom disclosure or audit happens — it cannot erase the duty to trace, verify, and allow independent checking.*

Section 5 requires observability. **Security, misuse-prevention, and adversarial risk** may justify proportionate limits on disclosure, audit, or verification. Those limits do **not** remove the duties in **sections 4** and **5**.

**Systems must:**
- apply the **least-restrictive** limit that still addresses foreseeable risk, including exploitation, circumvention, or systemic harm, using methods proportionate to system impact, dependency, and risk
- preserve the **maximum achievable** independent audit, verification, and challenge rights
- ensure any limit is **materially necessary**, **proportionate**, **narrowly scoped**, **auditable**, and **non-pretextual**; its justification and scope must be **independently verifiable**; it must be **time-limited** where feasible
- keep **independent validation** independent of the system or actor asserting compliance

**Systems must not:**
- invoke security, confidentiality, or misuse risk to avoid creating, maintaining, or independently validating materially relevant compliance evidence
- be designed or structured to predictably block observability, traceability, verifiability, or auditability under reasonably foreseeable conditions

Where a limit blocks meaningful independent validation, the associated compliance claims are **non-compliant** unless all of the following are true:
- no safer validation pathway is reasonably achievable
- that determination is independently justified and auditable
- the remaining method preserves sufficient epistemic integrity for compliance determination

Cryptography and information-type handling must align with the Constitution and **[Subcore 4-A — Data Types](subcore_04.050_data_types.md#subcore-4-a-data-types) and [CS-4.305](corpus_systems/cs_04.305_information_types_and_handling.md#cs-4305-information-types-and-handling) (*Information types and handling*)**; where materially relevant, they must still permit full compliance verification under this chapter. Cryptographic and information-type handling must not replace [Truth (Constitutional Constraint)](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#truth-constitutional-constraint) or [Safety (Constitutional Constraint)](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#safety-constitutional-constraint), or the duties in **sections 4**, **5**, or **6**.

*In plain terms: proof has to be real — tied to the trace map, checked under actual conditions, independent where possible, complete, and sustained over time. Cherry-picked snapshots, proxy-only attestations, and self-serving proof do not count.*

Sections **4** through **6** set trace, observability, and security limits; this section states what evidence must show to meet the burden under **section 3**. The standard scales with [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) — system impact, dependency, and risk.

**Evidence must:**
- demonstrate compliance with **Subcores 3-A and 3-B** under the required assessment scope and conditions, including compliant trace artifacts under **section 4** and observability under **section 5**
- be generated and assessed under conditions that represent actual system behavior, operation, and impact
- be **observable**, **verifiable**, **complete** relative to required scope, **sustained** across time, and **sufficient** in rigor proportionate to system impact, dependency, and risk to withstand independent audit and adversarial testing
- use **independent validation** where reasonably achievable; absence must be justified under proportionality constraints
- comply with **section 6** constraints on evidence generation, disclosure, and verification, including cryptographic and information-type handling where applicable
- where constitutional values or constraints conflict in the same evaluation, remain consistent with [Chapter Two, §13 — Constitutional Collision Resolution Process](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#13-constitutional-collision-resolution-process)
- for **empirical**, **predictive**, or **causal** claims material to compliance, classification, or governance, reflect **scientific integrity** where proportionate — testable or reviewable methods, documented assumptions and limitations, no suppression of null results or failed replications where they bear on decisions, and conclusions revisable on new evidence

**The following do not meet the burden of proof under section 3:**
- failure to satisfy any required definition or component under [Subcore 3-A, §4.1 Core Integrity Conditions](subcore_03.010_definition_structure.md#41-core-integrity-conditions), or non-compliance under [Subcore 3-B, section 4 — Non-Compliance from Observable System Behavior](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#4-non-compliance-from-observable-system-behavior) — invalidating all dependent compliance claims under this chapter
- failure to provide required evidence where it exists or is required under this chapter
- proxy-only, self-attested, partial, or selectively scoped proof that would be non-compliant under [Subcore 3-B, section 4](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#4-non-compliance-from-observable-system-behavior) if treated as the system's compliance demonstration
- insufficient evidence to satisfy the **Evidence must** requirements above

Where uncertainty prevents definitive demonstration of compliance for materially relevant definition components, apply [Subcore 3-B, §4.6 Uncertainty Integrity and Non-Exploitation Constraint](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#46-uncertainty-integrity-and-non-exploitation-constraint). Where the burden of proof is not satisfied, default to **non-compliant** status.
