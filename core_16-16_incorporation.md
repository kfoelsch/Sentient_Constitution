<br>

*In plain terms: the detailed operating rules live in the designated **implementation text** files — this section only names the bridge role, insists incorporated text cannot be used to cheat Chapter One or the Rights-Floor stack, and blocks “we merged the files” from changing legal status by packaging alone.*

This chapter is the **constitutional incorporation bridge**. It states **what** **implementation text** files count as binding implementation text, **how** adoption and custody make that text operative, and **that** post-adoption edits to those files do not silently change constitutional effect.

Within valid adoption scope, it incorporates designated **implementation text** files as **authoritative implementation text** for designated obligations.

This chapter **does not** relocate or restate that implementation layer as a second constitutional source. Implementation detail remains in the designated files identified in the [**Corpus**](core_05_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry in [Chapter Five](core_05__definitions_home.md#chapter-five-foundational-definitions).

Incorporated implementation requirements apply together with **Chapter One**. They must not be satisfied through [**Safety (Constraint)**](core_05_band_continuity.md#safety-constraint) or [**Truth (Constitutional Constraint)**](core_05_band_oversight.md#truth-constitutional-constraint) violations. They do not displace **Chapter One** proportionality, necessity, systemic evaluation, or interaction rules where those govern.

**Chapter Six** default constraint stack and **Chapter Five** ([**Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy**](core_05_band_integrative.md#authority-stack), [**Trust**](core_05_band_continuity.md#trust), and [**Trustworthiness**](core_05_band_continuity.md#trustworthiness)) supply further assessment anchors without restatement here.

Optional packaging that merges implementation files into fewer Markdown files **does not** alter normative status while those files remain listed as **implementation text** text in the [**Corpus**](core_05_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry in **Chapter Five**.

### 3. Custody, editions, and operative effect

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: [Chapters Two through Four](core_02-03_definition_mechanics.md) (*traceability and verification expectations that support custody discipline*); [doc_architecture.md](doc_architecture.md) (*non-binding mapping and edition metadata unless adopted*).

<br>

*In plain terms: treat designated **implementation text** files like versioned law libraries — only the edition written into the adoption record counts, keep receipts that prove who held the authoritative text and when it took effect, and treat “just a clarification” relabels as ineffective for changing constitutional meaning until a real re-adoption repairs the chain.*

- **Implementation-file scope (enumerated):** Without limitation, this section covers [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) (*systems implementation file*), [corpus_institutions.md](corpus_institutions.md) (*Constitutional Institutions*), [corpus_forum.md](corpus_forum.md) (*Constitutional Judicial Systems*), and [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md) (*Constitutional Joint Structure*). It also covers any further **implementation text** files listed in the [**Corpus**](core_05_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry in [Chapter Five](core_05__definitions_home.md#chapter-five-foundational-definitions). Each incorporated implementation file is binding **only as adopted** through a recorded adoption chain under **Chapter Sixteen**.
  - **Sentience-status adjudication packet status:** As of this edition, **no dedicated Article V-E / Sentience Status Adjudication implementation packet** is enumerated among those files. Operational minimum fields live in [Chapter Eleven §5](core_11-11_forum.md#5-escalation-and-certification) (*Sentience-status adjudication* hook) and must not be read as an incorporated appointments or filing statute. Adopters may incorporate a fuller packet later under this chapter; until then, the Rights Floor and Chapter Eleven hook bind without that packet.
- **Edition pinning:** Incorporation by reference at constitutional scope is operative only for **adopted edition identifiers** and **effective dates** recorded explicitly in the adoption chain. For constitutional-scope purposes, the operative text of an incorporated implementation file is the text at the **adopted edition identifier**. That text is **not** the adopter’s current working copy, and it is **not** the latest revision published upstream simply because it is newest.
- **Custody chain:** Adopters must maintain an **auditable custody chain** for incorporated **implementation text** artifacts. Records must include edition identifier, effective date, custodian identity, and linkage to the adoption instrument, in a form sufficient for provenance and applicable scope. Custody records must be proportionate to assigned system classification under *[corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md)*, **CS-3 — System classification and handling**, and to [**Material Impact**](core_05_band_oversight.md#material-impact), [**Dependency**](core_05_band_continuity.md#dependency), affected [**Stakeholder**](core_05_band_participation.md#stakeholder) position, and applicable [**Materiality Determination**](core_05_band_oversight.md#materiality-determination) requirements in [**Chapter Five**](core_05__definitions_home.md#chapter-five-foundational-definitions) (consistent with [**Classification-Scaled Governance**](core_05_band_oversight.md#classification-scaled-governance)).
- **No silent drift (anti-drift rule):** Post-adoption edits to implementation text files have **no constitutional effect** for an adopter unless validly adopted through **Chapter Sixteen** procedures. This rule applies to substantive revisions, editorial compressions presented as non-substantive, renumbering that changes binding cross-reference targets, and silent edition-relabeling without adoption.

    Later implementation-file revisions do **not** displace the adopted edition’s operative effect. They may be taken up through re-adoption on the **Chapter Sixteen** pathway; until they are validly re-adopted, they remain process-layer artifacts without constitutional effect for adopters bound to the earlier edition.

- **Anti-evasion.** Presenting post-adoption implementation-file edits as “clarification,” “correction,” “style alignment,” “non-substantive refactor,” or “editorial compression” does **not** cure this section when the substantive constitutional effect is materially equivalent. The anti-evasion **rule** of **Chapter Thirteen, section 3** applies. Where an adopter’s operational text has drifted from its adopted edition without valid re-adoption, the **stricter clearly adopted baseline** governs under **[Chapter One §8.4.3](core_01_b_interaction_interpretation.md#833-incorporation-layer)** until validity is restored under **Chapters Eleven and Thirteen**.
- **Process-layer alignment (evidence hook).** Architecture maps, TODO records, regression catalogs, and evidence logs **support** verification of custody and adoption framing. They **do not**, by themselves, alter constitutional effect absent valid adoption.

    Accepted governance-control changes — including custody-chain hardening, edition lineage updates, and incorporation-discipline clarifications — must be recorded in a dated artifact under [evidence/](evidence/) (`evidence/<YYYY-MM-DD>/`). That artifact must link the edition identifier, the adoption chain, and the regression coverage (**`RS-XD-003`** and adjacent `RS-XD-*` seeds) that exercises the custody and anti-drift safeguards. The artifact is a **verification aid**; it does not itself constitute adoption.

*In plain terms: this **Constitution** still says what it aims for on systems that affect **sentients**, but enforcement inside a place or organization only kicks in when that body actually adopts through the published rules — “you cannot enforce this here” is about power and process, not an erase-the-text argument.*

This section states the Sentient Constitution's **adoption framing self-description** — what the instrument is with respect to its own adoption — so that jurisdictional objections and adoption-contingency arguments are resolved by reference to the instrument's own stated nature rather than by silent assumption.

**The instrument's nature.** This Sentient Constitution is an **aspirational model constitutional instrument** whose substantive scope is stated in its own terms, claiming application over "all systems that materially affect sentients" as a matter of the instrument's own content, and whose **operative effect within any particular jurisdiction, federation, polity, organization, system, or adopter body is contingent on valid adoption** under **Chapter Sixteen** (*Ratification and Adoption*) and this chapter. Absent valid adoption, the instrument has no independent enforcement authority over non-adopters; it retains its aspirational content and may guide, inform, or serve as a reference point for non-adopter conduct without constituting an enforceable obligation.

**Substantive scope preservation.** The substantive content of this instrument — including its principles in **Chapter One**, its definitions in **Chapter Five**, its Rights Floor in **Chapter Six**, and all other substantive chapters — **does not become adoption-contingent**. The substantive content stands as stated, as the instrument's own position on what constitutional alignment for sentient systems requires, independent of whether any particular adopter has adopted the instrument. Adopter-specific operative effect, custody, and enforcement are governed by **Chapter Sixteen** and this chapter; substantive claims about what the instrument says, what it requires in principle, and what it identifies as compliant or non-compliant, are stated in the instrument's own authorship and do not require adoption to be *what the instrument states*. Narrowing substantive claims under jurisdictional-objection framing ("the instrument lacks authority here so its substantive content does not apply to our analysis") is non-compliant with the instrument's own stated authorship claim; the correct recourse for a non-adopter is that the substantive content does not bind them operatively, not that the substantive content is absent.

**Adopter scope.** Valid adopters may be — without limitation — constitutional federations, polities, sovereign or cooperative entities, organizations, system operators, system-developer consortia, or any other body capable of binding itself and the systems within its authority to the obligations stated in this instrument, subject to **Chapter Sixteen** ratification and **Chapter Sixteen** incorporation discipline. Adoption may be total (the whole instrument) or scoped (specified chapters, articles, or obligations as adopter-constitutional-requirements) consistent with **Chapter Sixteen** and **Chapter Thirteen** non-regression rules; partial adoption may not be used to nullify the non-regression floor.

**Pluralism preservation.** Nothing in this section compels a single global polity structure, a single federation structure, or a single adoption mechanism. Different adopters may adopt under different internal procedures consistent with **Chapter Sixteen**. This is consistent with the instrument's existing pluralism commitment in **Chapter Twelve §1** and the non-mandating clause of democratic-institution design.

**Non-adopter objection framing.** "The instrument lacks jurisdiction" is correct only as to **operative enforcement effect** against a non-adopter. It is not a substantive rebuttal of what the instrument says. It is not a basis for narrowing the instrument's substantive scope in any analysis that applies the instrument's own framework. Where an analysis invokes that framework — principles, definitions, Rights Floor, classifications — it must apply the framework as stated. Selective invocation that accepts the framework while denying its substantive content on jurisdictional grounds is non-compliant with this section.
