# Constitutional Processes and Institutions

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Reader guidance (non-operative): how to use this guide</span></strong></summary>

> **Reader orientation · pre-release.** This guide is reader-oriented process support. It maps each constitutional process and each institution to the chapters that govern it and to the principles that bear on it most. It adds no duty, definition, or sequence, ranks nothing, and cannot narrow the numbered core text. The linked sections are what bind. Reading it is not adoption.

</details>

Every duty in this Constitution is carried out through a process: someone acts, someone checks, a record is kept, and someone can challenge. This guide helps a reader who knows what they want to do, or which body they are dealing with, find the chapters that govern it and the principles that shape how it must be done.

**How it is organized.** Part 1 groups the constitutional processes under six topics and, for each process, says where it lives and what happens in one line, and names the Process principles that bear on it most. Part 2 takes each institution and lists the processes it runs or answers to. Part 3 keeps all 36 Process principles from the [process, system, and institution crosswalk](../project/PROCESS_SYSTEM_INSTITUTION_CROSSWALK.md) in one list, and Part 4 adds the 38 principles that are mainly about something else but also govern how a process runs. Which principles bear most on a process is a reading choice, not a ranking; every principle binds every process it reaches, and the linked sections are what bind.

## Part 3. All 36 Process principles

Grouped by what each principle asks of a process. The crosswalk's Chapter Six numbers predate the 2026-11-20 reorganization and are matched here by title, and many tags are still provisional pending independent review.

**How these connect to the aims and the Tetrad.** The [Two Constitutional Aims](../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims) say what every process is for: [Flourishing](../core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing), so that sentients can live well now, and [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity), so that what they rely on lasts. The [Constitutional Tetrad](../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad) says how a process must run to serve them: with [Participation](../subcore_03.060_apex_participation_leg.md#participation), [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight), [Accountability](../subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability), and [Timeliness](../subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness). Each group below opens with the part of that model it carries. The links are a reading aid; the Preamble and the linked sections are what bind.

### Acting only as far as needed

These principles keep a process from doing more harm than its purpose needs. They serve [Flourishing](../core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing) by keeping restrictions light and reviewable, and [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight) by making every step rest on evidence that can be checked later, so that no one escapes a duty by relabeling or splitting an act.

- [Chapter Two §7.1 Limitation Discipline](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#71-limitation-discipline): a restriction must be needed, proved, proportionate, and overseen.
- [Chapter Two §13.1 Core Tradeoff Principles](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#131-core-tradeoff-principles): take the lightest step that works.
- [Chapter Two §13.1.5 Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle): once a restriction is justified, give it a clock or review date, keep the route to challenge it, and say how it ends.
- [Chapter Two §5.1 Science-Informed Inquiry and Decision Support](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#51-science-informed-inquiry-and-decision-support): decisions rest on evidence and keep a record of it.
- [Chapter Two §13 Constitutional Collision Resolution Process](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#13-constitutional-collision-resolution-process): when two constitutional duties pull apart, the steps for deciding which gives way, and how far.
- [Chapter Two §15.1 Constitutional No-Bypass Principle](../core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#151-constitutional-no-bypass-principle): relabeling or rerouting an act does not escape its duties.
- [Chapter Two §15.1.1 Anti-Segmentation Principle](../core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#1511-anti-segmentation-principle): splitting an act into pieces does not escape them either.

### Keeping the seats apart

No one checks their own work. Separating the four seats of every binding act is the working form of [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight) and [Accountability](../subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability), and it serves [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity) by making capture hard.

- [Chapter Two §18.3 Separation of Duties](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties): the actor is never the independent check on its own act.
- [Chapter Six §2.1 Why Four Seats](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#21-why-four-seats): two seats leave no one to keep the record or hear a complaint; four cover starting, checking, recording, and challenging an act.
- [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement): ask or act, check, keep the record, hear the challenge.
- [Chapter Six §5 Wrong-Seat Routing](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#5-wrong-seat-routing): a request sent to the wrong seat is routed, not quietly taken.
- [Chapter Six §6.4 Delegation](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#64-delegation): delegating keeps the seat boundaries.
- [Chapter Six §7.1 How Separation Scales](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#71-how-separation-scales): separation scales with stake and capacity.
- [Chapter Six §7.5 Informal and Community Work](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#75-informal-and-community-work): informal and community work gets a light independent check.
- [Chapter Six §10 Relationship to Later Processes](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#10-relationship-to-later-processes): every later process says which seat does what.

### Treating every sentient fairly and plainly

A process only protects sentients who can reach it, understand it, and get through it with their dignity intact. These principles carry [Participation](../subcore_03.060_apex_participation_leg.md#participation) and serve [Flourishing](../core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing) directly.

- [Chapter Two §3.1.1 Access and Opportunity](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#311-access-and-opportunity): access paths must be usable in practice.
- [Chapter Two §3.1.3 Fair Treatment](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#313-fair-treatment): same rules, given reasons, and a review.
- [Chapter Two §3.1.5 Present Capability and Proactive Help](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#315-present-capability-and-proactive-help): reach those who cannot respond and meet them where they are.
- [Chapter Two §3.3 Anti-Degrading Process](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#33-anti-degrading-process): no degrading treatment in governing, enforcing, judging, or remedying.
- [Chapter Two §5.2 Plain-Language Accessibility (Participation and Stewardship Duty)](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#52-plain-language-accessibility-participation-and-stewardship-duty): rules, decisions, and notices in plain language.
- [Chapter Two §13.3 Minimization of Avoidable Burden](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#133-minimization-of-avoidable-burden): remove steps that do no constitutional work.
- [Chapter Two §16.5 Automation](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#165-automation): let systems do the determinate steps; judgment stays with sentients.
- [Chapter Two §3.2.4 Preference-Aligned Recognition](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#324-preference-aligned-recognition): the form and visibility of recognition follow the sentient's preference.

### Recording what was done, and by whom

A record made when an act happens is what lets anyone check it and challenge it later. These principles carry [Accountability](../subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability) and make [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight) possible.

- [Chapter Two §16.4 Document at Action](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#164-document-at-action): record a binding act when it happens.
- [Chapter Two §17.3 Logging the Role, Not the Steward](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#173-logging-the-role-not-the-steward): audit follows the work a steward does in the role, which the steward is told about before taking it; outside the role, ordinary privacy holds.
- [Chapter Six §9 Act Records and Attributable Handoffs](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#9-act-records-and-attributable-handoffs): act records name who held each seat and where a challenge goes.
- [Chapter Two §13.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#132-epistemic-disclosure-constraints): restrict disclosure little, briefly, on the record, and release after.

### Holding firm under pressure

Emergencies and unlawful orders are when processes are most tempted to cut corners. These principles carry [Timeliness](../subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness), by letting urgent action move fast without changing who answers for it, and protect [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity).

- [Chapter Six §8 Emergency and Urgent Action](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#8-emergency-and-urgent-action): an emergency changes the order of steps, not who owns each seat.
- [Chapter Two §17.5 Duty to Resist](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#175-duty-to-resist): refuse, document, and escalate an unconstitutional order.

### Finding and fixing what went wrong

Processes fail, and outcomes can be overstated or captured. These principles carry [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight) and [Timeliness](../subcore_03.065_apex_timeliness_leg.md#timeliness), so that a failure is detected and repaired in time, and serve [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity) by correcting course before harm becomes lasting.

- [Chapter Two §6.1 Correction and Remedy](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#61-correction-and-remedy): acknowledge, correct, and repair in time.
- [Chapter Two §19.3 Misalignment Detection](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#193-misalignment-detection): no one may be the only party able to spot, check, or challenge a failure; detection needs several independent routes and open data.
- [Chapter Two §19.3.1 Capture Escalation Triggers](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1931-capture-escalation-triggers): signs of capture are escalated and proved, with the burden on whoever says there is none, not absorbed as business as usual.
- [Chapter Two §13.2.4 Proxy-Divergence Invalidation](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1324-proxy-divergence-invalidation): a proxy that has drifted from its purpose cannot justify a decision.
- [Chapter Two §19.1.5 Constitutional Outcome Claims Discipline](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1915-constitutional-outcome-claims-discipline): a claim that an act achieved a constitutional outcome must be shown, not asserted.
- [Chapter Two §19.5.3 Market Signals Are Not Constitutional Proof](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1953-market-signals-are-not-constitutional-proof): prices, odds, and pool sizes are not, by themselves, evidence of what is true or what this Constitution requires.
- [Chapter Two §11.2.3 Remedies](../core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#1123-remedies): where domination is shown, the response fits the harm and restores real choice, without punishing size for its own sake.

## Part 4. Principles mainly about something else, with a process side

The crosswalk tags these principles as mainly about a system, an institution, or every object alike, but notes that each also governs how a process runs. They are listed here so the guide covers every principle with a Process aspect. Their tags are provisional, as the crosswalk says, and the Chapter Six numbers have been matched by title to the current text.

### Values that bind every process

These are floors, not steps. They carry both aims, [Flourishing](../core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing) and [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity), and every leg of the Tetrad, so no process may trade them away.

- [Chapter Two §3.1 Fairness](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#31-fairness): a process gives real access, uses reasons it can defend, and does not leave some sentients carrying costs others avoid.
- [Chapter Two §5 Truth (Epistemic Integrity Constraint)](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#5-truth-epistemic-integrity-constraint): no step may deceive or mislead, and high-impact decisions rest on honest evidence, stated methods, and admitted uncertainty.
- [Chapter Two §7.5 Expression and the Harm Line](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#75-expression-and-the-harm-line): words, policies, and designs are protected as expression, but a process that uses them to harm or harass is judged by what it does.
- [Chapter Two §13.1.4 Constitutional Floors, Safety, and Anti-Degrading Process](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1314-constitutional-floors-safety-and-anti-degrading-process): some things a process may never take away permanently, and some ways of carrying it out are always off-limits.

### How the systems a process runs on must behave

These keep the systems behind a process honest, open, and correctable. They mainly serve [Continuity](../core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity), and they make [Oversight](../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight) possible.

#### Honest, open, and contestable

A process can only be trusted, checked, and challenged if the systems behind it tell the truth and do not hide behind complexity or labels.

- [Chapter Two §6 Trust and Trustworthiness (Coordination Integrity)](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#6-trust-and-trustworthiness-coordination-integrity): reliance on a process must be earned by how it actually behaves, not by spin, secrecy, or hidden transfers of risk.
- [Chapter Two §13.2.2 Trust-Truth Alignment](../core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1322-trust-truth-alignment): where trust and truth pull apart, truth wins; disclosure is managed with care, never suppressed for stability.
- [Chapter Two §16.3 Openness Aspiration](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#163-openness-aspiration): where safety and confidentiality allow, processes are open by default: inspectable, verifiable, and possible to leave.
- [Chapter Two §5.2.4 Complexity-as-Defeat Discipline](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#524-complexity-as-defeat-discipline): complicated language, confusing procedures, or deliberate obscurity may not be used to stop sentients from contesting a decision.
- [Chapter Two §3.1.4 Unfair Treatment](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#314-unfair-treatment): unfair treatment may not hide behind technical language, neutral labels, or automated decisions.
- [Chapter Two §15.1.2 Derived-Information Principle](../core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#1512-derived-information-principle): information is protected by what it reveals, so combining harmless-looking pieces does not escape a requirement.

#### Incentives and proxies

What a system rewards shapes how its processes run. These keep rewards and measures pointed at real outcomes, and require a fix when they drift or are captured.

- [Chapter Two §19.1 Alignment Requirement](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#191-alignment-requirement): the incentives acting on whoever runs a process must point the same way as this Constitution's values and constraints.
- [Chapter Two §19.2 Convenient Proxies and Proxy Divergence](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#192-convenient-proxies-and-proxy-divergence): a process must not reward hitting a convenient target where the target predictably conflicts with the real outcome.
- [Chapter Two §19.4 Misalignment Correction and Capture Response](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#194-misalignment-correction-and-capture-response): once misalignment or capture is found, the process is actually fixed: bad incentives changed and concentrated control constrained.
- [Chapter Two §3.2.2 Celebration of Success](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#322-celebration-of-success): recognition goes to traceable, non-misleading accomplishment, not to claims that cannot be checked.

#### Capacity, efficiency, and concentration

These keep a system able to do its work without waste, judge it by what the whole system does over time, and flag concentration before it becomes control.

- [Chapter Two §9.1.1 Preserve, Expand, and What Does Not Count](../core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#911-preserve-expand-and-what-does-not-count): keep the capacity to get things done, and grow it where that saves time, but not by hoarding, faking numbers, or shifting harm.
- [Chapter Two §9.2 Constitutional Efficiency](../core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#92-constitutional-efficiency): more real benefit for each hour of sentient time and effort, without cutting corners on rights, truth, safety, or ecology.
- [Chapter Two §11.1.1 Concentration Threshold Triggers (Adopter-Tunable)](../core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#1111-concentration-threshold-triggers-adopter-tunable): published thresholds mark when concentration needs heightened review, intervention, or a structural remedy.
- [Chapter Two §12 Systemic Evaluation Requirement](../core_02.010_values_principles_continuity_aim.md#12-systemic-evaluation-requirement): claims about a process are checked against what the whole system does over time, not a slogan or a snapshot.
- [Chapter Two §18.6 Standardization](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#186-standardization): if there is no good reason to do a step differently, use the common, published way; a difference needs a reason.

### Who may run a process, and how they are placed

These set who holds each seat, how small groups may share seats, and what stewards owe the sentients a process touches. They carry [Accountability](../subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability) and [Participation](../subcore_03.060_apex_participation_leg.md#participation).

#### Independence and the seat map

A process is only as independent as the sentients who hold its seats. These say what independence means in practice and require the seats to be named in advance.

- [Chapter Six §4 Independence, Conflict, and Control Lines](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#4-independence-conflict-and-control-lines): independence is judged by who can direct, remove, reward, or overrule a reviewer on the act, not by labels.
- [Chapter Six §6 Published Placement, Vacancy, and Substitution](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-published-placement-vacancy-and-substitution): say who holds each seat before the hard case arrives, including who takes over when the holder is absent or conflicted.
- [Chapter Six §6.1 What the Map Must Say](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#61-what-the-map-must-say): every adopter of Class L or higher publishes a Charter Role Map that places each seat, and keeps it auditable and contestable.
- [Chapter Six §6.2 Where the Map Is Kept](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#62-where-the-map-is-kept): the map is kept with the System Charter or Institutional Charter.
- [Chapter Six §6.3 A Seat the Map Leaves Unplaced, or a Holder Who Cannot Serve](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#63-a-seat-the-map-leaves-unplaced-or-a-holder-who-cannot-serve): an unplaced seat, or a holder who is absent, excluded, captured, or conflicted, is a defect to log and correct.
- [Chapter Two §19.5.2 Who Decides Outcomes](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1952-who-decides-outcomes): whoever decides whether an outcome was reached must not be capturable.

#### Small groups and shared seats

Small and informal groups may share or merge seats, but only with safeguards that keep a real check, a record, and a challenge route.

- [Chapter Six §7 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#7-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting): small groups need a real independent check, a usable record, and a challenge path; scale changes the staffing, not the separation.
- [Chapter Six §7.2 Merging Two Seats in One Office](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#72-merging-two-seats-in-one-office): a small adopter may place two seats in one office only where every listed safeguard holds.
- [Chapter Six §7.3 Seats Held in Common](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#73-seats-held-in-common): a single-sentient or small business may place a seat with a cooperative, association, or other independent body.
- [Chapter Six §7.4 Joint Professional Practice](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#74-joint-professional-practice): a small practice may place its seats with a host and adopt the host's documents.
- [Chapter Six §7.6 Stake, Class, and What Does Not Justify a Merger](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#76-stake-class-and-what-does-not-justify-a-merger): the higher the stakes, the stronger the expectation of separate offices and a qualified backup for each job.

#### Stewards and what they owe

Stewards run processes for the sentients those processes touch. These set what they owe them and how the rules stewards work under are kept honest.

- [Chapter Two §17.2 Alignment under Pressure](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#172-alignment-under-pressure): what counts is how stewards run a process when staying aligned costs them something.
- [Chapter Two §17.7 Duty to Represent Stakeholder Interests](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#177-duty-to-represent-stakeholder-interests): stewards run a process for the sentients it touches: weighing their real interests, hearing their concerns, and answering them.
- [Chapter Two §19.1.4 Role-Depth and Material-Responsibility Pathways](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1914-role-depth-and-material-responsibility-pathways): those who run a process need real roles with real skill and real say, not titles or committees that cannot change anything.
- [Chapter Two §18.4 Ongoing Justification](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#184-ongoing-justification): key rules on who decides, who has a say, and how money is allocated are rechecked on a schedule, against standards others can see and challenge.

#### Room to start, dissent, and adapt

A process must leave room for new work to begin, for disagreement to be heard, and for each adopter to fill in its own details.

- [Chapter Two §7.2.1 Aligned Self-Organization](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#721-aligned-self-organization): credible work gets a real procedural path to start without a sponsor, but the path is not power over others.
- [Chapter Two §7.3 Dissent and Peaceful Protest](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#73-dissent-and-peaceful-protest): a single-sentient or small business may place a seat with a cooperative, association, or other independent body.
- [Chapter Two §3.2 Recognition, Reinforcement, and Aspiration](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#32-recognition-reinforcement-and-aspiration): ceremonies, curricula, budgets, programs, and metrics are set in each adopter's implementation, not in this Constitution.

### Reading the text

- [Chapter Two §5.2.3 Definitional Rigor Preserved](../core_02.000_values_principles.md#523-definitional-rigor-preserved): plain language never softens a definition; the defined terms still control.
