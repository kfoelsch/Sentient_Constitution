Chapter Eleven is the constitutional owner of **contribution consequences**: the effect, if any, of verified contribution at Question 3. Its rules start from verified contribution Standing Records and end at the gated pathway outcomes that Chapter Nineteen states.
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this chapter asks what verified contribution may do. It may support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked. It never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Eleven decides the effect, if any, of verified contribution at Question 3. It does so in two steps: recency and currentness, then competency bars and clearances for gated pathways that are not locked (**section 3**).

**Role:** The chapter is one of four that together own Question 3, with Chapters [Subcore 10-F — Sixteen](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#subcore-10-f-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows), [Eighteen](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#chapter-twelve-violation-consequences--remedy-correction-and-locks), and [Nineteen](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#chapter-thirteen-final-standing-effect-restoration-and-enforcement):

- It decides from the contribution Standing Record only.
- It takes the decision order from **Chapter Ten section 4.4** (*Processing Order*). The contribution track and the violation track do not wait for each other.
- Its result meets the violation track only at the final standing effect in Chapter Thirteen, where a lock controls.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- recency and currentness (**section 3.1**); and
- competency bars and clearances (**section 3.2**).

The chapter leaves:

- the decision order to Chapter Ten;
- remedy, correction, and lock rules to Chapter Twelve; and
- the final standing effect to Chapter Thirteen.

It points to them and does not restate them.

### 2. Why Contribution Consequences Exist

*In plain terms: verified good work should count for something, but never as a way to buy past harm. This chapter exists to say what contribution may and may not do.*

A system that never recognizes contribution teaches that effort does not matter. A system that lets contribution cancel harm teaches that harm can be paid for. This chapter lets verified contribution support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked, and says plainly that it never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.

### 3. Contribution Consequences

*In plain terms: this section asks what verified contribution may do. It may support recognition and readiness for gated pathways that are not locked. It never offsets a verified harm, cancels a lock, or opens a blocked gated pathway.*

This section starts from verified Chapter Ten contribution records that apply to the decision. From those Standing Records it asks two things:

- how current the contribution still is; and
- whether any competency clearance for an open gated pathway is met against its published bar.

**Independence:** past help does not set the remedy, the correction duties, the safeguards, or the locks under [§3 Violation, Correction, and Prevention](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#3-violation-correction-and-prevention) and [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#4-lock-design-and-enforcement). The one exception is help given after the violation, which can count as restitution under [§3.1.2 Remedy](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#312-remedy) and so reduces the remedy still owed. Help also cannot cancel or balance out a verified harm—that [Subcore 10-B — no-offset rule](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#44-linked-standing-records-and-no-offset-bridge) from Chapter Fourteen still applies.

**Lock precedence:** an applicable standing lock under [§3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) and [§4 Lock Design and Enforcement](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#4-lock-design-and-enforcement) controls. Neither recency nor a competency bar or clearance can open a blocked gated pathway, waive the Rights Floor ([Subcore 10-F §3.6 The Rights Floor Is Not Reduced](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#36-the-rights-floor-is-not-reduced)), erase a violation, or replace remedy or correction.

Decide contribution consequences in this order:

- Confirm recognition eligibility: recognition must be evidence-backed, contestable, reviewable, non-monopolistic, and available on equal standards to informal, unpaid, peer-organized, mutual-aid, maintenance, repair, teaching, and community-stewardship work.
  - Equal standards include separation of duties: under [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement) and [Subcore 10-B §7.9 Informal and Small-Scope Records](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#79-informal-and-small-scope-records) the verify seat for informal work is met by any independent body with published authority to rely on the Standing Record, not by the formality of the verifying authority.
  - A bar that demands formal-institution verification an informal group cannot reach is the [§3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](#32-competency-bars-and-clearances) gatekeeping pattern.
- Apply recency and currentness ([§3.1 Recency and Currentness](#31-recency-and-currentness)).
- Apply competency bars and clearances ([§3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](#32-competency-bars-and-clearances)); where a lock blocks a gated pathway, no clearance operates on it.
- Hand off each gated pathway outcome to [§3 Final Standing Effect](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#3-final-standing-effect); restoration and reassessment remain under [§4 Restoration and Reassessment](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-restoration-and-reassessment).

<a id="31-recency-and-currentness"></a>
#### 3.1 Recency and Currentness

*In plain terms: how much does an older verified contribution still say about present competence and reliability? The system answers that automatically for Question 3 readiness. No one has to re-weigh ordinary contributions by hand. Manual review starts only when a contribution is contested. Recency does not rewrite Chapter Ten measurement, and time alone does not settle an unresolved violation.*

Recency is strictly a **Question 3 clearance and readiness input**. It changes no slot, measurement, or descriptor ([Subcore 10-F §3.3 Facts and Measurements Stay Fixed](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#33-facts-and-measurements-stay-fixed)), and it does not change a Chapter Fourteen contribution Standing Record or historical recognition.

For a current trust-, role-, authority-, oversight-, or recognition-sensitive decision, the standing system must **automatically** calculate currentness weight for each applicable verified contribution. No additional manual approval is required for an uncontested contribution when verified inputs and the published formula (or a stricter published role-specific rule) produce a determinate result. The process must run seamlessly unless the contribution—or the inputs used to weight it—is contested, uncertain, or otherwise requires review under [Subcore 10-F §4 Automatic Integration, Review, and Continuity](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#4-automatic-integration-review-and-continuity).

The formula, an illustrative table, and the integration record's contents are in [Subcore 11-A — §3 Currentness Calculation](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#3-currentness-calculation). Its guarantees are:

- **Default rate:** unless a stricter published role-specific rule applies, current weight halves every ten years, calculated by whole days.
- **Each contribution separately:** the weight applies to each contribution on its own. After 50 years a contribution stays in the historical record but supplies no current clearance weight, and continuing contribution may be shown by current Standing Records.
- **No recency for unresolved violation:** the passage of time alone never corrects, remedies, supersedes, or resolves a violation.
- **Contest keeps the calculation:** a contested contribution keeps its automatic calculation, preserved and inspectable, while the contest is reviewed, and the historical contribution record is not erased.
- **Shown on the integration record:** the integration record shows, for each contribution, the basis and result of its weight and any stricter rule used.

<a id="32-competency-bars-and-clearances"></a>
#### 3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances

*In plain terms: a competency bar is the published standard for a gated pathway. Competency clearance is the standing-effect result that says verified readiness meets that bar. Once clearance is in force and the gated pathway is open, it stays open by default. Some roles need periodic retesting; technical forums decide which ones. A real change in role-relevant capacity — for example after a stroke or a mental-health condition that impairs needed judgment — can trigger reassessment. Clearance never opens a gated pathway that a standing lock still blocks.*

A **competency bar** is the published, gated-pathway-specific qualification standard. Anyone affected must be able to see the rule, check how it was applied, and challenge it if it is wrong. In short, it states what readiness the role or access requires.

A **competency clearance** is the positive standing-effect result when verified readiness meets that bar. In short, it answers: does this sentient currently meet the bar for this gated pathway?

Its full text is in [Subcore 11-A — §4 Competency Bars and Clearances in Detail](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#4-competency-bars-and-clearances-in-detail). Its guarantees are:

- **Proportionate bars:** a bar asks only for the readiness the gated pathway needs, scaled to its reach, others' reliance on it, and the foreseeable risk, and informal work meets it on the same standard as a formal credential ([Subcore 11-A — §4.1 What a Bar Requires](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#41-what-a-bar-requires)).
- **No gatekeeping:** a bar or clearance process may not become arbitrary gatekeeping, opaque credentialism, prestige screening, insider sponsorship, or a formal-channel monopoly ([Subcore 11-A — §4.2 Guarding against Gatekeeping](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#42-guarding-against-gatekeeping)).
- **Trust-sensitive is contestable:** an adopter publishes which gated pathways it treats as trust-sensitive and why, and any sentient affected may challenge that conclusion and the bar before an independent reviewer outside the body that set it.
- **No bundled clearances:** no one may require clearances from several gated pathways as a joint condition of another role, access, or standing.
- **Clearance continues:** once granted and open, clearance stays in force by default. Retesting happens only on published, role-proportionate, contestable standards ([Subcore 11-A — §4.3 Continuity, Retesting, and Capacity Change](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#43-continuity-retesting-and-capacity-change)).
- **Capacity, not condition:** reassessment after a change in capacity looks at the capacity the role needs now, not at disability or health status, and considers accommodation, support, or a narrower gated pathway before withdrawal.
- **Readable result:** every clearance result states the outcome, the bar, the evidence, the effect of currentness, the review route, and the reassessment triggers ([Subcore 11-A — §4.4 What the Clearance Record Must Say](subcore_11.010_recency_competency_clearance.md#44-what-the-clearance-record-must-say)).
- **Locks control:** clearance never opens a gated pathway that a standing lock blocks.

Gated pathway outcomes after bars and clearances are stated under [§3 Final Standing Effect](core_13.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#3-final-standing-effect).
