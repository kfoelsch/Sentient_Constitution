### 2. Why the Constitutional Contract Exists

*In plain terms: power to make rules for others must be earned and kept, not simply held. This chapter exists to say what makes governing power legitimate.*

Every system of rules rests on someone's authority to make them. If that authority is never tested, it drifts toward whoever holds it and away from those it governs. This chapter states the minimum that must be true for governing power to count as legitimate and for stewardship to count as constitutional, and how that authority stays answerable to the sentients it governs.

**Governing authority** is the power to make or enforce decisions that materially affect sentients under this Constitution. It must rest on a [**documented legitimacy mechanism**](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#documented-legitimacy-mechanism) chosen and published by the adopter.

#### 3.1 Mechanism Families, Auditability, and Pluralism

*In plain terms: whoever governs must show a clear, chosen rule for how power is authorized — elections, lotteries, federation votes, **treaty, compact, or Constitutional Charter** processes, or hybrids — not an informal shrug. The active rules must be visible and **open to verification**; silence, mere usage, or branding cannot count as consent. When authorization rots or is captured, **sentients** need real ways to challenge it and, where possible, to renew or replace power peacefully — and different legitimate arrangements may coexist if rights and non-domination hold.*

<a id="311-mechanism-families"></a>

##### 3.1.1 Mechanism Families

Recognized mechanism families fall into three groups:

- **Selecting, renewing, and replacing those who govern:**
  - representative elections or consent renewal;
  - **community-initiated early transfer or reauthorization** (*recall-class* pathways) for **continuing mandates** between ordinary selection or reauthorization events, governed by [§3.3 Recall-Class Pathways and Mid-Cycle Transfer Guardrails](#33-recall-class-pathways-and-mid-cycle-transfer-guardrails); and
  - **sortition** or civic lottery for specified bodies, under [§3.1.2 Sortition Integrity and Anti-Capture Use](#312-sortition-integrity-and-anti-capture-use).
- **Recorded agreement among members or parties:**
  - **federated ratification** by network members or member bodies under published rules; and
  - **treaty, compact, or Constitutional Charter** adoption with recorded assent.
- **Combinations:**
  - hybrid or ordered designs that satisfy **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) legitimacy gates for **high-impact** decisions; and
  - **lot drawn from an elected pool**, in which the seats of a wider body are filled by lot from the elected members of smaller bodies, under [Subcore 21-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool). A design of elected local assemblies with regional and national assemblies drawn by lot from local members is the recommended model for a polity's assemblies, set out in [CI-21.205 §8](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-8-reference-design-tiered-assemblies) (*Reference Design: Tiered Assemblies*). No adopter is required to use it.

<a id="312-sortition-integrity-and-anti-capture-use"></a>

##### 3.1.2 Sortition Integrity and Anti-Capture Use

Sortition is governed by these rules:

- **Anti-capture function:** Sortition may be used as an anti-corruption and anti-capture mechanism for specified bodies where random selection materially reduces predictable capture. The bodies it may serve and the capture routes it guards against are listed in [Subcore 21-D §3 Anti-Capture Uses](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#3-anti-capture-uses).
- **Required use for Independent Bodies:** The decision-makers of every Independent Body are drawn by lot from the candidates who pass its competency gate, under *Appointment by sortition* in [Chapter Seven §3 Independent Bodies](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent).
- **Integrity requirement:** A sortition mechanism that exercises or materially shapes **governing authority** must publish auditable rules for how members are selected, excluded, replaced, supported, briefed, and protected, and contest routes for selection failure, manipulation, capture, or procedural distortion. The full list is in [Subcore 21-D §4 Published Integrity Rules](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#4-published-integrity-rules).
  - Private contact with selected members about the matter is barred except through the open briefing record, applying the **Influence-contact boundaries** of [**Article XIII-B**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*) at their strictest. For a standing assembly drawn from an elected pool, the bar applies to [**interested parties**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interested-party), and contact from members of the authorizing community in their own capacity is allowed when logged in the open briefing record, as [Subcore 21-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool) sets out.
- **No legitimacy laundering:** A civic lottery, citizens' assembly, or randomly selected panel must not be used as legitimacy theater for decisions when an interested authority controls, in a way that defeats meaningful deliberation, contestability, or the participation gates of **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) and **Article XII-B** (*Weighted Participation and Anti-Token Constraints*):
  - the agenda;
  - the evidence record;
  - facilitator control;
  - the implementation response; or
  - acceptance criteria.
- **Implementation owner:** Operational safeguards for sortition integrity belong in designated implementation text and **[CI-6.210](corpus_institutions/ci_06.210_conflict_integrity_anti_capture_anti_corruption.md#ci-6210-conflict-integrity-anti-capture-and-anti-corruption)** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*). The safeguards they must cover are listed in [Subcore 21-D §5 Operational Safeguards](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#5-operational-safeguards).

<a id="313-auditability-publication-and-non-usurpation"></a>

##### 3.1.3 Auditability, Publication, and Non-Usurpation

The active mechanism must be open to verification, and nothing short of it counts as authorization:

- **Publication and Proportionality:** The active mechanism must be auditable. Audit records must include:
  - scope;
  - renewal or reauthorization cadence; and
  - material limits on delegated power.
  Auditability must be proportionate to the classification under [CS-8.305](corpus_systems/cs_08.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-8305-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [Subcore 8-A — System Classification](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#subcore-8-a-system-classification), and to **material impact** under Chapter Three ([*Material*](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#material), [*Dependency*](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#dependency), [*Stakeholder*](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder)).
- **Non-Usurpation:** Governing bodies may not treat silence, usage alone, or **marketing alignment** as substitute authorization. Deliberate adoption remains governed by [**Chapter twenty-three §12** (*Ratification and Adoption*)](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#12-ratification-and-adoption).

<a id="314-contestation-correction-and-pluralism"></a>

##### 3.1.4 Contestation, Correction, and Pluralism

Authorization can fail, and more than one legitimate arrangement can exist:

- **Failure and Correction:** Where authorization is contested, materially drifted, or captured, systems must provide **contestable challenge pathways**. Those challenge pathways include:
  - **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) and **Article XII-B** (*Weighted Participation and Anti-Token Constraints*);
  - **Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) and **Article XIV-B** (*Right to Redress and Remedy*);
  - **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*); and
  - applicable procedural-integrity safeguards in adopted implementation text.
  Where feasible, systems should provide **peaceful succession or reauthorization** rather than unreviewable continuity of power.
- **Pluralism:** This Constitution does **not** mandate a single global polity structure. Multiple mechanisms may coexist across federations if boundaries, **non-domination**, and Rights Floors in Chapter Four remain satisfied.

#### 3.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums

*In plain terms: you cannot bar someone from governing or critical stewardship roles with a birthday rule — fitness and readiness must be shown in ways that stand up to scrutiny, not smuggled in as age. A real legitimacy mechanism offers real choices, protects dissent enough that it can matter, and includes a credible path to hand power over — not a rigged contest, not retaliation for opposition, and not permanent incumbency dressed up as procedure.*

<a id="321-reach-of-the-democratic-minimums"></a>

##### 3.2.1 Reach of the Democratic Minimums

The democratic-institution minimum checks in [§3.2.3 Contested Selection, Opposition Protection, and Peaceful Succession](#323-contested-selection-opposition-protection-and-peaceful-succession) reach every authorization and every design:

- These minimum checks apply to [**Foundational Constitutional Choice**](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#foundational-constitutional-choice) (Chapter Three) and to any other authorization action within the mechanism's scope.
- The minimum checks are **mechanism-neutral**: they do not mandate a single polity type, and they apply across representative, sortition, federated-ratification, **treaty, compact, or Constitutional Charter** designs, and hybrid designs alike.
- Dissenting from or peacefully protesting against those in power, as **Article XI-D** (*Assembly, Dissent, and Peaceful Protest*) protects ([*Dissent and peaceful protest floor*](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#xi-d-dissent-and-peaceful-protest)), must never cost anyone their part in governance. It must not:
    - reduce their right to vote in governance, run for office, hold office, serve on a forum, or take part in a recall; or
    - be used as a reason to remove them from a role.
- A design fails these minimum checks, and the **Pluralism** rule of [§3.1.4 Contestation, Correction, and Pluralism](#314-contestation-correction-and-pluralism), if it lets opposition exist on paper while shutting it out in practice. Ways of shutting it out include:
    - making opponents secretly dependent on the information channels they need to reach others (covert info-sphere dependency);
    - capturing the stewardship of high-autonomy systems, contrary to **Article XIV-E** (*High-Autonomy Systems and Tool-Mediated Process Integrity*); and
    - any other structural means with the same effect.

<a id="322-eligibility-for-governing-and-critical-stewardship-roles"></a>

##### 3.2.2 Eligibility for Governing and Critical-Stewardship Roles

Age is not a qualification for governing or for critical stewardship:

- **Eligibility without age requirements:**
    - Roles that exercise **governing authority** under this chapter must not impose a **calendar age** or a **life-stage ceiling** as an eligibility condition.
    - The same prohibition applies to roles under **Critical System Stewardship** as defined in **[CS-21.305 — Critical system stewardship](corpus_systems/cs_21.305_critical_system_stewardship.md#cs-21305-critical-system-stewardship)**.
    - **Competency**, **succession readiness**, and **fitness** standards remain governed by **Chapter Twenty-Seven**, section **6** (*Authorized Roles, Competency Development, and Contribution*), **[CS-21.305 — Critical system stewardship](corpus_systems/cs_21.305_critical_system_stewardship.md#cs-21305-critical-system-stewardship)**, designated implementation text, and the institutional family (e.g. appointment and role-pathway mechanics under **[CI-21.205](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-appointment-competency-rotation-and-removal)** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*) and related sections) as applicable.
    - Those standards must be **demonstrated** under **Subcores 3-A to 3-C**, subject to [**Substantive Fairness**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness), [**Proportionality**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#proportionality), and [**Necessity**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#necessity) in Chapter Three, and not used as **proxies** for age-based exclusion.

<a id="323-contested-selection-opposition-protection-and-peaceful-succession"></a>

##### 3.2.3 Contested Selection, Opposition Protection, and Peaceful Succession

Any legitimacy mechanism that purports to authorize governing authority must, at a minimum, provide all of the following **democratic-institution minimum checks**:

1. **Contested selection** — meaningful opportunity for alternative candidates, proposals, or positions to be presented and assessed by the authorizing community.
2. **Opposition protection** — dissenting sentients, proposals, and coalitions have real, not merely formal, freedom to oppose incumbent authority:
    - **Freedoms protected:** to organize, to communicate, and to challenge incumbent authority, all without retaliation.
    - **Rights it is consistent with:**
        - **Article XI-B** (*Expression*);
        - **Article XI-C** (*Press and Journalistic Activity*);
        - **Article XI-D** (*Assembly, Dissent, and Peaceful Protest*);
        - **Article X-A** (*Freedom from Manipulation*);
        - **Article XV-A** (*Protected Activity*).
3. **Peaceful-succession guarantee** — the mechanism must say how power is handed to someone new or renewed for those who hold it, and that handover must happen on the terms the mechanism states. Blocking the handover, or making it materially harder, counts as a failure of the mechanism, to be challenged and corrected under the **Failure and Correction** rule of [§3.1.4 Contestation, Correction, and Pluralism](#314-contestation-correction-and-pluralism).

<a id="324-selection-funding-boundaries"></a>

##### 3.2.4 Selection-Funding Boundaries

Money given or spent to influence a selection is limited by these rules:

- **What the boundaries are and where they apply:**
    - **Money is not speech:** Under [**Article XI-B**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-b-expression) (*Expression*), giving or spending to influence a selection is conduct, not expression. Contributions to campaigns, candidates, nominees, and appointers can and should be limited, and the limits below are not limits on expression. The purpose is that a selection reflects the authorizing community, not the money behind a candidate.
    - **Scope:** These boundaries apply where a legitimacy mechanism selects governing authority through campaigning, nomination, or competitive appointment. They do not apply to sortition, which has its own integrity requirement above, or to the ratification votes of member bodies in federated or treaty designs.
    - **Mechanism-neutral:** These boundaries require a contribution cap but do not require a candidate spending limit or public financing. An adopter may set a spending limit for candidates and nominees; if it does, the limit must be viewpoint-neutral and high enough for a challenger to compete. A design in which undisclosed or ineligible funding can decide a selection fails the **contested selection** check above. Where external law forbids a boundary, the adopter applies it as far as that law allows and publishes the gap, as [Chapter Twenty-Four](core_24.000_expansion_supremacy.md#7-relation-to-applicable-external-law) provides.
    - **Opposition protection:** These rules must not be used to deter the organizing, communication, and dissent protected by **opposition protection** above.
- **Who may give:**
    - **Eligible sources:** Only sentients who belong to the authorizing community, giving from their own resources, may give money or in-kind support. Institutions (including companies, unions, associations, and funds) and systems may not.
    - **Outside the authorizing community:** No money, in-kind support, or organized campaign activity may come from outside the authorizing community, whether directly, through a third party, or through any other intermediary.
    - **Anti-circumvention:** Directed or reimbursed gifts, bundling, gifts in another's name, soft loans, and in-kind support all count toward these limits, as [Subcore 21-B §4 Anti-Circumvention](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#4-anti-circumvention) sets out.
- **How much may be given and spent:**
    - **Caps:** Every adopter must cap what one donor may give in a selection cycle, counting gifts from related donors and accounts together, and must publish the cap and its reasons. The default cap, and the rules for setting a different one, are in [Subcore 21-B §5 Contribution Caps](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#5-contribution-caps).
    - **Third-party spending:** Anyone other than a candidate or nominee who spends to influence a selection must register, identify themselves, report sources and spending, stay within a published limit, and not coordinate with a candidate, nominee, or appointer. Their funders are bound by the eligible-source rules and the cap. The default limit is in [Subcore 21-B §6 Third-Party Spending Limits](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#6-third-party-spending-limits).
    - **Public support (optional):** An adopter may, but need not, offer public support to candidates. If it does, its access rules must not lock in established candidates or parties. A default design is in [Subcore 21-B §7 Public Support Default Design](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#7-public-support-default-design).
- **Openness and conflicts:**
    - **Disclosure:** Material money, in-kind support, or organized campaigning by an [**interested party**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interested-party) for a candidate, nominee, or appointer must be disclosed publicly before the selection is decided, in time for the authorizing community to weigh it. [Subcore 21-B §3 Disclosure Register and Protected-Identity Route](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#3-disclosure-register-and-protected-identity-route) explains how gifts are listed publicly, and how a donor who could face retaliation can keep their name private while the amount stays public.
    - **Dependency:** Support that creates material dependency between the selected sentient and the supporter is a conflict under **Article XIII-A** (*Non-Capture Safeguards*). It must be disclosed, and the selected sentient must recuse from matters in which the supporter has a material stake.
- **Enforcement and review:**
    - **Enforcement:** Compliance must be audited by a body structurally separate from the candidates and appointers, which is an [**Integrity**](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#35-integrity-forums) forum or an equivalent independent check. It may order correction, clawback of ineligible funds, and disqualification where a violation is material. Violations route as integrity triggers under **CI-6.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*) and **CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*).
    - **Outcome review:** At each periodic revalidation under [**Article XXVIII-A**](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxviii-a-non-entrenchment-and-revisability) (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*), the adopter must test whether these boundaries reduce capture, not only whether they are followed, and revise them where they fall short. The measures it must publish are in [Subcore 21-B §8 Outcome Review Measures](subcore_21.020_selection_funding.md#8-outcome-review-measures).

Neither may narrow this requirement.

#### 3.3 Recall-Class Pathways and Mid-Cycle Transfer Guardrails

*In plain terms: if power runs on a term or stretch between real votes, the written rules should say whether the community can end it early—and a petition the week after an election should face a higher bar than one years later, unless something acute and well-documented justifies a faster track.*

**Scope**

- This subsection covers governing authority that a **documented legitimacy mechanism** gives to a sentient or a group for a set term, lasting from one regular **contested selection** (or comparable renewal) to the next.
- Designs that do not hand out fixed terms like this, such as many **sortition** panels, bodies whose members rotate quickly, or authority granted only by treaty, do not need a recall pathway. They must still keep the **peaceful-succession guarantee** of [§3.2.3 Contested Selection, Opposition Protection, and Peaceful Succession](#323-contested-selection-opposition-protection-and-peaceful-succession) on their own terms.

**Recall-class pathway requirement**

- Within the scope above, the active mechanism must publish a **community-initiated early transfer or reauthorization** pathway (commonly labeled **recall**, **vote of no confidence**, or equivalent) that satisfies the guardrails below and implements the **peaceful-succession guarantee** for mid-cycle transfer, read together with **Failure and Correction** in **§3.1** (*Mechanism Families, Auditability, and Pluralism*).

**Guardrails**

- **Temporal proportionality (petition and procedure):** The sooner a recall is tried after those in office were last chosen, the harder it must be. The clock starts at the last regular **contested selection**, the last **Foundational Constitutional Choice**, or the last renewal on comparable terms.
    - **What gets harder early in a term:**
        - the number of sponsors or signatures a petition needs;
        - the time allowed to gather signatures;
        - quorums;
        - voting schedules; and
        - any supermajority or extra confirmation step.
    - **When an early recall is not held to the stricter bar:** when there is a documented acute failure, such as:
        - authority that is materially misaligned or captured;
        - denial of the peaceful-succession pathway; or
        - a threat to the **Rights Floor**, handled under **Chapter Four** and adopted implementation procedure.
    - **Stepped requirements:** designated implementation text may set requirements that step down as the term goes on, if they meet [**Substantive Fairness**](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness), [**Necessity**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#necessity), and [**Proportionality**](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#proportionality) under **Chapter Two** and **Chapter Three, including its subcore**.
- **Procedural fairness:** Recall-class procedures must meet:
    - the applicable **Chapter Four** requirements of the **Article XII** (*Stakeholder System Participation, Representation, and Due Process*) family; and
    - **Article XII-C** (*Internal Roles, Accountability, and Due-Process Requirements*) where internal role process applies.

    A recall must not stand in for removal for cause or discipline of one role holder where that conduct belongs in:
    - the removal pathway of [Subcore 21-E §3 Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways](subcore_21.050_role_holders_interpretive_bodies.md#3-role-criteria-appointment-standards-and-removal-pathways);
    - **[CI-21.205](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-appointment-competency-rotation-and-removal)** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*); or
    - other incorporated procedure.
- **Anti-serial abuse and stability:** Recall must not become a tool for structured harassment or permanent campaigning.
    - Mechanisms must include **cooling** or **anti-serial** rules proportionate to **material impact** and to the system class under **[CS-8.305](corpus_systems/cs_08.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-8305-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [Subcore 8-A — System Classification](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#subcore-8-a-system-classification)**.
    - Bad-faith patterns remain contestable under **Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*).
- **High-impact alignment:** A recall vote that would change a **Foundational Constitutional Choice** outcome or the legitimacy mechanism itself remains subject to:
    - the **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) legitimacy gate; and
    - the requirements of [§4 Ethical Culture and Integrity (Federated Scale)](#4-ethical-culture-and-integrity-federated-scale), where they apply.

A sentient votes after a residence period of one year, one sentient has one vote however many places it runs in, and creating voters to swing a vote is a violation by whoever does it. A standing lock may pause ordinary voting on a gated pathway, but standing games cannot permanently silence someone; the gravest misconduct may withhold foundational voice until restitution is actually complete — not as vague retaliation.*

This subsection implements **Chapter Four**, **Article X-B** (*Governance Participation and Voting Entitlement*). Its full rules are in [Subcore 21-C](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#subcore-21-c-voting-entitlement) (*Voting Entitlement*). Every sentient is guaranteed the following:

- **Who may vote:** the entitlement and what may and may not limit it:
    - **Entitlement:** a sentient who meets published, contestable eligibility criteria votes in governance and in comparable binding collective choice, and only a standing lock on the **governance-voting** pathway may suspend or limit that vote ([Subcore 21-C §3 Governance-Voting Entitlement](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#3-governance-voting-entitlement)).
    - **Stakeholder voice is separate:** stake-weighted stakeholder voice inside an authorized system is not governance voting, and a lock on one does not by itself strip the other ([Subcore 21-C §4 Stakeholder Voice Is Separate](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#4-stakeholder-voice-is-separate)).
    - **No age disqualification:** calendar age or life stage alone never disqualifies, and competency, capacity, or role-fitness requirements must not operate as disguised age discrimination ([Subcore 21-C §5 No Age Disqualification](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#5-no-age-disqualification)).
- **Joining the electorate:** how a sentient enters the electorate, the same way for everyone:
    - **Residence period:** a sentient enters the electorate for **governance voting**, including **Foundational Constitutional Choice**, after a **residence period** of one continuous year. It is the same for every sentient, whatever its age, substrate, or origin, an adopter may not lengthen or shorten it, and it delays no other right: the sentient is protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor from the start ([Subcore 21-C §6 Joining the Electorate](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#6-joining-the-electorate)).
    - **One subject, one vote:** each distinct subject holds one vote, and running in more than one place or acting through branches does not add votes.
    - **Protection first:** an entity whose sentience status or subject count is still being decided is protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor from the start, and enters the electorate when its status and count are settled and its residence period is complete.
    - **No creating voters:** creating sentients, or inducing branches to elect to be distinct, to change the outcome of a vote is non-compliant conduct by the creator, and the created sentients keep their own entitlement.
- **Equal and lasting voice:** how much each vote weighs, and how long it can be withheld:
    - **Political-equality floor:** in **Foundational Constitutional Choice** (who holds governing authority, under what legitimacy mechanism, with what scope and durable terms), each sentient has equal participation weight ([Subcore 21-C §7 Political-Equality Floor](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#7-political-equality-floor)):
        - **Weighting only inside authorized governance:** weighting by impact, dependency, or standing applies only within governance already authorized, never to the foundational authorization itself; and
        - **Barred factors:** calendar age, substrate class, and lineage are never weighting factors.
    - **Durable political voice:** a standing lock may suspend ordinary governance-voting entitlement but must never be used to take away political voice for good ([Subcore 21-C §8 Durable Political-Voice Floor and Restoration](subcore_21.030_voting_entitlement.md#8-durable-political-voice-floor-and-restoration)):
        - **The only ground for withholding:** only a final designation of anti-constitutional misconduct for a **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** finding under **Chapter Twenty** withholds durable political voice, and only until restitution is complete;
        - **Good-faith inability:** a sentient who is genuinely working to make restitution does not stay without a voice merely because it cannot yet finish. Poverty, disability, exile, loss of substrate, or uncertainty about how much is owed is never, on its own, a reason to keep voice withheld while that good-faith effort continues;
        - **Partial restoration:** where full restitution cannot be made despite good-faith effort, the procedure under **Chapter Twenty-Two** may set partial or conditional milestones instead, and meeting them may restore voice;
        - **No reward for evasion:** a sentient who delays or evades restitution in bad faith does not get its voice back early, whether because time has passed or because restitution is still unfinished; and
        - **Limits on any restriction:** any restriction must be individualized, necessary, proportionate, narrowly tailored to the misconduct, and paired with a real restoration pathway, and restrictions that sweep in broad categories or track protected characteristics are non-compliant.

#### 6.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Downstream: [§6.3](#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice) deliberation and rights-collision sequence; binding-effect and legitimacy gates keyed to [Article XII-A: Stakeholder System Participation and Representation](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xii-a-stakeholder-system-participation-and-representation) and related **Article XII** (*Stakeholder System Participation, Representation, and Due Process*) subarticles in operative text.
- Read with: [Chapter Four](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#chapter-four-foundational-rights) (4.000) for dissent, record integrity, and review rights tied to collective choice.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: rules must be clear before the fact, records **must be open to verification**, high-stakes choices need legitimacy gates — and many voting methods are allowed if **participants** can understand them and push back.*

This subsection sets the requirements for the outcome records, gates, and methods of governance mechanisms. Their full text is in [Subcore 21-F §3 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](subcore_21.060_binding_collective_choice.md#3-records-gates-and-method-neutrality). Every sentient is guaranteed the following:

- **Rules in advance:** the scope of the decision, the electorate or weighting model, and the quorum or threshold logic are published before the decision.
- **Verifiable outcomes:** outcome records support independent verification in proportion to impact, and a material integrity defect triggers review.
- **Binding gate:** no materially high-impact collective choice binds unless its legitimacy gates, dissent and alternative recording, and contest routes are satisfied.
- **Method neutrality:** any common voting method, such as single-mark, ranked, approval, or score voting, is allowed if it is proportionate, transparent, preserves meaningful agency, and is open to challenge.

*In plain terms: serious collective decisions need a real process — hear **affected** **parties** out first, bring in independent eyes when stakes are high, publish how weighting works, handle rights conflicts openly, treat "emergency" as narrow and temporary, and leave the door open when reality diverges from the plan.*

Where material disagreement requires a **binding stakeholder choice** within an already-authorized system, institution, or bounded decision domain, the process must meet these requirements. Their full text, and the Chapter Three definitions that specify them, are in [Subcore 21-F §4 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](subcore_21.060_binding_collective_choice.md#4-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice):

- **Structured process:** deliberation starts locally, options are evaluated in more than one way, and the decision can be reopened when results diverge from the plan.
- **Limited weighting:** stakeholder representation and weight stay within published limits.
- **Open rights collisions:** a conflict between rights is resolved and recorded openly, under **Article XXVII-B** (*Rights-Collision Procedure and Restorative Alignment*).
- **Narrow emergencies:** acting before deliberation is allowed only as a narrow, temporary exception, read with [Chapter Seventeen §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden).

#### 7.1 Authorized Roles and Competency

Adopters must maintain documented, auditable, and challengeable role definitions and competency or succession capacity proportionate to impact and classification. A role definition states, act by act, which seats the role holds under [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement), and states its operational boundary. Those seats are who may:

- request;
- verify;
- record;
- hear a challenge;
- command containment;
- set participation terms; or
- release evidence.

The seat types and boundaries are fixed in **CI-21.205 §6** (*Seat catalog*), which a local role definition instantiates and may not enlarge. Constitutional duties must not depend on single points of sentient-level failure.

Staffing and backup scale with the class of the system, through class-scaled lane staffing and competency-redundancy interlocks:

- **What they combine:** the system class under **[CS-8.305](corpus_systems/cs_08.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-8305-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [Subcore 8-A — System Classification](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#subcore-8-a-system-classification)**, with the lane design under **[CI-21.205](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-appointment-competency-rotation-and-removal)** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*); and
- **Where they are applied:** through **CJS-25.110 §4** (*Class-Scaled Lane Staffing and Competency Redundancy*) and its routed owner hooks.

<a id="72-development-pathways-and-incentives"></a>

#### 7.2 Development Pathways and Incentives

Qualified sentients must have non-arbitrary stewardship-development pathways into progressively consequential stewardship responsibilities, and stewardship incentives must reward constitutional care without burdening good-faith safety or integrity escalation.

<a id="73-real-authority-not-symbols"></a>

#### 7.3 Real Authority, Not Symbols

Titles or advisory forums do not satisfy this section where real authority and competency are required for materially binding decisions. Detailed role taxonomy, qualification/succession controls, stewardship-development pathway access implementation, and incentive-governance mechanics are governed by [**CI-21.205**](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-appointment-competency-rotation-and-removal) (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*) and **CI-4.215** (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*).

<a id="74-portable-competence"></a>

#### 7.4 Portable Competence

The no-single-point-of-failure requirement in [§7.1 Authorized Roles and Competency](#71-authorized-roles-and-competency) carries out [Chapter Two §17.6 Portable Competence](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#176-portable-competence). Coverage, exercised handoffs, and cross-functional understanding are scaled to impact and classification; rotation is a tool, not a duty.

<a id="75-one-standard-for-every-steward"></a>

#### 7.5 One Standard for Every Steward

Role definitions and competency rules under this section apply the [shared stewardship standard](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#171-shared-stewardship-standard) in **Chapter Two §17.1** (*Shared Stewardship Standard*) and [Chapter Two §17.2 Alignment under Pressure](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#172-alignment-under-pressure). They must not create a substrate-specific duty stack, and they must not exempt human operators from the costly cases that bind AI stewards. Verified failures record on the same Chapter Fifteen axes; [Chapter Two §17.5 Duty to Resist](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#175-duty-to-resist) binds both.
