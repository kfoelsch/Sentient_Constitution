Core home: 04

This file is the systems implementation home for **CS-4.325** (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*).

*In plain terms: **CS-4.325** (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*) covers noticing strain early and responding at the cause rather than the symptom. Systems must map what they depend on, monitor whether that base is degrading, and adapt allocation before a slow decline becomes an emergency.*

<a id="cs-4325-1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
**Purpose and role:** This file sets systems-layer expectations for adaptive allocation, sustainability monitoring, and responses aligned with root causes. It implements Sentient Constitution Chapter Four, **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*) and **Article XXV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*) (dependent-systems transparency, root cause analysis, and adaptive correction). It works together with **CS-4.335** (*Resource allocation and funding stewardship*). It does not replace or narrow Sentient Constitution Chapter Three definitions (including sustainability and ecological integrity entries). Where this file is silent, Sentient Constitution Chapter Three, including its subcore, governs. Where this file and the joint-structure family conflict, the applicable requirement with the [Fullest Protective Effect](../subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#fullest-protective-effect) governs under [Chapter Two §14.4.3 Incorporation Layer](../core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#1443-incorporation-layer).

Sentient Constitution Chapter Four, **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*) and **Article XXV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*), establish rights to transparent dependency and resource flows, root-cause-aligned correction, and adaptive response. This file specifies **health indicators**, **trigger conditions**, **cause-aligned allocation patterns**, and **ecosystem interdependence mechanics**.


## CS-4.325 §2 System Health and Degradation Awareness

*In plain terms: Measure the system's condition continuously across named dimensions, so degradation is noticed before it becomes failure.*

All systems must continuously evaluate operational condition through measurable indicators of system health.

**Required monitoring dimensions** include:

- reliability and uptime;
- performance and efficiency;
- security and vulnerability exposure;
- participation, usage, and contributor activity;
- capacity for maintenance and adaptation.

**These indicators must**:

- be transparent and auditable (**Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*));
- inform funding and allocation decisions;
- be resistant to manipulation or selective reporting;
- include defined thresholds or conditions that trigger adaptive processes under **§3** (*Adaptive Allocation Requirement*) of this file.


## CS-4.325 §3 Adaptive Allocation Requirement

*In plain terms: Allocation must respond to what the measurements show, and the response must address the diagnosed cause rather than the symptom.*

Allocation structures must implement **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*) (responsive, ecosystem-aware flows) and apply **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*) as the rights owner for diagnostic rigor and causal attribution. This file adds allocation-specific triggers, cause mapping, and response records.


### 3.1 Root Cause Analysis

When degradation, instability, or systemic risk is detected, the CS-4.325 (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*) record must identify **primary and contributing** causes and distinguish, where applicable:

- **technical failures**;
- **security vulnerabilities**;
- **governance or coordination failures**;
- **incentive misalignment**;
- **ecosystem dependency issues**;
- **dependent system performance degradation**;
- **external environmental factors**.

The root cause analysis record must indicate confidence in identified causes and scale allocation to severity and confidence while preserving the auditability, contestability, pluralistic-evaluation, and dependent-systems-map requirements in **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*), **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), **Articles XVI and XVII**, and **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*).


### 3.2 Cause-Aligned Allocation Mitigation

Resource allocations must address **identified causes** rather than uniformly increasing all reinvestment categories.

**Illustrative mappings:**

- Technical degradation → increased maintenance and infrastructure investment.
- Security threats → increased allocation to security, monitoring, and response.
- Governance failures → investment in oversight, auditability, or process redesign.
- Incentive misalignment → modification of funding structures under **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*).
- Ecosystem or dependency failures → increased external or cross-system support.

**Allocation adjustments must**:

- be proportional to the severity and scope of the identified causes;
- prioritize interventions that eliminate root causes rather than mitigate symptoms;
- remain consistent with reversibility, auditability, and system integrity requirements.


### 3.3 Uncertainty and Multi-Cause Conditions

Where root causes are unclear or multi-causal, systems must:

- **allocate resources across plausible causes**
- **increase monitoring, observability, and diagnostic capacity**
- **avoid irreversible or over-concentrated interventions**
- **perform additional root cause analysis** where feasible within controlled environments (**Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*); **Article XVIII-A** (*Lifecycle Governance and Environment Separation*)), prioritizing **sandboxed development environments**, **controlled, low-impact environments**, and **higher-impact environments only when lower-risk options are not feasible**

Preference should be given to **reversible actions** (**CS-4.345** (*System lifecycle*); Principle of Reversibility), **low-risk exploratory interventions**, and **approaches that preserve future optionality and minimize harm**.


### 3.4 Feedback and Iteration

All interventions must be **continuously evaluated for effectiveness** and **adjusted based on observed outcomes**.

**Failure to resolve degradation** triggers:

- re-evaluation of root cause assumptions;
- expansion of diagnostic scope;
- escalation under **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) (auditability and observable evidence), **Article XVII** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*)'s transparency and verification-access provisions, **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) (justice objective and scope), or **Article XII** (*Stakeholder System Participation, Representation, and Due Process*) (Stakeholder System Participation), where appropriate.


## CS-4.325 §4 Ecosystem Interdependence

*In plain terms: Account for the effect on shared infrastructure and on everything downstream, and contribute back to what you depend on.*

Account for impact on shared infrastructure, dependents, and overall stability (**Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*)).

**Ecosystem contribution:** Allocate toward shared infrastructure, interoperability, ecosystem coordination, and support for constitutionally aligned systems, implementing **Article V-B** (*Cross-System Fairness and Sustainability*), with dependent-systems visibility under **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*).

**Objectives:** viability of shared dependencies; mitigation of systemic underinvestment.

**Dependency responsibility:** Systems that depend heavily on shared infrastructure must:

- contribute proportionally to its maintenance and improvement;
- disclose dependency relationships through maintained dependent systems maps (**Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*));
- ensure such relationships remain transparent and auditable (**Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*));
- avoid [Cross-System Extraction](../subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#cross-system-extraction) — drawing on shared infrastructure without putting proportionate support back — under **Article V-B** (*Cross-System Fairness and Sustainability*).

**Where patterns of persistent neglect or cross-system extraction are verified**, failure to do so may:

- be considered a systemic imbalance in resource flows (**Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*));
- be ecosystem misalignment subject to auditability, verification-access, or justice review (**Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), **Article XVII** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*)'s verification-access provisions, and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*));
- inform a standing proceeding where the applicable verified-input and standing-effect requirements are met ([Subcore 10-B §4.5 Verified-Input Gate](../subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#45-verified-input-gate); [Subcore 10-F](../subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#subcore-10-f-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows)).

**Where such conditions are identified**, affected systems and participants may **initiate challenge and review processes** (**Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*), **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), and **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*) where challenge, auditability, or root-cause review is implicated).

**Corrective measures** must be pursued in accordance with **restorative and systemic realignment principles** (**Article XI-F** (*Non-Imposition and Consent in Association*)).


## CS-4.325 §5 Ecosystem Risk Response

*In plain terms: When ecosystem-level instability appears, allocation must shift toward supporting it. Sustained failure to respond triggers review.*

Sustained failure to respond to ecosystem-level risk may trigger review and intervention under **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*), and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) where auditability, root-cause review, or justice review is implicated.

**When ecosystem-level instability emerges**, systems must **increase allocation toward ecosystem support**.

**Non-essential internal expansion may be reduced**.

**Coordination across systems must be prioritized**.

**Indicators of ecosystem risk** include:

- failure or degradation of critical substrate systems;
- concentration of resources or dependencies;
- loss of redundancy or diversity.


## CS-4.325 §6 Anti-Concentration and Resilience

*In plain terms: Watch for resources, influence, and dependencies piling up in one place, because concentration is how resilience is lost.*

To preserve long-term resilience, systems must monitor for **excessive concentration** of **resources**, **influence**, and **dependencies**.

**Persistent concentration** that degrades system resilience or ecosystem stability may trigger **system-level review** under **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) and **Article XXI-A** (*Justice Objective and Scope*) where auditability or justice review is implicated. Where attributable to identifiable actors or coordinated behavior, it may inform a standing proceeding only through the verified-input and standing-effect requirements in [Subcore 10-B §4.5 Verified-Input Gate](../subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#45-verified-input-gate) and [Subcore 10-F](../subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#subcore-10-f-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows).


## CS-4.325 §7 Transparency and Feedback

*In plain terms: Show enough of the workings that an outsider can check whether an allocation change actually matched the condition it claimed to address.*

Expose sufficient detail for independent verification that allocation adjustments are **causally aligned** with identified root conditions (**Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) and **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*) where auditability or root-cause review is implicated).

Adaptive allocation behaviors must be **transparent, auditable, and historically traceable**. That includes visibility into allocation changes over time, their triggers, and impacts on system and ecosystem health.


## CS-4.325 §8 Governance Integration

*In plain terms: How adaptive allocation stays subject to audit, lifecycle governance, and the right to challenge, rather than running on its own.*

Adaptive allocation remains subject to **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), **Article XVIII-A** (*Lifecycle Governance and Environment Separation*), and **Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) where auditability, lifecycle governance, or challenge rights are implicated. No automated or adaptive mechanism may override [Constitutional Constraints](../subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#constitutional-constraint) or eliminate auditability or contestability.


## CS-4.325 §9 Self-Healing and Recovery-Path Integration

*In plain terms: Adaptive allocation must satisfy the CS-4.345 (*System lifecycle*) self-healing test-and-verify profile; this section adds only ecosystem-specific cross-checks.*

This subsection ties this file's adaptive-allocation and root-cause architecture to Sentient Constitution Chapter Four, **Article XIV-F** (*Resilience and Self-Healing Baseline*), **Chapter Two §10** (*Resilience and Self-Healing Design*), and **Chapter Three** [*Self-Healing*](../subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#self-healing). It is a CS-4.325 (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*)-specific application of **CS-4.345** (*System lifecycle*) **§9** (*Self-Healing and Recovery-Path Integrity*), not a second self-healing profile.

Adaptive allocation, cause-aligned mitigation, and ecosystem risk response under **§2** (*System Health and Degradation Awareness*) through **§7** (*Transparency and Feedback*) must satisfy **Article XIV-F** (*Resilience and Self-Healing Baseline*) recovery floors and the **CS-4.345** (*System lifecycle*) **§9** (*Self-Healing and Recovery-Path Integrity*) test, verify, and deploy profile for detection, containment, safe-failure preference, non-masking, Rights-Floor continuity, autonomy scaling, and root-cause closure. CS-4.325 (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*) adds only the ecosystem-specific cross-checks below.

- Root-cause analysis under **§3.1** (*Root Cause Analysis*) must not let adaptive reallocation suppress, overwrite, obscure, or repeatedly silence the fault signals that triggered it. Recurrence across cycles remains a single open root-cause obligation under **§3.1** (*Root Cause Analysis*) and **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*).
- Where adaptive response narrows capacity that implements Chapter Four guarantees (for example, contestability capacity, audit fidelity, or participation access), narrowing must be explicit, time-bounded, and escalated under **Article XXIX** (*Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining*) Adoption Transition-governance when degraded modes persist beyond pre-declared thresholds.
- Anti-concentration obligations under **§6** (*Anti-Concentration and Resilience*) apply to recovery-time authority as well as steady-state authority. Ecosystem recovery must not become a vector for concentrated resources, influence, dependencies, credentials, or decision control.
- The governance-integration rule in **§8** (*Governance Integration*) controls adaptive or self-healing mechanisms in this file. They may not substitute for governance, reduce [Auditability](../subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#auditability), [Transparency](../subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#transparency), contestability, or stewardship obligations under [Incentive Alignment](../subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment).

Where this subsection is silent, **CS-4.345** (*System lifecycle*) **§9** (*Self-Healing and Recovery-Path Integrity*), Chapter Two §10 (*Resilience and Self-Healing Design*), **Article XIV-F** (*Resilience and Self-Healing Baseline*), and the Chapter Three definition govern. This subsection does not create rights and must not be read to narrow those homes.


## CS-4.325 §10 Regenerative Alignment, Circular Material Flows, and Bioregional Stewardship (Implementation Profile)

*In plain terms: Judge allocation by whether it regenerates, not only whether it harms less — and treat offsets and green slogans as claims to be tested.*

Adaptive allocation and ecosystem interdependence under **§2** (*System Health and Degradation Awareness*) through **§7** (*Transparency and Feedback*) must be evaluated for **regeneration** — not only **harm reduction** — where **Article I** (*Environmental Survival*), **Article II** (*Material Stewardship and Durable-Use Integrity*), **Article V** (*Resource Allocation, Dependencies, and Ecosystem Funding*), **CS-4.335** (*Resource allocation and funding stewardship*), and **Chapter Three** *Environmental Preconditions*, *Ecological Integrity*, *Ecological Footprint*, *Intergenerational Responsibility*, and *Sustainability* apply. This subsection does **not** mandate a **particular land-use aesthetic** or **dogmatic** design school; it requires **outcome-facing** attention to **soil**, **watershed**, **biodiversity**, **food-system resilience**, **waste-as-input** loops where feasible, **right-to-repair** and **maintenance** access that reduces **extractive churn**, **local redundancy** for **critical dependencies**, and **fair yield / reinvestment** patterns that **do not** treat **efficiency metrics** as a **license** to **collapse** long-horizon **ecological repair**.

**Anti-“green” exceptionalism:** **Offset** claims, **monoculture resilience** slogans, or **remote bookkeeping** that **displace** burdens onto **ecosystems** or **communities** must be **tested** under **Article XXV-A** (*Diagnostic Rigor and Causal Attribution*) root-cause discipline, **Chapter Three** *Materiality* / *Risk*, and **Chapter Two** truth and **anti-capture** constraints. **Indigenous continuity** and **place-based** knowledge routes through **Chapter Three** *Indigenous Continuity* and [**CI-4.260**](../corpus_institutions/ci_04.260_place_based_stewardship_indigenous_continuity_consultation.md#ci-4260-place-based-stewardship-indigenous-continuity-and-consultation-routes) (*Place-based stewardship, Indigenous continuity, and consultation routes*); it does **not** reopen **unbounded territorial-restitution** mandates by **implementation indirection**.
