Core home: 06

This file is the systems implementation home for **CS-6.315** (*Comprehensibility and complexity stewardship*).

*In plain terms: **CS-6.315** (*Comprehensibility and complexity stewardship*) exists because a system nobody can understand cannot be governed. Explanations must match the stake of the decision. Complexity must be measured and justified, not left to accumulate. A system must stay modular enough to inspect and change in parts.*

## CS-6.315 §1 Purpose and role

*In plain terms: what this file covers, which constitutional text it implements, and what to read alongside it.*

This file sets the systems-layer expectations for understandable systems and manageable complexity. It applies two Chapter Six articles to the systems layer:

- **Article XXIII-A** (*Proportional Comprehensibility Right*);
- **Article XXIII-B** (*Complexity Audit and Modularity Requirements*).

Read it together with these operational clusters:

- **CJS-5.120 §8** (*comprehensibility and cognitive accessibility terms*);
- **CJS-5.120 §9** (*salience integrity and attention-allocation terms*);
- **CJS-5.120 §10** (*disclosure sufficiency and observability terms*);

- **CJS-5.130 §19** (*graceful degradation and failure-mode integrity terms*);
- **CJS-5.130 §21** (*adversarial robustness and abuse-resistance terms*).

This file is not a second home for **Article XXIII** (*Comprehensibility and Complexity Stewardship*) or for those operational definitions.

## CS-6.315 §3 Systems-specific checks

*In plain terms: the six things this file adds beyond the constitutional text — keep complexity from blocking oversight, audit it, keep the design modular and redundant, learn from failure, and simplify on a schedule.*

CS-6.315 (*Comprehensibility and complexity stewardship*) adds the following systems-specific checks:

- **Complexity as a barrier:** Complexity must not become a practical barrier to audit, participation, accountability, or oversight beyond what these sources permit:
  - **Article XXIII** (*Comprehensibility and Complexity Stewardship*);
  - **CJS-5.125 §11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*);
  - **CJS-5.120 §7** (*quorum and participatory legitimacy terms*);
  - the applicable Chapter One and Chapter Five constraints.
- **Independent complexity audit:** Critical systems must undergo periodic independent complexity audits. Each audit must evaluate transparency and observability, dependency chains and hidden coupling, failure modes and cascading risks, and the capacity for human oversight and intervention.
- **Complexity audit scaling, triggers, and output:** The independent complexity audit is a sibling audit mode in the [CJS-5.115 §3 audit process home](../corpus_joint_structure/cjs_05115_audit_process.md#sibling-audit-modes-owner-map) (*Sibling audit modes (owner map)*). It is not a step of System Alignment Certification.
  - *Scaling.* Cadence follows the **Comprehensibility and Complexity Stewardship** line of the CS-9.305 and CS-9.310 (*System classification machinery*) Application Profiles ([§CS-6.315 §2 Classification-scaled application](#cs-6315-2-classification-scaled-application)). **Class A** carries the most demanding cadence. **Class B** carries a strict, high-assurance cadence. **Class C** runs the audit when scale, coupling, or coordination depth warrant. **Class L** is proportionate. **Class P** has no standing independent audit while it validly holds that class.
  - *Triggers.* Besides cadence, an audit is due on the triggers in *When we audit* in the [audit process home](../corpus_joint_structure/cjs_05115_audit_process.md#when-we-audit) wherever hidden coupling, layering, or opacity is implicated, and on reclassification to a higher class.
  - *Output.* Findings are audit output. They are disclosed at the most open feasible tier under **CJS-5.110 §4** (*Oversight: tiered transparency and audit-access terms*) and stay open to challenge. System Alignment Certification may integrate them as evidence under [Chapter Nine §3.1 Systemic Scope and Risk Factors](../core_09005_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#31-systemic-scope-and-risk-factors); certification does not replace the audit, and the audit does not replace certification.
- **Modularity:** Critical systems **must** use modular components with clear responsibilities and interfaces, with dependencies between components declared at those interfaces and consistent with the dependency map under **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*). Other systems should do so as far as proportionate to classification and risk. Component-level innovation must not create unmanaged consequences across other systems. Responsibility and observability must be preserved across every internal boundary (**Article XXIII-B** (*Complexity Audit and Modularity Requirements*); [Chapter One §18.5 Modular Architecture and Dependency Discipline](../core_01015_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#185-modular-architecture-and-dependency-discipline)).
- **Redundancy:** Critical infrastructure must incorporate redundancy and fail-safe mechanisms to prevent single points of failure. Where feasible, it must include multiple independent implementations of essential systems.
- **Learning from failure:** Failures, anomalies, and near-misses must feed transparent post-incident learning. That learning must focus on systemic weaknesses rather than on blame.
- **Periodic simplification:** Governance institutions must periodically simplify or remove unnecessary complexity, redundant processes, outdated rules, or excessive dependencies, where doing so preserves clarity, operability, and resilience.
