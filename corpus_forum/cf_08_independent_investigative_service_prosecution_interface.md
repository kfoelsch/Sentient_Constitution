Where institutions exercise or supervise **civil**, **criminal**, **constitutional**, or comparable public-law enforcement power, they must maintain or provide access to an **independent investigative service** with authority sufficient to develop facts for charging, civil enforcement, protective orders, administrative sanction, or adjudication support within lawful scope.

**Purpose.** Keep investigation institutionally distinct from both **operational enforcement command** and **final merits adjudication** so fact-development remains contestable, auditable, and resistant to capture. Institutions must not allow the same operational chain that may become a subject of investigation to control whether, how, or how far an investigation proceeds.

**Institutional location.** The investigative service should sit in the **independent assurance**, **integrity**, or similarly insulated constitutional line rather than under ordinary **police**, **security**, or **operational enforcement** command. It may interface with forums, prosecutors, civil enforcement counsel, inspectors general, ombuds functions, or external assurance bodies, but it must have its own published mandate, leadership, and reporting line.

**Core functions.** The investigative service may:
- receive complaints, protected disclosures, referrals, and self-initiated matters within lawful scope;
- gather, preserve, and analyze evidence;
- interview witnesses and subjects;
- prepare reasoned investigative records and referral packets for charging, civil filing, remediation, or adjudicative use;
- request judicial or otherwise lawful authorization for coercive or secrecy-constrained investigative steps;
- coordinate with external or cross-jurisdiction bodies where jurisdiction, conflict, or capacity requires.

**Required separations.**
- **Police and operational enforcement** may provide scene security, arrest execution, service of process, emergency stabilization, or other lawful operational support, but they must not exercise **exclusive control** over investigations, especially where police, carceral personnel, security services, prosecutors, forums, or closely aligned actors may be subjects, witnesses, or materially interested participants.
- **Prosecutors** or **civil enforcement counsel** may decide charges, claims, settlement **position**, or litigation strategy, but they must not monopolize underlying fact-development or suppress independent preservation of exculpatory, mitigating, inculpatory, or impeachment material.
- **Forums** may authorize warrants, compulsory process, preservation orders, secrecy constraints, and comparable intrusive steps; resolve scope, privilege, and rights disputes; and evaluate the finished record. Forums must not become the routine managerial home of investigators or silently absorb executive fact-development into chambers.

**Anti-self-investigation rule.** No institution may permit a body to retain sole investigative control over allegations concerning its own misconduct, capture, concealment, retaliation, corruption, recusal failure, evidence tampering, or comparable integrity breach. Where those allegations materially involve **police**, **prosecutors**, **forums**, detention personnel, executive leadership, or the investigative service itself, mandatory transfer, co-assignment, or external participation must activate through a published mechanism sufficient to provide a functionally independent investigation.

**Independence and appointment.** Appointment, removal protection, conflict screening, recusal, and challenge pathways must satisfy `corpus_institutions.md` **CI-4** and **CI-5**. For materially sensitive or high-impact scopes, institutions should use mixed appointment, fixed terms, transparent qualification criteria, and external participation sufficient to reduce dependence on any single operational appointing chain.

**Protected investigations and rights.** Investigative restrictions, delayed notice, compartmentalization, sealed steps, protected-activity handling, and remedy for tainted collection remain governed by `core_10-10_rights_part_c.md` **Article XIII-A**, **Chapter Five** (*Necessity*, *Proportionality*, *Oversight*, *Accountability*, *Transparency*) and [Evidence Preservation](../core_05-05_definitions_c_dependent_clusters.md#evidence-preservation), and applicable secrecy-implementation rules in implementation files. **CF-8** adds the forum-interface record rule: when those measures are requested, authorized, reviewed, renewed, narrowed, or found defective, the record must state the lawful objective, scope, duration or expiry, review point, minimization or segregation handling, protected-activity analysis where material, and the available exclusion, deletion, notice, derivative-use, or later-challenge path where lawful.

**Outputs and interfaces.** The investigative service must produce attributable, reviewable records stating scope, steps taken, evidentiary basis, uncertainty, preserved exculpatory and inculpatory material, and referral or closure reasons. Those records must support:
- prosecutorial or civil-enforcement decisions without making the investigators the final charging or merits authority;
- forum review without turning the forums into the investigators' command hierarchy;
- contest-integrity monitoring under `corpus_institutions.md` **CI-7.3** without collapsing structural oversight into case management.

**Cross-institution escalation.** Where local independence is not credible because of concentration, emergency incapacity, deadlock, or systemic capture indicators, institutions must escalate to `corpus_institutions.md` **CI-8**, external assurance, or another pre-designated backup body with lawful authority and practical capacity to investigate.
