This file is the forum implementation home for **CF-10** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*).

## CF-10.1 Basic rule

*In plain terms: **CF-10** (*Technical specialist forums*) lets a system use scientific, engineering, and medical expertise without letting that expertise harden into an unaccountable parallel judiciary. Specialist bodies sit inside the Chapter Eleven families, publish the standards they apply, and stay reviewable.*

When adopting instruments create **science specialist forums**, **engineering specialist forums**, **medical specialist forums**, or comparable technical specialist forums, those bodies must operate inside the forum families recognized in `core_11-11_forum.md` **Chapter Eleven**. They may be specialized chambers or designated panels, but they must not sit outside the Chapter Eleven forum structure.

## CF-10.2 Purpose

*In plain terms: Why these bodies exist: to give expert-evidence questions a reviewable home, so expertise informs decisions without becoming an authority no one can question.*

Technical specialist forums provide a reviewable forum for expert-evidence standards and for disputes whose main issue is scientific, engineering, medical, or comparable knowledge governance. They let a system use technical expertise without turning that expertise into an unaccountable parallel judiciary.

## CF-10.3 Core functions

*In plain terms: Mainly setting and revising the standards other bodies use for expert evidence — reproducibility, uncertainty, replication status, and what counts as sufficient proof.*

Technical specialist forums may:
- publish and periodically revise the expert-evidence standards other specialist forums use, including standards for:
  - reproducibility and replication status;
  - uncertainty and evidentiary sufficiency;
  - validation quality and causal inference;
  - safety margins and standards conformance;
  - meta-analysis quality and conflicting findings;
- publish and periodically revise, for use in system-alignment assessment and in **Integrity** forum recognition, validation, and revalidation records:
  - technical specifications;
  - measurement methods;
  - test protocols;
  - domain-specific expert criteria;
- adjudicate disputes about:
  - expert administrative procedure;
  - research-record custody;
  - correction and retraction process, and replication access;
  - infrastructure or product safety certification;
  - clinical-guideline integrity;
  - curriculum or textbook integrity in publicly governed education;
  - accreditation-equivalent quality controls, and comparable knowledge-governance questions;
- commission, fund, or direct independent research, testing, replication, standards development, or evidence synthesis within lawful scope, where unresolved uncertainty materially blocks reliable adjudication, classification, safety evaluation, or regulatory integrity.

## CF-10.4 Required chamber coverage

*In plain terms: The specialist chambers an adopting instrument should provide at minimum, such as science, engineering, and medical.*

Adopting instruments should specify at least:
- a **science chamber** for research integrity, publication and review governance, replication, meta-research, research-record custody, and textbook or curriculum evidence disputes;
- an **engineering chamber** for:
  - standards conformance and testing sufficiency;
  - failure analysis and infrastructure integrity;
  - safety margins and interoperability;
  - negligent-design or maintenance questions, where the primary issue is technical-governance procedure or evidentiary standards;
- a **medicine chamber** for:
  - clinical-evidence hierarchy and trial governance;
  - guideline integrity;
  - drug or device evidence disputes;
  - public-health evidence administration;
  - standard-of-care questions, where the primary issue is clinical evidence governance rather than ordinary tort or criminal liability.

## CF-10.5 Optional additional chambers

*In plain terms: Other domains that may warrant their own chamber where expert-evidence questions recur — ecology, education, statistics, and comparable fields.*

Adopting instruments may also create technical specialist forums or chambers for other domains where expert-evidence governance has recurring constitutional significance, such as:
- ecology and climate;
- education;
- information and computation;
- forensics;
- economics and public-policy analysis;
- statistics and methodology.

## CF-10.6 Routing and limits

*In plain terms: Specialist bodies take questions where the expert method is the real dispute. They do not become a general venue by absorbing cases that belong elsewhere.*

Technical specialist forums must not displace the primary-stakes routing rules in `core_11-11_forum.md` **Chapter Eleven**. They may hear certified questions, designated administrative matters, or merits disputes whose primary issue is expert procedure or expert-evidence governance. This includes technical system-alignment specifications and testing methods.

They must not become the default merits forum for ordinary civil injury, criminal liability, constitutional-rights enforcement, institutional-mandate disputes, or official constitutional alignment recognition or validation merely because expert evidence is involved.

## CF-10.7 Standard-setting constraints

*In plain terms: Published standards must separate what the evidence shows from what someone chose as policy, and must leave room for credible minority and emerging views rather than freezing one school in place.*

Published expert-evidence standards must be reasoned, auditable, contestable, and periodically reviewable. They must distinguish evidentiary weight from policy choice.

Standards must leave room for minority or emerging views that meet good-faith methodological standards. They must not freeze one school, method, professional guild, journal network, vendor bloc, or disciplinary cartel into binding orthodoxy.

## CF-10.8 Shared standards and decentralized enforcement

*In plain terms: One body maintains a shared standard; many bodies apply it. Maintenance is centralized so the standard stays coherent, enforcement stays distributed so it stays accountable.*

Where adopting instruments use common standards across multiple institutions, jurisdictions, or forum families, technical specialist forums should serve as the principal maintenance forum for those standards within their lawful subject-matter scope.

Ordinary enforcement should remain decentralized. The constitutionally designated lead forum for the dispute applies the shared standard to the parties before it, preserves local fact sensitivity, and issues the operative remedy.

## CF-10.9 Institutional-forum interface

*In plain terms: When a technical standard is used against an institution, the case is normally heard by the Institutional family — the standard is technical, but the dispute is about institutional conduct.*

When a shared technical, scientific, engineering, medical, or comparable operational standard is invoked against an institution, regulator, steward, operator, or supervised body, the ordinary merits and compliance forum should be the **Institutional** forum family unless `core_11-11_forum.md` **Chapter Eleven** assigns another lead family by primary stakes.

Technical specialist forums may hear certified questions, validity challenges to the standard itself, or disputes whose primary stake is standards governance. They must not become the routine venue for every enforcement action merely because a technical standard is relevant.

## CF-10.10 Departure and variance

*In plain terms: Published shared standards bind within their adopted scope. Departing from one requires a justified, recorded variance; treating a standard as merely aspirational is not an option.*

Shared standards published by technical specialist forums are presumed to apply when they fall within the adopted scope. They are binding requirements unless a justified departure or variance is granted; they are not merely aspirational ceilings.

A lead forum may adopt a stricter application where the constitution, adopting law, or materially distinct facts require it. A lead forum may depart from a published standard only through a reasoned and reviewable record that:
- identifies the standard and the point of departure;
- explains why the standard is materially underinclusive, overinclusive, outdated, misapplied, or otherwise unreliable for the dispute;
- states whether the departure is case-specific, provisional, or proposed for broader revision;
- preserves a path for certification, appeal, or standards reconsideration by the relevant technical specialist forum where appropriate.

Local variation must not be used as a pretext for regression, forum shopping, hidden discrimination, or evasion of shared constitutional floors.

## CF-10.11 Cross-jurisdiction recognition

*In plain terms: A standard already worked out elsewhere is presumptively recognized rather than relitigated from scratch, unless there is a stated reason it does not fit.*

Where a technical specialist forum acting within lawful adopted scope has published a shared standard, and another adopting jurisdiction or institution faces the same materially relevant standards question, the later forum should presumptively recognize that standard for local enforcement unless one or more of the following is shown in a reasoned record:
- the constitution, valid adopting law, or a stricter lawful protection in the later forum requires a different rule;
- the factual setting, dependency profile, risk profile, or evidentiary **situation** is materially different;
- the earlier standard is no longer reliable because of later evidence, demonstrated implementation failure, or material procedural defect in its adoption or maintenance;
- recognition would itself create regression, discriminatory effect, or a practical loss of challenge rights.

Non-recognition must identify whether the forum is adopting an interim local rule, certifying the issue for reconsideration, or invoking another recognized shared standard. Silent non-recognition is non-compliant where this subsection applies.

A repeated, unexplained refusal to recognize materially applicable shared standards may be treated as evidence of capture, evasion, or forum shopping. Where the verified facts satisfy the relevant standards, that pattern may support:
- escalation under `corpus_institutions.md` **CI-8** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*);
- external assurance;
- potential **Chapter Ten** anti-constitutional misconduct routing;
- cross-jurisdiction enforcement continuity rules.

## CF-10.12 Educational materials and textbook disputes

*In plain terms: In curriculum disputes the question is evidence integrity and honest presentation, not which conclusion the body prefers.*

When technical forums hear disputes about publicly governed textbooks, curriculum standards, or comparable educational materials, the question is evidence integrity. The forum should address the honesty of evidence presentation, methodological soundness, age-appropriate explanation, and administrative regularity.

These forums must not act as general ideological censors or displace the constitutional rights and educational principles stated in `core_06-06_rights_part_a.md` and related **Chapter Six** materials.

## CF-10.13 Independence and composition

*In plain terms: Panels need genuine domain competence plus terms and pay that do not leave members dependent on the funders, journals, or vendors whose work they review.*

Panels must include relevant domain competence, conflict screening, published selection criteria, and meaningful challenge pathways.

Service terms, compensation, and promotion structures must reduce dependence on journal prestige, dominant funders, vendor capture, or institutional retaliation against replication, criticism, or correction.

## CF-10.14 Research and uncertainty-reduction authority

*In plain terms: A specialist body may commission research, but only after publishing a finding that the existing uncertainty is actually blocking reliable decisions.*

Technical specialist forums may commission or fund the following only after they publish a finding that existing uncertainty materially blocks reliable adjudication, classification, safety evaluation, or regulatory integrity:
- independent research;
- replication and testing;
- field study;
- standards development;
- evidence synthesis.

Scope orders, selection criteria, conflicts, funding terms, and outputs must be public unless a narrower lawful exception applies.

This authority must not be used to:
- reward ideological alignment;
- burden dissent;
- evade ordinary appropriations controls where those controls lawfully apply;
- convert provisional funded findings into binding law without ordinary review and adoption pathways.

## CF-10.15 Publication and review governance

*In plain terms: Where the dispute involves suppressed publication, refused replication, or blocked correction, this file is read together with the institutional rules on scientific publication.*

Science-facing technical specialist forums should be read together with `corpus_institutions.md` **CI-17** (*Scientific publication, peer review, replication, and evidence stewardship*).

Where a dispute involves publication suppression, replication denial, correction refusal, or manipulation of scientific standing, a technical specialist forum may supply the specialist forum. Integrity and institutional review routes remain available where those stakes are primary.
