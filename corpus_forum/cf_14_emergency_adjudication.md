This file is the forum implementation home for **CF-14** (*Emergency adjudication*).

*In plain terms: **CF-14** (*Emergency adjudication*) governs how urgent matters are decided while ordinary operation is degraded — what gets priority, who reviews emergency decisions while they are still in force, and how everything is reconciled once normal operation returns. An emergency is not a licence to decide without record or review.*

This section governs emergency adjudicative priority, review, restoration, post-incident findings, and prohibited emergency patterns when ordinary forum operation is degraded or fallback operation is active.
Emergency adjudication must preserve access to urgent relief, Rights Floor protection, evidence preservation, challenge rights, auditability, and a path back to ordinary independent adjudication.
Where emergency adjudication materially depends on constitutional systems, this section must be read with `corpus_systems.md` **Protocol A**, subsection **G** (*Governance continuity, crisis communications, and exercises*), **CF-12** (*Forum continuity*), and **CF-13** (*Fallback operation*).

## CF-14.1 Emergency triage hierarchy

*In plain terms: When there is not enough capacity for everything, this is the order: imminent loss of life or irreversible harm first, then evidence about to vanish, then coercive measures needing prompt review.*

Where capacity is insufficient for normal operations, forums must prioritize:
- matters involving imminent loss of life or irreversible harm;
- [Evidence Preservation](../core_05_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation) needs likely to become impossible if delayed;
- detention, seizure, exclusion, or comparable coercive measures needing prompt review;
- inability-to-form and anti-self-judging cases that block lawful merits adjudication;
- matters where delay would predictably extinguish practical remedy.

The triage rule must be published in plain language accessible to everyday sentients.

## CF-14.2 Independent review during emergency mode

*In plain terms: Emergency powers are rechecked on a schedule while still in force — is the trigger still real, is the simplification still proportionate, is temporary authority hardening into permanent control?*

Continuity activation must be subject to independent review on a documented cadence.

That review must assess:
- whether the trigger still exists;
- whether emergency simplifications remain necessary and proportionate;
- whether continuity powers are drifting into durable concentration;
- whether Rights-Floor, challenge, and publication duties are being preserved enough to remain lawful.

Continuation beyond the published window requires reasoned reauthorization.

## CF-14.3 Restoration and reconciliation

*In plain terms: Once the trigger passes, ordinary rules resume, emergency orders that affected rights get reviewed, and delayed records are published or reconstructed.*

When the triggering condition subsides, the system must enter a restoration mode that includes:
- return to ordinary panel-formation and routing rules;
- review of emergency orders that materially affected rights or structure;
- publication or reconstruction of delayed records;
- correction of continuity-period docket inaccuracies;
- remediation for continuity failures that materially harmed parties;
- assessment of whether fallback actions exceeded lawful scope.

## CF-14.4 Post-incident review

*In plain terms: Every activation ends with findings: what triggered it, what failed, what was used instead, and whether the Rights Floor actually held.*

Every activation must produce the restoration and post-incident evidence required by `corpus_systems.md` **Protocol A**, subsection **G**, with forum-specific findings stating:
- what triggered the continuity mode;
- what functions degraded or failed;
- what fallback measures were used;
- whether Rights-Floor continuity was maintained;
- what delays or review failures occurred;
- what remediation and future-hardening steps are required.

## CF-14.5 Non-compliant emergency patterns

*In plain terms: The recognizable abuses — emergency mode extended indefinitely without reauthorization, emergency quorum used to concentrate lasting control, and loss of docket identity or evidence custody.*

The following are presumptively non-compliant unless clearly justified and cured:
- indefinite emergency forum mode without reauthorization;
- emergency quorum use that concentrates durable control;
- loss of docket identity or chain of custody under [Evidence Preservation](../core_05_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation);
- emergency orders with no later review path;
- continuity activation used to bypass recusal, anti-self-judging, or publication obligations;
- family-capture restoration controlled by the displaced or compromised family;
- restoration without retrospective review of materially impactful emergency decisions.
