Chapter Four is the constitutional owner of **burden allocation, compliance evidence, definition traceability, observability, and security-constrained verification**.

*In plain terms: if you say you're following the rules, you have to prove it — and show a clear, checkable path from what the Constitution defines to what sentients can actually observe.*

### 1. Exclusive Enforcement and Burden Allocation

*In plain terms: if you claim compliance, you prove it — through one five-step check. Miss any step and the claim fails.*

This section sets out who bears the burden of proof and how this chapter is enforced:

- **Burden on the asserter:** The burden of demonstrating compliance rests on the system or actor asserting it.
- **Exclusive enforcement pathway:** All evaluation standards referenced elsewhere in this chapter are enforced exclusively through sections **1** through **5** of this chapter.
- **Integrated pipeline:** Sections **1** through **5** implement one verification pipeline; failure in any section fails the whole.

Read in order. Each step builds on the last; skip none.

| Step | Plain job | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Who proves** | Burden sits on whoever claims compliance | [§1](#1-exclusive-enforcement-and-burden-allocation) |
| **2 — Show the map** | Structured trace from definitions to observable behavior | [§2](#2-definition-traceability-requirement) |
| **3 — Make it checkable** | Systems must expose enough to verify the map — in practice, not in theory | [§3](#3-observability-of-traceability-requirement) |
| **4 — Security limits** | Real security limits are allowed; pretextual blocks are not | [§4](#4-security-constrained-observability-and-verification-rule) |
| **5 — What counts** | Evidence must be real, complete, independent, and sustained | [§5](#5-compliance-evidence-standard) |

<br>

*In plain terms: show a checkable map from each definition's parts — O, M, A, and C — to what the system actually does, before **sections 3** through **5** judge whether anyone can verify it and whether the proof holds.*

This section requires a **structured trace map**. For every definition that materially applies to system behavior — including every materially required **Chapter Five** definition — the map must link each part to **observable** behavior and real-world effects:

- **what it is (O)** — what the term is about
- **how it is measured (M)** — which measure applies at each layer
- **how it is judged (A)** — how the term must be assessed
- **what must hold (C)** — what must be true in practice

The trace map must also:
- be **maintained** across system updates, version changes, and deployment contexts; failure to maintain tracing over time is non-compliant
- be **verified** under the operational and assessment conditions Chapters Two and Three require
- be stated in terms that can be observed and independently checked — the standard **sections 3** through **5** apply when deciding whether the system is open enough to verify under security limits and whether the evidence is sufficient
- be bidirectional, such that:
  - each definition component (O/M/A/C) maps to observable system behavior and effects, including direct, indirect, delayed, and aggregated effects where materially relevant
  - all materially relevant system behaviors must be mapped completely and representatively, without omission of materially relevant behaviors or conditions

Failure to invoke or correctly apply any materially required **Chapter Five** definition invalidates the associated evaluation, evidence, and compliance determination in full. Partial, selective, or non-representative tracing is non-compliant.

<strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Definition:</span></strong> [Foreseeability](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [O](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [M](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [A](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [C](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-c)

*In plain terms: section 2 requires a trace map — this section requires the system to expose enough of itself that authorized or affected parties can check the map in practice, not just in theory. Opaque architecture, outside dependencies, and black-box components do not get a pass.*

Section 2 requires a trace map; this section requires the system to expose enough of itself for that map to be checked in practice.

**Systems must:**
- provide sufficient **observability, instrumentation, and access** to components, data, and interactions to fully satisfy **section 2** — for appropriately authorized or affected parties, with proportionate methods, resources, and access conditions
- provide access within timeframes that preserve the ability to detect, challenge, and respond to non-compliance
- structure information so it is complete and usable for verification — not obscured through fragmentation, aggregation, or noise
- treat failure to observe, measure, or verify any materially relevant O/M/A/C part under this chapter's evaluation conditions as **non-compliant**
- treat limitations from system design, architectural opacity, external dependencies, or third-party components as **design failures** to be addressed through modification, constraint, or exclusion
- treat affected definition parts and any dependent compliance claims as **non-compliant** where full tracing cannot be achieved

**Systems must not:**
- rely on components or dependencies that prevent satisfaction of this requirement under **reasonably foreseeable** conditions
- treat design limitations, architectural opacity, external dependencies, or third-party components as exemptions from this requirement
- impose cost, complexity, delay, access restrictions, or informational conditions that materially impair independent verification
- rely on **theoretical** verifiability where **practical** verification is not achievable under reasonably foreseeable conditions
- require dependence on the system or actor asserting compliance beyond what is strictly necessary

Where verification is technically possible but not practically achievable, treat compliance claims as **non-compliant**.

<strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Definition:</span></strong> [Foreseeability](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [O](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability) · [M](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [A](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-a) · [C](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#foreseeability-diligence-c)

*In plain terms: security can narrow how, when, and to whom disclosure or audit happens — it cannot erase the duty to trace, verify, and allow independent checking.*

Section 3 requires observability. **Security, misuse-prevention, and adversarial risk** may justify proportionate limits on disclosure, audit, or verification. Those limits do **not** remove the duties in **sections 2** and **3**.

**Systems must:**
- apply the **least-restrictive** limit that still addresses foreseeable risk, including exploitation, circumvention, or systemic harm, using methods proportionate to system impact, dependency, and risk
- preserve the **maximum achievable** independent audit, verification, and challenge rights
- ensure any limit is **materially necessary**, **proportionate**, **narrowly scoped**, **auditable**, and **non-pretextual**; its justification and scope must be **independently verifiable**; it must be **time-limited** where feasible
- keep **independent validation** independent of the system or actor asserting compliance

**Systems must not:**
- invoke security, confidentiality, or misuse risk to avoid creating, maintaining, or independently validating materially relevant compliance evidence
- be designed or structured to predictably block observability, traceability, verifiability, or auditability under reasonably foreseeable conditions

Where a limit blocks meaningful independent validation, the associated compliance claims are **non-compliant** unless all of the following are true:
- no safer validation pathway is reasonably achievable
- that determination is independently justified and auditable
- the remaining method preserves sufficient epistemic integrity for compliance determination

Cryptography and information-type handling must align with the Constitution and **[CS-6.305](corpus_systems/cs_06.305_information_types_and_handling.md#cs-6305-part-a-information-types-and-handling) (*Information types and handling*) and [CS-6.310 — Data classifications](corpus_systems/cs_06.310_data_classifications.md#cs-6310-part-b-data-classifications)**; where materially relevant, they must still permit full compliance verification under this chapter. Cryptographic and information-type handling must not replace [Truth (Constitutional Constraint)](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#truth-constitutional-constraint) or [Safety (Constitutional Constraint)](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#safety-constitutional-constraint), or the duties in **sections 2**, **3**, or **5**.

*In plain terms: proof has to be real — tied to the trace map, checked under actual conditions, independent where possible, complete, and sustained over time. Cherry-picked snapshots, proxy-only attestations, and self-serving proof do not count.*

Sections **2** through **4** set trace, observability, and security limits; this section states what evidence must show to meet the burden under **section 1**. The standard scales with [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) — system impact, dependency, and risk.

**Evidence must:**
- demonstrate compliance with **Chapters Two and Three** under the required assessment scope and conditions, including compliant trace artifacts under **section 2** and observability under **section 3**
- be generated and assessed under conditions that represent actual system behavior, operation, and impact
- be **observable**, **verifiable**, **complete** relative to required scope, **sustained** across time, and **sufficient** in rigor proportionate to system impact, dependency, and risk to withstand independent audit and adversarial testing
- use **independent validation** where reasonably achievable; absence must be justified under proportionality constraints
- comply with **section 4** constraints on evidence generation, disclosure, and verification, including cryptographic and information-type handling where applicable
- where constitutional values or constraints conflict in the same evaluation, remain consistent with [Chapter One, §13 — Constitutional Collision Resolution Process](core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#13-constitutional-collision-resolution-process)
- for **empirical**, **predictive**, or **causal** claims material to compliance, classification, or governance, reflect **scientific integrity** where proportionate — testable or reviewable methods, documented assumptions and limitations, no suppression of null results or failed replications where they bear on decisions, and conclusions revisable on new evidence

**The following do not meet the burden of proof under section 1:**
- failure to satisfy any required definition or component under [Chapter Two, §2.1 Core Integrity Conditions](core_02.000_definition_structure.md#21-core-integrity-conditions), or non-compliance under [Chapter Three, section 2 — Non-Compliance from Observable System Behavior](core_03.000_definition_integrity.md#2-non-compliance-from-observable-system-behavior) — invalidating all dependent compliance claims under this chapter
- failure to provide required evidence where it exists or is required under this chapter
- proxy-only, self-attested, partial, or selectively scoped proof that would be non-compliant under [Chapter Three, section 2](core_03.000_definition_integrity.md#2-non-compliance-from-observable-system-behavior) if treated as the system's compliance demonstration
- insufficient evidence to satisfy the **Evidence must** requirements above

Where uncertainty prevents definitive demonstration of compliance for materially relevant definition components, apply [Chapter Three, §2.6 Uncertainty Integrity and Non-Exploitation Constraint](core_03.000_definition_integrity.md#26-uncertainty-integrity-and-non-exploitation-constraint). Where the burden of proof is not satisfied, default to **non-compliant** status.

Chapter Nine is a separate forum-supervised certification process that uses those rules when a system needs formal alignment certification. A current certification is not a free pass around Two through Four.*

This section is **non-operative**. It states a **layer boundary** only.

**Chapters Two, Three, and Four** supply the standing definition and verification substrate:
- **[Chapter Two](core_02.000_definition_structure.md#chapter-two-definition-structure-and-component-requirements)** — how definitions are built and kept internally consistent (O/M/A/C)

These apply whenever compliance is evaluated — with or without certification.

**[Chapter Nine — System Alignment Certification](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#chapter-nine-system-alignment-certification)** supplies the forum-supervised **certification process** where material: whole-system evaluation domains, certification-record duties, certification outcomes, certification interval, contestability chain, and verified-input bridge to Chapter Ten. It **applies** Chapters Two through Four; it does **not** restate them.

Readers must not treat:
- a **certification record** as a substitute for ongoing compliance under Chapters Two through Four
- **Chapter Nine** certification, conditional certification, or recertification as lowering the proof, trace, or evidence standards in this chapter
- **Chapters Two through Four** as optional because a system was previously certified — verified non-compliance under those chapters can reopen or defeat certification under Chapter Nine
