Chapter Twenty-Six is the constitutional owner of **additive expansion of protection, continuity of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment processes, source-layer authority and internal hierarchy relative to incorporated corpus and external law, non-displacement of applicable external law of its own force, alignment of constitutional processes with external legal frameworks in which they are used, and cooperation with external tribunals while preserving internal review pathways**.


*In plain terms: publishing or using this Constitution does not override the law of the land. You are allowed to strengthen the constitution and to respect stricter outside law when it fits — but internal supremacy, auditability, and the right to push back do not go on vacation during an amendment, and external forums cannot be used as an excuse to shred records or dodge rights.*
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: forward is fine, and new rights, stronger safeguards, and broader inclusion are welcome when they increase protection. This chapter also says how this Constitution relates to other binding norms and to outside law: it is not a secret override, and it cannot be turned off during amendment.*

**Purpose.** Chapter Twenty-Six states what is permitted and what holds when this Constitution meets other rules:

- additive expansion, which is permitted (**section 2**);
- supremacy and challenge rights, which stay in force during amendment (**section 3**);
- supremacy relative to other binding norms, stricter external protections, and conflict disclosure (**section 4**);
- disputes involving external legal orders (**section 5**); and
- how this Constitution relates to applicable external law, including non-displacement and limited binding effect (**section 6**).

**Role.** The chapter works with its two neighbors on change and adoption:

- The guardrail on expansion is Chapter Twenty-Five.
- A body can bind itself only by adopting through Chapter Twenty-Seven, and only as far as it already has lawful authority to bind itself.
- **Chapter Twenty-Eight section 4** applies where partial adoption could shrink duties an entity already bears.

**Chapter scope.** The chapter covers:

- additive expansion (**section 2**);
- continuity of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment (**section 3**);
- supremacy relative to other binding norms (**section 4**);
- disputes involving external legal orders (**section 5**); and
- the relation to applicable external law (**section 6**).

The chapter leaves:

- the test for whether a change is valid to Chapters [Twenty-Five](core_25.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-five-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity) and [Twenty-Seven](core_27.000_amendment_ratification.md#chapter-twenty-seven-amendment-ratification-and-procedural-validity); and
- the incorporation bridge, custody, and operative effect of adopted text to [Chapter Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_incorporation.md#chapter-twenty-eight-incorporation-bridge).

#### 4.2 Stricter External Protections

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

<br>

*In plain terms: if the law of the land (or a treaty) is tougher than this **Constitution** on protecting sentients, safety, or accountability, follow the tougher rule — unless it would force a violation of Safety or Truth, in which case interpret to maximize protection within those two hard limits.*

Where valid external law, regulation, or treaty obligation applicable to the adopter imposes stricter protections, the stricter requirement governs. Stricter protections may address sentients, safety, epistemic integrity, transparency, or accountability. That rule applies where the stricter requirement can be honored without forcing violation of Chapter One non-negotiable principle constraints (Safety and Truth). Where multiple interpretations are reasonably available, interpretation must favor the maximal protection consistent with those constraints.

#### 6.1 Non-Displacement and Limited Binding Effect
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§6](#6-relation-to-applicable-external-law) relation-to-external-law frame; [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4](core_28.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority) (28.000 §4) (*enforceable only after valid adoption*).
- Downstream: [§6.2](#62-alignment-when-used-within-external-legal-frameworks) alignment when used inside an external framework; [§4.1](#41-internal-hierarchy-for-adopters) internal hierarchy for adopters within that limited effect.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Seven §11.1](core_27.000_amendment_ratification.md#111-deliberate-adoption) (27.000 §11.1)–[§11.2](core_27.000_amendment_ratification.md#112-adoption-document) (*deliberate, documented, auditable document stating entity, scope, and effective date*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: the document does not repeal other law just by existing. Adoption makes it binding for the adopting body, inside that body’s lawful reach and the recorded scope — not a claim that every other legal order has been replaced.*

This Constitution does not, of its own force, repeal, displace, override, or render inoperative applicable local, national, or international law, including treaty and other international obligations where they apply to the entity.

Publication, distribution, citation, forking, operations-guide use, or any statement that a body “follows” this Constitution is not adoption and does not create supremacy over applicable external law.

This Constitution becomes binding for a particular entity only upon valid adoption under **Chapter Twenty-Seven §11** (*Ratification and Adoption*) and **Chapter Twenty-Eight §4** (*Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority*). Binding effect is limited to:

- the adopting entity;
- the systems under that entity's authority; and
- the stated scope of the [adoption document](core_27.000_amendment_ratification.md#112-adoption-document).

Valid adoption does not, by itself, repeal, displace, or render inoperative otherwise applicable local, national, or international law, except to the extent that:

- the adopting entity has lawful authority under that external law to bind itself and the systems under its authority to this Constitution; and
- the adoption document records that commitment within that authority.

Within that limited binding effect, [§4.1](#41-internal-hierarchy-for-adopters) (*Internal Hierarchy for Adopters*), [§4.2](#42-stricter-external-protections) (*Stricter External Protections*), and [§4.3 Conflict Disclosure and Mitigation](#43-conflict-disclosure-and-mitigation) continue to apply.

#### 6.2 Alignment When Used within External Legal Frameworks

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: if you are using this Constitution inside a company, university, protocol, city, or other body that already lives under other law, the certification, standing, contest, and participation processes here have to be ones you can actually run without violating that law — and you still may not use the outside rulebook as cover to skip floors this Constitution requires where they can be honored together.*

Where this Constitution is used, invoked, or applied inside an external legal framework — including operations-guide use, incorporation into policy or contract, or adoption by an entity that remains subject to that framework — processes, participation, records, forums, and remedies under this Constitution must be capable of being carried out consistently with applicable requirements of that framework.

Alignment with an external legal framework:

- does not convert operations-guide use, citation, or “we follow it” practice into adoption;
- does not authorize shrinking **Chapter Twenty-Five** non-regression floors, **Chapter Six** Rights Floors, or **Chapter One** Safety and Truth constraints by pointing at weaker external norms, as already forbidden by [§4.2 Stricter External Protections](#42-stricter-external-protections) and [§4.3 Conflict Disclosure and Mitigation](#43-conflict-disclosure-and-mitigation);
- does not excuse [§5.1 Good-Faith Cooperation](#51-good-faith-cooperation) duties where competent external jurisdiction is lawfully asserted; and
- does not permit using an asserted conflict as a pretext for selective compliance, forum shopping, or evasion of Rights-Floor obligations.
