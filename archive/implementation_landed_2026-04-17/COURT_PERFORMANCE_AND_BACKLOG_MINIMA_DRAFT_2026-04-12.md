# Court performance and backlog minima draft

Status: implementation drafting artifact (non-authoritative draft).  
Date: 2026-04-12.  
Primary constitutional anchors: `core_constitution.md` Article XII-B, Article XIV, Article XXII; `corpus_institutions.md` CI-6, CI-7.3, CI-12, and CI-12.2.

## Purpose

This draft closes the current court-structure gap around:
- court timeliness standards;
- backlog detection and escalation;
- publication timeliness;
- accessibility and usability of court pathways;
- measurable monitoring outputs for contest-integrity review.

It is designed to give court systems performance floors without collapsing into a one-size-fits-all schedule.

## Draft protocol text

### 1. Scope and role

This protocol applies to all court families recognized in `core_constitution.md` Chapter Eight, including any specialist chamber or designated technical panel operating within those families.

It sets minimum requirements for:
- timing and aging metrics;
- backlog review;
- accessibility and pathway usability;
- publication cadence for routing and merits records;
- escalation when delay or process failure materially threatens challenge rights or remedy.

This protocol does not displace stricter lawful deadlines.

### 2. Core rule

Court performance must be measured against whether affected parties retain a practical ability to:
- understand where their matter is;
- obtain a lawful forum without unreasonable delay;
- challenge adverse decisions before remedy becomes meaningless;
- secure urgent relief when delay would produce irreversible or materially compounding harm.

Backlog is constitutionally relevant when it materially degrades challenge rights, independent review, remedy, or trust in lawful adjudication.

### 3. Minimum metric categories

Each court family must publish and maintain metrics for at least:
- time from filing to initial routing decision;
- time from routing to panel formation;
- time from recusal challenge filing to recusal decision;
- time from inability-to-form finding to backup activation where applicable;
- time from full submission to provisional ruling, where that stage exists;
- time from final determination to publication of a reasoned record, subject to lawful restrictions;
- open-case aging by case class and urgency class;
- pending recusal challenges;
- pending transfer or certification requests;
- emergency matters awaiting interim review.

### 4. Case classes and urgency classes

Performance tracking must distinguish at minimum:
- ordinary matters;
- high-impact matters;
- emergency / imminent-harm matters;
- structural or class-wide matters;
- restricted-evidence matters where secrecy constraints affect processing.

Institutions may add finer classes, but must not merge categories in ways that conceal delay in the most rights-sensitive matters.

### 5. Minimum timing floors

Each adopting system must set published target windows for:
- initial filing acknowledgement;
- initial routing determination;
- notice of assigned or proposed panel;
- recusal challenge determination;
- emergency interim-relief review;
- transfer or certification decision;
- publication of routing, transfer, and merits records;
- backup-forum activation where Chapter Eight requires it.

For emergency or rights-floor matters, targets must be materially shorter than for ordinary civil matters.

If a system cannot yet justify its own calibrated numbers, the interim default should be:
- routing and emergency-screening windows measured in hours or a small number of days, not weeks, for emergency / imminent-harm cases;
- recusal and backup-activation windows short enough that the independence dispute does not itself defeat meaningful remedy;
- publication windows short enough that challenge rights remain real before practical effect hardens.

### 6. Backlog thresholds and alerts

Every court family must define:
- an `aging alert threshold`;
- a `serious backlog threshold`;
- a `constitutional risk threshold`.

Thresholds must be set by case and urgency class. They must trigger progressively stronger actions.

At minimum:
- aging alert threshold triggers internal review and scheduling explanation;
- serious backlog threshold triggers managerial remediation and contest-integrity review;
- constitutional risk threshold triggers independent review, publication in governance reporting, and, where material, external assurance or cross-institution escalation.

### 7. Required remediation actions for backlog

When a backlog threshold is crossed, the court system must produce an attributable record stating:
- the affected case class;
- the duration and scale of the delay;
- the likely causes;
- whether rights, review, evidence quality, or remedy have been materially jeopardized;
- what corrective actions will occur;
- who owns those corrective actions;
- when the condition will be reassessed.

Acceptable corrective actions may include:
- temporary reassignment;
- substitute or reserve panel use;
- priority triage;
- publication batching improvements;
- additional clerical or analytical support;
- temporary coordination changes with other court families;
- continuity or emergency measures where ordinary capacity has failed.

### 8. Accessibility and pathway usability minima

Court access pathways must be understandable and usable in practice. Each court family must measure and review:
- ease of filing and challenge initiation;
- availability of plain-language status information;
- accessibility accommodations sufficient for substantive use;
- whether parties can tell what step comes next;
- whether delay notices are timely and comprehensible;
- whether digital pathways preserve parity for filing, managing, and contesting matters.

Usability failures count as performance failures when they materially obstruct challenge rights or effective participation.

### 9. Publication timeliness

Reasoned records for routing, recusal, transfer, certification, interim relief, and merits outcomes must be published or otherwise made available on a time scale that preserves practical review.

Where lawful restrictions delay full publication, the court must still provide, as soon as lawfully feasible:
- a docket-visible status marker;
- the fact of action taken;
- the existence of any delayed-publication restriction;
- a later publication or reconstruction path.

### 10. Restricted-evidence timing

Restricted-evidence matters must not become a standing excuse for indefinite delay.

For such matters, systems must separately track:
- time to secure authorized access;
- time to create reviewable summaries where lawful;
- time to resolve secrecy-related scope disputes;
- whether delay from restricted-access process is materially impairing remedy or review.

### 11. Contest-integrity monitoring interface

Contest-integrity monitors under CI-7.3 must review court-performance data on a published cadence and assess:
- persistent delay by family or case class;
- whether backlogs track conflict, capture, or retaliation indicators;
- whether recusal disputes are being used to stall matters;
- whether emergency dockets are receiving timely attention;
- whether accessibility or publication failures are undermining contestability.

Material findings must feed remediation and, where warranted, CI-8 escalation or external assurance triggers.

### 12. Governance disclosure fields for court performance

The standard governance report should include at minimum:
- current backlog by court family and case class;
- number of cases beyond each threshold band;
- median and percentile routing times;
- panel-formation times;
- recusal challenge counts and determination times;
- transfer / certification counts and times;
- emergency review times;
- publication timeliness performance;
- remedial actions open and closed for court-performance failures.

### 13. Non-compliant performance patterns

The following patterns are presumptively non-compliant unless clearly justified and corrected:
- chronic delay concentrated in rights-floor or emergency matters;
- backlog aging that predictably defeats practical remedy;
- unexplained publication delay on materially impactful rulings;
- selective speed for favored parties or favored issue classes;
- recurring recusal or panel-formation delay without published remediation;
- inaccessible challenge pathways that materially reduce substantive use.

### 14. Court-performance review cadence

Each court family must review performance metrics on a fixed cadence.

At minimum:
- emergency and high-impact metrics should be reviewed more frequently than ordinary matters;
- trend review must compare current metrics with prior periods;
- repeated threshold breach must trigger corrective-action review rather than simple narration.

### 15. Evidence and artifacts

Each court system should maintain:
- a court-performance dashboard or equivalent report;
- threshold definitions and current values;
- remediation logs;
- publication-delay logs;
- backlog-cause analyses;
- annual or periodic court-performance summary linked to governance reporting.

### 16. Minimum adoption questions

Before adoption, confirm:
- what the urgency classes are;
- which role owns the court-performance dashboard;
- which threshold triggers contest-integrity review;
- which threshold triggers external assurance or CI-8 escalation;
- how delayed-publication cases remain visible and reviewable;
- how accessibility failures are reported and remedied;
- what metrics best reveal structural capture or selective delay.
