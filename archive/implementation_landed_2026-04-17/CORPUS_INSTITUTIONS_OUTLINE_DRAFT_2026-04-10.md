## Plan

Propose a new corpus file focused on institutional governance design that:
- fills the owner-layer gap between constitutional minima (`core_constitution.md`) and systems operations (`corpus_systems.md`);
- preserves single-home discipline (no duplicate constitutional term definitions, no competing rights language);
- provides stable governance interfaces for appointment, delegation, oversight, classification custody, and escalation.

## Act

## 1. File purpose and authority boundary

- **Proposed file:** `corpus_institutions.md`
- **Role:** Binding incorporated implementation text for institutional governance architecture.
- **Does not own:** rights floors, constitutional term definitions, amendment validity minima, or offense taxonomy.
- **Does own:** institutional structure, authority custody, governance process design, and cross-institution coordination patterns.
- **Classification interface:** institutional obligations must scale by applicable system class and stewardship tier from `corpus_systems.md` (Chapters S2-S3), without redefining those taxonomies.

Suggested opening language:
- This file implements institutional governance mechanics consistent with Sentient Constitution Chapters One through Fourteen, `corpus_primitives.md`, and `corpus_systems.md`.
- It must not redefine Chapter Three terms or create competing Chapter Four classification logic.
- Roman numerals are used only for **Article** citations. Unless another Sentient Constitution chapter is explicitly named, those **Article** citations refer to **Sentient Constitution Chapter Nine**.

### Foundational implementation principle — proportionality
- Institutional formation burden, compliance depth, oversight intensity, and operating cost expectations must scale with material impact, dependency, reversibility, and stewardship criticality.
- Low-impact institutional scopes should default to simple and inexpensive pathways.
- High-impact institutional scopes must carry correspondingly stronger diligence, assurance, and accountability obligations.
- Where uncertainty exists, apply precautionary provisional scaling with scheduled revalidation against observed impact.

## 2. Suggested top-level structure

### CI-1 — Institutional scope and legitimacy interfaces
- Institutional scope, actor classes, and mandate boundaries.
- Interface to Sentient Constitution Chapter Ten legitimacy/authorization minima.
- Anti-relocation statement: constitutional validity and rights interpretation remain in core.
- Proportionality anchor: all downstream institutional duties inherit class/tier-scaled burden from this chapter.

### CI-2 — Institutional design and separation of powers
- Minimum institutional functions (policy, assurance, adjudication support, publication, remediation oversight).
- Structural anti-capture constraints (separation, overlap controls, conflict checks).
- Delegation boundaries and retained non-delegable duties.

### CI-3 — Authority custody, delegation, and decision rights
- Authority provenance and custody chain requirements.
- Delegation admissibility, revocation triggers, and emergency delegation constraints.
- Decision-right matrix standards (who decides, who reviews, who can challenge).
- Required risk appetite/tolerance statement and review cadence aligned to class/tier and constitutional constraints.

### CI-4 — Appointment, qualification, rotation, and removal
- Eligibility and disqualification standards for institutional roles.
- Competency and continuity floors for high-impact governance functions.
- Rotation, cooling-off, term limits, succession, and removal due process interfaces.
- Periodic governance-body performance evaluation and capability refresh obligations.

### CI-5 — Conflict integrity and anti-capture governance
- Conflict identification, disclosure, and recusal architecture.
- Financial and relational conflict controls tied to stewardship obligations.
- Capture-risk indicators and mandatory corrective pathways.
- Related-party transaction and beneficial-interest transparency controls with auditable pre-clearance and ex post review.
- Anti-corruption and anti-fraud controls: prevention, detection, response, and remediation requirements with protected reporting integration.

### CI-6 — Institutional procedure integrity and adjudication interfaces
- Procedural baseline for institutional decisions: notice, record, rationale, contestability.
- Interface with `corpus_primitives.md` PROT6 / PROT-DRP.
- Boundary line: no duplicate canonical offense taxonomy (Chapter Four remains canonical).
- Appeal and review path for materially impactful institutional measures.

### CI-7 — Oversight, audit, and evidence discipline
- Three-lines assurance architecture: operational ownership, independent risk/compliance challenge, and independent assurance with role-separation safeguards.
- Internal and independent oversight lanes and cadence scaling.
- Record integrity, publication discipline, and evidence custody.
- Triggered review pathways and escalation protocols across institutions.
- Repeated, verified anti-constitutional misconduct triggers mandatory dissolution review with protected continuity planning.
- Annual material-controls effectiveness declaration with remediation disclosure for material control failures.
- Mandatory independent external assurance triggers based on class/tier and incident severity, with published trigger criteria.

### CI-8 — Cross-institution coordination and escalation
- Coordination requirements where multiple institutions share jurisdiction.
- Priority/conflict-order handling across institutions consistent with constitutional hierarchy.
- External-order interface constraints (points to Sentient Constitution **Chapter Twelve** where applicable; adoption and change mechanics in **Chapters Eleven and Thirteen**).

### CI-9 — Classification-linked institutional obligations
- Binding interface to `corpus_systems.md` Chapter S2 (system classes) and Chapter S3 (stewardship tiers).
- Scaling table for institutional governance intensity, assurance burden, and review cadence by class/tier.
- Rule for mixed-scope institutions: highest applicable class/tier controls where duties differ.
- **CI-9.1 — Formation proportionality:** Class P low-friction formation; Class A enhanced diligence; explicit scaling dimensions including contest-integrity depth (**CI-7**).
- **CI-9.2 — Survival floors, voluntary exchange, and markets (Article III-A interface):** connect essential-access outcomes to fiscal and voluntary-economy design without restating rights (`INST-PROTO-20`); consistency pointer to class-aligned revenue orientation in **CI-10.1**.

### CI-10 — Public revenue, fees, recurring charges, and billing integrity
- Standalone chapter for fiscal and subscription/billing minima (cross-ref **CI-9** for classification scaling and **Article III-A** interface).
- **CI-10.1 — Public revenue, user fees, and class-aligned burden:** orientation only (no tax bases/rates); A/B, L/P, C fiscal posture (`INST-PROTO-19`).
- **CI-10.2 — Recurring charges, renewals, and commercial billing integrity:** supervised subscription/trial-to-paid minima (`INST-PROTO-21`); cross-ref **CI-12.1** for pathway UX.
- Documentation duty: align revenue and fee choices with published class/tier assignments; reclassification when essentiality drifts.

### CI-11 — Institutional resource stewardship and capacity integrity
- Resource adequacy and resilience expectations for institutional continuity.
- Incentive alignment controls for institutional actors and governance units.
- Interface points to `corpus_systems.md` Protocol S5 where institutional financing meets system stewardship.
- Remuneration governance includes malus/clawback pathways where anti-constitutional misconduct or severe control failure is verified.
- Class-aligned fiscal orientation pointer to **CI-9.2**, **CI-10.1**, and **CI-10.2** (and **CI-10** generally).

### CI-12 — Transparency, participation, and accessible pathways to responsibility
- Public intelligibility and participatory access to institutional processes.
- Accessibility obligations for participation, challenge, and review channels.
- Explicit bridge to **Article III** (equal educational access (**III-B**)) capability pathways and **Chapter Ten**, section 5 — *Authorized Roles, Competency Development, and Contribution* stewardship pathways.
- Protected escalation and whistleblower channels with anti-retaliation guarantees and independent intake/escalation handling.
- **CI-12.1 — Digital self-service pathway integrity:** entry–exit symmetry, channel matching (e.g. no phone-only cancel for web signup), discoverability and honest labeling for obligation management and exit, limits on manipulative retention flows, accessibility parity for exit; pairs with **CI-10.2** (`INST-PROTO-22`) where charges apply.
- **CI-12.2 — Governance disclosure schema:** standardized governance report cadence and minimum required fields.

### CI-13 — Institutional failure, dissolution, and accountability
- Dissolution threshold for institutions demonstrating repeated, verified anti-constitutional misconduct after due process.
- Continuity and transfer protocols to preserve rights, services, records, and challenge pathways during dissolution.
- Individual and collective accountability route: responsible sentients remain fully accountable under ordinary constitutional law and Chapter Four/Chapter Five governance.
- Graduated sanctions ladder before dissolution (for example corrective mandate, constrained authority, supervised operation) where constitutionally sufficient.

### CI-14 — Transitional governance and institutional evolution
- Controlled migration patterns for institutional redesign and consolidation.
- Non-regression and continuity safeguards during institutional transitions.
- Triggered revalidation conditions after material institutional change.
- Scenario-based continuity and succession stress testing proportional to class/tier.

### CI-15 — Vulnerable personal services markets — general regulation and Article X-C interface
- **Purpose:** sets institutional expectations for **Chapter Nine**, **Article X-C** (*Adult consensual commercial sexual services and sexual exploitation*).
  It also covers parallel stewardship of other **high-vulnerability** lawful personal-service markets.
  This section does **not** restate rights. The single-home sources remain **Articles V-B, I-C, IV-A, IV-C** and **Chapter Five** *Protected Characteristics*, *Consent*, and *Coercion and Manipulation*.
- **Default:** implement **Article X-C** through **general** regulatory families used for comparable lawful personal services (labor, consumer/contract fairness, platform/payments access, professional standards, dispute resolution), **scaled** by impact, dependency, isolation, vulnerability—**not** stigma-only singling out.
- **1 — Criminal decriminalization vs non-penal tools:** civil/administrative/licensing must **not** replicate **forbidden criminal** prohibition **without** **Article X-C**-consistent predicates (read with **anti-circumvention** in core).
- **2 — Cross-sector:** coercion, fraud, incapacity, non-consensual acts in **paid** exchange framed as **general** market wrongs where possible.
- **3 — Risk-tiered due care:** escalation for concentrated risk; **S2/S3** alignment; **documented** risk, not stereotype profiling.
- **4 — Non-criminal remedies:** **X-A** (*Non-Imposition*; harassment/bullying; cooperative freedom of focus), **XI-C** (false trust), **CI-12.1** pathway integrity.
- **5 — Intermediaries/platforms/finance:** `corpus_systems.md` **Chapter S2/S3** and **market-mediated personal services** interpretation; **S1** data minimization; **AML** not moral de-banking.
- **6 — Lane separation + anti-pretext audit:** no exploitation labels to punish **protected** consensual conduct **without** facts; no decriminalization as excuse to deprioritize exploitation enforcement or victim remedies.
- **7 — Workplace parity, housing, licensing, transition:** match ordinary lawful cooperative work scaled to stigma risk; phased expungement/retraining/case review (`INST-PROTO-23`).

### CI-16 — Compliance mapping, stable IDs, and cross-reference registry
- Stable code registry for institution protocols (prefix: `INST-`; **INST-PROTO-1** through **INST-PROTO-23**, including **CI-15** / Article X-C interface).
- Mapping table to constitutional articles and PRIM/PROT/SYS owners.
- Edit discipline rules for renumbering and reference integrity checks.

## 4. Interface map (single-home discipline)

- **Core (`core_constitution.md`) keeps ownership of:** values hierarchy, definition validity (O/E/C), rights floors, compliance taxonomy, amendment/supremacy minima.
- **CP (`corpus_primitives.md`) keeps ownership of:** PRIM/PROT normative primitive logic and governance primitives.
- **CS (`corpus_systems.md`) keeps ownership of:** system/data class taxonomies and protocol-level technical/system stewardship controls; **market-mediated personal services** interpretation block ties **S2/S3** evaluation to **Article X-A** / **Article X-C** implementation (see **CI-15**).
- **New institutions file keeps ownership of:** institutional governance architecture, process custody, classification-linked institutional scaling, and multi-body coordination mechanics (including **CI-15** Article X-C operationalization without redefining rights).

## 5. Initial acceptance checks for introducing this file

1. No duplicate O/E/C definition blocks copied from Chapter Three.
2. No competing canonical offense classification language against Chapter Four.
3. Every institutional protocol section includes explicit owner pointers to core/CP/CS where relevant.
4. Reference-integrity audit includes this file in article/cross-file scans.
5. Regression scenarios add at least one boundary assertion preventing rights/process drift into the wrong owner layer.
6. Dissolution provisions require repeated and verified anti-constitutional misconduct, due-process findings, and continuity safeguards for affected sentients.
7. Accountability language must explicitly preserve ordinary constitutional-law accountability for responsible sentients; dissolution cannot be used as an accountability substitute.
8. Formation/authorization requirements are class-scaled: Class P-only institutions default to simplified low-cost formation, while Class A institutions require enhanced due diligence proportional to impact.
9. A named proportionality rule is present and operationalized across formation, oversight, evidence, transparency, and remediation burdens (not only at entry authorization).
10. Three-lines role separation, annual material-controls attestation, and disclosed remediation tracking are explicit.
11. Protected escalation/anti-retaliation, related-party transparency, and appeal routes are explicit and auditable.
12. Institutional sanctions are graduated and reviewable; dissolution remains available for repeated verified anti-constitutional misconduct.
13. Anti-corruption and anti-fraud controls are explicit, with defined incident response and accountability pathways.
14. External assurance triggers are objective, class/tier-aware, and auditable.
15. Public governance reporting uses a standardized disclosure schema with required minimum fields and publication cadence.
16. Recurring charges and digital self-service pathways: institutional minima in **CI-10.2** and **CI-12.1** reference core **Article II-D**, **Article XIII**, **Article X-A**, and **Chapter Four** without duplicating rights floors; billing-structure rules (**CI-10**) and pathway/UX rules (**CI-12**) stay cross-linked and class/tier-scaled.
17. **Article X-C** interface: **CI-15** and **`INST-PROTO-23`** align with core **decriminalization**, **exploitation/procurement**, **general-market integration**, and **anti-circumvention** language; **corpus_systems.md** **market-mediated personal services** block cross-references **CI-15** without creating a second rights home.
