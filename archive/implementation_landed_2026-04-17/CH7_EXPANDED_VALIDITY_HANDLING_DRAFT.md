## Proposed section title

### Amendment Validity Tests, Review Triggers, and Invalid-Change Handling

## Draft constitutional text (structured)

- **Validity-floor framing:** This chapter states minimum constitutional validity conditions for amendment and adoption actions. Any amendment that fails these conditions is invalid regardless of procedural completion.

- **Test 1 - Substantive non-regression validity:** A proposed amendment is invalid if it materially weakens Chapter One non-negotiable constraints, Chapters Two and Three integrity requirements, **Chapter Nine** rights floors, **Chapter Ten** legitimacy and stewardship minima, or **Chapter Six** compliance and enforcement-realism anchors. That includes indirect narrowing through definitions, standing gates, evidentiary burden manipulation, observability degradation, or emergency re-labeling.

- **Test 2 - Procedural validity and publication integrity:** A proposed amendment is invalid unless proposal text, scope, affected provisions, expected impacts, and contest pathways are published with sufficient specificity and lead time for materially affected review under **Chapter Four** burden and traceability requirements.

- **Test 3 - Authority-chain and custody validity:** A proposed amendment is invalid where the instrument of adoption, edition identifier, effective date, or custodian chain is missing, contradictory, or materially unreliable relative to **Chapter Eleven** adoption requirements and Corpus (Constitutional) custody controls.

- **Test 4 - Contestability and independent review validity:** A proposed amendment is invalid if it forecloses meaningful contest, suppresses independent review where heightened review is required, or conditions participation on coercive waivers of auditability, standing, or challenge rights that this Constitution protects.

- **Review triggers:** Heightened review is automatically triggered for amendments that materially affect rights (**Chapter Nine**), constitutional interpretation/review safeguards (**Article XXI-A** and related **Article IV** safeguards), standing effects (**Chapter Six** axis C interaction), emergency powers, or cross-jurisdiction enforcement pathways.

- **Provisional effect rule:** Rights-affecting amendments and authority-structure amendments do not take irreversible effect until minimum review windows and contest pathways are satisfied, except where immediate narrow interim measures are necessary to prevent imminent material harm and remain reversible where feasible.

- **Invalid-change handling:** Where a change is found invalid, it is treated as non-operative for constitutional supremacy purposes from the point of determination; decision records must identify scope, affected acts, remediation obligations, and required restoration steps for materially affected parties.

- **Remediation and continuity:** On invalidity determination, adopters must publish a time-bounded remediation plan, restore controlling prior protections or stricter lawful protections, preserve evidence and audit trails, and provide contestable remedy pathways proportionate to impact and dependency.

- **Anti-evasion clause:** Repackaging a regressive change as interpretation guidance, implementation memo, adoption metadata, or emergency temporary measure does not cure invalidity where substantive constitutional effect is materially equivalent.

- **Tiered constitutional offense classification (regressive-deception conduct):** **Operative** tiering is **only** under **Chapter Seven** in [`core_07-07_misconduct.md`](../../core_07-07_misconduct.md). Evaluators use a **unified incident** record, **six** criteria in **section 2**, and **incident-gravity** Tier 1–3 rules in **section 3** (including **single catastrophic incident**, **sustained high-gravity pattern**, and the **criterion 5 or 6 alone** alternate path to **Tier 2** with **section 4** safeguards). **This chapter** may describe **referral** patterns only. For **alignment** with **Chapter Seven**, **section 2**, change- and record-path regressive deception **typically** implicates **criteria 1–4** in this order: **(1)** willful deceptive intent; **(2)** knowing falsification or suppression; **(3)** material rollback / regressive effect; **(4)** causally significant obstruction or degradation. **Criteria 5** (forcible subversion) **and 6** (operative nullification) apply where **Chapter Seven** routes them as material.

- **Historical draft sketch (superseded tier counts, 2026-04-10):** The following **four-criterion** count model was an early placeholder and **does not** match the current **Chapter Seven** incident-gravity scheme: ~~Tier 1 (2 of 4), Tier 2 (3 of 4), Tier 3 (4 of 4)~~ — **do not use** for classification.

- **Due-process safeguards for tier classification:** No final Tier 2 or Tier 3 classification may issue without independent review, contestable notice, and reasoned publication under **Chapter Four** traceability standards and **Article XXI-A** (bounded interpretive mandate) / **Article IV** review safeguards.
- A good-faith drafting error that is promptly disclosed and cured before constitutional effect is not Tier 2 or Tier 3 conduct.

- **Layer discipline clause:** Detailed institutional procedure, staffing mechanics, and operational workflow for review execution belong to owner layers (Sentient Constitution Chapters Six through Ten; CP/CS where designated) and must not be inferred to relocate constitutional validity standards out of **Chapter Eleven**.

## Optional compact version (if you want minimal expansion)

Use this if you prefer a shorter insertion:

- This chapter establishes constitutional validity floors for amendment, adoption, and supremacy claims.
- An amendment is invalid if it fails substantive non-regression, procedural publication/contestability, or authority-chain custody requirements.
- Heightened review is required for rights-affecting and interpretation-safeguard changes.
- Invalid changes are non-operative for supremacy purposes and require published remediation and restoration.
- Re-labeling a regressive amendment as guidance, emergency practice, or metadata does not avoid invalidity.
- **Chapter Seven** supplies operative Tier 1–3 triggers (incident gravity, including **criteria 5–6** where material), independent review, and reasoned publication under **section 4**; do not infer tier counts from this draft.

## Design choices and complexity impact

- Keeps **Chapter Eleven** as the constitutional home for amendment/adoption validity standards.
- Avoids overloading **Chapter Three** (definition integrity) with institutional design mechanics.
- Adds explicit invalidity consequences without requiring immediate CP/CS procedural rewrite.
- Supports future expansion into detailed execution protocols in CP/CS.
