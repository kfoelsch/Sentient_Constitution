# Court continuity and emergency adjudication draft

Status: implementation drafting artifact (non-authoritative draft).  
Date: 2026-04-12.  
Primary constitutional anchors: `core_constitution.md` Article XXII and Article XXIV; `corpus_institutions.md` CI-8 and CI-14; `corpus_systems.md` Protocol R and Protocol D.

## Purpose

This draft closes the current court-structure gap around:
- continuity of adjudication during emergency, incapacity, deadlock, or capture;
- fallback venues and temporary quorum rules;
- preservation and transfer of records;
- restoration review after emergency measures;
- preventing continuity powers from becoming durable concentration of judicial power.

## Draft protocol text

### 1. Scope and role

This protocol applies when ordinary court operation is materially impaired by:
- emergency conditions;
- incapacity of key personnel;
- inability to form lawful panels;
- deadlock across court bodies;
- capture or compromise indicators;
- network or infrastructure disruption that impairs constitutional adjudication.

It governs continuity of adjudication. It does not authorize permanent alteration of constitutional court-family boundaries.

### 2. Rights-floor continuity rule

Emergency court operation must preserve, to the greatest feasible extent:
- baseline challenge rights;
- access to emergency relief;
- reviewability of coercive or materially rights-affecting action;
- auditability of emergency decisions;
- a path back to ordinary independent adjudication.

Emergency simplification must not become a pretext to extinguish contestability.

### 3. Continuity modes

Each adopting system must publish at least these court continuity modes:
- `Normal`
- `Degraded`
- `Emergency limited-capacity`
- `Fallback / backup-forum operation`
- `Restoration and reconciliation`

Mode transitions must be trigger-defined, attributable, auditable, and reversible when triggering conditions clear.

### 4. Activation triggers

Continuity mode may activate only on documented findings such as:
- inability to constitute lawful independent panels at ordinary scale;
- physical or digital infrastructure outage materially impairing filing, record access, hearing, or publication;
- emergency incapacity or absence of required judicial or administrative personnel;
- credible capture or compromise indicators affecting ordinary routing or decision security;
- overlapping crisis conditions that make ordinary scheduling constitutionally inadequate for emergency matters.

Activation records must state:
- the trigger condition;
- affected court family or families;
- expected scope and duration;
- what rights-protective functions must continue without interruption;
- who authorized activation;
- when independent review will occur.

### 5. Continuity minimum functions

In any degraded or emergency mode, the system must preserve at minimum:
- intake for urgent matters;
- emergency interim-relief authority;
- routing or triage sufficient to identify rights-floor, evidence-preservation, and safety-critical cases;
- record preservation and chain of custody;
- a way to challenge emergency orders;
- a path to restore ordinary forum review when feasible.

### 6. Emergency triage hierarchy

Where capacity is insufficient for normal operations, courts must prioritize:
- matters involving imminent loss of life or irreversible harm;
- evidence-preservation needs likely to become impossible if delayed;
- detention, seizure, exclusion, or comparable coercive measures needing prompt review;
- inability-to-form and anti-self-judging cases that block lawful merits adjudication;
- matters where delay would predictably extinguish practical remedy.

The triage rule must be published in plain language.

### 7. Temporary quorum and provisional authority

Adopting systems may define temporary reduced-size or provisional panels only if:
- the temporary quorum rule is predeclared;
- competence and independence are still preserved as far as feasible;
- the measure is limited in duration and scope;
- rights-affecting emergency decisions remain subject to prompt review by a fuller lawful panel or backup forum.

Temporary quorum rules must not be used to concentrate durable control or to bypass anti-capture constraints.

### 8. Fallback venues and backup forums

Each court family must identify, in advance:
- what fallback venue or operational mode applies if its ordinary forum is unavailable;
- how the designated backup family under Chapter Eight will receive emergency matters if anti-self-judging or inability-to-form conditions activate;
- how transfer records, docket identity, and review rights will be preserved across the shift.

Fallback operation must preserve dominant-purpose routing as much as feasible. It does not authorize silent reclassification of the matter.

### 9. Record continuity and custody

Emergency adjudication requires continuous preservation of:
- docket identity;
- filings and timestamps;
- panel-formation and recusal records;
- evidence custody records;
- emergency orders and reasons;
- publication-delay records;
- restoration and rollback decisions.

Where digital systems are impaired, institutions must maintain a lawful fallback for record capture and later reconciliation.

### 10. Communication duties

During continuity-mode operation, the system must publish or otherwise communicate, as lawfully feasible:
- the active mode;
- what functions remain available;
- where urgent filings should go;
- what publication or hearing delays to expect;
- how parties can seek emergency review;
- when the next continuity-status review will occur.

### 11. Independent review during emergency mode

Continuity activation must be subject to independent review on a documented cadence.

That review must assess:
- whether the trigger still exists;
- whether emergency simplifications remain necessary and proportionate;
- whether continuity powers are drifting into durable concentration;
- whether rights-floor, challenge, and publication duties are being preserved enough to remain lawful.

Continuation beyond the published window requires reasoned reauthorization.

### 12. Capture-sensitive continuity

If the emergency condition includes credible capture, concealment, or compromise within the ordinary court chain, continuity operation must:
- avoid leaving sole control in the compromised chain;
- use backup-forum and contest-integrity mechanisms where feasible;
- preserve independent record review;
- require later restoration review by a body not materially implicated in the compromise.

### 13. Cross-system and cross-institution coordination

Where multiple institutions or court families are affected, the continuity protocol must define:
- accountability for the continuity decision;
- evidence-sharing boundaries;
- transfer authority;
- deadlock-breaker path;
- conditions for external participation or assurance;
- restoration sequencing across affected bodies.

### 14. Restoration and reconciliation

When the triggering condition subsides, the system must enter a restoration mode that includes:
- return to ordinary panel-formation and routing rules;
- review of emergency orders that materially affected rights or structure;
- publication or reconstruction of delayed records;
- correction of continuity-period docket inaccuracies;
- remediation for continuity failures that materially harmed parties;
- assessment of whether fallback actions exceeded lawful scope.

### 15. Post-incident review

Every activation must produce a post-incident review record stating:
- what triggered the continuity mode;
- what functions degraded or failed;
- what fallback measures were used;
- whether rights-floor continuity was maintained;
- what delays or review failures occurred;
- what remediation and future-hardening steps are required.

### 16. Exercises and testing

Court continuity protocols must be exercised on a cadence proportional to impact and dependency.

Exercises should cover at least:
- inability to form an independent panel;
- major backlog surge;
- digital outage affecting filings or records;
- emergency relief under reduced staffing;
- capture-sensitive routing and backup activation.

Findings must be recorded and tied to remediation.

### 17. Non-compliant emergency patterns

The following are presumptively non-compliant unless clearly justified and cured:
- indefinite emergency court mode without reauthorization;
- emergency quorum use that concentrates durable control;
- loss of docket identity or chain of custody;
- emergency orders with no later review path;
- continuity activation used to bypass recusal, anti-self-judging, or publication obligations;
- restoration without retrospective review of materially impactful emergency decisions.

### 18. Minimum adoption questions

Before adoption, confirm:
- what activates each continuity mode;
- who can authorize it;
- who reviews continued activation;
- what emergency functions must never go dark;
- what fallback venue exists for each court family;
- how records survive infrastructure or staffing failure;
- how ordinary adjudication is restored;
- how the system proves continuity powers did not become covert entrenchment.

## Recommended fit in the corpus

If incorporated into the normative corpus, the cleanest homes appear to be:
- a new subsection near CI-8 and CI-14; or
- an `INST-PROTO` implementation protocol referenced from CI-8, CI-14, Article XXII, and Article XXIV-facing transition text.
