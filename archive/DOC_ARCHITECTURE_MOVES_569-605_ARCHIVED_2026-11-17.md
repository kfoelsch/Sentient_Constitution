**Five hundred and seventy-ninth move (Chapter One §16.5.2 Design for Parallelization; 2026-11-16; adds a design duty at principle layer; uncommitted).** Decision (custodian, asked in session, after signing off on the 578th move: "probably another subbullet 16.5.2, which would be that processes should be designed to support parallelization unless doing so creates an undue burden"). Rule: stewardship processes are designed to support parallelization unless doing so creates an undue burden on anyone. Supporting it means defining each step by what it needs and produces so independent steps can be seen as independent (no waiting by habit, form order, or a shared holder or queue), not making independent steps take turns on inputs and tools, building in the step where the strands rejoin and an Act Record that holds each strand's result, and using common published formats (§18.6) so different stewards or systems can take different strands. A process built in a line when a parallel design was feasible and not unduly burdensome carries the same Avoidable Burden defect as in §16.5.1. Matching edits: §16.5.1 points to §16.5.2; the section-wide *Scaling* paragraph is now headed "Scaling, for all of Automation" so it reads as covering §16.5, §16.5.1 and §16.5.2.
