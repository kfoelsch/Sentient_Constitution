**Nine hundred and twenty-sixth move (tiered assemblies: lot drawn from an elected pool; 2026-11-22; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian, in the project thread, after an evaluation: tiered assemblies are "the recommended model", the national term is five years, local terms are two years with half the seats elected each year, then "go please"). The design is elected local assemblies, with regional and national assemblies drawn by lot from local members. What changed:

- [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3.1.1 Mechanism Families](../core_27.000_governance.md#311-mechanism-families) names **lot drawn from an elected pool** as a recognized combination and points to the recommended model, stating that no adopter is required to use it. The Pluralism rule of §3.1.4 is unchanged.
- [Subcore 27-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](../subcore_27.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool) is new. It adds **musts** for any mechanism of that kind: only elected members are in the pool; the draw is stratified by area and population within a published tolerance; the mechanism publishes the chance that a bloc with less than half of the pool wins a majority and keeps it under a published bound; the draw is reproducible from a public randomness source; votes on materially binding acts are recorded, conduct is reviewed at term end by an independent seat, and removal for cause is available; a missing Article XII-A pathway for an affected class is added. §1 now names this section.
- **Narrower contact bar for standing assemblies drawn from an elected pool:** [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3.1.2](../core_27.000_governance.md#312-sortition-integrity-and-anti-capture-use), [Article XIII-B](../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Sortition bodies*), and Subcore 27-D §6 now say that in such an assembly the private-contact bar applies to interested parties, and that members of the authorizing community may contact a drawn member in their own capacity, logged on the open record. Every other sortition body keeps the full bar. Reason: the full bar was written for deliberative panels and would stop drawn legislators hearing from the community they serve.
- [CI-27.205 §8 Reference Design: Tiered Assemblies](../corpus_institutions/ci_27.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-27205-8-reference-design-tiered-assemblies) is new and optional: local terms of 2 years (half elected yearly, re-election allowed); regional 3 years and national 5 years, single terms, a third and a fifth drawn each year; eligible only after completing a full local assembly term and being re-elected, and drawn as the second local term begins (time filling a vacancy partway through a term does not count as a full term); if too few are eligible, the shortfall is drawn from other sitting local members; local members are elected by ranked voting in multi-member districts; the drawn member's local seat passes to the next candidate by a recount of that election's ranked ballots; separate draws from the local pool; 150 seats or more as a guide; serving by default with paid protection; vacancies by draw; independent two-source briefing; paid orientation; post-service cooling-off under Article XIII-C. It leaves executive formation open. The Trace and Quick orientation list it.
- `tools/time_reference_registry.json` registers the fixed period **assembly term**, owned by CI-27.205 §8, so the term lengths in its table meet TIME-REF-01. No audit rule changed.
- `TODO.md` gains an item for the open questions: executive formation, and whether the candidate pool and electorate rules need anything more for non-human sentients.

**Nine hundred and twenty-seventh move (new Chapter Twenty-Four: Assemblies; Chapters Twenty-Four to Thirty become Twenty-Five to Thirty-One; 2026-11-23; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian, in the project thread, 2026-11-22 to 2026-11-23: the role of the assemblies "should be a top level chapter"; executives at all three tiers are chosen directly by the assemblies; budgeting at all three tiers; defense and foreign policy national; typed emergencies with two renewal tiers, 72 hours and 90 days, and 7 days and 30 days; emergencies revocable by their assemblies; elections postponed only for mass displacement or an infrastructure or system collapse, and held within 90 days after; referendums on a 2% petition, screened by the forums, binding with a majority and support from a third of the electorate, and changeable by the assembly only by two-thirds; a no-confidence vote need not name a replacement, which is chosen within one week; executive terms up to 5 years; the two-thirds list including a national declaration of war; the chapter "goes immediately after the forums"; then "That's good. Go ahead"). What changed:

- [Chapter Twenty-Four](../core_24.000_assemblies.md#chapter-twenty-four-assemblies) (*Assemblies*) is new, from the reviewed draft. It owns what a polity's assemblies decide, how work is divided between tiers, how assemblies decide, executives, community referendums, typed emergencies, and the limits on every assembly. New **musts** include: two-thirds of members for acts that limit a right, assent under Chapter Twenty-Nine, a national declaration of war beyond responding to an armed attack, changes to election or draw rules (future terms only), and acting outside the tier's published matters; an emergency must be a named type, confirmed within its tier's window, and revocable by a majority at any time; an election may be postponed only as above. It adds to, and does not replace, the emergency discipline of [Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden).
- **Renumbering:** the chapters that were Twenty-Four to Thirty are now Twenty-Five to Thirty-One, with their subcore files (Subcore 25-A becomes 26-A; Subcores 26-A to 26-F become 27-A to 27-F) and the corpus files homed on them (CI-24.205, CI-24.210, CI-26.205, CS-26.305, CJS-30.105, CJS-30.110 move up by one). Twenty files were renamed, and chapter cites, reader numbers, ids, slugs and "Core home" lines were rewritten in 311 files outside `archive/`, `evidence/` and `translations/` by the one-off tool `tools/assemblies_chapter_insertion/shift_chapters.py`. Dated change-history rows keep their original wording (the announcement's v0.12 row was restored by hand). `tools/architecture/chapter_numbers.json` gains a row for 24.000 and keeps the old machine number of each shifted row in `pre_assemblies_2026_11_23`.

**Nine hundred and thirtieth move (new Subcore 24-A: Taxation; 2026-11-23; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian, in the project thread: "We are going to want a subcore file on taxation. We want lists of good tax policies vs. bad tax policies, some stuff about enforcement; those kinds of things. Please research best practices and draft. We want the result to be meaningful guidance on a simple, comprehensible, enforceable tax code that emphasizes fairness and ease of use."). What changed:

- [Subcore 24-A](../subcore_24.010_taxation.md#subcore-24-a-taxation) (*Taxation*) is new, attached to Chapter Twenty-Four and drafted from widely used tax-policy principles (Adam Smith's canons, the AICPA guiding principles of good tax policy, the Mirrlees Review, OECD tax-administration and taxpayer-rights guidance, and evidence on third-party reporting and the tax gap). It owns the standard detail of the tax power in [Chapter Twenty-Four §3 What Assemblies Decide](../core_24.000_assemblies.md#3-what-assemblies-decide): eleven tests every tax must pass, grouped as lawful and open, fair, easy to use, efficient, and dependable (§3); revenue priorities, preferred designs, taxes on harm, and tax breaks under review (§4); barred designs and disfavored designs that need reasons and independent review (§5); ease-of-use duties (§6); graduated enforcement, penalties, even enforcement, limits on collection, and separate seats including a taxpayer advocate (§7); how tax rules are made and changed (§8); taxpayer rights (§9); and outcome-review measures (§10).
- New **musts** include: no retroactive tax increase; no tax that leaves a sentient below the Article III-A *Survival* floor; no tax on voting, worship, speech, assembly, or bringing a case; no tax by protected characteristic, substrate, or viewpoint; no hidden tax; no collector pay, rating, or quota tied to amounts collected; every ruling for one taxpayer published; no taxing power unlocked by an emergency; no combined tax-and-benefit cliff past one hundred percent; every tax break reported, costed, and lapsing (default five years); free filing for every taxpayer; collection of a disputed amount paused during the first independent appeal; and audit rates at the top no lower than for low-income credit claimants. §4.1 *Revenue Priorities* (custodian, in the project thread: "what we don't have specifically is a priority list. For example, taxes on carbon, methane, fossil fuel use, should be prioritized. Please come up with a list based on our principles in chapter 2") orders revenue sources: harm to the living world first (greenhouse gases, fossil fuels, pollution, depletion), then other costs placed on others, then unearned gains from shared or scarce resources, then income by ability to pay, and work, production, and trade last; an assembly must say in its Act Record why it relies on a lower group while a higher source is untaxed or priced below its cost, and harm taxes rise to full cost on a published schedule, apply at the border, are never budgeted as permanent, and never license a forbidden harm. Provisional **defaults** that choose meaning, for the custodian to confirm: at most four rates per tax; a seventy percent combined marginal rate as the review trigger; ninety days' notice before a tax rise takes effect; penalty ceilings of twenty and seventy-five percent; reassessment limits of three and six years; one amnesty in ten years; a thirty-minute target for confirming a pre-filled return.
- Wiring: Chapter Twenty-Four's placement block, Trace, §3 *Sets tax policy* line, and next-file footer name the subcore; Chapter Twenty-Five's previous-file footer, the reading chain in `tools/footer_audit.py`, `tools/architecture/chapter_numbers.json` (row `S24-A`), and the README chapter table and subcore list follow. `tools/time_reference_registry.json` registers four fixed periods owned by Subcore 24-A (tax-break lapse period, amnesty interval, reassessment period, tax notice period) so their lengths pass TIME-REF-01; no audit rule changed.

**Nine hundred and thirty-second move (Subcore 24-A §4.1 and §4.3: gambling and intoxicants under a discouragement rule; 2026-11-23; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian, in the project thread: "One more thing to tax: gambling. At a rate high enough that it gets discouraged, but not so high that it results in significant organized crime"). What changed:

- [Subcore 24-A §4.1 Revenue Priorities](../subcore_24.010_taxation.md#41-revenue-priorities) adds **Gambling** to the second group (other costs placed on others), read with [Chapter Two §19.5 Contingent Claims, Games of Chance, and Event-Contract Markets](../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#195-contingent-claims-games-of-chance-and-event-contract-markets). The tax falls on operators' gross gaming revenue. New **musts**: the rate is set high enough to discourage gambling, in place of the cost-of-harm rate of §4.3, and below the level that drives gambling to unlicensed or criminal operators; the revenue office publishes a yearly estimate of the unlicensed share; where it is material or rising, the assembly lowers the rate or strengthens enforcement and says which in its Act Record. Follow-up the same sitting (custodian: "This should be a general principle, and apply to intoxicants etc."): the rate rules become the general **discouragement rule** in [§4.3 Taxes on Harm](../subcore_24.010_taxation.md#43-taxes-on-harm), for any activity adults may lawfully choose that the assembly decides to discourage; §4.1 lists **Discouraged activities** (gambling, and intoxicants taxed by quantity of the active substance). Further **musts**: a discouragement tax is never set so high that it works as a ban in place of a general rule, and each budget reports prevention and treatment spending beside the revenue. No audit rule changed.
