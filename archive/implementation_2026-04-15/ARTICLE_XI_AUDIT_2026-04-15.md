# Article XI audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XI: Stakeholder Governance, Participation, and Due Process`.

## Overall assessment

Article XI has the correct constitutional objective. It treats stakeholder governance, representation, participation weighting, legitimacy review, internal accountability, due process, and anti-capture safeguards as one integrated governance-rights cluster rather than as separate procedural conveniences.

The main issues were publication-shape and operational-clarity issues rather than doctrinal reversal:
- `Article XI-A` preserved the right governance and recordkeeping concepts, but it left the practical contest path for affected stakeholders too muted;
- `Article XI-B` and `Article XI-C` had the right anti-token and anti-dominance logic, but they read a little too much like implementation notes rather than direct constitutional limits on symbolic voice and binding legitimacy;
- `Article XI-D` carried the most important drafting gap because it named due-process minima without clearly surfacing meaningful opportunity to be heard or outcomes traceable to reasons and evidence;
- `Article XI-E` was directionally correct, but it was too compressed about detection, disclosure, and indirectly routed capture.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XI-A

- Operative claim: decisions materially affecting rights, access, or resources must remain recorded, understandable, and contestable, with governance pathways preserved for each materially affected stakeholder class.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom` and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Stakeholder`, `Procedural Fairness`, `Contestability`, and `Accountability`.
- Mechanics: the subsection must preserve external contestability of stakeholder inclusion and practical representation for dependency-affected groups, not just mention governance design in the abstract.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Operational blur] The subsection already pointed to records, explanation, and stakeholder mapping, but it left the actual contest pathway and standing-preservation rule too implicit for publication readers.
- Why it matters: if the page reads mainly as a governance-design note, systems can satisfy it ceremonially while still making challenge pathways impractical for materially affected stakeholders with weak market or institutional leverage.
- Smallest close: make the records bullet expressly require a practical opportunity to contest, and tighten the representation bullet so preserved standing for dependency-affected and high-risk groups is unmistakable.

### Article XI-B

- Operative claim: participation weighting may scale by impact, dependency, and demonstrated stake, but it cannot entrench narrow control or reduce materially affected stakeholders to formal voice without influence.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Stakeholder Participation Weight`, `Contestability`, `System Capture`, and functionally `Meaningful Agency`.
- Mechanics: weighting should remain auditable, revalidatable, and challengeable, and it must not become a proxy path to operator dominance or capital-based capture.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint underexposure] The anti-dominance concept was present, but the text did not say plainly enough that weighting cannot collapse materially affected stakeholder voice into symbolic participation.
- Why it matters: weighting rules are a classic place where systems preserve a surface of inclusion while shifting real influence elsewhere.
- Smallest close: add a direct sentence tying weighting limits to non-symbolic participation and prohibiting persistent control by narrow coalitions absent constitutionally sufficient justification.

### Article XI-D

- Operative claim: governance structures must connect documented roles to real responsibility, and materially impactful decisions must preserve due-process minima adequate to the stakes.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Procedural Fairness`, `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: the subsection must preserve timely notice, meaningful opportunity to be heard, unbiased decision structure, reviewable outcomes, and evidence-traceable reasons under Chapters Two through Four.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Major] [Underreach] The subsection named notice, timeliness, impartiality, and review, but it omitted the clearest publication-level signals that materially affected parties must have a meaningful chance to be heard and that outcomes must be traceable to reasons and evidence.
- Why it matters: without those points, the article can be read as satisfied by thin formal procedure even where the affected party has no real chance to respond and reviewers cannot reconstruct the basis for the decision.
- Smallest close: revise the due-process bullet to include meaningful opportunity to be heard and evidence-traceable reasons, and tighten the internal-roles bullet so title-only structures without practical influence are visibly non-compliant.

### Article XI-E

- Operative claim: governance processes must resist and correct structural capture, including concentrated or hidden control that defeats auditability or challenge.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `System Capture`, `Contestability`, and `Auditability`.
- Mechanics: Article XI-E should make clear that anti-capture work includes detection, disclosure, mitigation, and attention to indirectly routed control pathways.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint drop] The sentence correctly flagged monopolization, collusion, and structural capture, but it was too terse about the affirmative duties to detect, disclose, and mitigate capture, especially where control is hidden or indirectly routed.
- Why it matters: capture often appears through intermediaries, beneficial-control chains, or opaque governance influence rather than openly declared monopolization.
- Smallest close: rewrite the sentence so those duties are explicit and hidden or indirectly routed control is named as non-compliant where auditability or challenge is absent.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XI does not need expansion into a full procedural code. It needs a little more constitutional surface area so readers can see that stakeholder governance means practical contestability, non-symbolic influence, reviewable legitimacy, evidence-traceable due process, and anti-capture safeguards with real disclosure teeth.

Most important close points:
- make `Article XI-A` show practical contestability and preserved standing more directly;
- make `Article XI-B` state that weighting cannot reduce voice to ceremony or entrench narrow control;
- make `Article XI-D` surface meaningful hearing and evidence-traceable reasons as part of the due-process floor;
- make `Article XI-E` state detection, disclosure, and mitigation of hidden capture directly.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise `Article XI-A` so records, explanation, and contestability appear as one coherent governance minimum;
2. tighten `Article XI-B` and `Article XI-C` to read as direct constitutional limits rather than administrative notes;
3. revise `Article XI-D` to include meaningful opportunity to be heard and evidence-traceable outcomes;
4. rewrite `Article XI-E` so hidden or indirectly routed capture is expressly covered.
