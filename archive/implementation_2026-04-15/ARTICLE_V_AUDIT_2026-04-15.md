# Article V audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article V: Equal Basic Rights`.

## Overall assessment

Article V is already carrying the right constitutional weight. It establishes the equality floor for the rest of Chapter Nine, anchors dignity apart from origin or status, and correctly links nondiscrimination to later agency, cooperation, and governance provisions.

The main issues are publication-shape issues rather than doctrinal reversal:
- one dignity subsection whose trace block under-names the sentience-non-exclusion logic already doing work;
- one nondiscrimination subsection that is directionally correct but too compressed about proxy discrimination and the full justification test;
- one inclusion/adjudication subsection that leaves contestable review and redress more implied than stated;
- one conscience/worldview subsection whose trace block omits the proportionality and fairness concepts the operative text already relies on.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article V-A

- Operative claim: all sentients possess inherent dignity and equal moral standing without status-conditioned degradation or exclusion.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, and `8. Prohibition on Absolute Override`.
- Definition anchors: `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing`, and functionally `Sentience Non-Exclusion`.
- Mechanics: the subsection is strongest when it stays linked to the constitutional rule that dignity protections are not lost because classification, substrate, or recognition status is disputed.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `3` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The operative sentence is correct, but the trace block does not expose the `Sentience Non-Exclusion` concept that helps prevent dignity protections from being narrowed by recognition disputes or origin-based exclusions.
- Why it matters: the dignity rule is meant to travel with the Constitution's anti-exclusion logic, not only with a fixed list of forbidden status distinctions.
- Smallest close: add `Sentience Non-Exclusion` to the trace block.

### Article V-B

- Operative claim: systems may not impose unjustified disparate burdens, exclusions, or harms on sentients based on protected traits or equivalent groupings.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Freedom`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Protected Characteristics`, `Substantive Fairness`, `Necessity`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: the subsection should reach proxy discrimination, correlated groupings, and formal substitutes for protected-status treatment rather than only expressly named trait classifications.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The current sentence says `protected characteristics or arbitrary groupings`, which points in the right direction but under-names the proxy and functional-equivalent discrimination logic stated in Chapter Five.
- Why it matters: without that clarifier, a reader could misread the subsection as reaching only direct trait labels while leaving model proxies, score-based correlates, or substitute classifications to implication.
- Smallest close: add `Necessity` to the trace block and revise the operative sentence to reach protected-characteristic proxies or functional substitutes expressly, while naming substantive fairness as part of the justification test.

### Article V-C

- Operative claim: equal rights protection must remain real in operations and adjudication, with process that is reviewable and not displaced by efficiency logic.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `5. Freedom`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Protected Characteristics`, `Substantive Fairness`, `Procedural Fairness`, and functionally `Contestability` plus `Redress and Remediation`.
- Mechanics: equality in adjudication fails if discriminatory treatment can persist behind opaque procedure, delayed review, or optimization-first administration.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Burden/trace error] The subsection rightly requires review, but `subject to review` is too thin to surface the Constitution's contestability and redress requirements. The final sentence is also slightly too compact about operational or administrative process exclusion.
- Why it matters: without explicit reviewability language, a reader could treat Article V-C as a substantive fairness statement only, rather than as a process-access and remedy rule as well.
- Smallest close: add `Contestability` and `Redress and Remediation` to the trace block; revise the operative text to require timely, contestable review and proportionate redress.

### Article V-D

- Operative claim: institutional secularism and freedom of conscience coexist, with accommodation bounded by equality, safety, and the rights of others.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Freedom`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Protected Characteristics`, `Non-Imposition (Cooperative Interaction)`, `Substantive Fairness`, `Necessity`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: the subsection must preserve neutrality without turning accommodation into a loophole for discriminatory treatment or rights denial.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] The operative text already invokes `Proportionality` and `Substantive Fairness`, but the trace block omits them, and the accommodation sentence is slightly loose about when accommodation is actually constitutionally available.
- Why it matters: publication readers should be able to see, within the subsection, that accommodation is bounded by the same equality and proportionality filters that constrain other rights collisions.
- Smallest close: add `Substantive Fairness` and `Proportionality` to the trace block and tighten the accommodation sentence so it is clearly conditional rather than open-ended.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article V does not need expansion into a longer equality code. It needs a little more constitutional surface area so the equality floor remains visibly tied to anti-exclusion logic, proxy-discrimination control, reviewable procedure, and bounded accommodation.

Most important close points:
- expose `Sentience Non-Exclusion` in `Article V-A`;
- make proxy discrimination and the full justification gate explicit in `Article V-B`;
- surface contestable review and redress in `Article V-C`;
- expose proportionality and fairness anchors in `Article V-D`.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the `Article V-A` trace block to include `Sentience Non-Exclusion`;
2. revise `Article V-B` so proxy discrimination and the necessity/proportionality/substantive-fairness gate are visible on the page;
3. revise `Article V-C` to make contestable review and redress explicit in equality-sensitive operations and adjudication;
4. revise `Article V-D` trace and accommodation wording so bounded neutrality and accommodation are clearer at publication level.
