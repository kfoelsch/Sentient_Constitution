# Article VII audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article VII: Self-Ownership`.

## Overall assessment

Article VII is conceptually strong and already carries the right constitutional boundary: self-ownership over embodiment, protected mental interiority, and limits on inference from behavior into protected internal states.

The main issues are publication-shape issues rather than doctrinal reversal:
- the Article VII-A trace block under-names the self-determination and dignity logic already doing work in embodiment and mental self-direction;
- the body, mind, and focus bullets are directionally correct but too compressed about refusal, anti-manipulation, and protection against inference or reconstruction;
- the Article VII-B text correctly bars internal-state reconstruction, but it leaves the same-authority and anti-circumvention handling rule more implied than stated.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article VII-A trace and embodiment cluster

- Operative claim: sentients possess primary authority over their own bodies, minds, and substrate-defining identity information, subject only to constitutionally valid limits.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.3 Freedom-Limitation Constraints`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Consent`, `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Self-Determination`, `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing`, plus `Privacy (Informational)`.
- Mechanics: Article VII-A should preserve substantive self-direction, resist body-data separation that defeats autonomy, and make clear that restrictions are bounded by constitutional collision rules rather than local discretion.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The subsection is directionally correct, but its trace block does not expose `Self-Determination` or `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing`, and the body/mind bullets are compressed enough that refusal and protection against unauthorized inference or reconstruction remain undernamed.
- Why it matters: without those cues, the subsection can read more like a shorthand list of interests than a constitutional protection of embodied and mental self-direction.
- Smallest close: add `Self-Determination` and `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing` to the trace block, revise the body sentence to cover making and refusing such decisions, and revise the mind sentence to bar unauthorized inference, reconstruction, or exposure.

### Article VII-A focus

- Operative claim: sentients retain authority over attention, thought direction, and ordinary interaction boundaries rather than being open to manipulative capture.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom` and `6.3 Freedom-Limitation Constraints`.
- Definition anchors: `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Coercion and Manipulation` plus `Self-Determination`.
- Mechanics: focus protection should be substantive, not merely formal, and should resist design strategies that capture attention or structure interaction to bypass voluntary choice.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Proxy drift] `To govern our own thought processes and regular communication and interaction with others` points in the right direction, but it leaves the constitutional problem of attention capture and coercive or manipulative interference too implicit.
- Why it matters: without naming that risk, the provision can be read as a general preference for personal space rather than as a bounded-agency safeguard against designed interference with cognition and interaction boundaries.
- Smallest close: revise the focus sentence so it speaks in terms of governing attention, thought processes, and ordinary communication or interaction boundaries free from coercive or manipulative capture.

### Article VII-B

- Operative claim: internal cognitive and emotional states are constitutionally protected against reconstruction, approximation, and correlational bypass, and outputs that functionally replicate them must receive Type N treatment.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, and `6.3 Freedom-Limitation Constraints`.
- Definition anchors: `Protected Internal-State Boundary`, `Privacy (Informational)`, `Consent`, and functionally `Necessity` plus `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: the article should preserve the anti-circumvention logic of the Chapter Five internal-state definitions and make clear that any permitted handling remains subject to the same constitutional authority chain and classification rules.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Burden/trace error] The subsection already prohibits reconstruction and approximation, but the page-level text leaves the same-authority rule and corresponding classification/access/consent handling burden more implied than stated.
- Why it matters: readers should be able to see from the article itself that relabeling, proxy methods, or behavioral analysis do not create a lower-protection pathway around internal-state safeguards.
- Smallest close: add `Necessity` and `Proportionality` to the trace block and revise the operative paragraph so any permitted handling must satisfy the same constitutional authorities and corresponding classification, access, consent, and handling restrictions.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article VII does not need expansion into a longer privacy or autonomy code. It needs a bit more constitutional surface area so embodiment, mental interiority, focus protection, and anti-circumvention handling remain visibly tied to the Chapter Five concepts already controlling them.

Most important close points:
- expose `Self-Determination` and `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing` in `Article VII-A`;
- tighten the body, mind, and focus bullets so refusal, anti-inference, and anti-manipulation protections are visible on the page;
- make `Article VII-B` explicit that functional approximation triggers the same constitutional authority and Type N handling constraints rather than a weaker substitute pathway.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the `Article VII-A` trace block to include `Self-Determination` and `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing`;
2. revise the `Article VII-A` body, mind, and focus bullets to name refusal, anti-inference, and anti-manipulation protections more directly;
3. revise `Article VII-B` trace and operative text so anti-circumvention and the same-authority handling rule are explicit at publication level.
