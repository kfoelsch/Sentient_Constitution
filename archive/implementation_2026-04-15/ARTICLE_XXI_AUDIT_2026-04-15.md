# Article XXI audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XXI: Constitutional Interpretation, Review, and Anti-Capture Safeguards`.

## Overall assessment

Article XXI has the right constitutional objective. It protects final interpretive authority only as a bounded, reviewable, anti-capture function rather than as a free-standing sovereignty claim. Its main weakness was surface clarity: too much of the anti-self-authorization, anti-entrenchment, and practical review logic had to be inferred from Chapter One and the institutions companion instead of appearing directly in the article.

The main issues were visibility and anti-evasion clarity rather than doctrinal error:
- the article lead correctly required interpretive authority to remain authoritative, bounded, auditable, and contestable, but it did not show clearly enough that final interpretation must remain a reason-giving constitutional function rather than a vehicle for self-expanding jurisdiction;
- `Article XXI-A` bounded the mandate, yet it left too implicit that the review body may not conclusively enlarge its own jurisdiction, suspend challenge pathways, or treat implementation detail as interpretive necessity without constitutional grounding;
- `Article XXI-B` correctly required impartiality and anti-capture structure, but it underexposed disclosure, recusal, and interim-vacancy continuity as practical safeguards against hidden control;
- `Article XXI-C` already required public reasons and external review, but it did not make timing, usable access, and correction or reversal pathways visible enough on the page;
- `Article XXI-D` allowed removal for cause and periodic revalidation, but it did not expressly guard against using removal or redesign as a pretext for retaliatory capture or rights-floor narrowing.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XXI (chapeau)

- Operative claim: final constitutional interpretation must remain authoritative only within bounded constitutional limits and through stated reasons.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, `8. Prohibition on Absolute Override`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Constitutional Review Body`, `Authority Stack`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: the lead should make clear that interpretive authority derives from the constitution and its reason-giving discipline, not from institutional self-assertion or operational convenience.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The prior lead stated the right values, but it did not surface strongly enough that final interpretation cannot bootstrap itself into broader power through necessity language, structural convenience, or claims of exclusive expertise.
- Why it matters: interpretive bodies are common sites of mandate drift because constitutional authority can be recast as a generic power to define the scope of everything else.
- Smallest close: add language tying final interpretation to the constitutional text, authority stack, and public reasons and stating that it cannot expand by self-assertion.

### Article XXI-A

- Operative claim: the review body may resolve constitutional meaning on bounded topics, but it may not convert interpretation into open-ended governance control.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4`, `8`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Constitutional Review Body`, `Authority Stack`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: bounded mandate language should explicitly prevent jurisdiction drift, challenge-right suspension, and displacement of designated implementation owners except where constitutional review is actually required.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Boundary compression] The subsection already pointed to constitutional scope, rights-floor compatibility, and conflict resolution, but it left the anti-self-expansion implication too implicit for publication reading.
- Why it matters: bodies that can conclusively define their own jurisdiction or absorb implementation control can defeat contestability while remaining formally inside an interpretive label.
- Smallest close: add a sentence barring self-enlargement of jurisdiction, suspension of challenge pathways, and displacement of implementation roles without constitutional basis.

### Article XXI-B

- Operative claim: anti-capture design must prevent not only overt bloc control but also hidden conflicts and continuity failures that let actors shape outcomes indirectly.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4`, `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Procedural Fairness`, `System Capture`, and `Constitutional Review Body`.
- Mechanics: practical anti-capture structure includes ongoing conflict disclosure, recusal where material conflicts arise, and continuity rules that prevent recusals or vacancies from becoming a route to selective paralysis or takeover.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Safeguard underexposure] The current text required composition and conflict safeguards, but it did not make disclosure, recusal, and continuity controls visible enough on the page.
- Why it matters: capture often operates through hidden affiliations, selective participation, or vacancy manipulation rather than formal appointment rules alone.
- Smallest close: require ongoing disclosure and recusal and note that continuity mechanisms must not enable selective paralysis or capture.

### Article XXI-C

- Operative claim: challenge and external review rights must be timely and usable enough to permit real correction, not merely archival transparency.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.4`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Auditability`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: public reasons should be available in time to matter, understandable enough for affected parties and reviewers, and paired with practical correction or reversal hooks where material error, capture, or rights-floor breach is shown.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Practical review gap] The subsection already named public reasons and secondary review, but it did not show clearly enough that publication timing and correction pathways are part of the constitutional protection.
- Why it matters: review rights lose force when reasons arrive too late, in unusable form, or without an identified path to suspend, correct, or reverse tainted outcomes.
- Smallest close: add timing and accessibility language to the publication duty and add correction or reversal language to the review duty.

### Article XXI-D

- Operative claim: removal for cause and institutional redesign must protect integrity without becoming a pretext for purges, retaliation, or entrenchment by reconstitution.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4`, `7.2`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Procedural Fairness`, and `System Capture`.
- Mechanics: anti-capture rules should apply to removal and redesign themselves so those powers cannot be used to disable pending review, retaliate against principled dissent, or narrow the independence of the body.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Anti-pretext undernaming] The subsection correctly allowed removal for cause and periodic revalidation, but it did not expressly bar pretextual restructuring or retaliatory removal practices.
- Why it matters: institutions are often captured through justified-sounding redesign or personnel cleansing rather than through static entrenchment.
- Smallest close: add a sentence stating that removal and redesign cannot be used to defeat independence, pending review, or challenge rights.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XXI does not need a larger institutional blueprint. It needs a sharper constitutional surface so readers can see that interpretive authority is bounded by the constitution rather than self-defining, that anti-capture safeguards include practical conflict controls and review usability, and that redesign powers cannot be used as a covert route to capture.

Most important close points:
- make the lead explicitly anti-self-authorizing;
- make `Article XXI-A` visibly resistant to jurisdiction drift and challenge-right suspension;
- make `Article XXI-B` disclose/recusal/continuity aware;
- make `Article XXI-C` timely and correction-oriented;
- make `Article XXI-D` expressly anti-pretext.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. tighten the chapeau so interpretive authority is anchored to the constitutional text, authority stack, and public reasons rather than self-asserted necessity;
2. revise `Article XXI-A` so jurisdiction drift, challenge-right suspension, and displacement of implementation roles are expressly barred without constitutional basis;
3. revise `Article XXI-B` so disclosure, recusal, and vacancy or continuity safeguards are visible on the page;
4. revise `Article XXI-C` so reasons are timely and usable and review includes correction or reversal pathways;
5. revise `Article XXI-D` so removal and redesign cannot be used as retaliatory or capture-enabling tools.
