# Article XXIII audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XXIII: Constitutional Evolution and Non-Entrenchment`.

## Overall assessment

Article XXIII has the right constitutional objective. It keeps governance arrangements revisable and denies entrenchment by inertia, convenience, or tradition alone. Its main weakness was surface discipline: too much of the anti-evasion, trigger-based revalidation, and formal change-validity logic still had to be inferred from Chapters Eleven through Thirteen and adjacent anti-capture provisions rather than appearing directly in the article.

The main issues were visibility and anti-lock-in clarity rather than doctrinal misdirection:
- the article lead correctly rejected entrenchment by habit and precedent, but it did not show clearly enough that ongoing justification should track evidence of constitutional fit rather than persistence alone;
- `Article XXIII-A` correctly required periodic revalidation and stakeholder-visible continuation criteria, yet it left too implicit that revalidation should accelerate when evidence turns, especially where capture, exclusion, challenge-right degradation, or material under-performance appears;
- `Article XXIII-B` correctly required observable, auditable, contestable change, but it did not make public-reasons discipline, non-regression compatibility, and anti-evasion against emergency or implementation rerouting visible enough on the page.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XXIII (chapeau)

- Operative claim: governance rules must remain open to revision because constitutional legitimacy depends on continuing justification rather than legacy status.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, `8. Prohibition on Absolute Override`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Governance`, `Accountability`, and `Transparency`.
- Mechanics: the article lead should communicate that revisability is meaningful only if governance structures remain answerable to observable evidence, constitutional fit, and updated evaluation rather than ceremonial openness.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The chapeau already rejects entrenchment by inertia, convenience, and precedent, but it leaves the evidentiary side of continuing justification more implicit than ideal.
- Why it matters: systems can remain formally revisable while becoming practically entrenched if the constitution does not visibly tie continuation to renewed constitutional fit.
- Smallest close: reinforce in downstream text that revalidation must respond to evidence and rights-relevant performance, not just elapsed time.

### Article XXIII-A

- Operative claim: periodic review is constitutionally insufficient if it waits passively while evidence of capture or exclusion accumulates.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, `7.1`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Stakeholder`, `Accountability`, and `Procedural Fairness`.
- Mechanics: periodic review should visibly include an early-trigger discipline for capture pressure, stakeholder exclusion, challenge-right degradation, and material under-performance.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Trigger underexposure] The subsection already required periodic revalidation and visible continuation criteria, but it did not say clearly enough that adverse evidence accelerates the duty to revalidate.
- Why it matters: periodic review language is often satisfied formally while harmful arrangements persist between cycles.
- Smallest close: add a sentence requiring accelerated revalidation when adverse evidence is materially shown.

### Article XXIII-B

- Operative claim: constitutional evolution must remain transparent and contestable, and it must not smuggle substantive change around non-regression or formal validity controls.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.4`, `8`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Transparency`, `Contestability`, and `Auditability`.
- Mechanics: material changes should carry public reasons, expected effects, and compatibility with the amendment-validity chapters, and they must not be rerouted through emergency, implementation, or custody mechanisms to avoid those controls.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Evasion gap] The subsection already required auditable and contestable change, but it left too much of the anti-evasion link to Chapters Eleven through Thirteen implicit and it contained a duplicated article citation.
- Why it matters: constitutional drift often occurs through procedural rerouting rather than overt claims of amendment.
- Smallest close: add public-reasons and change-validity compatibility language, expressly bar alternate-route evasion, and correct the duplicated citation.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XXIII does not need expansion into a full amendment chapter. It needs a sharper constitutional surface so readers can see that revisability is evidence-sensitive, that revalidation is not just calendar-based, and that formal change-validity constraints cannot be evaded by routing substantive change through other mechanisms.

Most important close points:
- make revalidation visibly responsive to capture, exclusion, challenge-right degradation, and under-performance;
- make material change visibly reason-giving and Chapters Eleven-Thirteen aware;
- make anti-evasion explicit, especially against emergency, implementation, and custody rerouting;
- correct the duplicated article citation in `Article XXIII-B`.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise `Article XXIII-A` so periodic review accelerates when adverse evidence is materially shown;
2. revise `Article XXIII-B` so material changes carry public reasons, expected effects, and compatibility with **Chapters Eleven through Thirteen** where applicable;
3. revise `Article XXIII-B` so substantive change cannot evade constitutional change-validity rules through emergency, implementation, or custody routing;
4. correct the duplicated article citation in `Article XXIII-B`.
