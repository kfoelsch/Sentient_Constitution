# Article XIV audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XIV: Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification`.

## Overall assessment

Article XIV has the right constitutional objective and is already doctrinally coherent with Chapters Two through Four and the controlling Chapter Five definitions. Its main weakness was compression: too much of the operative rule had to be inferred from linked mechanics rather than read directly from the article text.

The main issues were publication-shape and visibility issues rather than doctrinal error:
- the chapeau correctly established a proportional audit and verification right, but it left transparency, reconstructability, and contestable review more implicit than ideal;
- `Article XIV-A` had the right evidentiary direction, but it did not state reconstruction strongly enough for the full meaning of `Auditability`;
- `Article XIV-B` preserved plural oversight and anti-monopoly logic, yet it did not make clear enough that oversight must be able to influence review and correction in practice rather than merely exist;
- `Article XIV-C` stated practical achievability, but it was still a little too abstract about gatekeeping and barriers that defeat meaningful challenge and verification.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XIV (chapeau)

- Operative claim: Article XIV establishes a proportional right to audit, transparency, and independent verification for systems whose impact, dependency, and risk warrant them.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Auditability`, `Transparency`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: the chapeau should signal that the protected object is not merely nominal audit permission, but practical reconstructability and contestable review under the burden, traceability, and observability rules of Chapters Two through Four.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The lead was directionally correct, but it left transparency and reconstructable contestability more implicit than ideal for publication reading.
- Why it matters: Article XIV is the constitutional bridge between rights text and proof structure, so readers should be able to see immediately that audit rights include usable access to understanding and verification, not just the existence of an audit label.
- Smallest close: add transparency and a short clause on reconstructability and contestable review to the chapeau.

### Article XIV-A

- Operative claim: systems must maintain evidence and disclosure pathways that permit independent evaluation of constitutional alignment.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Auditability`, `Accountability`, and `Transparency`.
- Mechanics: `Auditability` requires systematic examination, reconstruction, and evaluation through observability and verifiability, so the subsection should show more than generic record retention.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The prior wording covered records, disclosures, and tracing, but it did not surface reconstruction strongly enough to match the controlling definition of `Auditability`.
- Why it matters: without explicit reconstructability, readers can underread the provision as a recordkeeping rule rather than as a practical requirement for examining and rebuilding the path of system behavior.
- Smallest close: add reconstruction pathways to the list of required evidentiary supports.

### Article XIV-B

- Operative claim: oversight must remain plural and anti-capture, with no single actor able to dominate constitutional review in practice.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Oversight`, `Contestability`, and `System Capture`.
- Mechanics: owner-layer oversight design may vary, but the constitutional floor requires meaningful influence, distributed review where feasible, and resistance to performative or monopolized oversight.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The subsection correctly rejected monopoly control, but it did not make clear enough that plural oversight must be able to contribute materially to review and correction rather than existing as symbolic participation only.
- Why it matters: anti-capture doctrine fails in practice when nominally independent reviewers exist but cannot affect findings, access, or remediation.
- Smallest close: specify that oversight pathways must materially contribute to detection, review, and correction, and bar monopoly over effective oversight and audit access in practice.

### Article XIV-C

- Operative claim: verification must be feasible in practice and not defeated by unjustified barriers.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Freedom`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Auditability`, `Contestability`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: `Verification Accessibility` and `Verification Feasibility` require that verification be usable under actual access conditions, not merely theoretical or formally available.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] The subsection already prohibited cost, delay, opacity, and structural barriers, but it still left gatekeeping and challenge-defeating review barriers somewhat implicit.
- Why it matters: in practice, verification often fails not through express denial but through access throttling, qualification bottlenecks, or process obstacles that make review nominal only.
- Smallest close: add affected and appropriately authorized parties to the sentence and name gatekeeping and challenge-defeating barriers expressly.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XIV does not need doctrinal expansion. It needs a clearer surface so readers can see that auditability means reconstructable evidence, that oversight must be functionally plural and anti-capture, and that verification rights must remain practically usable rather than formally available.

Most important close points:
- make the chapeau show transparency and contestable reconstructability more directly;
- make `Article XIV-A` reflect the reconstruction element of `Auditability`;
- make `Article XIV-B` state that oversight must be able to influence review and correction in practice;
- make `Article XIV-C` identify gatekeeping and other barriers that defeat meaningful verification.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. tighten the chapeau to name transparency, reconstructability, and contestable review;
2. add reconstruction pathways to `Article XIV-A`;
3. clarify in `Article XIV-B` that plural oversight must materially contribute and cannot be monopolized in practice;
4. tighten `Article XIV-C` so practical verification barriers are easier to recognize on the page.
