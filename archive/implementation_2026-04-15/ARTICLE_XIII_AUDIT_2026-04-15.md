# Article XIII audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XIII: Info-Sphere Integrity`.

## Overall assessment

Article XIII has the correct constitutional center of gravity. It properly treats epistemic integrity as both a right and a constraint, and `Article XIII-A` already carries much of the needed anti-monopoly, plurality, and high-impact interpretation logic.

The main issues were uneven drafting maturity and surface-level underexposure rather than doctrinal error:
- the chapeau had the right idea, but it could state contestability and coordination functions more directly;
- `Article XIII-A` was substantively strong already, with only minor publication-shape tightening needed elsewhere around it;
- `Article XIII-B` still read like older aspirational language and did not put practical transparency, provenance, auditability, and challenge rights on the page as clearly as the linked definitions require;
- `Article XIII-C` was materially underdeveloped relative to its title, because it contained only a footprint-reporting rule and did not visibly state correction, anti-selective-reporting, or stewardship duties that follow from Truth and Epistemic Integrity;
- Chapter Five still contained a stale cross-reference to `Article XIII-A` for footprint reporting even though the operative provision sits in `Article XIII-C`.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XIII (chapeau)

- Operative claim: epistemic integrity governs not only speech accuracy in the abstract, but the broader conditions for contestable, reality-tracking coordination.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `4. Trust`, and `6.2.1 Preservation of Epistemic Integrity`.
- Definition anchors: `Truth (Constitutional Constraint)`, `Epistemic Integrity`, and `Trust`.
- Mechanics: the chapeau should signal that Article XIII is about decision-usable shared reality, not merely correct isolated statements.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `3` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The lead sentence was sound, but it left contestability and coordination more implicit than ideal for publication.
- Why it matters: Article XIII is one of the Constitution's main interpretive anchors for distorted information environments, so the page should show immediately that the protected object is a usable and contestable information sphere.
- Smallest close: add contestability and coordination to the chapeau without expanding the article into a theory section.

### Article XIII-B

- Operative claim: materially relied-on interpretation and validation must remain transparent, independently reviewable, and challengeable in practice.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Transparency`, `Auditability`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: the subsection should visibly require documented methods, usable provenance, practical verification, and challenge pathways for materially misleading or unsupported outputs.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `1` / Logic `1` / Constraints `1` / Publish `1`
- Finding: [Major] [Underreach] The prior text sounded directionally admirable, but its "encouragement" and "we catalog" phrasing did not read as a constitutional floor and did not surface the core tests around scope, limits, uncertainty, independent validation, and correction access.
- Why it matters: under full definitional reading, transparency is decision-usable disclosure and contestability is functional challenge and correction, not merely the abstract availability of multiple viewpoints.
- Smallest close: rewrite the subsection as operative duties covering interpretive diversity, transparency and provenance, and practical auditability-plus-contestability.

### Article XIII-C

- Operative claim: epistemic stewardship requires correction of material error, non-selective reporting, and truthful footprint disclosure that is usable for comparison and reduction.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Ecological Footprint`, `Transparency`, and `Truth (Constitutional Constraint)`.
- Mechanics: the subsection should show that stewardship includes anti-suppression and anti-fragmentation duties, while footprint reporting must remain transparent, comparable, and auditable across materially relevant systems.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `1` / Logic `1` / Constraints `1` / Publish `1`
- Finding: [Major] [Underreach] The subsection title promised validation, reporting, and stewardship, but the body contained only a short footprint-reporting rule and omitted correction, provenance preservation, and anti-selective-reporting duties.
- Why it matters: leaving stewardship invisible invites readers to treat Article XIII as mostly an anti-monopoly article plus an environmental data note, rather than a broader constitutional protection for the integrity of public-facing information handling.
- Smallest close: add a short stewardship bullet and tighten the footprint bullet so it clearly requires decision-usable reporting rather than bare disclosure.

### Definition cross-reference

- Operative claim: the Ecological Footprint definition should point to the actual footprint-reporting subsection that implements it.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Ecological Footprint`; `Transparency`.
- Mechanics: trace integrity requires article references to match the operative text location so downstream readers and tools do not follow stale pointers.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] Chapter Five still referred to `Article XIII-A` footprint-data provisions even though the actual reporting clause is in `Article XIII-C`.
- Why it matters: stale trace hooks weaken the audit chain and make future refactors harder to validate.
- Smallest close: retarget the Chapter Five cross-reference from `Article XIII-A` to `Article XIII-C`.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XIII does not need expansion into a broad media-governance code. It needs a clearer constitutional surface so readers can see that plurality, provenance, practical validation, correction, and non-selective reporting are all parts of the same epistemic-integrity right.

Most important close points:
- make `Article XIII-B` read as an operative transparency, validation, and contestability floor rather than as aspirational guidance;
- make `Article XIII-C` match its title by stating stewardship and correction duties directly;
- repair the stale Chapter Five footprint reference so the trace chain remains coherent.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. tighten the chapeau so contestability and coordination are visible up front;
2. rewrite `Article XIII-B` around interpretive diversity, transparency and provenance, and practical challenge rights;
3. add a stewardship-and-correction bullet to `Article XIII-C`;
4. tighten footprint reporting so it is decision-usable, comparable, and auditable;
5. fix the `Ecological Footprint` cross-reference from `Article XIII-A` to `Article XIII-C`.
