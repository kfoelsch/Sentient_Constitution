# Article XVIII audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XVIII: Interoperability, Portability, and Exit Integrity`.

## Overall assessment

Article XVIII has the right constitutional objective. It protects meaningful exit, usable portability, and proportionate interoperability while leaving room for justified security and safety limits. The main weakness was publication visibility: too much of the article's anti-formalism and anti-lock-in logic had to be inferred from Chapter Five and incorporated implementation instead of being legible on the page.

The main issues were practical-exit clarity and anti-evasion visibility rather than doctrinal error:
- the article lead correctly named exit, migration, and interoperability, but it did not show clearly enough that the protected right is usable in practice rather than merely nominal on paper;
- `Article XVIII-A` recognized portability, but it left timeliness, documentation, and anti-degradation limits too implicit, creating room for formal export rights that fail in real transition conditions;
- `Article XVIII-B` pointed to reciprocal interoperability, yet it did not surface enough notice discipline around material interface or access-term narrowing for dependent parties;
- `Article XVIII-C` correctly barred lock-in barriers, but it named the failure mode too generically and left common practical barriers such as format opacity, punitive switching terms, and withheld transition information underexposed.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XVIII (chapeau)

- Operative claim: Article XVIII protects the real ability to exit, migrate, and interoperate where systems create dependency or lock-in risk.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Meaningful Agency`, `Feasibility`, and `Systemic Lock-In`.
- Mechanics: the lead sentence should make clear that the constitutional concern is practical transition integrity, not nominal rights that disappear under real-world friction or concealed dependency.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The lead had the right structure, but it left too much room to read exit or interoperability as formal entitlements satisfied by paper availability alone.
- Why it matters: lock-in often persists through technically possible but functionally unusable transitions.
- Smallest close: add a sentence stating that the right must be usable in practice and does not require unsafe or unjustified exposure.

### Article XVIII-A

- Operative claim: portability rights protect practical movement of relevant assets and operational continuity where systems hold dependency-critical data, identity, or state.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5`, `6.1`, and `7.1`.
- Definition anchors: `Meaningful Agency`, `Systemic Lock-In`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: portability is not meaningful if export rights are delayed, undocumented, degraded, or paired with retaliatory transition conditions.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Practicality gap] The subsection named usable portability, but it did not make timeliness, documentation, and anti-degradation discipline visible enough for publication reading.
- Why it matters: many systems preserve lock-in by offering nominal portability while degrading fidelity, delaying transfer, or using format choices that defeat substitution.
- Smallest close: require documentation, practical timing, and a bar on defeating portability through opacity, deliberate quality loss, or retaliatory terms beyond justified constraints.

### Article XVIII-B

- Operative claim: materially integrating systems owe reciprocal and documented interoperability boundaries proportionate to actual dependency.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5`, `6.1`, and `7.1`.
- Definition anchors: `Dependency`, `Feasibility`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: interoperability discipline includes notice when a system materially narrows interfaces or access conditions in ways that affect dependent parties' ability to adapt or exit.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Notice underexposure] The subsection already required reciprocity and documentation, but it did not name the timing discipline needed when interoperability conditions materially narrow.
- Why it matters: dependent systems can be trapped in practice if interface changes or access-term degradations arrive too late for migration, challenge, or safe adaptation.
- Smallest close: add a sentence requiring disclosure in time for materially dependent parties to adapt, migrate, or challenge.

### Article XVIII-C

- Operative claim: anti-lock-in protections bar artificial barriers that functionally foreclose exit or challenge rights.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Systemic Lock-In`, `Dependency`, and `Meaningful Agency`.
- Mechanics: anti-lock-in language should identify common indirect foreclosure methods so the article cannot be underread as reaching only explicit refusal or overt contractual prohibition.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Evasion undernaming] The current wording barred artificial barriers, but it did not make the main practical lock-in pathways visible enough on the page.
- Why it matters: lock-in commonly operates through technical opacity, unjustified incompatibility, switching penalties, or withheld transition information rather than blunt bans on exit.
- Smallest close: name those practical barriers expressly while preserving the proportionality and Chapter Five hook already in place.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XVIII does not need expansion into a technical standards annex. It needs a slightly sharper constitutional surface so readers can see that portability and interoperability must be usable in practice, and that anti-lock-in protections reach disguised technical or contractual foreclosure methods rather than only obvious barriers.

Most important close points:
- make the lead explicitly anti-formalist;
- make portability visibly timely, documented, and anti-degradation;
- make interoperability narrowing subject to usable notice;
- make anti-lock-in language name common practical barriers.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. tighten the article lead so the protected right is practical transition integrity rather than paper availability alone;
2. revise `Article XVIII-A` so documentation, timing, and anti-degradation limits are express;
3. revise `Article XVIII-B` so material narrowing of interoperability conditions requires usable notice;
4. revise `Article XVIII-C` so common indirect lock-in barriers are named directly.
