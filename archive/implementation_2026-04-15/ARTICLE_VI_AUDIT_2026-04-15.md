# Article VI audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article VI: Right to Sentient-Centered Education`.

## Overall assessment

Article VI is already conceptually strong. It correctly treats education as a capability right rather than a credentialing preference, links learning to agency and participation, and preserves a needed bridge between baseline educational access in `Article III` and later governance responsibilities.

The main issues are publication-shape issues rather than doctrinal reversal:
- the chapeau still uses a local `listed grounds` phrase that under-names the Chapter Five protected-characteristics framework already meant to govern educational equality;
- the capability-building subsection is directionally correct but slightly too compressed about ongoing access, exercise of capability, and the self-determination and dignity functions that the controlling definition already names;
- the adaptive-learning subsection correctly resists obsolescence and lock-in, but it could more clearly require practical rather than merely formal continuing-learning pathways;
- the learning-systems sentence is close, but it leaves proxy-driven educational sorting and intelligible review mechanics a bit too implied.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article VI chapeau

- Operative claim: Article VI protects education as a capability right while routing baseline equal-access and equality constraints through `Article III` and related rights.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing` and `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`.
- Definition anchors: `Educational Agency`, `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Protected Characteristics`.
- Mechanics: cross-references may distribute doctrinal work across the chapter, but shorthand phrasing should not substitute for the canonical Chapter Five equality framework.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `3` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The chapeau's phrase `listed grounds` points toward equality protection, but it under-names the more canonical `Protected Characteristics` and substantive-fairness structure that Article III and Article V are meant to carry.
- Why it matters: leaving the local phrase in place risks preserving an older, list-like reading of educational equality even though the surrounding corpus is moving toward direct Chapter Five anchoring.
- Smallest close: replace `listed grounds` with `protected-characteristics constraints` or equivalent direct constitutional wording.

### Article VI-A

- Operative claim: every sentient must have education sufficient to build the core capabilities needed for agency, participation, and safe system use.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Educational Agency`, `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Self-Determination`, `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing`, plus `Wellbeing`.
- Mechanics: the governing definition is not only about access in the abstract; it requires practical, ongoing learning sufficient to develop and exercise capability and rejects symbolic or merely formal educational inclusion.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The current sentence is directionally correct, but it compresses away two parts of the controlling definition: that access should be `practical, ongoing`, and that education must support not only development of capacity but its exercise in self-determination and dignified participation.
- Why it matters: without those cues, the subsection can be read as satisfied by nominal availability of training or by a curriculum statement untethered from real capability-building.
- Smallest close: add `Self-Determination` and `Dignity and Equal Moral Standing` to the trace block and revise the operative sentence to require practical, ongoing access sufficient to develop and exercise core capabilities.

### Article VI-B lifelong and adaptive learning

- Operative claim: where competencies shift because systems evolve, sentients must have real opportunities to keep up, retrain, and transition rather than being trapped in obsolescence.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, with functionally relevant pressure from `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors` and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Educational Agency`, `Meaningful Agency`, and functionally `Systemic Lock-In` plus `Feasibility`.
- Mechanics: adaptive-learning guarantees should work over time and in practice, not merely as formal offerings that exist on paper while dependency, cost, or structure make them unusable.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint drop] The sentence correctly names retraining and transition support, but it does not quite say that the opportunities must be practical and reasonably usable rather than nominal.
- Why it matters: absent that clarification, institutions could claim compliance through thin or inaccessible retraining pathways while still allowing imposed obsolescence and lock-in to nullify agency.
- Smallest close: revise the sentence so continuing-learning opportunities are expressly practical and reasonably accessible, while retaining the current anti-obsolescence and stewardship language.

### Article VI-B learning systems

- Operative claim: educational ranking, assessment, and recommendation systems must remain transparent and challengeable rather than insulated, manipulative gatekeepers.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Educational Agency`, `Contestability`, and functionally `Transparency`, `Auditability`, `Coercion and Manipulation`, plus `Epistemic Integrity`.
- Mechanics: where educational systems sort, score, or recommend in materially impactful ways, compliance depends on observability, intelligible challenge pathways, and resistance to opaque proxy-driven gating.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Proxy drift] The current sentence already requires transparency, auditability, and contestability, but it leaves opaque or unreviewable proxy sorting too implied.
- Why it matters: educational opportunity can be materially shaped by inferred scores, behavioral proxies, or recommendation logic that looks neutral while functioning as unchallengeable gatekeeping.
- Smallest close: revise the sentence to state that materially impactful educational systems must not rely on opaque or unreviewable proxies, in addition to existing anti-coercion and anti-manipulation limits.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article VI does not need expansion into an education code. It needs a little more constitutional surface area so capability-building, adaptive learning, and educational-system reviewability remain visibly tied to the Chapter Five concepts already controlling them.

Most important close points:
- replace the chapeau's local `listed grounds` phrasing with direct constitutional equality language;
- make `Article VI-A` visibly about practical, ongoing capability-building for self-determination and dignified participation;
- make `Article VI-B` resistant to formal-only retraining pathways and opaque proxy-driven educational sorting.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the `Article VI` chapeau to replace `listed grounds` with direct protected-characteristics language;
2. revise `Article VI-A` trace and operative text to expose self-determination and dignity functions together with practical, ongoing access;
3. revise `Article VI-B` so lifelong-learning pathways must be practical and reasonably accessible;
4. revise the learning-systems sentence to bar opaque or unreviewable proxy sorting in materially impactful educational systems.
