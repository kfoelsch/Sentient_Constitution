# Article III audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article III: Survival and Equal Educational Access`.

## Overall assessment

Article III is already close to publication shape. It states a recognizable survival floor, preserves the link between baseline access and meaningful participation, and correctly routes deeper education-capability detail into `Article VI` and institutional implementation.

The main issues are not doctrinal reversal. They are:
- one survival subsection whose trace and operative labels under-name the agency and info-sphere concepts already doing work;
- one institutional-guarantees sentence that could be read too permissively as to eligibility, jurisdiction, or residence gating;
- one educational-access subsection that substitutes a local list for the governing Chapter Five protected-characteristics framework and leaves the necessity/proportionality gate implicit.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article III-A

- Operative claim: sentients must retain access to essential survival inputs, stable operating conditions, and participation-enabling connectivity.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `3.1 Safety`, and `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`.
- Definition anchors: `Wellbeing`, `Dependency`, and functionally `Meaningful Agency` plus `Info-Sphere`.
- Mechanics: the subsection is strongest when read systemically across supporting environments and dependencies rather than as a narrow shelter-only rule.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The subsection's trace block names `Wellbeing`, `Dependency`, and `Material Impact`, but the operative text also depends on `Meaningful Agency` and `Info-Sphere`. In addition, the label `Safe Housing` with a universal `domicile` framing is slightly narrower and more localized than the upstream wellbeing/dependency logic, which is about stable shelter or operating environment across substrates.
- Why it matters: the current text is understandable, but it invites an unnecessarily housing-centric reading for digital or hybrid sentients and makes the connectivity sentence look less anchored than it really is.
- Smallest close: add `Meaningful Agency` and `Info-Sphere` to the trace block and revise the shelter sentence so it speaks in terms of stable shelter or operating environment sufficient for continued existence and baseline wellbeing.

### Article III-A institutional guarantees

- Operative claim: adopting orders may use fiscal and allocation mechanisms to secure survival floors, but implementation criteria may not defeat the floor itself.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2`, `5`, and functionally `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Wellbeing`, `Dependency`, and functionally `Protected Characteristics` plus `Substantive Fairness`.
- Mechanics: implementation detail is rightly routed to institutions text, but constitutional floors must remain non-defeasible by local program structure.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] The sentence allowing indexing, eligibility, and jurisdiction or residence rules is structurally sensible, but it does not say that those rules may organize delivery only and may not be used to defeat minimum access.
- Why it matters: without that qualifier, a reader could over-read the sentence as permitting exclusionary gatekeeping that would conflict with the article's own survival floor and with equality protections in `Article V`.
- Smallest close: add a short non-defeat clause making clear that such rules cannot be used to deny minimum access to survival essentials or to impose invidious exclusion contrary to Articles III and V.

### Article III-B

- Operative claim: educational access and advancement must remain equally available, accessible, and substantively participatory.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `3.2 Truth`, and `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, with functionally relevant collision review under `6.4`.
- Definition anchors: `Educational Agency`, `Protected Characteristics`, `Substantive Fairness`, and functionally `Necessity` plus `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: the upstream definition requires substantive access, accessibility accommodation, continuity, and protection against structural gating, not just formal non-denial.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The subsection uses a local list of `listed grounds` rather than simply anchoring to `Protected Characteristics (Constitutional)` and related fairness definitions. It also leaves the `Necessity` and `Proportionality` gate implicit even though Chapter Five makes those justification standards operative.
- Why it matters: the local list is directionally correct, but it risks being read as exhaustive or as a substitute test. The current wording also understates that structural gating and material limitations require affirmative constitutional justification.
- Smallest close: replace the local list with direct Chapter Five anchoring, add `Necessity` and `Proportionality` to the trace block, and state that material limitations or differential treatment must satisfy those standards.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article III is doctrinally sound. The value of the audit is mostly in exposing where compact drafting started to rely on shared background knowledge instead of naming the controlling concepts directly.

Most important close points:
- expose the agency and info-sphere anchors already doing work in `Article III-A`;
- clarify that implementation criteria may structure delivery but may not defeat survival floors;
- replace the local educational-access grounds list with direct Chapter Five anchoring plus explicit necessity/proportionality language.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise `Article III-A` trace and shelter language to use stable shelter or operating-environment wording and expose `Meaningful Agency` plus `Info-Sphere`;
2. revise the `Article III-A` institutional-guarantees sentence to add a non-defeat clause for eligibility, jurisdiction, and residence rules;
3. revise `Article III-B` so equal educational access is governed directly by `Protected Characteristics`, `Substantive Fairness`, `Necessity`, and `Proportionality` rather than by a local replacement list.
