# Article IV audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article IV: Resource Allocation, Dependencies, and Ecosystem Funding`.

## Overall assessment

Article IV is doctrinally sound and already points at the right constitutional cluster: systemic evaluation, incentive alignment, dependency visibility, and transparent funding stewardship.

The main issue is compression. The current text is so compact that some of its load-bearing safeguards remain implied by annexes and Chapter Five rather than stated in the article itself. In particular, the published text under-names auditability and contestability, leaves the material shape of dependency mapping slightly too thin, and states cross-system fairness without clearly naming substitutability, alternative providers, or anti-extraction logic.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article IV chapeau

- Operative claim: resource flows across interdependent systems must remain visible enough for constitutional review.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `4. Trust`, `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Dependency`, `Transparency`, and functionally `Auditability` plus `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: the chapeau does meaningful work only if reviewability survives and cannot be displaced by opaque local accounting.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint drop] The phrase `subject to challenge` points in the right direction, but it leaves the article's observability and review mechanics under-specified. Chapter Five `Transparency` requires decision-relevant clarity, and the surrounding constitutional structure expects auditable and contestable claims, not only abstract challenge rights.
- Why it matters: without explicit auditability/contestability language, a reader could treat Article IV as an aspiration toward openness rather than as a review-ready governance requirement.
- Smallest close: revise the chapeau so resource flows must remain `transparent, ecosystem-aware, auditable, and contestable`.

### Article IV-A

- Operative claim: systems must keep dependency and resource-flow maps adequate for ecosystem governance and audit.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2`, `7.1`, and `7.2`.
- Definition anchors: `Dependency`, `Transparency`, `System Boundary Integrity`, and `Materiality Determination`.
- Mechanics: the subsection should resist fragmentation, omission of indirect links, and scope gaming through incomplete system boundaries.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The current sentence correctly requires documented upstream and downstream dependencies, but it does not say enough about auditability of the representation itself or about non-transparent and asymmetric relationships that Chapter Five `Dependency` expressly treats as evaluatively relevant.
- Why it matters: a narrow reading could satisfy the subsection with a partial map that lists formal interfaces while omitting the hidden or asymmetric relationships most relevant to risk, fragility, and unfair extraction.
- Smallest close: require `documented, auditable representations` and specify inclusion of `non-transparent or asymmetric relationships where materially relevant`, while keeping update cadence and Article XIV-A review.

### Article IV-B

- Operative claim: allocation must reflect ecosystem fairness and cannot permit materially destabilizing imbalance.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Dependency`, `Substantive Fairness`, and `Materiality Determination`.
- Mechanics: evaluation must stay systemic, weigh alternatives and substitutability, and preserve review pathways when imbalance becomes materially threatening.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] `criticality, dependency asymmetry, and long-term sustainability` is directionally correct but too compressed. The annex logic also turns on substitutability, alternative providers, and the problem of extraction from shared infrastructure without corresponding support. The current review sentence also under-names the audit and standing pathways that make the subsection enforceable.
- Why it matters: without those clarifiers, the text could be read as endorsing a vague balancing exercise instead of a concrete anti-fragility and anti-extraction obligation.
- Smallest close: add `substitutability` and `availability of alternatives`, state that systems must avoid persistent extraction from shared infrastructure or foundational dependencies without corresponding support, and route material imbalance through `Articles IX, XII, XIV, and XVII`.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article IV does not need expansion into annex-length detail. It needs a small amount of constitutional surface area added back into the article so readers can see, on the page, the safeguards that already govern it upstream.

Most important close points:
- make reviewability explicit through auditability and contestability language;
- make dependency mapping broad enough to capture hidden and asymmetric relationships where material;
- make cross-system fairness concrete enough to reach extraction, substitutability, and corrective review.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the Article IV chapeau to add `auditable` and `contestable`;
2. revise `Article IV-A` to require auditable dependency maps that include non-transparent or asymmetric relationships where material;
3. revise `Article IV-B` to add substitutability, alternative-provider context, anti-extraction language, and explicit audit/challenge review hooks.
