# Article I audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article I: Environmental Survival`.

## Overall assessment

Article I is structurally strong and already unusually close to the target audit shape. Its trace blocks are useful, its subsection logic mostly follows the governing Chapter One principles, and its definitions are generally anchored in Chapter Five rather than recreated locally.

The main issues are not wholesale doctrinal conflict. They are:
- one opening phrase that substitutes a non-canonical concept for the governing definition;
- one subsection that compresses the controlling definition enough to drop some operative safeguards;
- one subsection whose operative sentence relies on a Chapter Five concept that is not named in its own trace block.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article I (chapeau)

- Operative claim: environmental survival is upstream of sentient survival and therefore Chapter Five Environmental Preconditions must control under the definition pipeline.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2. Foundational Objective: Wellbeing`, `3.1 Safety`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Environmental Preconditions`, `Wellbeing`, and `Safety (Constraint)`.
- Mechanics: Chapters Two through Four are expressly invoked and that is a strength.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `3` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Definition substitution] The opening sentence says, `The wellbeing of planet Earth is a prerequisite for the existence of all sentients.` The governing Chapter Five terms are `Environmental Preconditions` and `Ecological Integrity`, not `wellbeing of planet Earth`.
- Why it matters: the sentence is intuitively clear, but it imports a non-canonical concept that could invite anthropomorphic or extra-definitional readings. The article becomes slightly less precise at exactly the point where it announces its constitutional basis.
- Smallest close: replace the opening sentence with wording anchored directly to Chapter Five, for example by making the prerequisite the Earth's life-supporting environmental preconditions or biophysical and ecosystem conditions rather than the Earth's `wellbeing`.

### Article I-A

- Operative claim: the constitutional protection of Environmental Preconditions and Ecological Integrity is operative here, and material degradation is non-compliant where applicable.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2`, `3.1`, and `7.1`.
- Definition anchors: `Environmental Preconditions`, `Ecological Integrity`, `Wellbeing`, and functionally `Natural Systems Standing`.
- Mechanics: systemic and cumulative evaluation are preserved; no local-compliance shortcut is introduced.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `3` / Logic `3` / Constraints `3` / Publish `3`
- Finding: [Minor] [Underreach] The operative sentence on continuity and integrity interests appears to rely on the Chapter Five definition `Natural Systems Standing`, but that definition is not named in the trace block.
- Why it matters: this does not create doctrinal conflict, but it slightly weakens audit readability because one of the key interpretive concepts is present in substance without being named as an anchor.
- Smallest close: add `Natural Systems Standing` to the trace block, or add a short pointer in the operative sentence.

### Article I-B

- Operative claim: ecological footprint is governed by Chapter Five and disclosure/comparison/reduction efforts must align with transparency and epistemic integrity.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2`, `6.2`, and `7.1`.
- Definition anchors: `Ecological Footprint`, `Transparency`, and `Epistemic Integrity`.
- Mechanics: disclosure integrity and systemic reporting logic are preserved without turning non-binding encouragement into a false mandate.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `3` / Logic `3` / Constraints `3` / Publish `3`
- Finding: none.
- Why it matters: this subsection is a good model of compact, definition-faithful drafting. It preserves the distinction between disclosure governance and non-binding reduction encouragement.
- Smallest close: none.

### Article I-C

- Operative claim: Intergenerational Responsibility bars disproportionate, irreversible, or unrepresented burdens on future sentients and continuity of environmental preconditions.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `2`, `3.1`, and `7`.
- Definition anchors: `Intergenerational Responsibility`, `Environmental Preconditions`, and `Materiality Determination`.
- Mechanics: the subsection clearly carries delayed and cumulative logic, but only part of the governing definition is carried forward.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The subsection compresses the governing definition enough to drop explicit references to mitigation, disclosure, and representation that appear in the Chapter Five definition's O and C components.
- Why it matters: the current sentence preserves the high-level prohibition, but a reader could miss that displacement of ecological or existential burden is not only forbidden in outcome terms; it also requires mitigation, disclosure, and representation safeguards. That is a logic-loss issue, not just a wording preference.
- Smallest close: add a short clause restoring those safeguards, for example after `future sentients and on continuity of environmental preconditions`.

### Article I-D

- Operative claim: existential-risk pathways trigger heightened scrutiny, expanded evaluation, stronger burden, uncertainty disclosure, and anti-pretext guardrails.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `7 Systemic Evaluation Requirement`.
- Definition anchors: `Existential Risk`, `Foreseeability`, `Dependency`, `Safety (Constraint)`, `Truth (Constitutional Constraint)`, and `Intergenerational Responsibility`.
- Mechanics: this subsection properly pulls in burden, alternatives analysis, uncertainty disclosure, reversal feasibility, and anti-pretext review.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `3` / Logic `3` / Constraints `3` / Publish `3`
- Finding: none.
- Why it matters: this is the strongest subsection in the article from a logic-chain standpoint. It carries upstream constitutional controls into operational language without obvious drift.
- Smallest close: none.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article I does not show major structural misalignment. The pilot suggests the rubric is workable because it identifies a small number of high-value issues rather than generating noise.

Most important close points:
- tighten the chapeau so it speaks in canonical Chapter Five terms;
- restore the missing safeguard content in `Article I-C`;
- optionally improve trace visibility in `Article I-A`.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the opening sentence of `Article I` to anchor the prerequisite in `Environmental Preconditions (Constitutional)` rather than `the wellbeing of planet Earth`;
2. revise `Article I-C` to restore mitigation, disclosure, and representation language for intergenerational burden shifting;
3. optionally add `Natural Systems Standing` to the `Article I-A` trace block.
