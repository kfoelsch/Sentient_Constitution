# Article XXIV audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XXIV: Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining`.

## Overall assessment

Article XXIV has the right constitutional objective. It treats transition as a constrained bridge from legacy operation to constitutional operation rather than a freestanding source of exceptional power. Its main weakness was anti-drift visibility: too much of the protection against phase-creep, convenience reauthorization, degraded-baseline normalization, and mixed bounty-enforcement incentives still had to be inferred from CI-14, Article XXII, Article XXIII, and the Chapter One constraint stack rather than read directly from the article.

The main issues were anti-evasion clarity and practical usability rather than doctrinal direction:
- the article lead correctly barred durable exception authority, but it did not show clearly enough that transition implementation cannot become a de facto amendment path or practical lowering of the rights floor;
- `Article XXIV-A` correctly required phased, reviewable adoption and preserved baseline protections, yet it left too implicit that phases cannot advance by elapsed time, convenience, or pressure alone and that rights floors must remain usable in practice;
- `Article XXIV-B` correctly imposed scope, sunset, and reauthorization discipline, but it did not expressly bar convenience, self-created delay, or mere unfinished migration work as sufficient grounds for renewal;
- `Article XXIV-C` preserved fallback, rollback, and traceability, yet it underexposed the rule that a degraded transitional state cannot become the new baseline by persistence alone;
- `Article XXIV-D` already contained a strong deprivation and anti-bounty stack, but it left notice and contestability too implicit and did not surface structural separation between incentive adjudication and enforcement execution clearly enough.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XXIV (chapeau)

- Operative claim: transition is constitutionally valid only as a temporary continuity-preserving bridge rather than an alternate source of higher-order authority.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, `8. Prohibition on Absolute Override`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Governance`, and the non-regression logic carried across the corpus.
- Mechanics: transition language should visibly bar implementation drift from hardening into lower floors or amendment-by-practice.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Anti-evasion underexposure] The lead already rejected durable exception authority, but it left too much of the anti-drift and anti-amendment logic implicit.
- Why it matters: constitutional erosion commonly occurs through temporary mechanisms that become normalized rather than openly declared as amendments.
- Smallest close: add language barring de facto amendment and practical floor-lowering through transition implementation drift.

### Article XXIV-A

- Operative claim: a phase cannot constitutionally advance unless published gate conditions and verification artifacts are actually satisfied.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `6.1`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Proportionality`, and `Wellbeing`.
- Mechanics: phased adoption should be criteria-driven, evidence-driven, and rights-usable in practice rather than calendar-driven.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Trigger/constraint drop] The subsection already required phased, reviewable plans, but it did not say clearly enough that phase progression cannot rest on elapsed time, convenience, or political pressure alone, and it did not make practical usability of rights floors explicit.
- Why it matters: transition plans often comply formally while rights become slower, weaker, or harder to invoke during the “temporary” period.
- Smallest close: require documented gate satisfaction for advancement and say that simplification cannot materially impair practical exercise of the protected floor.

### Article XXIV-B

- Operative claim: reauthorization is exceptional and must become harder to justify as temporary power persists.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1`, `6.1`, and `8`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Necessity`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: unfinished migration work, convenience, or self-created delay should not be enough to prolong interim authority.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] The subsection already required sunset bounds and reauthorization, but it did not surface what does not count as sufficient justification and it did not make the growing continuation burden visible.
- Why it matters: temporary powers are most often extended through operational inertia rather than openly claimed emergency.
- Smallest close: add an anti-convenience sentence and state that the burden of justification increases with duration and rights impact.

### Article XXIV-C

- Operative claim: fallback and re-baselining exist to restore lawful continuity, not to normalize degraded temporary arrangements.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.4`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Auditability`, `Accountability`, and `Reversibility`.
- Mechanics: rollback and baseline reset require traceable reasons and valid authority rather than persistence alone.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Normalization gap] The subsection correctly required fallback pathways and public traceability, but it did not directly state that a failed or degraded transitional state cannot become the new baseline just because it lasted.
- Why it matters: normalization through delay is one of the easiest ways for temporary degradation to become effectively permanent.
- Smallest close: say expressly that material baseline reset requires constitutionally valid authority and traceable justification.

### Article XXIV-D

- Operative claim: transition-phase seizure and voluntary-turnover incentives remain remedial only if they are procedurally usable, evidence-bound, and structurally protected against bounty capture.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `3.2 Truth`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Necessity`, `Substantive Fairness`, and `Protected Reporting (Whistleblowing)`.
- Mechanics: affected parties should receive prompt notice, stated reasons, and a practicable contest path where feasible, and incentive decisions should not control enforcement execution.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Practical-access / capture risk] The subsection already imposed strong anti-pretext and anti-bounty conditions, but it left practical notice and contestability too implicit and did not state the separation-of-lanes principle directly enough on the constitutional surface.
- Why it matters: even lawful remedial powers become abuse-prone when seizure pathways are opaque or when payout incentives shape enforcement choices.
- Smallest close: add notice-and-contest language and require separation between incentive adjudication and enforcement execution where practical.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XXIV does not need expansion into a transition code. It needs a sharper constitutional surface so readers can see that transition cannot mutate into amendment-by-drift, phase progression cannot run on inertia, temporary power becomes harder rather than easier to justify over time, failed transitional states cannot self-ratify as the new baseline, and bounty-style incentives must remain structurally separated from coercive remediation where practical.

Most important close points:
- make the article lead explicitly anti-drift and anti-floor-lowering;
- make phase progression criteria-based rather than calendar- or convenience-based;
- make reauthorization visibly resistant to convenience and self-created delay;
- make re-baselining visibly anti-normalization;
- make deprivation and incentive pathways visibly notice-giving, contestable, and lane-separated.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the article lead so transition cannot become de facto amendment or practical floor-lowering through implementation drift;
2. revise `Article XXIV-A` so phase advancement depends on documented gate satisfaction and rights floors remain practically usable;
3. revise `Article XXIV-B` so convenience, self-created delay, and unfinished migration work alone cannot justify renewal and continuation burden increases with duration;
4. revise `Article XXIV-C` so degraded transitional states cannot become the new baseline by persistence alone and baseline resets require traceable constitutional justification;
5. revise `Article XXIV-D` so prompt notice, stated reasons, and practicable contest pathways are surfaced and incentive adjudication is separated from enforcement execution where practical.
