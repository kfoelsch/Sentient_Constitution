# Article XXII audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article XXII: Conflict Resolution, Escalation, and Emergency Proportionality`.

## Overall assessment

Article XXII has the right constitutional objective. It keeps justice protective, restorative, and rights-bounded while giving the constitution a real emergency and conflict-resolution discipline. Its main weakness was operational visibility: too much of the practical access, individualized burden, anti-pretext emergency control, and restorative-limit logic still had to be inferred from Chapter Five definitions and adjacent rights articles rather than read directly off the page.

The main issues were usability and anti-evasion clarity rather than doctrinal direction:
- the article lead correctly required transparent, fair, timely, and contestable resolution, but it did not show clearly enough that adopted governance implementation cannot narrow practical access, reviewability, or restoration;
- `Article XXII-B` correctly imposed joint punishment conditions and a narrow capital-punishment gate, but it left too implicit that severity labels and tier classifications cannot substitute for individualized, auditable proof and reviewable justification;
- `Article XXII-C` preserved least-restrictive and time-bounded discipline, yet it underexposed the practical conditions that make escalation and appeal usable in real cases, including notice, reasons, and record access;
- `Article XXII-D` already required time limits, review cadence, and continuation burden, but it did not expressly bar convenience, self-created urgency, or scrutiny avoidance as emergency predicates, and it did not surface restored challenge access clearly enough;
- `Article XXII-E` and `Article XXII-F` protected retrospective review and restorative alignment, but they left confidentiality control and the boundary between restorative pathways and non-waivable protection/accountability somewhat too implicit.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article XXII (chapeau)

- Operative claim: constitutional disputes must be resolvable through accessible, fair, timely, and contestable processes that remain constitutionally supreme over owner-layer procedure.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure` and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Adjudication and Dispute Resolution`, `Contestability`, and `Redress and Remediation`.
- Mechanics: owner-layer procedure may specialize escalation and emergency operations, but it cannot narrow practical access, reviewability, or restoration minima secured by the constitutional definition chain.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The lead already named transparent, fair, timely, and contestable resolution, but it did not state clearly enough that adopted governance implementation is subordinate and cannot compress those minima in practice.
- Why it matters: procedure is often narrowed indirectly through institution-layer implementation even when the constitutional surface text sounds rights-protective.
- Smallest close: add a non-narrowing sentence tying implementation to practical access, reviewability, restoration, and rights-floor constraints.

### Article XXII-B

- Operative claim: serious deprivations and sanctions require joint satisfaction of safety, remediation, recurrence-reduction, and accountability conditions on an individualized evidentiary basis.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `6.1 Core Tradeoff Principles`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Accountability`, `Redress and Remediation`, and `Safety`.
- Mechanics: Chapters Two through Four require auditable proof, attributable reasoning, and reviewable burdens rather than categorical punishment by label, outrage, or status.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Burden/trace error] The subsection already imposed a strong joint test, but it left too implicit that severity tiers and even valid Chapter Seven classifications are threshold gates rather than substitutes for individualized proof and reviewable justification.
- Why it matters: sanction systems commonly drift from evidence-bound adjudication toward category-driven punishment once severity labels are treated as doing more work than the constitution permits.
- Smallest close: say expressly that measures must remain individualized, attributable, and reviewable and that tier labels do not replace the Article's joint requirements or appeal rights.

### Article XXII-C

- Operative claim: least-restrictive intervention is constitutionally valid only when affected parties can practically use the review pathways that police it.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.1`, `6.4`, and `8`.
- Definition anchors: `Necessity`, `Proportionality`, `Reversibility`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: review usability depends on timely notice, reasons, and sufficient record access to make escalation and appeal real rather than theoretical.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint drop] The subsection correctly required least-restrictive, time-bounded measures and appeal or multi-layer review, but it did not surface the procedural conditions that make those review rights functional.
- Why it matters: challenge rights become nominal if affected parties are not told enough, soon enough, to invoke them.
- Smallest close: add timely notice, stated reasons, and practical record-access language subject only to narrow justified restrictions.

### Article XXII-D

- Operative claim: emergency deviation is allowed only as a temporary, documented, necessity-bound response that cannot be used to evade ordinary constitutional scrutiny.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1`, `6.1`, and `6.4`.
- Definition anchors: `Emergency and Contingency`, `Necessity`, `Proportionality`, and `Contestability`.
- Mechanics: the emergency definition forbids pretextual or indefinite bypass of safety, truth, contestability, and audit and requires continuation burdens and restoration discipline.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Ambiguity risk] The subsection already had strong continuation-burden language, but it did not expressly identify convenience, self-created urgency, or scrutiny avoidance as invalid emergency predicates, and it left restored challenge access underexposed.
- Why it matters: emergency abuse often presents itself as urgency management rather than open suspension of rights.
- Smallest close: add an anti-pretext sentence and require notice and restored challenge access as soon as feasible.

### Article XXII-E

- Operative claim: post-emergency review must remain auditable enough that exception claims can be evaluated rather than merely asserted.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.2 Truth`, `6.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Auditability`, `Accountability`, and `Truth (Constitutional Constraint)`.
- Mechanics: bounded confidentiality requires traceable justification, limited scope, and a visible expiry or review cadence.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Burden/trace error] The subsection correctly required retrospective review and disclosure, but it did not make the handling of confidentiality exceptions explicit enough for publication-grade auditability.
- Why it matters: disclosure duties lose force when exceptions are asserted at a high level without a reviewable structure.
- Smallest close: require each exception to state justification, protected scope, and expiry or review cadence.

### Article XXII-F

- Operative claim: rights-collision handling may align with restorative norms, but restorative process cannot override protection, accountability, or remedy where conditions make it constitutionally unsuitable.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, `8`, and `9`.
- Definition anchors: `Restorative Justice`, `Contestability`, and `Proportionality`.
- Mechanics: restorative priority is conditional on feasibility, proportionality, dignity, and truthful accountability; voluntariness alone is not enough if risk, coercion, or asymmetry makes the format unsuitable.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Conflict-rule error] The subsection already protected voluntariness in public accountability expression, but it did not expressly state that restorative pathways cannot displace necessary protection, accountable attribution, or proportionate remediation.
- Why it matters: restorative language is sometimes misread as a reason to soften protection or to pressure harmed parties into reconciliation formats that are not actually safe or fair.
- Smallest close: add a sentence preserving protection, accountability, and remedy where restorative process is inappropriate due to ongoing risk, material power asymmetry, or non-consent.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article XXII does not need a wholesale redesign. It needs a sharper constitutional surface so readers can see that justice pathways must be usable in practice, serious sanctions remain individualized and evidence-bound, emergency powers are anti-pretext as well as time-limited, and restorative language does not silently displace protection or accountability.

Most important close points:
- make the article lead explicitly non-narrowable by owner-layer procedure;
- make `Article XXII-B` visibly individualized and label-resistant;
- make `Article XXII-C` practically usable through notice, reasons, and record access;
- make `Article XXII-D` expressly anti-pretext and restore challenge access quickly;
- make `Article XXII-E` confidentiality handling reviewable;
- make `Article XXII-F` expressly preserve protection, accountability, and remedy.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. tighten the article lead so implementation layers cannot narrow practical access, reviewability, restoration, or rights-floor constraints;
2. revise `Article XXII-B` so severe sanctions remain individualized, attributable, and reviewable and so tier labels cannot substitute for the Article's joint burden;
3. revise `Article XXII-C` so escalation and appeal rights include timely notice, stated reasons, and practical access to the record;
4. revise `Article XXII-D` so convenience, self-created urgency, and scrutiny avoidance are expressly invalid emergency predicates and challenge access is restored as soon as feasible;
5. revise `Article XXII-E` so confidentiality exceptions carry stated justification, scope, and expiry or review cadence;
6. revise `Article XXII-F` so restorative pathways cannot displace necessary protection, accountable attribution, or proportionate remediation where conditions make them inappropriate.
