# Article IX audit

Status: implementation review artifact (non-authoritative).  
Date: 2026-04-15.  
Scope: pilot application of the Article-Principle-Definition audit rubric to `Article IX: Self-Determination and Agency`.

## Overall assessment

Article IX has the right constitutional center of gravity. It correctly treats agency, anti-manipulation, stakeholder participation, governance entitlement, challenge rights, and covert-power limits as one connected rights cluster rather than scattering them across unrelated autonomy, voting, and security provisions.

The main issues were publication-shape and operational-clarity issues rather than doctrinal reversal:
- the agency subsection was directionally strong but too compressed about refusal, manipulative interference, and designed exclusion from materially affecting systems;
- the stakeholder and challenge subsections preserved the right concepts, but they repeated Chapter Five terms while leaving the practical shape of participation, explanation, and contestability too submerged;
- the voting-entitlement subsection correctly deferred to Chapter Ten, section 4, but it left the anti-self-definition rule and independent challenge path too easy to miss;
- the covert-power subsection was already strong, but some accountability and anti-bypass logic could be surfaced more cleanly.

Overall judgment: `publishable after targeted edits`.

## Findings

### Article IX-A

- Operative claim: sentients retain meaningful authority over their own decisions, future direction, attention, and participation free from predatory manipulation and unjustified exclusion.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.3 Freedom-Limitation Constraints`, and `7.2 Incentive Alignment and System Capture`.
- Definition anchors: `Self-Determination`, `Meaningful Agency`, `Surveillance Boundary`, and functionally `Coercion and Manipulation`.
- Mechanics: the subsection should protect not only nominal choice, but also refusal, freedom from bypass-style manipulation, and freedom from system design that quietly removes materially affected sentients from decision pathways.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Underreach] The subsection already points to informed choice and anti-manipulation, but the current bullets are compressed enough that refusal, designed exclusion, and manipulative interference with attention or vulnerability remain easier to narrow than they should be.
- Why it matters: without those clarifiers, a reader can treat Article IX-A as a broad value statement rather than a rights floor against surveillance-driven shaping, coercive nudging, and participation-denial in materially affecting systems.
- Smallest close: revise the agency bullet to include making and refusing decisions, tighten the manipulation bullet to name bypass of conscious choice and exploitative design more directly, and sharpen the focus bullet so attention-boundary protection reads as an agency safeguard rather than a mere preference.

### Article IX-B

- Operative claim: materially affected sentients have a meaningful and proportionate role in decision-making, scaled to impact and dependency and preserved against manipulation or unfair qualification drift.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, and `7.1 Required Evaluation Factors`.
- Definition anchors: `Stakeholder`, `Stakeholder Participation Weight`, `Procedural Fairness`, and functionally `Participant Standing`.
- Mechanics: the article should show on its face that participation rights are impact-linked, proportionate, challengeable, and not reducible to whatever role a system currently offers.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Operational blur] The subsection repeats Chapter Five sourcing correctly, but it takes too long to surface the actual operative rule: materially affected sentients are owed a meaningful and proportionate role, and any qualification gates must remain fair, challengeable, and manipulation-resistant.
- Why it matters: if readers mostly see definitional repetition, they can miss that the constitutional floor is participation itself, not merely a pointer to later implementation.
- Smallest close: condense the definitional repetition, state the participation right in a single clean lead bullet, and keep the qualification/challenge sentence focused on procedural fairness, standing rules, and anti-manipulation.

### Article IX-C

- Operative claim: governance voting and comparable binding collective choice must follow Chapter Ten entitlement minima and cannot be solely defined by the systems being governed.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Participant Standing`, `Stakeholder Participation Weight`, and `Governance`.
- Mechanics: the article should make visible both halves of the rule at once: constitutional entitlement comes from Chapter Ten and related standing rules, and governed systems cannot quietly self-define the electorate without independent audit and challenge.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Visibility gap] The subsection includes both concepts already, but the anti-self-definition rule is stranded in a prefatory sentence and the entitlement sentence is dense enough that the practical challenge path is easy to lose.
- Why it matters: governance rules are a classic site of capture, and the page should make it obvious that voting eligibility is neither entirely internal nor immune from review.
- Smallest close: split the prefatory sentence into a clear anti-self-definition bullet and tighten the entitlement bullet so the Chapter Ten cross-reference, good-standing rule, and legitimacy mechanism are easier to parse.

### Article IX-D

- Operative claim: stakeholder-boundary determinations must remain externally challengeable, auditable, and not resolvable solely by the excluding authority.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `5. Governance Principle: Freedom`, `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`, and `9. Interpretive Role`.
- Definition anchors: `Stakeholder`, `Contestability`, and `Procedural Fairness`.
- Mechanics: exclusion rights should be real in practice, which means affected sentients need a route to know the basis of exclusion, challenge the determination, and reach an authority other than the original gatekeeper.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `2` / Logic `2` / Constraints `2` / Publish `2`
- Finding: [Moderate] [Constraint underexposure] The subsection correctly bars unilateral closure, but it remains too terse about practical contestability and explanation, especially for inclusion/exclusion decisions that materially affect agency or participation.
- Why it matters: a right to challenge exclusion is weakened if the text does not clearly signal that exclusions must remain auditable, explainable in a lawful manner, and reviewable by an external pathway.
- Smallest close: add a sentence making reason-giving and practical review explicit while keeping the adjudication and escalation routing already in place.

### Article IX-E

- Operative claim: covert and intelligence-like powers are exceptional, necessity-bound, independently reviewable, and unable to use secrecy or intermediaries to escape constitutional limits.
- Principle anchor: Chapter One `3.1 Safety`, `6.3 Freedom-Limitation Constraints`, and `6.4 Rights-Collision Procedure`.
- Definition anchors: `Necessity`, `Proportionality`, and `Protected Internal-State Boundary`.
- Mechanics: Article IX-E should keep anti-circumvention and accountability on the surface so covert-power readers cannot confuse secrecy with exemption.
- Scores: Principle `3` / Definition `3` / Logic `3` / Constraints `3` / Publish `3`
- Finding: [Low] [Readability/structure] The subsection is doctrinally strong already, but a few lines still bury the core idea that secrecy may narrow disclosure while leaving review, remedy, and anti-bypass obligations fully intact.
- Why it matters: this provision is likely to be read under stress, and readability itself is part of making the constraint usable.
- Smallest close: preserve the current substance but tighten wording so independent authorization, anti-bypass evasion, and secrecy-does-not-erase-accountability are easier to scan.

## Cross-cutting conclusion

Article IX does not need expansion into a longer autonomy, elections, or intelligence code. It needs a bit more constitutional surface area so agency, stakeholder participation, challenge rights, and anti-capture limits remain visible at the point of reading rather than only by reconstruction from Chapter Five and Chapter Ten.

Most important close points:
- tighten `Article IX-A` so refusal, anti-bypass manipulation, and attention-boundary protection read as concrete agency safeguards;
- rewrite `Article IX-B` and `Article IX-C` so the participation right and anti-self-definition rule are easier to parse;
- make `Article IX-D` more explicit that exclusion must be auditable, explainable, and externally challengeable;
- preserve `Article IX-E` doctrine while slightly improving readability under stress.

## Suggested next-step edits

If this audit is used as a drafting basis, the smallest likely edits are:

1. revise the `Article IX-A` body bullets so they name refusal, exploitative manipulation, and focus protection more directly;
2. condense `Article IX-B` and `Article IX-C` into cleaner operative bullets that keep Chapter Five and Chapter Ten cross-references but reduce repetition;
3. revise `Article IX-D` so practical contestability is visible on the page;
4. lightly tighten `Article IX-E` for scanability without changing its doctrinal floor.
