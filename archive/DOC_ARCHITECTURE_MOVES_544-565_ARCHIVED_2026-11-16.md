**Five hundred and fifty-first move (honest error opens no actor-specific violation record; negligence inside and outside institutions and systems; 2026-11-15; changes rules; uncommitted).** Decision (custodian, in session, on the point that honest errors do not belong in violation records because they are governance problems of systems and institutions, a Deming / TQM view, and "please draft 1-4", with "negligence both inside and out of institutional and system contexts"). What changed: [Chapter Fifteen §5.3](../core_15.000_standing_assessment.md) now requires verified fault (knowledge, intent, recklessness, or negligence) as well as linkage before an actor-specific violation record opens, so a causal role alone is never enough; defines honest error as a mistake without any of those; says it opens no actor-specific violation record, is not counted against the sentient, leaves the harm recorded as an event with remedy still owed under Article XIII-B, and that reporting an honest error is protected reporting; routes the error to root-cause analysis under Article XXIV, which must say whether the conditions the institution or system gave made the error likely, in which case the violation record opens on the institution or system as structural enablement (and the Chapter Twenty §2.3 notice names it), with the same rule for those who set the conditions and for a maker, owner, publisher, or provider outside an institution; and keeps concealment and obstruction individual, recorded on its own and not turning the error into a violation. [Chapter Nineteen §3.4.1](../core_19.000_violation_consequences.md) now defines negligence, honest error (judged at the time, not by the outcome), and feasible capacity (information, time, training, tools, authority to stop, a working route to escalate without reprisal; capacity that was withheld is not the sentient's), and states the rule inside an institution or system (conditions first; duty unclear or capacity not given is an honest error at most and the failing is the institution's; duty clear and capacity real is negligence, with both violation records opening if conditions also contributed; failure to escalate is individual where a working route existed; those who set the conditions are judged by the same rule; following a rule is not negligence unless the sentient knew it would cause material harm, when Duty to Resist applies) and outside one (own capacity decides; where the duty comes from; when negligence is recordable; outside causes; mixed settings), with the same standard on every substrate. The concealment paragraph now says that where the underlying mistake was an honest error the concealment is recorded on its own. [Chapter Sixteen §3.2.2](../core_16.000_standing_measurement.md) attribution table gained an honest-error row. Effect on a rule: negligence is narrower than before (it needs real capacity and a clear duty), the institution or system now carries what it set up, and a record opens on an actor only on fault. Drafted beyond the request, for the custodian to keep or strike: the definition of negligence, the "judged at the time" rule, the list of what feasible capacity includes, the escalation rule, the following-a-rule rule, the outside-causes rule, the statement that remedy does not depend on a violation record against any individual, and the protected-reporting line for reporting an honest error. Not done: the data-broker tightenings in Chapter Twenty §2.3 (a narrower no-compiling bar, a sentence applying the Privacy (Informational) rules to public notices, a bar on using a third-party compilation in place of the gatekeeper check), which were proposed and not requested; a worked example (to be shelved); regression scenarios; the independent read; translations (paused).
