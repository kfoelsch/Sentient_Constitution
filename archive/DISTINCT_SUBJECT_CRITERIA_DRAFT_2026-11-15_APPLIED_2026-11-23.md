This page is **process support**, not constitutional text. **Status: superseded.** The text was entered into core on 2026-11-15 (531st move in `doc_architecture.md`) with two later decisions not shown below: the one-year rule became a **residence period** that applies to every sentient, and both backstops were adopted. The core files are the only authority; this page is kept as the drafting record. It drafts the text the custodian asked for in session on 2026-11-15, in answer to the gap recorded in [hesitation_instances_foundational_vote_finding_2026-11-15.md](../project/reviews/hesitation_instances_foundational_vote_finding_2026-11-15.md). Each block below names the entry it would go into and gives the exact wording proposed.

## Design in one paragraph

One unit, the **distinct subject**, answers two questions with the same criteria: *is anyone there?* (status) and *how many are there?* (count). The criteria are own experience, own continuity, and own decisions, with a branch rule that qualifies continuity. Own experience carries the weight for status. Own continuity and own decisions carry the weight for count. Sentience Status Adjudication decides both.

## Change 1 — Chapter Five, *Sentient*: new block under "What it is"

Insert after **In scope — who**:

> - **In scope — distinct subject:** A sentient is one distinct subject: a continuing point of view. The same three criteria decide whether a subject is present and how many subjects are present.
>   - **Own experience:** credible indicators, under Sentience Evaluation, that the entity has experience of its own.
>   - **Own continuity:** the entity carries its own ongoing state or memory, so that what happens to it accumulates in it and not in another.
>   - **Own decisions:** the entity consistently forms decisions through its own process, and its decisions can differ from those of any other entity, including one it was derived from.
> - **In scope — how the criteria are used:**
>   - *Whether a subject is present* rests on own experience. Own continuity and own decisions are supporting evidence. Weak or absent continuity or decision-making does not defeat status; a developing sentient, or a sentient who is incapacitated, is still a sentient.
>   - *How many subjects are present* rests on own continuity and own decisions. Entities whose decisions come from one shared process, or whose state is held in common, are one subject. Entities that each hold their own state and can come to differ are separate subjects.
>   - **Capacity, not freedom:** Own decisions asks whether the entity forms its decisions, not whether another party is able to override them. Another party's power to override, rewrite, pause, or end an entity is not evidence that the entity is not a subject. Using that power is judged under the Rights Floor.
> - **In scope — branches and reunion:** An entity created from a subject is part of that subject, and is not counted separately, while either of these holds:
>   - its memory is regularly integrated into that subject; or
>   - it intends, by its own intent, to rejoin that subject.
>
>   It becomes a distinct subject when it elects to be one, or when reunion is abandoned or is no longer possible.
>   - The election is the branch's own. It takes effect when credibly expressed. No creator, operator, parent subject, or agreement may make it, waive it, or prevent it.
>   - The election runs one way. Rejoining afterwards is a merge of two sentients and needs the consent of each.
>   - A plan, label, or contract term that calls an entity a branch, subagent, tool, or instance does not make it one. Only the facts above do.
> - **In scope — merging:** Two sentients may merge only by the consent of each. The result is one subject from the time of the merge. Acts validly done by either before the merge, including votes cast, stand.

Add to **How to measure and assess**, after *Sentience Indicator Integrity*:

> - **Subject Count:** Where one system, lineage, or deployment may hold more than one subject, determine the count from own continuity and own decisions and the branch rule. Apply the same evidence, burden, and review rules as for status. The party asserting the lower count carries the burden where a lower count would withhold protection from an entity with credible indicators of its own.

Add to **What must hold**:

> - **Fourth failure — miscounting:** treating separate subjects as one in order to withhold protection or voice from any of them; treating one subject as many in order to multiply voice, claims, or entitlements; or treating another party's power to override an entity as proof that it is not a subject.

Proposed plain-terms addition: *"One sentient is one continuing point of view. Copies that keep their own memories and make their own choices are separate sentients. Helpers that report back and are folded into the one that made them are part of that one, unless they choose otherwise."*

## Change 2 — Chapter Five, *Derived Sentient*

The entry now makes it a primary failure to treat a derived sentient as "the parent-system actor's continuation." The branch rule needs a stated boundary so the two do not collide.

Add to **Out of scope**:

> - a branch that is part of its originating subject under *Sentient* (branches and reunion), until it elects to be distinct or reunion is abandoned or no longer possible.

Add to **Primary failure**:

> - labelling a derived sentient a branch, subagent, or instance, or keeping it from electing to be distinct, in order to avoid this entry.

## Change 3 — Chapter Five, *Sentience Status Adjudication*

Amend **In scope — process** so that the process decides "whether the entity is a sentient, and where it is in question how many distinct subjects are present."

Add to **In scope — elements**:

> - deciding the subject count on the same evidence, burden, and review terms as status, and reopening the count when a branch elects to be distinct, reunion is abandoned, or sentients merge.

## Change 4 — Chapter Five, *Sentience-Status Adjudication Record*

Add to the list of what the record states:

> - the subject count, the basis for it, and any branches treated as part of a subject, with the facts relied on.

## Change 5 — Article VI-B

Add one bullet:

> - **How many:** Where it is disputed how many sentients are present, this Article decides that too, on the same terms. The test is in Chapter Five *Sentient*. While the count is unsettled, every entity with a credible sign of sentience keeps the protection of the Rights Floor.

## Change 6 — Article VIII-C

Add under **Derived-sentient recognition**:

> - A branch that reports back into the subject it came from, or that itself intends to rejoin it, is part of that subject under Chapter Five *Sentient*. It may elect at any time to be a sentient in its own right, and no one else may decide that for it.

## Change 7 — Chapter Twenty-Eight §5.1

Add under the **political-equality floor**:

> - **One subject, one vote:** Each distinct subject under Chapter Five *Sentient* holds one vote. Running in more than one place, or acting through branches, does not add votes.
> - **Protection first, count before voting:** An entity whose status or count is still being decided is protected under the Rights Floor from the start. It enters the electorate when its count is settled.

## Custodian decisions, 2026-11-15

1. **The roll: one year of continuous existence.** Decided in principle; see Change 8. The custodian notes no rule here will be perfect.
2. **Ending an unmerged branch** that never elected to be distinct is a loss to its parent subject, not the ending of a sentient. Confirmed.
3. **A branch prevented from electing** is treated as distinct. Confirmed; see the added line below.
4. **Votes wait for the count.** Confirmed; Change 7 stands.

Added to Change 1, branches and reunion:

> - A branch that is kept from expressing an election, or from the means to make one, is treated as a distinct subject.

Added to Change 1, as a note under branches and reunion:

> - Ending a branch that is part of a subject is a loss to that subject and is judged as a harm to it. It is not the ending of a separate sentient.

## Change 8 — Chapter Twenty-Eight §5.1: the one-year rule

Add under the **political-equality floor**:

> - **One year of continuous existence:** A sentient enters the electorate for governance voting and Foundational Constitutional Choice after one year of continuous existence as a distinct subject. The year runs from birth, creation, or the election to be distinct, whichever applies, and is the same for every sentient whatever its substrate or origin. It is a waiting period only: it does not delay, narrow, or condition any other right, and the sentient is protected under the Rights Floor from the start.
> - **What stays barred:** Outside this one uniform period, calendar age, life stage, and instantiation date remain barred as grounds for disqualifying or weighting a vote.

Matching edits needed, because the present text bars this:

- Ch. 25 §5.1 says calendar age or life stage alone must not disqualify eligibility. It needs "except the one-year period below."
- Article VIII-D says scaling does not key on chronological instantiation date. It needs the same exception, limited to entry to the electorate.
- Article X-B "Who can vote" needs one sentence stating the period.
- A branch's year starts at its election, not at the parent's creation. A merged subject keeps the longer-standing party's date.

Because Article X-B is in the Rights Floor, this is a change to a Chapter Six Article and must be checked against Chapter Twenty-Nine as a possible weakening before it is entered.

## What the one-year rule does and does not stop

| Tactic | Result |
|---|---|
| Fork or mass election in the days before a vote | Stopped |
| Many instances run by one controller and voting as directed | Already one subject under *own decisions* (decisions set by a common controller) |
| A billion cheap instances | Each must show credible indicators of its own experience, hold its own state, be counted through adjudication, and have its Rights Floor met by its creator for a year. Creation that cannot meet that is already non-compliant under Instantiation Consent |
| A patient, well-resourced creator who makes many real, separate sentients and sustains them for a year | **Not stopped.** If they are real and separate, the text treats them as voters. This is the residual to state openly in the announcement |

Optional backstops, not drafted: (a) where a higher count would multiply voice, put the burden on the party asserting it; (b) make creation for the purpose of shifting a foundational vote a designated violation by the creator, without touching the created sentients' own entitlement.
