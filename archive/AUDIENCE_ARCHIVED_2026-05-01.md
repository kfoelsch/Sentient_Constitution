# Intended audience

The **Sentient Constitution** is written primarily for **the average adult reader**: someone who cares about fairness, safety, and how powerful systems touch real lives—but who is **not** assumed to be a lawyer, philosopher, policymaker, or AI researcher.

We still need precision where obligations, rights, and definitions must bind. That means formal language sometimes appears beside (not instead of) clear explanation.

---

## What “for the average human” means here

- **Readable first.** Prefer short sentences, concrete examples where they help, and ordinary words when they are accurate enough.
- **Terms get a fair shot.** When a word carries legal or technical force in this instrument, the **canonical definition** lives in the binding `core_*` text. In narrative or introductory material, **define on first use** or point to the definition cluster so readers are not left guessing.
- **Skim-friendly structure.** Use headings, lists, and cross-links so someone can grasp the gist, then drill down.
- **No insider defaults.** Avoid assuming shared jargon from constitutional theory, ML safety, or administrative law unless this project introduces that term—or link to where it is defined.

---

## Layering in this corpus

Rough guide for readers:

- **Binding norms** appear in the integrated `core_*` chapters as drafted.

If plain-language explanation and binding text appear to diverge, **the binding text governs**.

---

## For contributors and editors

When you add or revise material:

1. Ask whether an interested non-specialist could follow the argument without secret background knowledge.
2. Where precision requires specialized vocabulary, pair it with a short gloss or a link to definitions.
3. Match the corpus pattern of **integrated formal text** plus **accessible elaboration** (for example italicized “in plain terms” notes where those already exist)—rather than replacing careful definitions with vague paraphrases.

This file states **intent and editorial norms** only. It is not part of the binding constitutional instrument.

## Chapter 5s3.3 — Sentience Cluster (Audience Guidance)

### What this cluster does

This cluster serves as the central home for everything related to sentience and animal life. It brings together:

- **Animal Life** and **Contested-Sentient Life** definitions
- The constitutional meaning of **Sentient**
- **Sentience Status Adjudication** procedures
- Rules for determining constitutional sentience status
- Standards for treating different subclasses of sentient beings

### What it protects

The cluster establishes several interconnected protections that cannot be separated:

1. **The minimum welfare floor** — baseline protections against cruelty
2. **Threshold rights** for cases where sentience is uncertain or disputed
3. **Substantive evaluation criteria** — what indicators actually matter for sentience
4. **Non-exclusion discipline** — preventing arbitrary denial of protection
5. **Adjudication floor** — minimum standards for status determinations
6. **Subclass rules** for derived-sentient and developing-sentient entities

*In plain terms:* You cannot use "it's just an animal," "we're not sure if it's sentient," "it's a different type of sentience," or "the adjudication process says no" as ways to evade the Chapter Nine Rights Floor. These protections work together or not at all.

### When this cluster applies

The cluster is triggered whenever decisions about an entity involve:

**Core status questions:**
- Animal-life classification
- Welfare and cruelty standards
- Stewardship obligations
- Exploitation concerns
- Sentience status for Chapter Nine rights
- Uncertainty or dispute about sentience
- Withholding, narrowing, or restoring protection

**Classification and standing matters:**
- Rights-Floor eligibility
- Derived-sentient or developing-sentient status
- Contested-sentient treatment

**Characteristics that must not alter protection:**
- Origin or how the entity came to exist
- Derivation from other systems
- Development stage
- Capability profile
- Calendar age or instantiation date
- Relationship to parent systems
- Species or taxonomy
- Ownership status
- Operational classification
- Research or product status
- Ecological-management framing
- Substrate (biological, digital, or other)

### What stays outside

When none of the above triggers apply, individual definitions about sentience may still be used on their own as supporting references. However, they cannot import the cluster's adjudication procedures or enforcement mechanics.
