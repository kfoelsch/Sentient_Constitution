# Intended audience

The **Sentient Constitution** is written primarily for **the average adult reader**: someone who cares about fairness, safety, and how powerful systems touch real lives—but who is **not** assumed to be a lawyer, philosopher, policymaker, or AI researcher.

We still need precision where obligations, rights, and definitions must bind. That means formal language sometimes appears beside (not instead of) clear explanation.

---

## What “for the average human” means here

- **Readable first.** Prefer short sentences, concrete examples where they help, and ordinary words when they are accurate enough.
- **Terms get a fair shot.** When a word carries legal or technical force in this instrument, the **canonical definition** lives in the binding `core_*` text. In narrative or introductory material, **define on first use** or point to the definition cluster so readers are not left guessing.
- **Skim-friendly structure.** Use headings, lists, and cross-links so someone can grasp the gist, then drill down.
- **No insider defaults.** Avoid assuming shared jargon from constitutional theory, ML safety, or administrative law unless this project introduces that term—or link to where it is defined.

## Layering in this corpus

Rough guide for readers:

- **Binding norms** appear in the integrated `core_*` chapters as drafted.
- **How to navigate** edition metadata and reading order is in [README.md](README.md).
- **Chapter Five** (definitions) begins with a **non-operative** reader box in [Foundational Definitions](core_05-05_definitions_a_independent.md#chapter-five-foundational-definitions): plain-language notes on **participation** versus **routine defaults**, and links to the binding [**Accessibility**](core_05-05_definitions_a_independent.md#accessibility-constitutional) entry for the full O/E/C text.
- **Conceptual backdrop** without adding new binding rules appears in explanatory files such as [architecture_primer.md](architecture_primer.md).

If plain-language explanation and binding text appear to diverge, **the binding text governs**.

## For contributors and editors

When you add or revise material:

1. Ask whether an interested non-specialist could follow the argument without secret background knowledge.
2. Where precision requires specialized vocabulary, pair it with a short gloss or a link to definitions.
3. Match the corpus pattern of **integrated formal text** plus **accessible elaboration** (for example italicized “in plain terms” notes where those already exist)—rather than replacing careful definitions with vague paraphrases.

This file states **intent and editorial norms** only. It is not part of the binding constitutional instrument.
