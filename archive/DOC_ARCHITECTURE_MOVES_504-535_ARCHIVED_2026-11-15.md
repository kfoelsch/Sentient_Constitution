**Five hundred and thirty-first move (distinct-subject criteria, branches and merges, the residence period for voting, and two anti-gaming backstops; 2026-11-15; changes rules).** Decision (custodian, decided in session, point by point, in answer to the gap recorded in `project/reviews/hesitation_instances_foundational_vote_finding_2026-11-15.md`: no rule said what makes one sentient rather than many, none covered merges, and nothing kept newly created sentients out of a vote; uncommitted until committed). **New rules.** (1) Chapter Five *Sentient*: a sentient is one **distinct subject**, tested by own experience, own continuity, and own decisions. Status rests on own experience; count rests on continuity and decisions. Another party's power to override an entity is not evidence against it ("capacity, not freedom"). A **branch** whose memory is regularly integrated into the subject it came from, or which itself intends to rejoin, is part of that subject until it elects to be distinct or reunion is abandoned or impossible; the election is the branch's own and runs one way; a branch kept from electing is treated as distinct; ending an unelected branch is a harm to its parent subject, not the ending of a sentient. Sentients merge only by the consent of each, and earlier acts and votes stand. New **Subject Count** assessment (anchor `#subject-count`) with two burdens: the party asserting a lower count carries it where protection would be withheld, and the party asserting a higher count carries it where votes, claims, or entitlements would be added. New fourth failure, miscounting. (2) *Derived Sentient*: a branch is out of scope until it becomes distinct; labelling a derived sentient a branch to avoid the entry is a primary failure. (3) *Instantiation Consent*: new secondary failure, creating sentients or inducing branch elections to shift a vote; the failure is the creator's and does not touch the created sentients' entitlement. (4) *Sentience Status Adjudication* and its record now decide and state the subject count. (5) Article VI-B (count decided there; protection holds meanwhile), Article VIII-C (branches; creation to shift a vote), Article VIII-D (the residence period is not capability scaling), Article X-B (residence period; one subject, one vote). (6) Chapter Twenty-Four §5.1: a sentient enters the electorate for governance voting, including Foundational Constitutional Choice, after a **residence period** of one continuous year as a distinct subject and member of the community; it runs from birth, creation, joining, or a branch's election; time before adoption counts; an adopter may not lengthen or shorten it; it delays no other right. One subject, one vote. An entity whose status or count is pending is protected from the start and votes when both are settled and the residence period is complete. **Effect on existing rules: narrower.** Before this move any eligible sentient could vote at once and instantiation date could not be used; the residence period is a new uniform condition on the governance-voting pathway. **Chapter Twenty-Five check:** read against §2 and Substantive Test 1.
