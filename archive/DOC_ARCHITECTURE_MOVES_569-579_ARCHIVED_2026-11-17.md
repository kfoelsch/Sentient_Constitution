Change: new §16.5 Automation in `core_01.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md`, after §16.4, with Trace and Definitions blocks and an *In plain terms* line. Four duties: automate what verified inputs and published rules settle (no extra manual approval unless this Constitution or adopted implementation requires review); judgment stays with sentients (uncertain, disputed, novel, materially discretionary or high-impact questions go to an authorized reviewer or forum, and automation may assemble and route but not decide); automation is still an act (attributable to the owning role, leaves its Act Record, open to inspection and challenge, and never merges the four seats of one act); the system carries the burden, not the steward (a design duty scaled to Materiality and Dependency, with manual work that a feasible automation would remove an Avoidable Burden defect). New §16.5.1 Parallelization: every stewardship process that can be run in parallel without creating an undue burden on anyone is run in parallel. Undue burden is tested on stewards, reviewers, affected sentients, and the process itself (overlapping jobs beyond what a steward can do well, several simultaneous demands on an affected sentient, coordination that costs more than it saves, duplicated work); where it is undue the steps run in sequence and the reason can be stated. Dependencies and any order that Safety, Truth, due process or a Chapter Six right requires stay; seats stay separate; the strands rejoin on the Act Record. It sets no deadline (time limits stay in Chapter Twenty-Three §6), does not make speed the goal, does not remove required review, and does not open anyone's inner life. Scaled to Materiality and Dependency; lower-stake and informal stewardship follows as far as is proportionate. Effect on rules: adds a new default duty at principle layer (the parallelization sentence above) and a general automation default that §16.4 and §13.3 already carried in part; nothing in the operative chapters was changed, and no deadline, threshold, clock or scope was set. **Five hundred and seventy-ninth move (Chapter One §16.5.2 Design for Parallelization; 2026-11-16; adds a design duty at principle layer; uncommitted).** Decision (custodian, asked in session, after signing off on the 578th move: "probably another subbullet 16.5.2, which would be that processes should be designed to support parallelization unless doing so creates an undue burden"). Change: new §16.5.2 in `core_01.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md`, after §16.5.1. Rule: stewardship processes are designed to support parallelization unless doing so creates an undue burden on anyone. Supporting it means defining each step by what it needs and produces so independent steps can be seen as independent (no waiting by habit, form order, or a shared holder or queue), not making independent steps take turns on inputs and tools, building in the step where the strands rejoin and an Act Record that holds each strand's result, and using common published formats (§18.6) so different stewards or systems can take different strands. The undue-burden test is the one in §16.5.1, plus design effort that costs more than the delay it would remove; where it is undue the process is built in sequence and the reason can be stated. A process built in a line when a parallel design was feasible and not unduly burdensome carries the same Avoidable Burden defect as in §16.5.1. Matching edits: §16.5.1 points to §16.5.2; the section-wide *Scaling* paragraph is now headed "Scaling, for all of Automation" so it reads as covering §16.5, §16.5.1 and §16.5.2. Effect on rules: adds a design-stage duty beside the run-stage duty of the 578th move. No deadline, threshold or clock set. Left open for the custodian: whether §16.5.2 reaches only processes designed or revised from now on, or also asks existing processes to be rebuilt; the text is silent, so it reads as applying whenever a process is designed or revised, and nothing sets a date for rebuilding existing ones.
