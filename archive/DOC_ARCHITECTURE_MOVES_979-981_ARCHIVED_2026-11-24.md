**Nine hundred and seventy-ninth move (tax incentives limited to strategic development, with an incentive floor; 2026-11-24; Subcore 24-A, operative; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian: "In our taxation chapter, we haven't solved the problem of the race to the bottom on tax incentives. There ought to be a floor for that, and they ought to be limited to strategic technology and industry development."). What changed:

- New [Subcore 24-A §4.6 Tax Incentives](../subcore_24.010_taxation.md#46-tax-incentives) (first placed as §4.5; see the follow-up below). A tax incentive may support only a technology or industry on an enacted strategic-development list, each entry stating its aim, the market gap, why a tax tool beats a grant or rule, and a measure of success; location alone is never a strategic purpose.
- The incentive floor: no incentive may take a beneficiary's effective tax rate below fifteen percent by default, measured as tax on profits over accounting profit across the group in the polity, with a top-up tax collecting any shortfall. Payable credits count as grants, and small taxpayers on the simplified method are outside the floor. The design follows the OECD/G20 global minimum tax (fifteen percent, top-up, qualified refundable credits as income) and the OECD harmful-tax-practices and nexus criteria (no ring-fencing, no negotiated rulings, IP benefits only in proportion to own research).
- Incentives must be tied to qualifying spending, open on the same terms to all who qualify, repaid in proportion to broken commitments, and reviewed by an independent seat before the vote. Tiers do not bid against each other, and a polity joins and never undercuts any shared minimum-tax agreement.
- §5.1 now bars bidding for location and tax holidays. §1, §2, the §3 *Neutral* test, §4.4, and the §10 outcome report are aligned. The payable-credit period (four years) is registered, and the tax-break lapse period's owner is corrected to §4.4.
- A follow-up (custodian: "yes, the floor should cover general business taxes too"): the floor moves into its own [§4.5 The Profits Tax Floor](../subcore_24.010_taxation.md#45-the-profits-tax-floor) and now binds every tax on business profits, whether the rate is low through a low general rate, a narrow base, deductions, a tax break, or an incentive; every polity that taxes profits enacts the top-up. Small taxpayers on the simplified method and sentients taxed on business income under the one income tax are outside it. Incentives become §4.6 and must stay within the floor; §10 reports businesses below the floor before top-up.
- A second follow-up (custodian: "Please reorganize the bullets and use categories and subbullets"): §4.5 is grouped as *The floor*, *Measuring the rate*, *Holding the floor*, and *Outside the floor*; §4.6 as *Strategic purpose only*, *Form*, *Fair terms*, and *Checks*. No rule changed.
- A third follow-up (custodian: "This should come from the PSO, not the assembly"): the strategic-development list is now published by the independent [Subcore 7-C Public Stewardship Office](../subcore_07.030_public_stewardship_office.md#6-stewardship-guidance-and-the-governing-authoritys-response) with its stewardship guidance, drawing on the Long-Term Outlook, so no assembly can list what it wishes to favor. Assemblies or any sentient may ask for an entry to be added or withdrawn; the office decides with published reasons, open to correction under Subcore 7-C §12 *Records, Accuracy, and Challenge*. Entries are reviewed each Yearly Stewardship Report; a withdrawn entry supports no new incentive, and existing ones end at their lapse. Subcore 7-C §1, §6, §11 (the list is the one part of its guidance with effect outside the office, and it requires no one to offer an incentive), and its placement block are aligned.

**Nine hundred and eighty-first move (one amendment procedure for core, subcore, and implementation text; 2026-11-24; Chapters Twenty-Nine and Thirty-One and the Chapter Three *Corpus* entry, operative; number provisional under MOVE-NUMBER-01).** Decision (custodian, in the project thread: "I think having two different procedures is unnecessary complexity, and readoption sounds like a nightmare."). What changed:

- [Chapter Twenty-Nine §1](../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#1-purpose-role-and-scope) *Purpose* now names the implementation text in the Corpus beside the core and subcore, says all three are amended by the one procedure in §13, and drops the doubled "subject to".
- [Chapter Twenty-Nine §13](../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#3-amendment-procedure-requirements): a change to an implementation text file listed in the Corpus is amended by the same procedure as a subcore change, with heightened review under §3 where it affects rights. A core or subcore change still may not be made as an edit to implementation text. §13.2 adds that, for implementation text, the recorded effective date also names the new edition identifier, and until it is recorded an adopter stays bound by the edition it holds.
- [Chapter Thirty-One §1, §3, and §4](../core_31.000_incorporation.md#3-custody-editions-and-operative-effect): "re-adoption" is replaced by amendment under Chapter Twenty-Nine §13 throughout. Edition pinning and the no-silent-drift and anti-evasion rules are kept: a later edition becomes operative for an adopter only by amendment, on the edition and date recorded in that adopter's adoption chain.
- The [Corpus](../subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry says every tier, implementation text included, changes only by amendment under Chapter Twenty-Nine §13. Its subcore bullet now points to heightened review under section 3 (it said section 2, a stale number).
- Effect on rules: a wider scope for Chapter Twenty-Nine §13 (implementation text now goes through the full amendment procedure, including the hearing gate and the 30-day comment window); the separate re-adoption path is removed. Adoption under §12 still decides which text an adopter first takes up.
