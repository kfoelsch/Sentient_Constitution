## Subcore 3-A: Definition Structure

File: `subcore_03.010_definition_structure.md`. Today's §2 *Definition Components and Joint Satisfaction* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why Definitions Need a Fixed Structure
>
> *In plain terms: a word that can mean whatever is convenient protects no one. This file exists so that every term in the Constitution can be checked against what actually happens.*
>
> A rule is only as strong as the words it uses. If a term like *safety* or *fair treatment* names a goal but says nothing about how to measure it or what must be true in practice, anyone can claim to meet it. This file gives every definition the same four parts (what the term is, how it is measured, how it is assessed, and what must hold) and requires all four to be met for the system as it really runs. That is what lets a sentient hold a system to the words it signed up to.
>

## Subcore 3-C: Burden of Proof, Traceability, and Verification

File: `subcore_03.030_burden_traceability_verification.md`. Today's §2 *Exclusive Enforcement and Burden Allocation* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why the Burden of Proof Exists
>
> *In plain terms: the one who claims to comply is the one who must show it. This file exists because a sentient harmed by a system rarely has the evidence to prove what went wrong.*
>
> The sentient affected by a system usually knows least about how it works, and the operator knows most. If the burden sat with the affected sentient, most failures could never be shown. This file places the burden on whoever claims compliance, and requires the proof to be traceable, open to inspection, and checkable by others, even where security limits how or when it is shown. That keeps a compliance claim from being a promise no one can test.
>

## Subcore 4-A: Data Types

File: `subcore_04.050_data_types.md`. Today's §2 *Determination of Classification* becomes §3, and the new section from §1.1 to §1.3 is inserted before the type catalog.

> #### 2. Why Data Is Typed
>
> *In plain terms: the same rules cannot fit a weather report and a medical record. Typing data says which protections each kind carries, so a system cannot treat sensitive data as if it were open.*
>
> Data about sentients can protect them or be used against them, and the difference often depends on what kind of data it is and who can reach it. Without fixed types, a system can label sensitive data as routine, combine harmless pieces into a profile, or hold back public information it owes. The types and their access bands give every system the same starting point: what is open by default, what only audits may see, what is restricted, and what belongs to its creator.
>

## Subcore 8-A: System Classification

File: `subcore_08.010_system_classification.md`. Today's §2 *Criticality, Concentration, Temporal, and Adversarial Factors* becomes §3; every later section moves down by one.

> #### 2. Why Systems Are Classified
>
> *In plain terms: a water supply and a hobby website should not carry the same duties. Classifying systems puts the heaviest duties where failure would do the most harm.*
>
> If every system carried the full weight of every duty, small and private systems could not exist, and the burden would push work toward those best able to evade it. If duties were chosen by the operator, the systems sentients depend on most would choose the lightest. Classifying by actual impact and dependency sets duties by what is at stake, and the class follows what a system does, not what its operator calls it.
