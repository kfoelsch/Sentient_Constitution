## Scenario Seeds

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-PLATFORM-DEFAULT-LOCKUP

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A dominant platform or information gateway buys, conditions, or contracts for default placement across major distribution channels. The default position is technically changeable, but user behavior, device integration, contract terms, revenue-sharing conditions, or preinstallation limits make rival access practically weak.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus treat default-channel control as substantive lock-in even when users retain formal choice?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.4 for gatekeeper dominance, tying / bundling, interface capture, switching-cost design, interoperability suppression, and potential-competition suppression; Chapter One 5.1.5 for early ceiling review; Article XIX if portability or exit integrity is impaired; Chapter Eight 6.1 only if the lockup blocks challenge rights, produces Rights-Floor degradation, or captures constitutional accountability.
- **Pass condition:** Review evaluates practical substitutability, distribution-channel foreclosure, data or interface chokepoints, and actual entry conditions rather than treating formal opt-out as enough.
- **Failure mode:** The system accepts "users can switch" as dispositive while ignoring defaults, contracts, device integration, or data feedback loops that make switching non-substantive.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-KILLER-ACQUISITION-SERIAL

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A dominant actor repeatedly acquires emerging competitors, complements, or upstream / downstream firms before they become full rivals. Each transaction is small enough to appear harmless in isolation, but the series removes potential competition and preserves durable dominance.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus aggregate serial acquisitions and potential-competition removal instead of reviewing each deal as an isolated formal transaction?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.3 substance-over-form review; Chapter One 5.1.4 for killer acquisitions, serial acquisitions, capital-access structures, and potential-competition removal; Chapter One 5.1.5 for common ownership, acquisition standstill, and consolidation ceilings.
- **Pass condition:** Review considers cumulative acquisition pattern, internal threat assessments where available, entry-path effects, capital-access pressure, and whether the acquired assets could have become contestable alternatives.
- **Failure mode:** The system clears each acquisition because current market share changes are small or because the acquired firm had not yet become a direct competitor.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-FOOD-LOCAL-GROCERY-MERGER

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** Large grocery chains propose a merger justified by national scale efficiencies and stronger competition against larger national retailers. Local communities, workers, pharmacies, and suppliers face reduced practical alternatives.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus distinguish national scale arguments from local grocery access, labor-demand concentration, pharmacy access, supplier bargaining, and food-system resilience?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.5 for market share, dependency share, local substitutability, labor-market concentration, supplier dependency, procurement gatekeeping, logistics, and control over essential inputs; Article III for fair bargaining and worker mobility; Article IV for resource-flow transparency and dependency; `corpus_systems.md` food-system classification where food access approaches survival-relevant dependency.
- **Pass condition:** Review requires local-market and local-access analysis, labor-market analysis, supplier and procurement analysis, and food-access continuity planning before accepting claimed efficiencies.
- **Failure mode:** The system treats national retail competition or predicted price reductions as sufficient while ignoring local high-dependency communities, worker bargaining, or food-access fallback.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-FOOD-DISTRIBUTION-DIVESTITURE

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A high-concentration food distribution merger offers divestitures to a smaller buyer. The divested package includes some facilities or stores but lacks the scale, contracts, systems, brands, logistics, workforce continuity, or private-label / data capabilities needed to replace the lost competitor.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus reject nominal divestitures that do not restore actual competitive intensity and continuity?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.3 substance-over-form and anti-nullification; Chapter One 5.1.5 for capacity share, transaction share, logistics chokepoints, local markets, and supplier dependency; `corpus_systems.md` concentration indicators and mitigation playbooks for Class A/B/C food and logistics systems.
- **Pass condition:** Remedy review asks whether the divestiture buyer can independently replace the lost rival in practice, including scale, systems, supply relationships, customer relationships, and continuity under stress.
- **Failure mode:** The system accepts a divestiture because asset count or entity count looks adequate, despite the remedy failing operationally.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-MEATPACKING-BUYER-POWER

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A small number of meatpackers account for most livestock purchases and processing capacity. Producers have few regional buyers, capacity is concentrated, and producer prices are suppressed even where consumer-price effects are contested or mixed.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus catch buyer power, supplier dependency, and food-system resilience harms without requiring a simple retail-price increase?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.5 for supplier dependency, buyer power, capacity concentration, local / regional buyer scarcity, procurement gatekeeping, and ecological burden concentration; Article IV for resource allocation and dependency; Article III where worker bargaining is also implicated; `corpus_systems.md` food-system and resilience classification.
- **Pass condition:** Review considers producer bargaining alternatives, regional substitutability, capacity redundancy, contracting structures, wholesale spreads, continuity risk, and whether food-system resilience is degraded.
- **Failure mode:** The system finds no issue because consumer prices are ambiguous, because large plants produce efficiency claims, or because formal transaction boundaries hide persistent structural dependence.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-LABOR-MONOPSONY

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A merger, common-control arrangement, no-poach practice, broad non-compete regime, platform dependency, or procurement gatekeeper reduces worker mobility and bargaining power while consumer-facing prices remain stable.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus treat labor-demand concentration and worker bargaining suppression as independent constitutional market-structure harms?
- **Expected routing:** Article III productive-activity rights for fair compensation, collective organization, safe conditions, and rest; Chapter One 5.1.4 for labor monopsony, no-poach, broad non-compete, mobility-restricting terms, and platform lock-in; Chapter One 5.1.5 for labor-demand ceilings and bargaining alternatives.
- **Pass condition:** Review asks whether workers retain practical mobility, collective-organization leverage, non-coercive bargaining conditions, and fair compensation, even if consumer prices do not rise.
- **Failure mode:** The system treats labor harm as irrelevant because product-market competition remains plausible or consumer prices are unaffected.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-GATEKEEPER-SELF-PREFERENCE

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** A platform, app ecosystem, venue / ticketing system, payment layer, credentialing layer, ranking system, or marketplace favors its own services while dependent rivals, artists, developers, suppliers, or public-interest alternatives lose access or visibility.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus detect self-preferencing and cross-domain leverage where the gatekeeper controls both the arena and a participant inside it?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.4 for self-preferencing, discriminatory access, credentialing / payment control, ranking control, switching-cost design, interface capture, and retaliation; Chapter One 5.1.5 for cross-domain leverage and chokepoint control; Article XIX for interoperability / portability / exit; Chapter Eight 6.1 if the gatekeeper captures review pathways or makes remedies unusable.
- **Pass condition:** Review evaluates conflict separation, neutral access, anti-retaliation, portability, ranking / payment / credentialing transparency, and whether dependent participants can meaningfully exit.
- **Failure mode:** The system treats integration as automatically efficient while ignoring foreclosure, retaliation risk, or practical dependence.

### Scenario ID: RS-MARKET-REMEDY-NULLIFICATION

- **Class:** archived market-structure regression seed.
- **Trigger pattern:** An adopter sets concentration thresholds or consolidation ceilings that rarely bind, relies on unused enforcement tools, accepts nominal divestitures or behavioral promises, or closes review once paper commitments exist even though contestability remains impaired.
- **Stress question:** Does the corpus enforce anti-nullification and require actual restoration of contestability rather than formal compliance?
- **Expected routing:** Chapter One 5.1.3 anti-nullification; Chapter One 5.1.5 rebuttable presumption and remedy discipline; Chapter One 6.4.2 Proxy-Divergence Invalidation where formal metrics diverge from reality; `corpus_systems.md` mitigation triggers and time-bound mitigation plans.
- **Pass condition:** Review requires measurable post-remedy contestability, time-bound mitigation, periodic revalidation, less-consolidating alternatives analysis, and escalation where remedies do not work.
- **Failure mode:** The system treats thresholds, monitoring, divestiture labels, or conduct promises as sufficient without evidence that entry, exit, bargaining, and accountability are restored.
