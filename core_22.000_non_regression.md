Chapter Twenty-Two is the constitutional owner of **substantive non-regression and substantive amendment validity** (Substantive Test 1), anti-evasion controls, and validity-protection measures.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: a change that weakens the protections this Constitution depends on is invalid, even if the words say otherwise and even if it is done through procedure, definitions, guidance, a temporary measure, or an emergency label. This chapter is Substantive Test 1.*

**Purpose:** The purpose of Chapter Twenty-Two is to prevent regressive changes to the Constitution. It is a guardrail against alterations that degrade the core constraints, Rights Floors, integrity and verification requirements, and legitimacy and stewardship obligations the rest of the Constitution depends on. No constitutional change is valid if it violates these non-regression requirements.

**Role:** The chapter is the substantive half of change validity. Four tests judge a change: Substantive Test 1 and Procedural Tests 2 to 4. This chapter owns Substantive Test 1:

- **Substantive Test 1** (this chapter), asks what a change actually does, not what it is called, so relabeling a rollback as guidance or a temporary measure does not save it. Amendment validity must also preserve both aims, Flourishing and Continuity, and must not hollow the Tetrad below material stake.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- the non-regression floor (**section 3**);
- Substantive Test 1 (**section 4**);
- the anti-evasion clause (**section 5**); and
- the validity-protection controls the chapter may impose (**section 6**) and the owner non-relocation rule (**section 7**), with the detail of sections 3, 4, 6, and 7 in [Subcore 22-A](subcore_22.010_non_regression_determinations.md#subcore-22-a-non-regression-determinations) (*Non-Regression Determinations*).

The chapter leaves:

- numeric standing measurement to [Subcore 10-D](subcore_10.040_standing_measurement.md#subcore-10-d-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it);
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designation to [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-fourteen-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation);
- Procedural Tests 2 to 4, ratification, and the referral of serious bad-faith conduct to [Chapter Twenty-Three](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#chapter-twenty-three-amendment-ratification-and-procedural-validity):
  - **Procedural Test 2:** procedural validity and publication integrity;
  - **Procedural Test 3:** authority-chain and custody validity; and
  - **Procedural Test 4:** contestability and independent review validity; and
- additive expansion of protection and supremacy relative to other norms to [Chapter Twenty-Four](core_24.000_expansion_supremacy.md#chapter-twenty-four-expansion-supremacy-and-external-legal-orders).

### 2. Why Non-Regression Exists

*In plain terms: a protection that can be quietly removed is not a protection. This chapter exists so that no change can weaken what this Constitution guarantees.*

Protections are most often lost not by repeal but by small steps: a narrower definition, a new procedure, a temporary measure, or an emergency label that never ends. This chapter makes any change that weakens the protections the Constitution depends on invalid, however it is made. It is the substantive test every amendment must pass before the procedural tests in Chapter Twenty-Three are even reached.

**Grounded in:**

  - [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims): both aims, which this chapter serves:
    - [Flourishing](core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing): protections sentients rely on cannot be quietly removed;

<br>

*In plain terms: weakening **Safety (Constitutional Constraint)**, **Truth (Constitutional Constraint)**, **Epistemic Integrity**, **Dignity and Equal Moral Standing**, **Meaningful Agency**, or other Chapter Four protections — including by tightening definitions, raising impossible proof bars, or carving emergencies so they eat the rule — counts as invalid even if the words "amendment" or "interpretation" are on the label. Where a change helps one group and costs another, the minority is favored by default, and no group may be pushed below the minimum.*

No constitutional change is valid if it materially weakens core protections for any covered sentient class. Core protections include:
- Safety (Constitutional Constraint);
- Truth (Constitutional Constraint);
- Epistemic Integrity;
- Dignity and Equal Moral Standing; and
- Meaningful Agency and other Rights Floor protections stated in Chapter Four.

Material weakening includes indirect narrowing through:
- definitions;
- procedures;
- standing gates;
- verification barriers; or
- emergency carve-outs.

**Trade-offs between classes.** A change that helps one covered class and weakens another is judged class by class: no class may be taken below the minimum, and above it the minority is favored by default, under [Subcore 22-A — §3](subcore_22.010_non_regression_determinations.md#3-trade-offs-between-classes) (*Trade-Offs Between Classes*).

### 4. Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity

<br>

*In plain terms: Substantive Test 1 is the checklist — if a proposal would gut Chapter Two constraints, Subcores 3-A to 3-C integrity, Chapter Four Rights Floors, or Chapter Twenty-One legitimacy, it fails, including when the damage is done through procedure, evidence rules, or re-tagging rather than a single blunt deletion. The Constitutional Forum decides whether a proposal fails it, in public, with reasons, after everyone has had time to respond. A change that fixes a real error in a protection, and keeps its core, is a correction and not a weakening. Replacing an office or body is not a weakening if the protection it served stays at least as strong.*

A proposed amendment is invalid if it materially weakens any of the following:
- Chapter Two non-negotiable principle constraints
- **Subcores 3-A to 3-C** integrity requirements
- Chapter Four Rights Floors
- Chapter Twenty-One legitimacy obligations

Invalidity includes indirect narrowing through:
- definitions;
- standing gates;
- evidentiary burden manipulation;
- observability degradation; or
- emergency re-labeling.

**Who decides.** The **Constitutional Forum** decides whether a proposed amendment fails Substantive Test 1, in a published determination with reasons that is open to independent review, under [Subcore 22-A — §4](subcore_22.010_non_regression_determinations.md#4-who-decides) (*Who Decides*). A proposer's own statement is an input, not the determination.

**Replacing a mechanism.** Replacing a mechanism, office, or body is not a weakening where the protection it served stays at least as strong, under [Subcore 22-A — §5](subcore_22.010_non_regression_determinations.md#5-replacing-a-mechanism) (*Replacing a Mechanism*).

**Corrections.** A change that narrows a Chapter Four protection is not a weakening only where the **Constitutional Forum** finds it to be a correction on the findings in [Subcore 22-A — §6](subcore_22.010_non_regression_determinations.md#6-corrections) (*Corrections*). A proposer's label of a change as a correction is never enough.

### 5. Anti-Evasion Clause
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

<br>

*In plain terms: calling a rollback "implementation guidance," burying it in metadata, or calling it an emergency patch does not save it if the real-world effect is the same as a bad amendment.*

Repackaging a regressive change as any of the following does not fix invalidity:
- interpretation guidance;
- implementation memo;
- adoption metadata; or
- emergency temporary measure.

Invalidity remains where substantive constitutional effect is materially equivalent.

Serious bad-faith conduct in amendment or adoption is referred under [Chapter Twenty-Three §11](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#11-invalid-change-handling-and-remediation-continuity) (*Invalid-Change Handling and Remediation Continuity*), not handled here.
