*In plain terms: this section names the bridge: which implementation files can bind, how adoption makes them operative, and which exact edition and custody trail count. Later edits do not silently rewrite what was adopted; a newer draft changes obligations only once it passes amendment under Chapter Twenty-Three and takes effect in the adopter's adoption chain. Pointing here, or to an incorporated file, invokes only the obligations inside the recorded adoption scope.*

**Purpose:** This chapter is the **constitutional incorporation bridge**. It answers three questions:

1. **Which** designated **implementation text** files can bind as incorporated implementation text.
2. **How** adoption and custody make that text operative for an adopter.
3. **What** happens when those files are edited later: an adopter's obligations stay as adopted until the change is validly amended under [Chapter Twenty-Three §3 Amendment Procedure Requirements](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#3-amendment-procedure-requirements) and takes effect in that adopter's adoption chain.

**Role:** This chapter is what makes the implementation files count. Where an adoption is valid:

- The designated **implementation text** files listed in the [**Corpus**](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry in [Chapter Three](core_03.000_definitions_home.md#chapter-three-foundational-definitions) bind as **authoritative implementation text** for the obligations they cover.
- Those files provide detail for carrying out this Constitution. They are read under **Chapter Two** and do not replace the Constitution.
- Combining the files into one document for packaging does **not** change their status, as long as they stay on that list.
- A reference to **Chapter Twenty-Five** or to an incorporated file, anywhere in these volumes, brings in the matching **adopted** obligations, but only **where they apply** and **only within the stated adoption scope**.
- The [subcore](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#subcore) is **not** implementation text. It is part of this Constitution, binds every adopter without separate incorporation, and is pinned by the same edition as the chapters it belongs to.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- custody, editions, and operative effect (**section 3**);
- safeguards (**section 4**); and
- adoption framing and scope of authority (**section 5**).

When a rule points to this chapter or to an incorporated file, it brings in only the obligations that fall within the adopter's recorded adoption scope.

### 2. Why Incorporation Exists

*In plain terms: an adopter is bound by the exact text it adopted, no more and no less. This chapter exists so everyone can tell which text that is.*

Implementation text keeps improving, and adopters adopt it at different times. Without clear rules, an adopter could be held to a draft it never accepted, or escape a duty by pointing to a newer version. This chapter makes implementation text bind only as adopted, pinned to a recorded edition, with a custody chain that shows who held the authoritative text and when it took effect.

*In plain terms: treat designated **implementation text** files like versioned law libraries — only the edition written into the adoption record counts, and keep receipts that prove who held the authoritative text and when it took effect.*

This section sets out how incorporated text is pinned to editions, held in custody, and given operative effect:

#### 3.1 Edition Pinning

When an implementation file is incorporated by reference at constitutional scope:

- It takes effect only if the adoption chain explicitly records the **adopted edition identifier** and **effective date**.
- The text that applies at constitutional scope is the text of that adopted edition.
- The adopter’s current working copy does not take its place, and a newer version published by the source does not apply just because it is newer.
- A later edition takes effect for an adopter only through amendment under **Chapter Twenty-Three section 3**, using the edition identifier and effective date recorded in that adopter’s adoption chain (**Chapter Twenty-Three section 3.3**).

#### 3.2 Custody Chain

Adopters must maintain an **auditable custody chain** for incorporated **implementation text** artifacts. Records must include:

- edition identifier;
- effective date;
- custodian identity; and
- linkage to the adoption document, in a form sufficient for provenance and applicable scope.

The adoption records in the adoption chain and the custody records required above are [Essential Records](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#essential-record). Each must have an offline physical backup in at least three separate places in different regions, held by different custodians.

#### 3.3 Implementation-File Scope

Without limitation, this section covers:

- [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) (*systems implementation file*)
- [corpus_institutions.md](corpus_institutions.md) (*Constitutional Institutions*)
- [corpus_forum.md](corpus_forum.md) (*Constitutional Judicial Systems*)
- [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md) (*Constitutional Joint Structure*)
- any further **implementation text** files listed in the [**Corpus**](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#corpus) entry in [Chapter Three](core_03.000_definitions_home.md#chapter-three-foundational-definitions)

Each incorporated implementation file is binding **only as adopted** through a recorded adoption chain under **Chapter Twenty-Three** and this chapter.

#### 3.4 Process-Layer Alignment

**Sentience-Status Adjudication Record:** This edition lists [`corpus_forum/cf_16.475_sentience_status_record.md`](corpus_forum/cf_16.475_sentience_status_record.md) and [`implementation/schemas/sentience_status_adjudication_record.schema.json`](implementation/schemas/sentience_status_adjudication_record.schema.json) as the dedicated [Sentience-Status Adjudication Record](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#sentience-status-adjudication-record) format. They implement, and do **not** narrow, [Article VI-B](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-b-sentience-status-adjudication-floor) (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*) and the [Chapter Seventeen §4 Escalation and Questions for Ruling](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-escalation-and-questions-for-ruling) (*Sentience-status adjudication* hook). The schema is machine-checkable form, **not** who-counts.

The following **support** verification of custody and adoption framing:

- architecture maps;
- TODO records;
- regression catalogs; and
- evidence logs.

These supporting records **do not**, by themselves, alter constitutional effect absent valid adoption.

Accepted governance-control changes — including custody-chain hardening, edition lineage updates, and incorporation-discipline clarifications — must be recorded in a dated artifact under [evidence/](evidence) (`evidence/<YYYY-MM-DD>/`). That artifact must link the edition identifier, the adoption chain, and the regression coverage (**`RS-XD-003`** and adjacent `RS-XD-*` seeds) that exercises the custody and anti-drift safeguards. The artifact is a **verification aid**; it does not itself constitute adoption.

*In plain terms: quietly swapping in a newer draft — or relabeling a real change as “just a clarification” — does not change what was adopted. Later revisions stay process-layer notes until they pass amendment under Chapter Twenty-Three and take effect in the adopter's adoption chain.*

This section sets out the safeguards that protect incorporated text:

- **No silent drift (anti-drift rule):** Post-adoption edits to implementation text files have **no constitutional effect** for an adopter unless validly amended under **Chapter Twenty-Three section 3** and recorded in that adopter's adoption chain under this chapter. This rule applies to:
  - substantive revisions;
  - editorial compressions presented as non-substantive;
  - renumbering that changes binding cross-reference targets; and
  - silent edition-relabeling without adoption.

    Later implementation-file revisions do **not** displace the adopted edition’s operative effect. They may be taken up by amendment under **Chapter Twenty-Three section 3**; until that amendment takes effect for an adopter, they remain process-layer artifacts without constitutional effect for adopters bound to the earlier edition.

- **Anti-evasion:** Calling a post-adoption edit any of the following does **not** make it count as non-substantive when the real constitutional effect is the same as a substantive change:
  - “clarification”;
  - “correction”;
  - “style alignment”;
  - “non-substantive refactor”; or
  - “editorial compression.”

  The anti-evasion **rule** in **[Chapter Twenty-Two, section 5](core_22.000_non_regression.md#5-anti-evasion-clause)** applies. If an adopter’s working text has drifted from its adopted edition without a valid amendment, the **clearly adopted baseline** with the [Fullest Protective Effect](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#fullest-protective-effect) under **[Chapter Two §15.4.3 Incorporation Layer](core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#1543-incorporation-layer)** governs until the official record is fixed under **Chapters Twenty-Two and Twenty-Seven**.

*In plain terms: this is a model constitution about systems that affect sentients. A body can make it enforceable for itself only by deliberately adopting it through the published rules. If a body has not adopted it, the Constitution cannot be enforced against that body on the basis of adoption. That does not change what the Constitution says or mean its protections can be read more narrowly. Making this Constitution public does not, by itself, change or cancel any applicable law.*

#### 5.1 What It Is

This Sentient Constitution is a **model constitution**. In its own terms, it speaks to systems that materially affect sentients. It becomes **enforceable** for a particular adopter — a jurisdiction, federation, polity, organization, system, or other body — **only after valid adoption** under **Chapter Twenty-Three** (*Ratification and Adoption*) and this chapter. Until then, it may guide, inform, or serve as a reference, but it has no independent power to enforce against non-adopters.

#### 5.2 Who May Adopt

The following rules address who may adopt and the scope of adoption:

- Valid adopters may include — without limitation — constitutional federations, polities, sovereign or cooperative entities, organizations, system operators, system-developer consortia, or any other body that can bind itself and the systems under its authority to these obligations.
- Adoption may cover the whole Constitution or only specified chapters, articles, or obligations, consistent with **Chapter Twenty-Three** and **Chapter Twenty-Two** non-regression rules.
- Partial adoption may not wipe out the non-regression floor.
- Incorporation and partial adoption must not be used to shrink land, consultation, or free, prior, and informed consent (FPIC) duties the adopter already bears under its own law or binding documents.
- Nothing here requires one world government, one federation design, or one adoption method. Different adopters may use different internal procedures consistent with **Chapter Twenty-Three**. That matches the pluralism commitment in **Chapter Twenty-One §3** (*Authorization and Legitimacy of Governing Authority*).

#### 5.3 Adoption and Applicable Law

[Chapter Twenty-Three §12 Ratification and Adoption](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#12-ratification-and-adoption) governs deliberate adoption and its documentation. [Chapter Twenty-Four §7 Relation to Applicable External Law](core_24.000_expansion_supremacy.md#7-relation-to-applicable-external-law) governs limited binding effect, non-displacement of applicable law, and use within an external legal framework.

#### 5.4 What Non-Adoption Means

The substance of this Constitution — including principles in **Chapter Two**, definitions in **Chapter Three**, the Rights Floor in **Chapter Four**, and the other substantive chapters — **does not wait on adoption** to be what this Constitution states. “This Constitution lacks jurisdiction” is correct only about **enforcement** against a non-adopter. It is not a reason to shrink the substance in any analysis that uses this framework. Accepting the framework while denying its substance on jurisdictional grounds is non-compliant with this section.
