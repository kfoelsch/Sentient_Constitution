# Finding record: contested policy entrenched as Rights Floor — 2026-11-15

This page is **process support**, not constitutional text. It cannot change or narrow what the numbered `core_*` files say, and it authorizes no edit to core text. It is the finding record for the third item in [evaluation_hesitations_identification_2026-11-15.md](../plans/evaluation_hesitations_identification_2026-11-15.md) §3, covering method steps 1, 2, 3, and 5. Step 4 (independent read) is still open, and every reading below is the preparer's.

## Result in brief

**Classification: partly a misreading of the text, partly two text gaps, and one deliberate choice to defend.**

- **The numbers are mostly not entrenched as minimums.** The 30-year creative term, the 15 + 15-year invention term, the one-half cap on the fee share and the 50-year draft cap are written as **outer limits on exclusion**. They protect the public against enclosure. Chapter Six states no minimum term for creators or inventors. Only the 1% fee floor runs the other way.
- **Two Articles are fixed in direction, and they are real contested policy.** [Article VII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-d-voluntary-discontinuation-of-ones-own-existence) (the right to decide to discontinue one's own existence, with safeguards) and [Article VII-E](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-e-adult-consensual-commercial-sexual-services-and-sexual-exploitation) (adopting documents must not criminalize adult consensual commercial sexual services) cannot be narrowed under Chapter Twenty-Eight. That is a deliberate choice, and a critic will press it.
- **Text gap 1: two-sided trade-offs.** Chapter Twenty-Eight §2 invalidates a change that "materially weakens core protections for any covered sentient class". Read literally, a change that moves protection from one class to another is invalid even when it is net-better. Nothing says how to judge it.
- **Text gap 2: the binding text names no one to decide "weakening".** No core rule says who proposes a change to the Constitution's own text, and none says who decides that a change fails Substantive Test 1. The non-binding [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) does answer for this repository today: a proposer self-checks against Substantive Test 1 (Lane D) and the custodian decides the merge. That is one person, and its lanes are not enforced before the announcement (AGENTS.md). Nothing carries the decision into an adopted edition.
- **Consequence: there is no named correction route for a mistaken floor.** The route is the ordinary amendment path, and it only passes if the change is not a weakening. That makes the floors a one-way ratchet. The text may intend that, but it does not say so.

The TODO item asked to "confirm and document how a mistaken Article gets corrected, and who decides whether a correction is a weakening". Answer: **by the amendment path in Chapter Twenty-Nine, with no named decider and no correction-specific route.**

## 4. How a correction would run

1. **A change to a Rights Floor is a rights-affecting change.** It gets [Heightened Scrutiny](../../core_05.050_band_oversight.md#heightened-scrutiny), independent review, and contestable publication (Chapter Twenty-Nine §2 and §8), and it may not take irreversible effect before the review windows run (§9).
2. **It must pass Substantive Test 1.** A change that materially weakens a Rights Floor, Chapter One constraint, integrity requirement, or legitimacy obligation is invalid whatever the procedure (Chapter Twenty-Eight §2 and §3; Chapter Twenty-Nine §4). Relabeling it as guidance, a memo, adoption metadata, or an emergency measure does not help (§4).
3. **It must satisfy the purposes in Chapter Twenty-Nine §1:** to "improve protection, clarity, binding effect, or institutional fitness".
4. **Additions are fine.** Chapter Thirty §2 permits new rights, safeguards, and classes where they increase protection.
5. **Article XXVII-A does not help.** Its revisability and revalidation duties cover governance and allocation mechanisms, not the content of a Chapter Six Article. Article XXV-D applies it to Constitutional Forum design.
6. **If the change fails,** it is non-operative from the point of determination, and the adopter publishes a time-bounded remediation plan (Chapter Twenty-Nine §10). The passive wording ("where a change is found invalid") names no one who finds it.

## 5. Scenarios

These are the preparer's readings and need the independent read in method step 4.

**Scenario A: cut the creative term from 30 to 20 years.** The Article protects creators through credit and fair pay (Article IX-D) and protects the public through the cap. A shorter cap keeps every stated creator protection and tightens the public one. On the text I read, it does not weaken a stated floor, so Test 1 should pass, and heightened review still applies because it touches Chapter Six. Separately, an adopter can already set 20 without amending anything, because 30 is a cap. **Classification: passes. The "entrenchment" is not there for caps.**

**Scenario B: raise the creative term from 30 to 40 years.** This weakens the anti-enclosure protection and fails Test 1. **Classification: blocked, as intended.**

**Scenario C: lower the invention fee floor from 1% to 0%.** This helps users and hurts right-holders. Chapter Twenty-Eight covers "any covered sentient class", so the text does not say whether a right-holder's 1% floor is a "Rights Floor protection" that this change weakens. **Classification: text gap 1. The text does not decide it.**

**Scenario D: narrow Article VII-E to allow criminal penalties for buyers only.** This narrows the decriminalization floor, which is a stated Chapter Six protection. It fails Test 1 on any reading. **Classification: blocked, as intended. This is the deliberate choice a critic will press.**

**Scenario E: Article VII-D proves to push vulnerable sentients toward discontinuation in a particular adopter.** The Article already carries an anti-care-substitute rule that bars discontinuation presented as a substitute for required care, and "review must determine whether those floors are being satisfied first". A tightening of that rule would add protection and pass. A loosening would fail. **Classification: handled for tightening; no route for the case where the right itself is judged mistaken.**

**Scenario F: who decides?** Someone proposes Scenario C. Chapter Twenty-Nine §2 requires independent review, §7 requires meaningful contest, and Article XXV-A gives Constitutional Forums competence over "Rights-Floor compatibility" in interpretive determinations. But nothing says that a proposed change to the Constitution's own text goes to a Constitutional Forum, which panel, or what the record looks like. **Classification: text gap 2.**

## 7. Decisions for the custodian

These are choices of meaning, so none was applied.

1. **Direction for two-sided Articles.** Options: (a) each such Article states which side is its floor, and Test 1 asks only whether that side is weakened; (b) a net-protection test with a named reviewer; (c) leave "any covered class" as written and say in the announcement that trade-offs are frozen both ways.
2. **Name the decider.** Options: a Constitutional review panel under CF-23.420 §2.5, the adopter's own body with Constitutional Forum review on challenge, carrying the CONTRIBUTING.md Lane D self-check and independent-review step into core, or leaving it to the amendment process and saying so. A related choice is whether to name who may propose a change to the base text.
3. **Correction route.** Options: (a) keep the one-way ratchet and defend it in the announcement; (b) add a narrow correction route for a floor that independent review finds harms the class it protects, with the highest scrutiny and published reasons; (c) extend Article XXVII-A revalidation to the specific figures in Chapter Six.
4. **Caps and floors in plain terms.** Whether each numeric entry says "outer limit; an adopter may go lower", so a reader does not take 30 as a required term.
5. **Announcement.** Whether the "Open questions" block in [ANNOUNCEMENT.md](../../implementation/adoption/ANNOUNCEMENT.md) gains an entry for VII-D, VII-E and the one-way ratchet.

## 9. Update — 539th move (custodian decisions on gap 2, same day)

The custodian decided decision 2 above in session and chose these terms. Text entered in core:

- **The Constitutional Forum decides** whether a proposed change fails Substantive Test 1, in a published determination with reasons ([Chapter Twenty-Eight §3](../../core_28.000_non_regression.md#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity); [Article XXV-A](../../core_06.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-a-bounded-interpretive-mandate-and-public-reasons)). Review follows Article XXV-B.
- **Anyone may propose.** A proposal reaches the forum through a **hearing gate**: sign-off by at least 1% of the electorate for governance voting, or the governing authority puts the change forward ([Chapter Twenty-Nine §12.3](../../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#123-proposal-hearing-and-comment-window)).
- **Amendment comment window:** no shorter than 30 days, the Tier C outer bound, counted from the **last recorded change** to the proposal's wording. Any later change restarts the count. The forum may extend the window but not shorten it.
- The new window is named in [CJS-0.105 §4](../../corpus_joint_structure/cjs_00.105_registry_and_reading_rules.md#cjs-0105-4-time-window-names) (block E). It is not called a "response window", because that name already means a Chartered Party's window to answer.

Drafted beyond what the custodian stated, for the custodian to keep or strike: the governing-authority route (without it the gate would block an adopter's own change, which section 2 already requires to be heard); the duty to say how responses were treated; and "any recorded change restarts the count", with no exception for typographical fixes.

Still open: other routes to the hearing gate beyond those listed in section 10; a minimum count for very small electorates and a lower-share route for very large ones; decisions 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 in section 7.

## 10. Update — 540th move (two more routes to the hearing gate, and a steward duty)

The custodian asked for two more routes to the hearing gate and for a steward duty to represent stakeholder interests. A search of Chapter One §16–§19, Article XII-A, the Chapter Five *Stakeholder* and *Stewardship* entries, and the stewardship implementation files found **no rule that stewards must represent the interests of their stakeholders**. Chapter One §17 binds stewards to resist unlawful instructions and keep alignment under pressure. Article XII-A requires representation pathways for each affected class, but as a design duty on governance, not a duty on the steward.

Text entered in core:

- **New [Chapter One §17.7 Duty to Represent Stakeholder Interests](../../core_01.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#177-duty-to-represent-stakeholder-interests).** A steward must act for the interests of the stakeholders of the system, institution, or decision domain served, and must not put the steward's own advantage or an operator's or owner's interests ahead of material stakeholder interests. It asks for weighing interests in each materially affecting decision (shown in the Act Record), a route for stakeholders to raise concerns that is heard and answered, and use of the open routes to ask for a change where the rules materially harm stakeholders. It is not a duty to obey stakeholders, it never overrides Safety, Truth, or a Rights Floor, and no instruction, contract, or implementation text may narrow it.
- **Two more routes in [Chapter Twenty-Nine §12.3](../../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#123-proposal-hearing-and-comment-window):** a vote by a majority of the members then seated of **any forum**, and a decision by a majority of the stewards then holding the role in **one Class A, B, or C system or institution**. Together with the 1% sign-off and the governing-authority route there are now four routes, and any one is enough.
- **Counting and guards:** votes and decisions are recorded on the Forum Case Record with dissent; each distinct subject counts once; creating, appointing, or seating members or stewards to form a majority is non-compliant conduct by whoever does it; and a Constitutional Forum panel that voted to bring a proposal to hearing does not decide it (a panel with no overlapping decision-makers decides, drawn from the CF-23.470 reserve roster).

Drafted beyond what the custodian stated, for the custodian to keep or strike: the self-judging guard; the one-subject-counts-once and no-stacking rules; the recording of dissent; reading "Class C or higher" as Class A, B, or C (the corpus's own wording) and "stewards of a Class C or higher system / institution" as the stewards of **one** such system or institution and not all of them together; and the limits in §17.7 (not a duty to obey, never overrides Safety, Truth, or a Rights Floor).

Not done: `project/PROCESS_SYSTEM_INSTITUTION_CROSSWALK.md` (which says all Chapter One subsections are tagged) and `project/plain_language/PRINCIPLE_TO_DEFINITIONS_MAP.md` have no row for §17.7; the map is generated from a 2026-10-29 evidence file and regenerating it changed 27 unrelated lines, so it was left as it was. ## 11. Update — 541st move (trade-offs, outer limits, quick fixes)

Custodian decisions: **(1)** two-sided trade-offs favor minorities by default; **(4)** caps are outer limits; the items drafted beyond the request in §9 and §10 are **kept**; the rest (decisions 3, 5, 6, the open gate questions, the independent reads) need more information first.

Text entered in core:

- **[Chapter Twenty-Eight §2 Trade-offs between classes](../../core_28.000_non_regression.md).** Minimums first (no class below what a protection guarantees to every sentient); above them the minority is favored (a change fails if it weakens the minority to strengthen another class, and passes that test if it weakens a non-minority to strengthen the minority); the minority is the class with less market or institutional power, else the smaller class, by real effects; the default is set aside only on a published determination that the minority keeps the core of the protection, with classes named in the justification under Chapter Twenty-Nine §3.
