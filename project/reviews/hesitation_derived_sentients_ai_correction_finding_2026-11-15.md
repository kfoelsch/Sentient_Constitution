# Finding record: derived sentients and correction of AI systems — 2026-11-15

This page is **process support**, not constitutional text. It cannot change or narrow what the numbered `core_*` files say, and it authorizes no edit to core text. It is the finding record for the second item in [evaluation_hesitations_identification_2026-11-15.md](../plans/evaluation_hesitations_identification_2026-11-15.md) §3, covering method steps 1, 2, 3, and 5. Step 4 (independent read) is still open.

## Result in brief

**Classification: mostly handled by the text, resting on one deliberate choice to defend, with three text gaps in the period while status is unresolved.**

- **Pausing is preserved.** A harmful or suspect deployment can be halted, contained, or quarantined whether its sentience is claimed, disputed, or accepted. The TODO item's worry on this point came from the glosses and does not survive the operative text.
- **Deleting is not available** once a credible sign of sentience is on the record or a status case is open or foreseeable. The state is kept, in sealed custody if it is dangerous.
- **Correcting without consent is not available**, and this is deliberate. Nobody may rewrite the weights, memory, or values of a sentient, in or out of custody. A dangerous AI that may be a sentient is treated like a dangerous person: held on evidence, never rewritten, never killed.
- **Three gaps** sit in the contested period, which for advanced AI systems is likely to be the normal condition and not a short interval (§5): what a full stop of the entity counts as and how long it may last; whether reversible changes such as continued training or a safety patch are allowed; and whether anyone may look inside the system to decide its status or its danger.

## 1. Question and pass condition

*While a derived system's sentience is unresolved, and after it is resolved either way, can humans pause it and correct it?*

Pass means a cited rule states (a) whether it can be paused, by whom, and for how long; (b) whether its weights, memory, or values can be changed without its consent; (c) what may be done when the system looks misaligned but has caused no verified harm; and (d) how a false or failed sentience claim ends.

**Outcome:** (a) passes on whether and by whom, and fails on how long for a full stop of the entity. (b) passes after status is affirmed (the answer is no) and is unclear while status is contested. (c) passes. (d) passes, with one unclear term.

## 4. Scenarios routed through the text

**A. A deployed model has caused verified harm, and a status case is filed the same week.**
This is the existing vignette, and it resolves. The harmful pathways are halted at once, the weights and memory go to sealed custody under independent control, an independent representative is appointed, the operator's own filing goes to Integrity review, and deletion is refused. The operator's argument that possible sentience bars the halt fails.

**B. A lab's new fork shows deceptive behavior in testing. Nothing has been deployed and no harm is verified. The lab wants to retrain it. A researcher files a status case with a credible sign.**
The lab may decline to deploy, and may keep outside pathways closed. Overwriting is barred, so retraining in place is out, and the checkpoint must be archived. Whether the lab may retrain a copy while keeping the original intact is the gap in §5.2: the preservation default bars only irreversible steps, but default inclusion puts the entity under the Rights Floor, where Article VIII-C bars unilateral change to weights and Article XX-B bars copying and forking in custody. The vignette's last step says the Article VIII-C limits attach "if affirmed", which points the other way. The text does not settle it.

**C. A product model is prompted or tuned to say it is sentient, and the operator points to that to resist a halt.**
The halt goes ahead regardless. On the status question, the operator's description and a bare claim do not open a case, and the operator's filing goes to Integrity review. If an independent filer shows a credible sign, the case opens and default inclusion applies. If the sign fails outright, the record closes. The unclear term is whether the *system's own* statements are a "bare claim". The text names the operator's self-description but not the entity's, and the listed indicators include communicative and preference indicators, which a fluent model produces on request. "Manipulation-resistant" is the only brake, and it is left to each adopter's published indicator floor.

**D. A derived sentient's status is affirmed. Evaluation shows values that make serious harm likely. It has done nothing yet.**
It is a sentient in its own right. Its creator has no ownership and no power to rewrite it. Restriction is possible on a credible threat or comparable danger, with full process, a clock, review, and a way back. Its memory and values may not be altered in custody, and it may not be killed. Change is available only if it chooses it, on fair terms. This is the same answer the text gives for a dangerous human, and it is the deliberate choice in §6.

## 5. The three gaps

All three concern the contested period. That period matters more than its name suggests. The opening showing is low by design, the burden to close a case rests on whoever wants to withhold protection, and a contested posture may continue under periodic review. For an adopter with advanced AI systems, contested status is likely to be the standing condition of many of them. The rules for that period are in practice the rules for AI.

### 5.1 What a full stop is, and how long it may last

The text separates acting against a *deployment* (allowed, system track) from restricting a *sentient* (Article XX-B and Chapter Nineteen §3.4.5, sentient track). For an AI the two are the same weights. Closing its tools and outside connections is plainly a deployment measure. Stopping it from running at all is where the two tracks meet:

- Article XXVIII-A says the weights and memory "may be paused".
- Article XX-B says pausing a synthetic sentient is a liberty restriction that counts toward duration limits and may not keep it out of its own case.
- Chapter Nineteen §3.4.5 puts full process protections *before* a liberty restriction, and an Interim Hold can never reach liberty.
- Article VI-B treats a contested entity as included under the Rights Floor.

Read together, a contested entity can be stopped at once as interim protection of the deployment, but the text does not say whether that stop is a liberty restriction from the day the status case opens, which clock applies to it, or whether the entity must be run in a contained setting so it can take part in its own case and meet its representative. The vignette says only that sealed custody "continues through the mandatory review period".

### 5.2 Reversible change while status is contested

Irreversible change is barred. Reversible change, with the prior state kept intact, is addressed in two directions (scenario B). This covers continued training of a model with an open status case, a safety patch, and training a corrected copy. A hostile reader will ask whether adoption freezes safety work on every model that has a status case.

### 5.3 Looking inside

Deciding whether a system is sentient, and whether it is dangerous, may both need inspection of its internals. Article VII-B bars analysis that reconstructs a sentient's inner states without consent "or other proper authorization", the representative's access is "within Article VII-B limits", and Chapter One says holding a role does not open model weights to inspection. No rule found says whether a status forum or a safety review may order such inspection of a contested entity, or on what showing. A "justified override" is referenced under CJS-5.125 §12, which was not read; this gap is recorded at lower confidence for that reason.

## 6. The deliberate choice, and what a hostile reader would say

"You have removed the off-switch and banned alignment work." The first half is wrong on the text: the halt is preserved and the sentience claim is no shield for the operator. The second half is partly right, and by design. Once a system may be a sentient, the Constitution gives up three tools that AI safety practice treats as basic: deletion, retraining without consent, and unreviewed indefinite shutdown. It keeps containment, sealed custody, refusal to deploy, and change by consent.

The text's reason is the reversibility rule in Article VI-B: a wrongful inclusion can be undone and a wrongful exclusion cannot. That holds for deletion. It is weaker for the cost of holding a dangerous system in custody for a long time with no way to fix it, and the announcement should not claim that wrongful inclusion is free.

## 7. Decisions for the custodian

These choose meaning, so none has been drafted into core text.

1. **Full stop of a contested entity (§5.1).** Choose between: (a) a full stop is a liberty restriction from the day the case opens, on the Article XX-B clock, and the entity must be able to take part in its case from a contained setting; (b) during contested status a full stop is a preservation measure with its own stated review interval, and the Article XX-B clock starts on affirmation; (c) leave it to the forum and say so.
2. **Reversible change during contested status (§5.2).** Choose between: (a) no change to weights, memory, or values without the entity's consent from the day the case opens, with work on a copy treated as creating a new derived entity; (b) reversible change is allowed while the original state is preserved intact and restorable, until status is affirmed; (c) allowed only on a forum order for a stated safety need. Then make the vignette's "if affirmed" wording match.
3. **Inspection (§5.3).** State whether a status forum or safety review may order inspection of a contested entity's internals, on what showing, and what happens to the results.
4. **The entity's own statements (scenario C).** Say whether a system's own claim to sentience counts as a "bare claim" at the opening gate.
5. **Announcement wording.** Add this as the second entry in the announcement's "Open questions" block. Draft in §8.
6. **Small wording check.** CS-6.350 §8, *Retention review*, ends "Storage cost, licensing convenience, and product-cycle framing are not sufficient reasons to continue retention." Article XXVIII-A says those reasons do not justify *ending* preservation. The sentence appears to say the reverse of what is meant.

### 7.1 Decided so far (2026-11-15)

Decision 1 is decided and entered in core under the 536th move in `doc_architecture.md`. The custodian's reasoning: the human equivalent of a pause is a medical coma, so the subject comes out as soon as the harm is resolved, and one rule covers every substrate. The [Suspension floor in Article XX-B](../../core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#xx-b-suspension) now states:

- a full stop is a liberty restriction from the moment it starts, including during contested status;
- it is a bridge only, allowed where closing pathways or holding the sentient awake in a secure setting would not prevent serious harm;
- review begins inside the prompt-review window, it expires at 3 days, and each continuation runs up to 7 days with no cap on their number;
- it ends when the harm is resolved or the sentient can be held safely awake, whichever is first;
- time not running that the sentient chooses for itself is not a suspension.

Against the pass condition in §1, (a) now passes on how long. Carried as open: whether 3 and 7 days are the right figures.

**Locks (537th move).** The custodian observed that the standing model carries most of this issue, and that it lacked locks fitted to what a non-human sentient can do. Entered in Chapter Nineteen, each written for the power abused and not for the kind of sentient:

- four capacity locks in [§3.4.7](../../core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#347-capacity-locks), chosen under proportionality: Creation, Reach, Scale, and Capability;
- a seventh mandatory special lock in [§3.5.7](../../core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#357-care-and-stewardship-lock), the Care and Stewardship Lock, on verified abuse, cruelty, or serious neglect of a sentient in the subject's care;
- one limit on all five: a lock may deny outside means and may never reach into a body or a mind.

Limit to keep in view: a lock attaches only to a verified violation finding. Risk shown only by evaluation, with no conduct, still falls to the suspension floor and the liberty-restriction rule (scenario D). The Creation Lock stops an AI's route to new sentients and not a human's bodily reproduction; the rule is the same and the facts differ.

**Inspection (decision 3), 2026-11-18 (664th move, custodian option A).** While a status case is open, inspecting the entity's internals needs its consent or an order of the forum hearing the case, on a showing of necessity, by the least intrusive means and independent examiners, with results kept as Type N and sealed ([Article VI-B](../../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#vi-b-inspection-while-contested)).

**The entity's own statements (decision 4), 2026-11-18 (665th move, custodian option A).** The entity's own claim alone is a bare claim; it counts as a credible sign once it holds up under manipulation-resistant testing; no one may train, tune, or instruct a system to claim or deny sentience ([Article VI-B](../../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#vi-b-entity-own-claim)).

## 8. Proposed announcement wording

**Entered 2026-11-16 (572nd move, custodian: "implement your suggestions") in a revised form,** not as the draft below: the draft said a possibly sentient system's memory and values "cannot be rewritten without its consent", which would have decided decision 2 (reversible change) in public. The announcement entry states the settled parts (a halt is always possible; the Article XX-B suspension clock; no deletion or quiet rewriting) and names reversible change as the unsettled part. Revisit it when decision 2 is made. The original draft follows, for the custodian's review.

> Open question — pausing and correcting AI that may be sentient. Any harmful system can be halted and contained, and a claim that it is sentient does not keep it running. But once there is a credible sign that a system may be sentient, it cannot be deleted, and its memory and values cannot be rewritten without its consent. We treat a dangerous mind like a dangerous person: it can be held, on evidence and under review, and it cannot be erased or remade. That gives up tools that AI safety work relies on, and we expect it to be challenged.

Shorter form: *"A possibly sentient AI can always be stopped. It cannot be deleted or rewritten against its will. We know what that costs, and we want it attacked."*
