## 1. Question and pass condition

**Question.** For each of five cases, does [Chapter Twenty](../../core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md) attach an anti-constitutional misconduct designation (ACMD) where it should, and refuse one where it should not?

**Pass.** Organized capture is designated. Failed good-faith administration, dissident reform, mass protest, and investigative reporting are not designated by Chapter Twenty, though ordinary Chapter Sixteen to Nineteen consequences may still follow a verified violation. The answer must come from the text, not from an evaluator's sympathy.

**Assumption for every case.** Chapter Fifteen has already placed the verified violation at Violation Axis slot 7 or above (at least one LEQU of verified constitutional loss, [Chapter Fifteen §8](../../core_15.000_standing_measurement.md#8-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes)). Below slot 7, Chapter Twenty never opens, so the tests start at the hardest point.

## 3. How a case moves through Chapter Twenty

1. Slot gate: the Chapter Fifteen slot is 7, 8, or 9 (§3.1, §4 step 1).
2. Unified incident: one episode or campaign, not split (§5.1).
3. Criteria: one or more of the six §5.3 criteria on the verified record (§6).
4. Carve-outs: §5.4 (good-faith drafting) and §5.5 (dissent and peaceful protest).
5. Eligibility: §6 says a single catastrophic incident, a sustained high-gravity pattern, criterion 5, or criterion 6 "may make the ACMD eligible".
6. Safeguards: independent review, contestable notice, reasoned publication (§7).

## 5. Gaps found

**Gap A: no-intent criteria plus the scale limb can designate failed good faith.** For case 2, every step of the text can be met without intent, knowledge, or recklessness: slot 7 by impact, criterion 4 by causal contribution, the sustained-pattern route by institutional scale. Chapter Twenty-One already uses a mental-state line for its patterns ("purpose or reckless disregard" in §4.2; good-faith decisions in §6.2 and §7.2 are not misconduct), but Chapter Twenty §5 does not. The frame in §3 ("conduct that captures or hollows out constitutional duties") points the right way but is not an operative test.

*Options.* (a) Criteria 3 and 4, standing alone (without criterion 1, 2, 5, or 6), support an ACMD only where the record shows purpose, knowledge, or reckless disregard, or continuation after a fair chance to correct. Recommended: it matches Chapter Twenty-One's existing line and the first limb of §5.2, and still catches capture that hides behind incompetence. (b) A new §5.6 good-faith administration carve-out modeled on §5.4. (c) Leave as is and rely on §7 review.

**Gap B: the §5.5 carve-out does not name press, journalism, or protected reporting.** Article XI-C protects journalistic activity by function, and Chapter Twenty-One excludes it from one pattern, but the Chapter Twenty carve-out that says "satisfy no criterion" covers only Article XI-D conduct. *Option:* add press and journalistic activity under Article XI-C and protected reporting (whistleblowing) to §5.5, with the same separable-conduct rule. This is a smaller change and follows from Gap A's decision.

**Note 1 (no change proposed).** §6's eligibility sentence ("may make the ACMD eligible") reads as permissive. Whether one of the four routes is *required* is not stated. Option (a) under Gap A would reduce the weight on this sentence. If the custodian prefers, the sentence can say "must" in the same edit.

## 6. Resolution

The custodian approved both recommendations ("please close the gaps and implement your suggestions"), and the 961st move made them: Chapter Twenty §5.3 gains *Rollback or causal contribution standing alone* (Gap A, option a), and §5.5 now names press and journalistic activity under Article XI-C and protected reporting, with a criterion 2 sentence on selecting, withholding, and publishing (Gap B). Note 1 is left as it stands; the new §5.3 rule takes the weight off it. Case 2 now **holds** and case 5 **holds** without the gap.
