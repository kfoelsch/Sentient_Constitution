# Desk test: affordable, accessible remedy capacity for minimum-viable adopters — 2026-11-23

This page is **process support**, not constitutional text. It cannot change or narrow any numbered `core_*` file. It is the record for the TODO item *Test affordable, accessible remedy capacity using the minimum viable adopter profiles, including actual staffing, cost, delay, and claimant burden* in [TODO.md](../../TODO.md). It is a desk test: no adopter has run remedy, so the caseload and hours below are stated assumptions, not observations.

## 1. Question and pass condition

**Question.** For each profile in [MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md §6](../../implementation/adoption/MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md#6-profiles), can a claimant get a remedy matter opened and decided inside the tier windows, at a cost the claimant can bear, with the staffing and money the profile names?

**Pass.** The profile names a remedy route a claimant can use; its staffing band can carry a plausible caseload inside the windows; the text bars cost and complexity from blocking the claimant; and the text says what happens when capacity runs out.

## 2. Rules the test applies

- **Windows.** Tier outer bounds: A 7 days, B 14, C 30, L 60, P 90; overruns without a documented extension are non-compliant, and interim protection continues ([Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#time-scale-settings)).
- **Parity.** Funded remedy capacity no less than lock capacity per class, with a starting reserve ([Chapter Nineteen §5.2](../../core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#52-remedy-parity-funding-minimum); [Chapter Eighteen §3.4](../../core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#34-remedy-parity-and-lock-preconditions)).
- **Publication and failure.** Each remedy system names its owner, funding, and succession, publishes backlog and milestone figures ([CI-25.210 §3–§4](../../corpus_institutions/ci_25.210_remedy_systems_institutional_redress_capacity.md)), and a capacity-failure claim is heard outside the body ([Chapter Twenty-Two §6.5](../../core_22.000_forum.md#65-capacity-failure-is-heard-outside-the-starved-body)).
- **Claimant burden.** Cost, delay, opacity, and gatekeeping that defeat a challenge break [Article XVII-C](../../core_04.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-c-verification-accessibility), and whoever relies on a barrier must justify it. The first-touch desk may not use fees to defeat routing ([Subcore 22-A §3](../../subcore_22.010_forum_intake_default_venue.md#3-intake-triage-body-first-touch-desk)).

## 3. Throughput assumptions

- One loaded FTE gives about 1,500 productive hours a year (the profile page's convention, §6).
- Work per remedy matter, from intake to remedy commencement: Tier L or P about 10 hours, Tier C about 25, Tier B about 40.
- To keep waits inside the windows, a desk should run at no more than about 70% of its hours. Above that, queues grow faster than staff can clear them.

So one remedy FTE carries about **105** Tier L matters, **42** Tier C matters, or **26** Tier B matters a year.

## 5. Gaps found

**Gap 1: profiles 1 and 2 name no remedy turnaround or cost to claimants.** Operations-guide and Track A users are not adopters, so the Chapter Twenty-Three windows do not bind them as law. But the profile page tells them to name an independent reviewer and says nothing about how fast it answers or who pays. *Proposed (process support, not core):* add a **Remedy route** row to profiles 1 and 2: name the reviewer, publish its turnaround against the Tier C window (30 days), and state that a claimant pays nothing to file.

**Gap 2: no intake ceiling or overflow trigger in profile 3.** A small in-house remedy file meets its windows only up to a caseload ceiling, about 42 Tier C matters per remedy FTE a year. The text already makes chronic overrun a capacity failure (Chapter Twenty-Two §6.5), but only after the windows break. *Proposed (process support):* profile 3 states the ceiling arithmetic and tells the adopter to name, in advance, the hosted body or CF-22.470 reserve that takes overflow when published intake passes about 70% of capacity.

**Gap 3 (core, for the custodian): no explicit rule that filing for a remedy is free to the claimant.** Article XVII-C bars costs that defeat a challenge, and Subcore 22-A bars fees used to defeat routing, but no core rule says plainly that opening a remedy matter costs the claimant nothing. *Option:* one sentence in Chapter Twenty-Two or Subcore 22-A that filing, intake, and routing are free to the claimant, and that fees or cost-shifting may not fall on a claimant for a matter that is heard. This is a choice for the custodian.
