# Draft: Process-ownership disputes (proposal, not adopted text)

**Status:** working draft for author review. Not part of the numbered `core_*` files. Nothing here has been run through the audits (`make plain-language-audit`, lexical guardrails).

**Problem under review.** Chapter Twenty-Three assigns forum-family jurisdiction and, in §4.7, allows provisional rulings on adopted implementation operational law. CI-23.205 already requires institutions to name procedure-map owners, records, challenge routes, and review schedules. This draft explores whether those sources need a clearer rule for disputes about local procedure ownership and procedure changes, including when an operational dispute becomes a constitutional question. That gap statement remains subject to review; this proposal adds no third governance layer to Preamble §3.3.

**Current role mapping (working, not a new office).** The Corpus does not define generic offices called *process steward* or *stakeholder steward*. For this draft:

- **Procedure owner** means the local owner of a required procedure-map step, named by the institution under [CI-23.205](../corpus_institutions/ci_23.205_procedure_integrity_contestability_secondary_review.md) and placed within the authority/lane map under [CI-8.205](../corpus_institutions/ci_08.205_institutional_design_separation_of_powers.md).
- **Affected party** means any materially affected sentient, with representation and accommodations governed by applicable participation and standing rules. This draft proposes the principle that every materially affected sentient may participate in and challenge a procedure change affecting them; [Article X-C](../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-x-c-stakeholder-role-and-participation-rights), [Article XII](../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xii-stakeholder-system-participation-representation-and-due-process), and Chapter Five's [Procedural Fairness](../core_05.055_band_participation.md#procedural-fairness) supply the existing participation framework. Where a binding-governance pathway applies, [CI-6.205 §1](../corpus_institutions/ci_06.205_transparency_participation_accessible_pathways.md) identifies who owns stakeholder-status identification and requires the participation-window and notice records; it does not establish an exclusive filing role for a "stakeholder steward."
- **Forum roles** follow Chapter Twenty-Three: Institutional forums hear institutional-duty and scope disputes; Constitutional forums decide questions sent to them for ruling under §5.

These names are descriptive mappings to current records and functions, not new steward classes. The proposed filing principle is open access for any materially affected sentient, with representatives participating under existing standing rules.

## Part 1. Candidate core text (placement unresolved)

**Placement is unresolved.** The earlier target, Chapter Twenty-One §3, is stale: current Chapter Twenty-One is the anti-constitutional-misconduct designation chapter. The closest current location appears to be Chapter Twenty-Three §4.7 (*Provisional implementation operational law*), which already covers Institutional-forum rulings on adopted implementation duties and sends inseparable constitutional questions to §5. This is a candidate insertion point for author review, not an approved relocation or core edit. If retained, use Chapter Twenty-Three house style and preserve §3 anti-self-judging and §5 question-for-ruling routes.

*In plain terms: the local procedure-map owner may change how and when assigned steps run, with notice and a chance to object. The owner may not change what affected sentients are owed. If the owner and an affected party dispute whether a change crosses that line, an independent forum decides.*

**Process-ownership disputes.** A dispute over who owns an operating procedure, or whether a procedure change was within the local procedure-map owner's authority, is a **process-ownership dispute**. *Local procedure-map owner* refers to an existing role assignment under CI-23.205, not a new office.

- **Owner envelope.** The local owner named for a procedure-map step may change how, when, by whom, on what record, and on what clock an assigned duty is carried out. That owner may not change what is owed, to whom, or at what threshold.
- **Floor-touching change.** A procedure change is **floor-touching** if it would, in practice, do any of the following:
  - delay a remedy or lengthen a clock beyond the **Article XXVI-C** tier outer bounds;
  - narrow who may participate, be represented, or contest;
  - remove, hide, or make costlier a published challenge path;
  - reduce the record available to an affected sentient or to a reviewing forum.
- **Cumulative effect and anti-evasion.** Assess a procedure change together with related changes to the same or connected procedures where their combined practical effect could cross any floor-touching trigger. A decision-maker must look to actual operation and control, not labels, sequencing, separate records, or the size of each step in isolation. A party challenging the changes need only identify a plausible connection and cumulative effect; the forum may order production of the change records and decide whether to consolidate review. This proposed rule follows the existing anti-evasion and look-through approach in [Chapter Twenty §4.4](../core_17.000_standing_integration.md#104-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority) and cumulative-effect assessment in [Collective Harm Boundary](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#collective-harm-boundary).
- **Participation and filing.** Any materially affected sentient may file a process-ownership challenge and participate in its resolution. A representative may act where permitted under existing standing rules. A procedure owner may also file when materially affected by a demand that changes its assigned procedure or imposes an unfunded or open-ended duty. Notice, access, accommodation, representation, and participation windows follow the existing participation framework; this rule creates no exclusive steward gate.
- **Floor-touching changes are constitutional questions.** They are not decided by the procedure's owner alone. The question goes to a Constitutional forum for ruling under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../core_23.000_forum.md#5-escalation-and-questions-for-ruling).
- **Change record and contest window.** A material procedure change must carry a public change record (reasons, expected effects, and the clocks or paths it touches) under the **Material-change record** floor in **Article XXVII-B**. The notice and contest window use the applicable A/B/C/L/P materiality tier and existing stage targets and outer bounds in [Chapter Twenty-Three §6](../core_23.000_forum.md#6-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) and [CF-23.445 §3.1](../corpus_forum/cf_23.445_performance_backlog_publication_accessibility.md#cf-23445-31-stage-targets-and-outer-bounds); do not create a second clock table. Where the change responds to an emergency, any immediate temporary measure, notice restoration, challenge, and continuation must follow [Chapter Twenty-Three §6.1](../core_23.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden), including its tier outer bounds and continuation burden. This cross-reference applies existing windows to procedure changes; it does not authorize a new emergency exception. Retroactive change of a pending matter's procedure is not allowed unless the affected sentient consents or an interim order under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../core_23.000_forum.md#5-escalation-and-questions-for-ruling) permits it.
- **Lead forum.**
  - Disputes about ownership or scope of a procedure go first to the **Institutional** forum, which already applies **section 4.7** operational law.
  - A dispute whose primary issue is that the owner's own process is biased, captured, or abusive is covered by the **cross-forum anti-self-judging rule** and routes as that rule assigns.
  - Where the dispute turns on whether a change is floor-touching, the Institutional forum sends that question to a **Constitutional** forum for ruling under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../core_23.000_forum.md#5-escalation-and-questions-for-ruling). It keeps the operational remainder.
- **No self-judging by the rule-writer.** The office or local procedure owner that wrote or changed a procedure may not be the sole final decider of a challenge to that change. Role placement and separation follow **CI-8.205**; local procedure maps, challenge routes, and secondary review follow **CI-23.205**.
- **Interim rule.** While a process-ownership dispute is pending, the prior published procedure stays in force unless an interim order under **section 5** says otherwise. One coordinating forum or rule resolves conflicting interim orders, as **section 5** already requires.

## Part 2. Companion text for the implementation corpus (outline only)

For a later implementation proposal, the likely home is the source file [CI-23.205](../corpus_institutions/ci_23.205_procedure_integrity_contestability_secondary_review.md), not the `corpus_institutions.md` landing page and not the core. It should connect to the existing event-level participation and notice records in [CI-6.205 §1](../corpus_institutions/ci_06.205_transparency_participation_accessible_pathways.md) where a binding-governance pathway changes. CI-6.205 §4 is a periodic governance report; it should not replace an event-level change record.

Outline only:

1. **Change-record template.** Fields: what changed; the procedure-map owner and authority/lane; version and effective date; reasons and expected effects; clocks, records, participation and challenge paths touched; floor-touching self-assessment; contest window and challenge route; and the prior version. Link to the applicable CI-6.205 §1 notice and participation-window records rather than duplicating them.
2. **Floor-touching self-assessment.** The owner states yes or no for each of the four triggers above. A "no" can be challenged.
3. **Contest window and emergency timing.** Use the existing materiality-tier windows and stage targets in Chapter Twenty-Three §6 / CF-23.445 §3.1, and the existing emergency notice-restoration and continuation rules in Chapter Twenty-Three §6.1. No second window schedule or new emergency exception is proposed.

## Part 3. Integration notes

### Option B connection: Standard Process Recognition

The post-adoption Option B outline is named **Standard Process Recognition** ([resolved TODO record](../archive/TODO_RESOLVED_2026-10-23.md)); it is not a new form of System Alignment Certification. The floor-touching test above is the proposed guardrail for that outline: it marks when a procedure change raises a constitutional question that the process owner cannot decide alone. It does not itself establish a process class, a process-certification duty, or a requirement to obtain recognition before ordinary operation. Option B remains an outlined post-adoption goal unless the steward separately brings it into the core.

The draft previously used *certification* for sending a question between forum families. Chapter Twenty-Three §5 describes that route as sending a question to a Constitutional forum for ruling. The proposed clauses now use that current routing language, keeping the forum question distinct from System Alignment Certification and the proposed name **Standard Process Recognition**. These are terminology clarifications in this working draft; they do not settle the six open design questions below or authorize core edits.

**What this reuses.** Institutional-forum jurisdiction and provisional operational-law rulings under **Chapter Twenty-Three §4.7**; the question-for-ruling route in [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../core_23.000_forum.md#5-escalation-and-questions-for-ruling); the §3 anti-self-judging assignments; interim protection and coordinating-forum language; the **Article XXVI-C** anti-delay floor and **Article XXVII-B** material-change record; procedure-map and challenge requirements in **CI-23.205**; stakeholder notice and participation-window records in **CI-6.205 §1**; and role/separation maps in **CI-8.205**.

**What is new.** The term *process-ownership dispute*, the *floor-touching* test, the contest window, and the "prior procedure stays in force" interim default.

**What it does not do.** It does not add a governance layer to Preamble §3.3 and does not change who may govern. It stays on the Stakeholder System Participation side and relies on forum review.

**Candidate edit footprint, subject to placement decision.** Chapter Twenty-Three §4.7 (candidate subsection), one pointer from Chapter Twenty-Three §2.2 (*Mixed stakes and routing asymmetry*), the Chapter Twenty-Three Trace widgets, a CI-23.205 implementation companion, and generated indexes. No core or implementation text has been edited. Translations would follow after English is settled and separately authorized.

## Part 4. Open questions for the author

1. **Floor-touching test — answered for this draft.** Keep the four narrow triggers listed above. Assess their practical and cumulative effect, so a sequence of small changes cannot evade the floor. Ordinary form changes remain outside the constitutional referral unless their combined effect reaches a trigger.
2. **Who files — answered for this draft.** Any materially affected sentient may file; representation follows existing standing rules. Make this a participation principle in the proposal. The exact core placement remains for human editing.
3. **Windows and emergencies — answered for this draft.** Reuse Chapter Twenty-Three §6 and CF-23.445 §3.1 tier windows, with §6.1 governing emergency measures and restoration of notice/challenge. No separate clock table or new exception.
4. **Aggregation — answered for this draft.** Add the cumulative-effect and anti-evasion principle above, drawing on existing anti-evasion/look-through and collective-harm patterns. This is proposed wording, not adopted doctrine.
5. **Procedure-owner standing — answered for this draft.** A procedure owner may file when materially affected by the challenged demand, on the same participation basis. Existing independence and anti-self-judging rules govern adjudication; the owner cannot be sole final decider of its own challenge.
6. **Pre-release stance — answered.** Core files will be human edited. This document remains a supporting proposal for that work; it does not authorize assistant edits to core files.

**Still open:** Exact placement in Chapter Twenty-Three, whether the companion change-record template belongs in CI-23.205, and the human-edited core wording and scope.
