> **Choices this draft makes (all provisional, for the custodian to confirm):**
> 1. **One office for the whole electoral cycle.** It keeps the electorate roll (the core job in the TODO item), and also runs elections and referendums and counts and certifies results (§5). Most constitutions give these to one independent electoral management body; splitting the roll from the count adds a hand-off that can be gamed. Alternative: the roll only, with elections run by each tier's assembly secretariat under published rules.
> 2. **The selection-funding register stays here (§6).** The office keeps the disclosure register of [Subcore 27-B §3](../../subcore_27.020_selection_funding.md#3-disclosure-register-and-protected-identity-route) and enforces the caps. The protected-identity decision, which Subcore 27-B gives to "an independent seat", is a separate panel inside the office that never sees campaign strategy. Alternative: a separate funding-integrity seat.
> 3. **The office runs the public draws (§7).** It runs the draws for the upper assemblies under [Subcore 27-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](../../subcore_27.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool) and, where an Independent Body's designating text does not say otherwise, the appointment draws of [Chapter Seven §3](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent), which now cover every forum family (940th move). That takes the governing authority's last hand off those draws. Its own seats are drawn by an Integrity Forum registrar, so it never draws itself. Alternative: each body runs its own draw.
> 4. **No standing judgement.** The office records governance-voting standing locks and their lifting as forums and Chapter Eighteen decide them; it never decides a lock, a sentience status, or a subject count itself.
> 5. **Defaults:** roll open for inspection all year; a correction decided within 14 days, and within 3 days in the 30 days before a vote; the roll closes for a vote no earlier than 14 days before it; preliminary results within 24 hours of the close of a vote; certified results within 14 days; a full public audit of a random sample of ballots after every vote; selection-funding gifts in the register within 7 days, and within 24 hours in the 14 days before a vote.

# SUBCORE 7-[E]: ELECTORAL OFFICE — WHO VOTES, AND WHETHER THE COUNT IS TRUE

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: one independent office keeps the list of who may vote, runs the votes and the lotteries that fill public seats, counts the results in public, and publishes who paid for each campaign. No one who wants to win a vote controls any of it.*

**Purpose:** Subcore 7-[E] makes sure that every sentient entitled to vote can vote, that no one votes who is not entitled or votes twice, and that every result, and every public draw, can be checked by anyone.

**Role:** Subcore 7-[E] is the constitutional owner of **electoral administration**: the electorate roll, the running of elections and referendums, the count, the selection-funding register, and the public draws. Where it sits:

- [Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](../../core_27.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) and [Subcore 27-C](../../subcore_27.030_voting_entitlement.md#subcore-27-c-voting-entitlement) say who may vote. This office applies those rules; it does not change them.
- [Chapter Twenty-Four](../../core_24.000_assemblies.md#chapter-twenty-four-assemblies) and [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3](../../core_27.000_governance.md#3-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority) say what is elected and how. The office runs the vote by the method they set.
- Forums decide disputes about entitlement, results, and draws. The office decides first, and its decisions can be challenged.

**Scope:** The office:

- keeps the electorate roll for every tier (§4);
- runs elections, recall votes, and community referendums, and counts, audits, and certifies their results (§5);
- keeps the selection-funding disclosure register and checks gifts against the caps (§6);
- runs the public draws that fill seats by lot (§7); and
- reports each year on turnout, access, errors found, and how each was corrected (§8).

**Out of scope:** The office does not decide who governs, set electoral boundaries or methods (those are set in advance under Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.2), decide a sentience status, a subject count, or a standing lock, endorse or oppose any candidate or question, or decide any matter that belongs to a forum.

### 2. Why the Electoral Office Exists

*In plain terms: whoever controls the voter list or the count can win without winning. So neither may sit with anyone who stands to gain.*

The Constitution gives every sentient an equal and lasting vote, but a right to vote is only as good as the list it depends on and the count that follows. An incumbent who keeps the roll can quietly drop opponents' supporters; one who counts the ballots can shade the result; one who runs a lottery can pick who wins it. This Constitution also adds questions no older electoral system had to answer: when a new sentient has served its residence period, how many subjects a sentient that runs in many places is, and when a sentient whose status is still being decided joins the electorate. Those answers must be the same for everyone and visible to everyone. An independent office that keeps the roll, runs the vote and the draw, and publishes every step lets any sentient check that the result is the one the electorate chose.

**Grounded in:**

- Preamble:
  - [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad): the legs this file carries:
    - [Participation](../../subcore_03.060_apex_participation_leg.md#participation): every entitled sentient is on the roll and can vote;
    - [Oversight](../../subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight): the roll, the count, and the draws are run outside those who compete;
    - [Accountability](../../subcore_03.040_apex_accountability_leg.md#accountability): every result and every correction is published and can be checked;
- Chapter Twenty-Seven:
  - [§6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](../../core_27.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility), whose entitlement, residence, and one-subject rules the roll applies;

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office meets every test for an Independent Body, and no candidate, party, assembly, or executive can choose its members, cut its money, or tell it what to do.*

The office is an Independent Body and meets [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) in full. For this office:

- **Decision seats:** drawn by lot from the candidates who pass the office's published competency gate, under [Subcore 27-E §3 Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways](../../subcore_27.050_role_holders_interpretive_bodies.md#3-role-criteria-appointment-standards-and-removal-pathways). The office does not run its own draw: an Integrity registrar under [CF-22.415 §8](../../corpus_forum/cf_22.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-22415-8-integrity-registrar) (*Integrity Registrar*) runs it by the same published rule.
- **No candidates:** no one may hold a decision seat or senior staff post in the office while a candidate, a candidate's agent, or an officer of a party or campaign, or within one full electoral cycle after.
- **No dual roles (added for this office):** no one may at the same time hold a seat in the office and a seat in any assembly, executive, or forum whose members are elected or drawn by the office.
- **Funding:** its floor covers a full electoral cycle and may not be cut between the call of a vote and the certification of its result.
- **Access to information:** under *Access to information* in Chapter Seven §3, the office may obtain the records it needs to keep the roll from any public body that records births, creations, arrivals, departures, branches, merges, deaths, sentience-status decisions, and standing locks. It obtains only what the roll needs, and uses it for nothing else.

### 4. The Electorate Roll

*In plain terms: the roll lists everyone who may vote and no one else. You can see your own entry, fix a mistake, and challenge a refusal, and the rules for who counts are the same for every kind of sentient.*

The office keeps one electorate roll for each tier, applying [Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](../../core_27.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility) and [Subcore 27-C](../../subcore_27.030_voting_entitlement.md#subcore-27-c-voting-entitlement) as written:

- **Who is on it:** every sentient who meets the published eligibility criteria and whose residence period is complete, without its having to apply where the office already holds the facts. A sentient may also apply.
- **Residence period:** the office counts the one-year residence period under [Subcore 27-C §6 Joining the Electorate](../../subcore_27.030_voting_entitlement.md#6-joining-the-electorate) from the date that section names (birth or creation, joining, or a branch's election to be distinct; a merged sentient keeps the earlier date), and counts time before adoption. It publishes the method once, for every kind of sentient, and applies no other waiting period.
- **One subject, one entry:** each distinct subject has one entry, however many places it runs in. A branch that elects to be distinct gets its own entry when its own residence period is complete; a merge leaves one entry.
- **Pending entries:** an entity whose sentience status or subject count is still being decided under [Article VI-B](../../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-b-sentience-status-adjudication-floor) (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*) has a pending entry, so that its residence time is counted. The entry becomes active on the day the status and count are settled and the residence period is complete. The office never decides a status or a count itself; it records what the forum decides.
- **Standing locks:** the office records a lock on the governance-voting pathway under [Subcore 27-C §3 Governance-Voting Entitlement](../../subcore_27.030_voting_entitlement.md#3-governance-voting-entitlement), and its lifting, only on the record of the forum or Chapter Eighteen decision that sets or lifts it, on the day it takes effect. It records a withheld durable political voice under [Subcore 27-C §8](../../subcore_27.030_voting_entitlement.md#8-durable-political-voice-floor-and-restoration) only on a final designation under Chapter Twenty, and restores it on the record of restitution or a met milestone.
- **Your own entry:** every sentient can see its own entry and the facts it rests on at any time, free of charge, and can ask for a correction. A correction is decided within the correction period (by default, 14 days, and 3 days in the 30 days before a vote).
- **No silent removal:** no entry is removed or suspended without notice to the sentient, with the reason and the route to contest it. Removals in bulk are barred: each removal rests on a fact about that sentient.
- **Closing the roll:** the roll for a vote closes no earlier than the closing period before it (by default, 14 days). A sentient wrongly left off may still vote by a provisional ballot that is counted once its entitlement is confirmed.
- **Privacy:** the roll is personal data under *Information handling and privacy* in Chapter Seven §3. The office publishes counts by tier, area, and kind of sentient, never a list of names, except to candidates and auditors under a published lawful rule that bars any other use.
- **Creation to shift a vote:** where the office sees a pattern of new entries that suggests sentients were created, or branches induced to be distinct, to change a vote, it reports the pattern to an Integrity Forum. The entries stand: under Subcore 27-C §6 the created sentients keep their own entitlement.

### 5. Running and Counting Votes

*In plain terms: the office runs every public vote by rules set before it starts, counts in public, checks a sample of ballots by hand after every vote, and publishes everything anyone needs to check the result.*

The office runs every election, recall vote, and community referendum under [Chapter Twenty-Four §7 Community Referendums](../../core_24.000_assemblies.md#7-community-referendums) and [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3.2.3 Contested Selection, Opposition Protection, and Peaceful Succession](../../core_27.000_governance.md#323-contested-selection-opposition-protection-and-peaceful-succession), using the method published in advance under [Subcore 27-F §3 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](../../subcore_27.060_binding_collective_choice.md#3-records-gates-and-method-neutrality):

- **Access:** every entitled sentient can vote in a form it can use, including sentients with disabilities and non-human sentients, at no cost to it. The office publishes how each form of voting works and keeps the secrecy of each vote.
- **Equal treatment of candidates:** every candidate and every side of a question gets the same information, the same deadlines, and the same access to observe.
- **Open count:** candidates, parties, and any sentient may observe every stage of the count, by a published observer rule.
- **Results:** preliminary results are published within the preliminary-results period (by default, 24 hours after the close of the vote), and certified results within the certification period (by default, 14 days).
- **Audit after every vote:** before a result is certified, the office checks a random sample of ballots against the count, by a published risk-limiting method, and widens the check until the method's confidence is met. The sample, the method, and the findings are published.
- **Records anyone can check:** the office publishes the records the count rests on, in a form that lets anyone redo it without revealing any sentient's vote, as Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.2 *Verifiable outcomes* requires.
- **Machines:** any software or device used to record or count votes is published for inspection, leaves a record a person can check, and is tested by an outside reviewer before each use.
- **Emergencies:** where an emergency under [Chapter Twenty-Four §8 Emergencies](../../core_24.000_assemblies.md#8-emergencies) may make a fair election impossible in an area, the office, not the executive, certifies whether it is, publishes its reasons, and sets the new date within the limits of that section.

### 6. Selection-Funding Register

*In plain terms: who paid for each campaign is in a public, searchable list, kept by the same office that runs the vote, and checked against the caps.*

The office keeps the disclosure register of [Subcore 27-B §3 Disclosure Register and Protected-Identity Route](../../subcore_27.020_selection_funding.md#3-disclosure-register-and-protected-identity-route) and applies the caps and limits of Subcore 27-B §5 and §6:

- **Timely entries:** each gift is entered within the entry period (by default, 7 days, and 24 hours in the 14 days before a vote).
- **Protected-identity panel:** the "independent seat" of Subcore 27-B §3 is a panel of the office drawn separately from its other seats. Its members see the donor's identity and the risk, never a campaign's plans, and do not sit on decisions about that campaign's other filings.
- **Breaches:** the office publishes each apparent breach of a cap or limit and refers it to the forum that hears it. It does not itself impose a sanction.

### 7. Public Draws

*In plain terms: when a public seat is filled by lottery, the office runs the lottery, so no assembly or executive can choose who wins it.*

The office runs, by the published method of [Subcore 27-D §4 Published Integrity Rules](../../subcore_27.040_sortition_integrity.md#4-published-integrity-rules):

- the draws that fill the seats of upper assemblies from the elected members of smaller ones, under [Subcore 27-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](../../subcore_27.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool); and
- the appointment draws of every Independent Body under *Appointment by sortition* in [Chapter Seven §3](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent), including the forum families, except its own draw (§3) and where a designating text names another body.

Each body keeps its own competency gate and conflict screen; the office runs the draw from the pool the body certifies, publishes the pool size and the result together, and does not change the pool.

### 8. Records, Accuracy, and Challenge

*In plain terms: the office says each year how many mistakes it made and how it fixed them, and anyone can challenge an entry, a result, or a draw.*

- **Yearly report:** each reporting year the office publishes turnout and access figures by tier, area, and kind of sentient, the number of corrections, removals, provisional ballots, and complaints, the results of every post-vote audit, and every error it found and how it was corrected.
- **Challenge:** a refusal of an entry, a removal, a correction, a certified result, or a draw may be challenged under *Audit and challenge* in Chapter Seven §3. A challenge to a result filed before certification is decided before the result takes effect, unless the forum finds that it could not change the outcome.
- **Remedy:** a successful challenge to a result leads to a recount, a rerun of the affected vote, or a correction of the result, as fits.

### 9. Proportional Scaling

*In plain terms: a small community can share an electoral office or borrow one, but every voter keeps the same protections.*

*Scaling* in Chapter Seven §3 applies. A small adopter may share an electoral office with other adopters, or use another adopter's office, by published agreement. Even then, each roll is kept separately for each adopter's electorate, every sentient keeps its access to its own entry and its routes to contest, and the post-vote audit is never skipped.
