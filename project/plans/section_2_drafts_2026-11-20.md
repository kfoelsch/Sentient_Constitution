### Chapter Two

File: `core_02.000_definition_structure.md`. Today's §2 *Definition Components and Joint Satisfaction* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why Definitions Need a Fixed Structure
>
> *In plain terms: a word that can mean whatever is convenient protects no one. This chapter exists so that every term in the Constitution can be checked against what actually happens.*
>
> A rule is only as strong as the words it uses. If a term like *safety* or *fair treatment* names a goal but says nothing about how to measure it or what must be true in practice, anyone can claim to meet it. This chapter gives every definition the same four parts (what the term is, how it is measured, how it is assessed, and what must hold) and requires all four to be met for the system as it really runs. That is what lets a sentient hold a system to the words it signed up to.
>

> #### 2. Why Definition Integrity Exists
>
> *In plain terms: a definition can be beaten without breaking it openly, by narrowing it where it is applied or by splitting a harm into pieces too small to count. This chapter exists to close those routes.*
>
> Systems rarely say they are ignoring a rule. More often they keep the words and change what they mean in practice: a test is run on a sample that avoids the problem, a harm is split across offices, or a duty is redefined at the point where it applies. This chapter says what counts as failing a definition, including these quieter ways, so that meeting the words while defeating their purpose is still a failure.

### Chapter Four

File: `core_04.000_burden_traceability_verification.md`. Today's §2 *Exclusive Enforcement and Burden Allocation* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why the Burden of Proof Exists
>
> *In plain terms: the one who claims to comply is the one who must show it. This chapter exists because a sentient harmed by a system rarely has the evidence to prove what went wrong.*
>
> The sentient affected by a system usually knows least about how it works, and the operator knows most. If the burden sat with the affected sentient, most failures could never be shown. This chapter places the burden on whoever claims compliance, and requires the proof to be traceable, open to inspection, and checkable by others, even where security limits how or when it is shown. That keeps a compliance claim from being a promise no one can test.
>

### Chapter Seven

File: `core_07.000_charters.md`. Today's §2 *When System and Institutional Charters Are Required* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why Charters Exist
>
> *In plain terms: no one can tell whether a system or institution stayed within its limits unless the limits were written down first. This chapter exists so those limits are public before anyone relies on them.*
>
> Every later check (certification, standing, forum review) asks whether a system or institution did what it was allowed and promised to do. Without a published charter, that question has no fixed answer, and the limits can be redrawn after the fact. This chapter requires a System Charter or Institutional Charter at the scale where it matters, says what each must state, and keeps it true over time. It does this without making anyone ask permission to form or to start work.

>
> *In plain terms: an evaluation is only trustworthy if someone independent reviews it and the result is published. This chapter exists so that no system certifies itself.*

This chapter sends the result to independent forums, in a fixed order, with a published outcome and a gate before a high-impact system goes live. > #### 2. Why Certification Records and Recertification Exist
>
> *In plain terms: a certificate is only as good as the record behind it and the date it was last checked. This chapter exists so certification stays true after the decision.*
>
> Systems change after they are certified: new versions ship, owners change, and risks appear. A certificate that is never re-checked becomes a label that outlives its truth. This chapter keeps the outcome on a record that the system does not control, sets when the system must be checked again, and lets sentients challenge a certification that has gone wrong. It also keeps certification apart from standing, so a certificate is never treated as a verdict on anyone's conduct.

### Chapter Fifteen

File: `core_15.000_standing_assessment.md`. Today's §2 *The Three Questions* becomes §3.

> #### 2. Why Standing Records Exist
>
> *In plain terms: before anyone is credited or held to account, the facts must be settled and written down. This chapter exists so that standing rests on what happened, not on rumour, popularity, or a desired result.*
>
> A society that credits help and answers harm needs a fair way to establish what actually happened. Without one, recognition goes to the popular and blame to the unpopular, and an allegation does the work of a finding. This chapter opens the standing pipeline by asking only what happened, on verified facts, and keeps help and harm on separate records so one can never erase the other. Rating and consequences come later, in other chapters.

> #### 2. Why Standing Measurement Exists
>
> *In plain terms: once the facts are settled, how good or how harmful they were has to be measured the same way for everyone. This chapter exists so like cases get like measures.*
>
> Without a shared scale, the same harm could be called minor in one place and grave in another, and the same help ignored or celebrated depending on who gave it. This chapter measures verified contribution and verified violation on separate axes of one common scale, never netted into a single score. That keeps measurement consistent and stops a sentient's good work from being used to excuse harm, or their past harm from erasing their good work.

## Part C: section 2 for the two new chapters

### Constitutional Map

(new chapter, from Preamble §3.2 and §4–§9)

> #### 2. Why the Map Exists
>
> *In plain terms: a text this large needs a guide that shows how the parts fit and who owns each topic. This chapter exists so a reader can find the rule that answers their question.*
>
> The Constitution spreads its work across many chapters, a subcore, and four adopted corpora. A sentient trying to find out what they are owed, or an adopter trying to find a duty, can lose their way. This chapter is the overview and ownership guide: what each part does, which chapter owns which topic, how the pipelines run, and how the tiers relate. It adds no rule of its own.
>

### Independent Bodies

(new chapter, from Chapter Eight §11)

> #### 2. Why Independent Bodies Exist
>
> *In plain terms: some public jobs (keeping money sound, publishing honest findings, supervising banks) only work if the officials doing them cannot be leaned on. This chapter exists to make that independence real.*
>
> Where an official's decision can be overturned by a phone call, the public gets the decision the caller wants. The jobs that most need to be trusted are the ones most tempting to capture. This chapter sets the rules every independent body follows (selection by lot, protected funding, open decisions, and no override) and lists the bodies this Constitution creates, with each one's promises to sentients. Each body's operating rules are in its subcore file.
>
