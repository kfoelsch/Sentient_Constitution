# CHAPTER [F]: PUBLIC FINANCE OFFICE — FUNDING THE CONSTITUTIONAL MACHINERY WITHOUT STEERING IT

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Corpus placement (non-operative): file structure and reading rules</span></strong></summary>

> This chapter would be **part of the Sentient Constitution** and **binding only together** with the other numbered `core_*` files read as one text.
>
> - **Constitutional owner:** the office that holds, allocates, disburses, and accounts for the public money that keeps forums, registrars, remedy systems, oversight bodies, and the Public Information Office running; the funding floors that protect them; research funding at forum request; and the bar on using money to steer any forum, finding, or record.
> - **Not owned here:** how much any one system must pay into shared infrastructure ([CS-6.335](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.335_resource_allocation_funding_stewardship.md#cs-6335-resource-allocation-and-funding-stewardship)); how an institution raises fees or stays solvent (**CI-6.210**, **CI-6.215**); who answers for a forum's adequacy ([Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability)); who holds governing authority ([Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship)).
> - **Implementation owner:** a new corpus-institutions file (formula, reserves, ledger fields).
>
> Reading order: §1 purpose → §2 why the office exists → §3 independence → §4 funding floors → §5 no steering by money → §6 research funding → §7 other programs → §8 ledger → §9 the office's own funding → §10 audit and challenge → §11 reserve fund → §12 shortfalls and emergencies → §13 scaling.

Chapter [F] is the constitutional owner of **public funding of the constitutional machinery**.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

<br>

*In plain terms: rights are only as real as the forums that enforce them, and forums are only as real as their funding. This chapter puts that funding in one accountable office, and makes sure the office can pay for the machinery without being able to steer it.*

**Purpose:** Chapter [F] makes sure the bodies that enforce this Constitution are funded well enough to work, and that whoever holds the money cannot use it to steer them. It does this through one independent office that pays by published rule, keeps a public ledger, and answers to an outside check.

**Role:** Chapter [F] is the constitutional owner of public funding of the constitutional machinery. Where it sits:

- [Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability) requires forums and remedy systems to be adequately funded and keeps each forum's primary-stakes family answerable for its own adequacy. This chapter supplies the money path that meets that requirement; it adds no new answerable party.
- [Chapter Twenty-Three §2.2.2](../../core_23.000_forum.md#222-incentives-and-non-substitution) bars payoffs and cost rules from replacing proper routing or a real merits decision. This chapter applies that bar to funding.
- [Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) governs who holds governing authority, including the authority to set the total budget under §4.
- The Public Information Office chapter runs forum-requested research and checks this office's figures. This office pays for that work and does not direct it.
- The Public Monetary Office chapter issues currency and public debt. This office may not issue currency or borrow, and the Public Monetary Office may not decide spending.
- A later corpus-institutions file carries the formula, reserve sizes, and ledger fields. It may not narrow this chapter.

**Chapter scope:** The office holds, allocates, disburses, and accounts for public money for:

- forum families, their benches, intake triage bodies, registrars, and backup bodies;
- remedy systems under [Chapter Twenty §4](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems);
- oversight, audit, and independent verification bodies under [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification);
- research that a forum requests;
- the Public Information Office; and
- other programs the governing authority funds through it.

The chapter's sections cover:

- why the office exists and what it is grounded in (**section 2**);
- the office's independence, appointment, and seats (**section 3**);
- funding floors for forums and remedy systems (**section 4**);
- the bar on steering by money (**section 5**);
- research funding at forum request (**section 6**);
- wellbeing programs, public revenue, and business activity (**section 7**);
- the public ledger (**section 8**);
- the office's own funding and the Public Information Office's funding (**section 9**);
- audit and challenge (**section 10**);
- the reserve fund (**section 11**);
- shortfalls and emergencies (**section 12**); and
- proportional scaling (**section 13**).

**Out of scope:** The office administers money. It does not decide any matter, finding, classification, or Standing Record, and it does not set the policy of any program it pays for. The chapter leaves cross-system funding (what one system owes to shared infrastructure) to [CS-6.335](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.335_resource_allocation_funding_stewardship.md#cs-6335-resource-allocation-and-funding-stewardship), how an institution raises fees or stays solvent to **CI-6.210** and **CI-6.215**, and case-specific forensic support to **CF-23.430**. Where this chapter is silent, the rest of the Constitution governs.

### 2. Why the Public Finance Office Exists

*In plain terms: whoever controls a body's money can control the body. If forums depend on yearly favors from the people they rule on, they are not really independent, however good their procedures look on paper.*

This section explains the reasons for the chapter. It is non-operative: the binding rules are in [§3](#3-independence-and-placement) through [§13](#13-proportional-scaling).

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter One principles:
  - [§18.3 Separation of Duties](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties);
  - [§19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture), especially [§19.1.2 What Incentives Must Not Do](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#1912-what-incentives-must-not-do) and [§19.3 Misalignment Detection](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#193-misalignment-detection); and
  - [§6.1 Correction and Remedy](../../core_01.000_values_principles.md#61-correction-and-remedy).
- Chapter Six Articles:
  - [Article XIV-B](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xiv-b-right-to-redress-and-remedy) (*Right to Redress and Remedy*);
  - [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*);
  - [Article XVII-A](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-a-auditability-and-observable-evidence) (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*);
  - [Article XIII](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-non-capture-influence-and-anti-corruption-safeguards) (*Non-Capture, Influence, and Anti-Corruption Safeguards*), with [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*);
  - [Article V-A](../../core_06.000_rights_part_a.md#article-v-a-dependency-mapping-and-resource-flow-transparency) (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*); and
  - [Article XXVII-C](../../core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxvii-c-timely-resolution-and-anti-delay-floor) (*Timely Resolution and Anti-Delay Floor*).

**The problem it answers.** The Constitution already says that forums and remedy systems must be adequately funded, and that chronic underfunding is a failure ([Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability)). It did not say who holds the money, how much each body gets, or what stops the holder from using the budget as a lever. Without that, three failures are easy:

- **Starving by neglect:** a forum's budget drifts below its caseload until backlog becomes the decision.
- **Steering by budget:** funding moves toward bodies whose rulings please the funder and away from those whose rulings do not, with no single act that looks like interference.
- **Research by favor:** a forum that needs evidence must ask someone with a stake in the answer to pay for it.

**Why this approach.** Several traditions reached the same answer on their own:

- **Protected judicial pay:** Article III of the United States Constitution (1787) bars reducing judges' pay while they hold office. Alexander Hamilton explained why in *Federalist No. 79* (1788): "a power over a man's subsistence amounts to a power over his will."
- **Independent audit institutions:** the INTOSAI *Lima Declaration* (1977) treats financial independence as part of what makes a public audit body independent: it must have the means to do its job, and may go directly to the body that decides the budget when those means are cut.

The chapter turns those lessons into four design choices:

- **Rules, not discretion:** a published formula sets each floor from real workload, so no one has to ask for favors and no one can grant them quietly.
- **Cycle protection:** floors cannot be cut mid-cycle, so a budget cannot punish a ruling already made.
- **Separate offices:** the office that pays is not the office that produces evidence, and neither sets the other's budget, so money cannot shape the evidence.
- **Public books and outside checks:** a public ledger, an independent audit, and a challenge route make steering visible and answerable.

**How it carries the Aims and the Tetrad.** Funded, independent forums keep remedy real, which serves [the Two Constitutional Aims](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims). Within the [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):

- **Oversight:** a public ledger and an independent audit.
- **Accountability:** an answerable office and a challenge route.
- **Participation:** public comment on formula changes.
- **Timeliness:** floors tied to caseload, so backlog is funded instead of left to grow.

These reasons do not add duties. They explain why the duties in §3 to §13 take the shape they do.

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office is its own body, with the standard protections every independent body gets. On top of those, the people who decide funding may not sit on anything they fund.*

**Independent Body:** The Public Finance Office is an Independent Body under [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), and meets every standard requirement there. For this office:

- it sits outside the Material Control Line of every forum family and body it funds, of the governing authority except for the appointment and budget-setting powers this chapter names, and of the Public Information Office; and
- its own funding is protected under *Protected funding* in Chapter Eight §11, as §9 applies it.

**Seat map:** Every materially binding funding act places the four seats as follows:

| Seat | Who holds it for a funding act |
|------|--------------------------------|
| Ask or act | the Public Finance Office |
| Check | an independent financial auditor outside the office's Material Control Line |
| Keep the official record | the Integrity registrar under **CF-23.415 §6** |
| Hear the challenge | an Integrity forum, with backup routing under §10 |

**No dual roles (added for this office):** In addition to Chapter Eight §11, no sentient, office, or AI system may at the same time hold a funding seat in this office and:

- sit on a bench or panel that the office funds, or advise one on a pending matter; or
- hold any seat in the Public Information Office.

### 4. Funding Floors for Forums and Remedy Systems

*In plain terms: each forum gets at least a published minimum, worked out by a published formula from its real workload. The minimum cannot be cut mid-cycle, and it cannot be cut quietly.*

**The floor.** Each funded body has a **funding floor** for each funding cycle. The floor is worked out by a published formula from:

- the caseload, backlog, and tier figures published under **CF-23.445** (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*);
- the capacity standards set by Technical Forum Domains under [Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability);
- the [remedy-parity funding minimum](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-parity-funding-minimum); and
- accessibility, interpretation, and representation costs that intake must carry.

**The total.** The governing authority under Chapter Twenty-Seven sets the total budget. The total must be at least the sum of the floors. A total below that sum is a [Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability) chronic-underfunding failure on its face, and the governing authority must publish why it happened and how it will be cured.

**Cycle protection.** A floor, once set for a cycle:

- may not be reduced during that cycle, except under §12 after the §11 reserve fund has been drawn;
- may be raised during the cycle when published caseload figures cross a threshold the formula names; and
- is reauthorized for the next cycle on a multi-year schedule published in advance.

**Changing the formula.** A change to the formula:

- is published with its reasons and a comparison of old and new floors for every funded body;
- is open to public comment for a published period; and
- takes effect no earlier than the next cycle.

A formula change that would lower the floor of a body with a pending matter against the governing authority or the office is presumed to be steering under §5 unless shown otherwise.

### 5. No Steering by Money

*In plain terms: money can keep a forum running. It must never decide what the forum decides. The general rule against steering by money, gifts, and apparent conflicts lives in Articles XIII-C and XIII-D; this section adds what is special to the office that holds the purse.*

**General floor:** The funding-integrity, gifts-and-benefits, and apparent-conflict floors of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*) apply to the office, the governing authority, and every funder of the office, with records kept under **CI-8.210 §4** (*Gifts, Apparent Conflicts, and Funding Integrity*).

**Added for this office:** In addition, the office must not:

- condition funding on a forum's docket order, routing, or choice of panel;
- tie funding to a classification or a Standing Record; or
- see a pending matter's record, except the aggregate figures that §4 uses.

These apply the rule in [Chapter Twenty-Three §2.2.2](../../core_23.000_forum.md#222-incentives-and-non-substitution) that payoffs and cost rules must not take the place of proper routing or a real merits decision.

**Pattern evidence (added for this office):** Beyond the Article XIII-D (*Funding Integrity*) pattern rule, either of the following is evidence of steering and shifts the burden to the office or the governing authority to show a lawful, published reason:

- funding moves toward bodies whose rulings favor a funder with a material stake; or
- discretionary money outside the formula grows as a share of the total.

### 6. Research Funding at Forum Request

*In plain terms: when a forum needs research to decide well, the office pays for it out of a set-aside fund, by rule. The office does not get a vote on whether the research is a good idea.*

**Research reserve.** Each cycle, the office holds a **research reserve** sized by the published formula. The reserve funds research, replication, testing, field study, standards development, and evidence synthesis requested by a forum under [CF-23.440 §14](../../corpus_forum/cf_23.440_technical_specialist_forums_specialist_chambers.md#cf-23440-14-research-and-uncertainty-reduction-authority) or a comparable published finding by another forum family.

**Funding by rule.** When a forum issues a published scope order, the office must fund it unless:

- the scope order is missing a required element, which the office names so the forum can fix it;
- the reserve is exhausted, in which case §12 applies and the request is queued in published order; or
- the cost exceeds a published threshold, in which case the forum's own family confirms the order and the office then funds it.

The office must not refuse or delay funding because of the question asked, the likely answer, or who the answer may help.

**Who runs the research.** The Public Information Office runs intake, researcher selection, and publication under its own chapter. The Public Finance Office pays. Neither office decides what the findings are.

### 7. Other Programs: Wellbeing, Public Revenue, and Business Activity

*In plain terms: the governing authority may also run wellbeing programs and public enterprises through this office's books. The office pays and keeps the accounts. The policy stays with whoever is accountable for it.*

The governing authority may designate the office as the disbursing and accounting office for:

- wellbeing and survival-floor programs under **Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*), whose fiscal orientation stays with **CI-6.215** and **CI-9.210**;
- public revenue, fees, and recurring charges under **CI-6.210**; and
- public enterprises and other business activity of the adopter.

For each designated program, the office:

- disburses only on a published authorization from the accountable body;
- keeps the program in its own ledger account, separate from the §4 floors and the §6 reserve; and
- reports flows, delays, and shortfalls under §8.

**Firewall.** Money from these programs must not be moved into or out of the §4 floors or the §6 reserve, except by a published act of the governing authority that does not reduce any floor. Revenue from a program or enterprise must not be earmarked to a forum that hears disputes about that program or enterprise.

### 8. Public Ledger and Disclosure

*In plain terms: anyone can see where the money came from and where it went.*

The office keeps a **public ledger** that shows, for each cycle and each funded body or program:

- the floor, the formula inputs, and the amount actually paid;
- every reduction, delay, or freeze, with its reason;
- every research reserve request, its scope order, and its status;
- the reserve fund balance, its target, and every draw and repayment under §11;
- sources of the office's money, including any single source above a published share; and
- the audit status under §10.

The ledger follows [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification). Where an item cannot be public, for example because it would identify a protected party, a qualified independent audit path must still exist, and the ledger shows that the item was withheld and why. The Public Information Office publishes an independent check of the ledger's figures.

### 9. The Office's Own Funding and the Information Office's Funding

*In plain terms: whoever pays the payer can steer it. The general rule that protects the funding of every independent body lives in Chapter Eight; this section only says how it applies to the two offices.*

The *Protected funding* requirement of [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies) applies to the Public Finance Office and the Public Information Office. For these two offices:

- each office's floor is worked out under the same rules as §4, on its own track;
- the Public Finance Office pays the Public Information Office's floor but may not set, reduce, delay, or condition it, and the protections of §5 apply to it in full; and
- the Public Information Office checks this office's ledger, so neither office sets the other's floor.

### 10. Audit and Challenge

*In plain terms: the books are checked by someone outside the office, and anyone harmed by a funding decision can challenge it.*

**Audit.** An independent financial audit, and a resource audit where shared resources are involved, covers the office on the review cadence in [Chapter Twenty-Four §4](../../core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline). Results go to the Integrity registrar under **CF-23.415 §6** and are published on the ledger.

**Challenge.** A funded body, a party, a researcher, or any sentient affected by a funding act may challenge it before an Integrity forum. The challenge may claim:

- a floor set below the formula, or a formula that does not meet §4;
- steering under §5;
- refusal or delay of research funding contrary to §6;
- a breach of the §7 firewall; or
- a draw, transfer, or failure to replenish contrary to §11.

**Self-judging.** Where the Integrity forum that would hear the challenge is itself funded under the act being challenged, the matter routes to an independent backup forum under [Ways a Matter Moves Between Forums](../../core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#ways-a-matter-moves-between-forums).

**Interim relief.** While a challenge is pending, the forum may order the disputed floor restored where a reduction foreseeably lets ongoing harm continue or makes redress useless.

### 11. Reserve Fund

*In plain terms: the office keeps money set aside for bad years, so a dip in revenue does not shut down the forums. The reserve can be spent only on keeping the machinery running, everyone can see the balance, and no one can raid it for something else.*

**The fund.** The office keeps a **reserve fund**, held in its own ledger account, separate from the §4 floors, the §6 research reserve, and the §7 programs.

**Target size.** The fund has a published target, worked out under the formula in §4. The default target is one quarter of the sum of the floors for a cycle. The governing authority may set a higher target, and may set a lower one only with published reasons that an independent check seat reviews.

**Building and keeping it.** The office sets aside a published share of revenue each cycle until the fund reaches its target. Money left unspent at the end of a cycle goes to the fund first. Once the fund is at its target, any surplus may return to general revenue only by a published act at the end of a cycle.

**What it may pay for.** The fund may be drawn only to:

- cover a shortfall under §12, before any floor is reduced;
- meet a sudden surge in intake or emergency adjudication that the formula has not yet caught up with; and
- keep floors paid when a budget for the next cycle has not been adopted in time.

**What it may not pay for.** The fund may not be used to:

- raise one body's funding above its floor while others stay at theirs;
- reward or penalize any forum, finding, or record, or any other use barred by §5;
- lend to, or cover the spending of, the governing authority or any other body; or
- back any debt, or be pledged as security for one.

**Holding.** The fund is held in safe and readily available form under a published policy. It may not be held in any asset issued by a funded body or by an **interested party** with a pending matter before a funded body.

**Drawing.** Each draw is a published decision that states the reason, the amount, and the use. A draw above a published share of the fund also needs the sign-off of the independent check seat. Each draw is entered in the §8 ledger.

**Replenishment.** After a draw, the office restores the fund on a published schedule. Restoring it comes before any increase above the floors.

**No raiding.** No one may take money from the fund for any other purpose. The governing authority may not move it into general revenue or into other programs except under *Building and keeping it* above. A transfer outside these rules is steering under §5 and an integrity trigger under **CI-8.210 §3** (*Integrity Trigger Taxonomy and Cross-Layer Routing (Control Package)*), and any sentient may challenge it under §10.

### 12. Shortfalls and Emergencies

*In plain terms: when money really runs short, the reserve fund is used first; after that, cuts are shared by a published rule, the most urgent protections are cut last, and every cut ends on a date.*

When actual revenue falls below the sum of the floors, the office must:

- publish the shortfall and its cause;
- draw on the §11 reserve fund before reducing any floor;
- apply reductions by a rule published in advance, not case by case;
- protect last, in this order:
  - intake and emergency adjudication;
  - remedies for survival-relevant and rights-floor matters; and
  - the remedy-parity minimum under [Chapter Twenty §4.2](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-parity-funding-minimum);
- set an end date for each reduction; and
- restore floors first when revenue recovers.

A shortfall does not excuse a forum's tier milestones. It is a fact the forum may cite when it seeks a tier-appropriate extension under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4](../../core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline).

### 13. Proportional Scaling

*In plain terms: a small community does not need a ministry. It still needs the money to be counted by someone other than the person spending it.*

A small adopter may place the office's seats in fewer hands under [Chapter Eight §7](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#7-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting). It may share buildings, clerks, and systems with other bodies under **CF-23.470 §3**. Even when hosting is merged:

- the check seat stays outside the office;
- no funding decision-maker may sit on a funded bench; and
- the Public Finance Office and the Public Information Office keep separate decision-makers.
