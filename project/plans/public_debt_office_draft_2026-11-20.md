# CHAPTER [D]: PUBLIC DEBT OFFICE — BORROWING IN THE OPEN

Chapter [D] is the constitutional owner of **public debt**.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: borrowing lets today's decision-makers spend money that later generations will repay. This chapter puts all public borrowing in one independent office that borrows only what the governing authority openly authorizes, sells debt on the same terms to everyone, shows every obligation, and keeps debt payable.*

**Purpose:** Chapter [D] keeps public debt honest and payable. It does this through one independent office that borrows only under a published authorization and a published fiscal rule, sells debt openly, publishes every contract and obligation, and pays what the public owes when it is due.

**Role:** Chapter [D] is the constitutional owner of public debt. Where it sits:

- The Public Finance Office chapter governs how public money is spent and protects the funding floors. It may not borrow, and this office may not decide spending.
- The Public Monetary Office chapter governs the currency and interest rates. It may not decide or advise on how much debt is issued or when, and this office has no say over interest rates.
- The Public Information Office chapter produces the figures this office relies on, including the cyclical adjustment in §7, and checks this office's ledger.
- [Chapter Twenty-Eight](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) governs who holds governing authority, including the authority to decide whether and how much to borrow, within §7.
- A later corpus-institutions file carries the cyclical-adjustment method, the sustainability test, auction rules, and ledger fields. It may not narrow this chapter.

**Chapter scope:** The office:

- issues, sells, manages, buys back, and repays public debt on behalf of the governing authority;
- publishes and follows a debt strategy and an issuance calendar;
- records every public obligation, including guarantees and long-term payment contracts; and
- keeps the public debt ledger.

The chapter's sections cover:

- why the office exists and what it is grounded in (**section 2**);
- the office's independence, appointment, and seats (**section 3**);
- authorization and sale of debt (**section 4**);
- debt strategy and calendar (**section 5**);
- every obligation shown, and no hidden security (**section 6**);
- the fiscal rule, the escape clause, and no forced default (**section 7**);
- paying what is owed (**section 8**);
- the public debt ledger (**section 9**);
- audit and challenge (**section 10**); and
- proportional scaling, including adopters that use another currency (**section 11**).

**Out of scope:** The office decides no matter, finding, classification, or Standing Record. It does not decide spending, taxes, or interest rates.

### 2. Why the Public Debt Office Exists

*In plain terms: borrowing is the easiest way for those in power to spend without asking. The cost falls on people who were never asked: those who will repay, and those not yet born.*

This section explains the reasons for the chapter. It is non-operative: the binding rules are in [§3](#3-independence-and-placement) through [§11](#11-proportional-scaling-and-adopters-without-their-own-currency).

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter One principles:
  - [§18.3 Separation of Duties](../../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties);
  - [§19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](../../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture); and
  - [§19.2 Convenient Proxies and Proxy Divergence](../../core_02.050_governance_incentive_application.md#192-convenient-proxies-and-proxy-divergence).
- Chapter Six Articles:
  - [Article V-A](../../core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-v-a-dependency-mapping-and-resource-flow-transparency) (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*);
  - [Article XIII-D](../../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*);
  - [Article XVII](../../core_04.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*); and
  - [Article XXVIII-A](../../core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxviii-a-non-entrenchment-and-revisability) (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*).

**The problem it answers.** A governing authority that borrows on its own terms has easy paths around every limit on its spending:

- **Hidden debt:** loans kept secret, obligations moved into controlled bodies or long contracts, and public assets quietly pledged as security.
- **Favored lenders:** debt sold privately to friends on special terms.
- **Pushing the bill forward:** borrowing for today's spending, leaving repayment to those who come after.
- **Brinkmanship:** a debt limit used as a hostage, risking a missed payment that raises borrowing costs for everyone.

**Why this approach.** International guidance on public debt management, and the practice of leading debt offices, keep debt management apart from interest-rate policy, run debt to a published strategy and calendar, sell it in open auctions, and publish every contract. Experience with fiscal rules favors a balance rule adjusted for the business cycle, with an account that records every departure and must be corrected, over a fixed cap that can force a default. The [best-practice sweep](public_monetary_office_best_practice_sweep_2026-11-20.md) gives the sources.

**How it carries the Aims and the Tetrad.** Debt that stays payable, and that pays only for lasting benefit when it outlives those who borrowed, leaves later generations room to choose, serving [the Two Constitutional Aims](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims). Within the [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):

- **Oversight:** every obligation on a public ledger, checked from outside.
- **Accountability:** every departure from the fiscal rule recorded and corrected.
- **Participation:** borrowing decided in the open by the governing authority.
- **Timeliness:** a published issuance calendar, and debts paid when due.

These reasons do not add duties. They explain why the duties in §3 to §11 take the shape they do.

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office borrows what it is told to borrow, but how it borrows is its own job, done by published rules.*

**Independent Body:** The Public Debt Office is an Independent Body under [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent), and meets every standard requirement there. For this office:

- it sits outside the Material Control Line of the governing authority, every forum family, the Public Finance Office, the Public Monetary Office, and every buyer, dealer, or adviser it deals with;
- the protected decision-makers are the members of its debt board, drawn by lot under *Appointment by sortition* in Chapter Eight §11 from candidates who pass a competency gate in public finance, debt markets, or financial risk management; and
- its funding is protected under *Protected funding* in Chapter Eight §11 and paid through the Public Finance Office.

**Seat map:** Every materially binding act of the office, such as setting the debt strategy, running an auction, or buying back debt, places the four seats under Chapter Eight. The independent check seat is an auditor outside the office.

**No dual roles (added for this office):** In addition to Chapter Eight §11, no one may at the same time hold a decision seat in this office and any seat in the Public Finance Office, the Public Monetary Office, the governing authority, or a buyer, dealer, or adviser the office deals with. The office's auctions and strategy move asset prices, so *Market-moving information* in Chapter Eight §11 applies to it in full, including the quiet period before each auction and the bar on holdings.

**Separation from interest-rate policy.** The Public Monetary Office has no say over how much debt is issued, when, or on what terms, and this office has no say over interest rates. The two offices share information only through published channels.

### 4. Authorization and Sale of Debt

*In plain terms: the governing authority decides whether to borrow; the office does the borrowing openly and on the same terms for everyone.*

**Authorization.** Public debt may be issued only under a published act of the governing authority that names the amount, the purpose, and how it will be repaid, within the §7 fiscal rule.

**How debt is sold.** The office sells debt in open auctions or on other published terms open to all eligible buyers. It may not sell debt privately to a favored buyer or on terms not offered to others. Where the office uses dealers, their selection, duties, and terms are published and open to every firm that qualifies.

**Debt contracts.** Every debt contract carries terms that let most holders agree a restructuring that binds all holders, so that a few holders cannot block an orderly fix.

**No borrowing by others.** No public body other than this office may borrow on the public's behalf. A borrowing outside this chapter is void against the public and an integrity trigger.

### 5. Debt Strategy and Calendar

*In plain terms: the office says in advance how it will borrow and when, and aims for the lowest cost over time at a risk it can defend.*

**Objective.** The office meets the public's borrowing needs at the lowest cost over the medium term that is consistent with a prudent level of risk, and it publishes how it measures both.

**Strategy.** The office publishes a medium-term debt strategy, refreshed at least each funding cycle, that sets out:

- the mix of maturities, currencies, and fixed and floating rates it aims for, and why;
- the cost and risk measures it will report against; and
- how it will refinance debt that falls due.

**Calendar.** The office publishes an issuance calendar in advance and keeps to it. A departure is published with reasons.

**Buybacks and exchanges.** The office may buy back or exchange debt only under a published framework, open to all holders of the same debt on the same terms.

### 6. Every Obligation Shown, and No Hidden Security

*In plain terms: no debt is secret, no contract is confidential, and no public asset is pledged without an open decision.*

**Every obligation counts.** The following are public debt under this chapter, must be authorized and recorded the same way, and count against the §7 fiscal rule at their published value:

- guarantees of another's debt;
- promises to pay later under contracts, including long-term payment commitments under public-private partnerships;
- debts of bodies the governing authority controls; and
- any other obligation that the sustainability test treats as a material call on public money.

Contingent obligations whose cost is uncertain are recorded with a published estimate of their likely cost and how it was made.

**Contracts published.** Every debt contract and every guarantee is published in full. A confidentiality clause in a public debt contract is void.

**No hidden security.** No public asset, revenue stream, or natural resource may be pledged as security for a debt except by a specific published act of the governing authority that names the asset and the debt.

### 7. The Fiscal Rule, the Escape Clause, and No Forced Default

*In plain terms: over a full business cycle, the public does not borrow to pay for everyday spending. Every departure is written down and paid back. A debt limit can never be used to make the public miss a payment.*

**Balance rule.** The governing authority sets, by a published act, a limit on borrowing for current spending, measured after adjusting for the business cycle. By default, the cycle-adjusted balance of current spending and revenue is zero or better. The Public Information Office computes the cyclical adjustment by a published method and publishes its rung on the confidence ladder.

**Borrowing for lasting benefit.** Borrowing for investment that gives lasting public benefit, such as long-lived infrastructure, sits outside the balance rule only where:

- the benefit will last at least as long as the debt;
- the case is published with the authorization; and
- the total stays within the sustainability test below.

**Compensation account.** Every year's departure from the balance rule, above or below, is recorded in a published compensation account. When the account's deficit passes a published threshold, the governing authority must publish and follow a plan to bring it back under the threshold within a published period (by default, five years).

**Sustainability test.** Total public debt, including every obligation under §6, must pass a published sustainability test showing it can be serviced without cutting any funding floor under the Public Finance Office chapter. The Public Information Office runs the test each cycle and publishes the result. Where the test fails, the governing authority must publish a plan to return within it.

**Escape clause.** In a declared emergency or a severe downturn, the governing authority may depart from the balance rule by a published act with reasons that:

- states the amount and the end date;
- books the departure to the compensation account; and
- sets the correction plan that starts when the emergency ends.

The escape clause never lifts the bar on direct financing in the Public Monetary Office chapter or the duty to record every obligation.

**No forced default.** No procedural limit may cause a missed payment on debt already lawfully issued, or on an obligation already lawfully authorized. Where a limit would otherwise do so, the office borrows what is needed to pay on time, books it to the compensation account, and publishes it, and the governing authority must publish a correction plan.

**Long-term debt.** Debt that will still be owed after a published long horizon must be justified by a lasting public benefit for those who will repay it, and the justification is published with the authorization.

### 8. Paying What Is Owed

*In plain terms: the public's word is good. Debts are paid when due, even in hard times.*

**Paid when due.** Interest and repayments on debt already lawfully issued are paid when due.

**Debt service first.** Where revenue falls short, payments on debt already lawfully issued come before the shortfall rules for everything else. The shortfall rules of the Public Finance Office chapter and *Protected funding* in Chapter Eight §11 then govern what remains.

**No new borrowing at the floors' cost.** New borrowing may never be justified by cutting a funding floor below its formula, and the sustainability test in §7 must show that debt can be serviced without doing so.

**Restructuring.** Where debt truly cannot be serviced, any restructuring is decided by a published act of the governing authority, offered to all holders of the same debt on the same terms, and recorded on the ledger.

### 9. Public Debt Ledger

*In plain terms: anyone can see how much is owed, to whom, on what terms, and when it is due.*

The office keeps a public ledger that shows:

- every debt and every obligation under §6, with its terms, holder class, security, and repayment schedule;
- every auction, buyback, and exchange, with its results;
- the compensation account and every use of the escape clause; and
- the audit status under §10.

The ledger follows [Article XVII](../../core_04.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*). Where an item cannot be public, a qualified independent audit path must still exist, and the ledger shows that the item exists.

### 10. Audit and Challenge

*In plain terms: outsiders check the books, and anyone harmed can challenge a decision. The general rule is in Chapter Eight; this section lists what a challenge to this office may claim.*

**General rule.** The office meets *Audit and challenge* in [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent). In addition, the Public Information Office publishes an independent check of the ledger each cycle.

**Grounds.** A challenge may claim:

- debt issued outside an authorization or outside this chapter;
- a private sale or terms not open to all, contrary to §4;
- an obligation, contract, or pledge hidden contrary to §6;
- a departure from the fiscal rule not booked to the compensation account, or a correction plan not published or not followed, contrary to §7; or
- a missed or delayed payment contrary to §8.

Under *Scope of review* in Chapter Eight §11, a forum does not decide how much to borrow; it decides whether the office and the governing authority followed this chapter.

### 11. Proportional Scaling and Adopters Without Their Own Currency

*In plain terms: a small community, or one that uses someone else's money, still needs its debts counted by someone other than the borrower.*

The *Scaling* rule in [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](../../core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) applies to this office in full. For this office it also means:

- **Sharing:** a small adopter may share this office with other adopters, or use another adopter's office, by published agreement.
- **Combining:** a small adopter may host this office together with the Public Finance Office.
- **Never scaled down:** the debt decision-makers stay separate from the spending decision-makers, and §6, §7, §8, and §9 apply in full.
- **Adopters that use another currency:** an adopter that uses a currency it does not issue needs no Public Monetary Office. It keeps this chapter in full.
