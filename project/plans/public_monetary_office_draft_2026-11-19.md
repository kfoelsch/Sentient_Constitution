# CHAPTER [M]: PUBLIC MONETARY OFFICE — CURRENCY AND PUBLIC DEBT WITHOUT CAPTURE

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Corpus placement (non-operative): file structure and reading rules</span></strong></summary>

> This chapter would be **part of the Sentient Constitution** and **binding only together** with the other numbered `core_*` files read as one text.
>
> - **Constitutional owner:** the office that issues the public currency, manages its value under a published target, and issues and manages public debt; and the bars on printing money to pay for spending, on borrowing to steer, and on hiding debt.
> - **Not owned here:** spending and funding floors (Public Finance Office chapter); public statistics, including the price figures the office relies on (Public Information Office chapter); who holds governing authority and decides how much to borrow ([Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship)); private money, banks, and payment systems, except where this chapter says otherwise.
> - **Implementation owner:** a new corpus-institutions file (target method, debt sustainability test, auction rules, ledger fields).
>
> Reading order: §1 purpose → §2 why the office exists → §3 independence → §4 currency and the stability target → §5 public borrowing → §6 no money for spending and no steering → §7 debt limits → §8 public ledger → §9 audit and challenge → §10 emergencies → §11 scaling.

Chapter [M] is the constitutional owner of **public currency and public debt**.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

<br>

*In plain terms: whoever can print money or run up debt can quietly take value from everyone who holds that money and push costs onto people not yet born. This chapter puts those powers in one independent office that works to a published target, cannot print money to pay for spending, and has to show every debt.*

**Purpose:** Chapter [M] keeps the public currency trustworthy and public debt honest. It does this through one independent office that issues currency to a published stability target, borrows only as the governing authority authorizes and within a published limit, and shows every obligation in a public ledger.

**Role:** Chapter [M] is the constitutional owner of public currency and public debt. Where it sits:

- The Public Finance Office chapter governs how public money is spent and protects the funding floors. The Public Finance Office may not issue currency or borrow; this office may not decide spending.
- The Public Information Office chapter produces the price and economic figures this office uses, and checks this office's ledger.
- [Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) governs who holds governing authority, including the authority to decide whether and how much to borrow, within §7.
- A later corpus-institutions file carries the target method, the debt sustainability test, auction rules, and ledger fields. It may not narrow this chapter.

**Chapter scope:** The office:

- issues the public currency, in every form, including digital;
- sets and pursues a published stability target for the currency's purchasing power;
- issues, sells, manages, and repays public debt on behalf of the governing authority;
- keeps the public ledger of currency in circulation and of all public debt; and
- acts as lender of last resort to the payment system in a declared emergency under §10.

The chapter's sections cover:

- why the office exists and what it is grounded in (**section 2**);
- the office's independence, appointment, and seats (**section 3**);
- currency and the stability target (**section 4**);
- public borrowing (**section 5**);
- the bar on printing money for spending and on steering (**section 6**);
- debt limits and sustainability (**section 7**);
- the public ledger (**section 8**);
- audit and challenge (**section 9**);
- emergencies (**section 10**); and
- proportional scaling, including adopters that use another currency (**section 11**).

**Out of scope:** The office decides no matter, finding, classification, or Standing Record, and it decides no spending. It does not supervise banks or private money except as §10 allows, and it does not set tax policy.

### 2. Why the Public Monetary Office Exists

*In plain terms: printing money and borrowing are the two easiest ways for those in power to spend without asking. Both shift costs onto people who were never asked: savers, people on fixed incomes, and future generations.*

This section explains the reasons for the chapter. It is non-operative: the binding rules are in [§3](#3-independence-and-placement) through [§11](#11-proportional-scaling-and-adopters-without-their-own-currency).

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter One principles:
  - [§18.3 Separation of Duties](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties);
  - [§19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture); and
  - [§19.2 Convenient Proxies and Proxy Divergence](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#192-convenient-proxies-and-proxy-divergence).
- Chapter Six Articles:
  - [Article V-A](../../core_06.000_rights_part_a.md#article-v-a-dependency-mapping-and-resource-flow-transparency) (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*);
  - [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*);
  - [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*); and
  - [Article XXVIII-A](../../core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxviii-a-non-entrenchment-and-revisability) (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*).

**The problem it answers.** A governing authority that controls the currency and its own borrowing has three easy paths around every limit on its spending:

- **Printing to spend:** new money pays for spending today, and the cost arrives later as rising prices that fall hardest on those with the least.
- **Hidden debt:** obligations kept off the books, guaranteed for favored parties, or pushed into later cycles where no one now present answers for them.
- **Timed money:** easy money before a selection, or credit steered to supporters.

**Why this approach.** Experience with independent central banks and public debt offices points the same way: a published target, a bar on direct financing of the government, debt sold in open auctions on published terms, and full public accounts of what is owed. The chapter turns those lessons into four design choices:

- **Separation:** the office that spends cannot print or borrow, and the office that prints and borrows cannot spend.
- **A published target:** the office answers for one measurable aim, so its choices can be judged.
- **No direct financing:** the office does not buy the governing authority's debt when it is issued or lend to it, except a capped bridge in a declared emergency.
- **Every debt shown:** all debt, guarantees, and promises to pay later are on one public ledger, checked by the Public Information Office.

**How it carries the Aims and the Tetrad.** A currency that holds its value and debt that stays payable protect the means of every sentient now and leave later generations room to choose, serving [the Two Constitutional Aims](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims). Within the [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):

- **Oversight:** a public ledger and an outside check of it.
- **Accountability:** the office answers publicly for missing its target.
- **Participation:** borrowing and limits are decided in the open by the governing authority.
- **Timeliness:** decisions are published on a calendar set in advance.

These reasons do not add duties. They explain why the duties in §3 to §11 take the shape they do.

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office answers to its target and its rules, not to whoever is in power this year.*

**Independent Body:** The Public Monetary Office is an Independent Body under [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), and meets every standard requirement there. For this office:

- it sits outside the Material Control Line of the governing authority, every forum family, the Public Finance Office, and every bank or payment provider it deals with;
- the protected decision-makers are the members of its decision board, who serve long, staggered, non-renewable terms; and
- its own costs are paid from its operating income under a published budget, and any surplus goes to the governing authority's general revenue by a published rule, never at the office's choice.

**Seat map:** Every materially binding act of the office, such as setting a policy rate, issuing debt, or lending in an emergency, places the four seats under Chapter Eight. The independent check seat is an auditor outside the office.

**No dual roles (added for this office):** In addition to Chapter Eight §11, no one may at the same time hold a decision seat in this office and any seat in the Public Finance Office, the governing authority, or a bank or payment provider the office deals with. Former members follow the cooling-off rules of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) for any role with such a body.

### 4. Currency and the Stability Target

*In plain terms: the office's main job is to keep the money's buying power steady, by a target everyone can see.*

**Issuing currency.** Only the office may issue the public currency. No other public body may issue money or anything meant to circulate as money.

**The target.** The office pursues a published target for the stability of the currency's purchasing power, measured by price figures from the Public Information Office. The governing authority sets the target by a published act; the office chooses how to meet it.

**Other aims.** Without undermining the target, the office may also support employment and the stability of the payment system, and it must say in each decision how it weighed them.

**Explaining itself.** The office publishes:

- a calendar of decisions set in advance;
- each decision with its reasons, and a record of the vote by member; and
- a public explanation, with a plan to return, whenever the target is missed by more than a published margin.

**Changing the target.** A change to the target is published with its reasons, is open to public comment, and takes effect no earlier than a published notice period after adoption. A change adopted within a published period before a selection takes effect only after it.

### 5. Public Borrowing

*In plain terms: the governing authority decides whether to borrow; the office does the borrowing openly and on the same terms for everyone.*

**Authorization.** Public debt may be issued only under a published act of the governing authority that names the amount, the purpose, and how it will be repaid, within the §7 limit.

**How debt is sold.** The office sells debt in open auctions or on other published terms open to all eligible buyers. It may not sell debt privately to a favored buyer or on terms not offered to others.

**Every obligation counts.** Guarantees of another's debt, promises to pay later under contracts, and obligations of bodies the governing authority controls count as public debt under this chapter. They must be authorized and recorded the same way.

**No borrowing by others.** No public body other than this office may borrow on the public's behalf. A borrowing outside this chapter is void against the public and an integrity trigger.

### 6. No Money for Spending, and No Steering

*In plain terms: the office may not print money to pay the government's bills, and no one may use money or credit to steer a decision.*

**No direct financing.** The office may not:

- buy public debt directly from the governing authority when it is issued;
- lend to the governing authority, the Public Finance Office, or any body they control; or
- pay for any spending,

except the capped bridge in §10.

**Buying debt in the market.** The office may buy and sell public debt in the open market only to pursue the §4 target, under published rules, and never to make room for new borrowing.

**No steering.** The floors of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*) apply to the office and its members. In addition, the office may not:

- direct credit to a person, party, cause, or region because of who they support;
- time a decision to help or harm anyone in a selection; or
- use its powers to reward or penalize a forum, a finding, or a record.

### 7. Debt Limits and Sustainability

*In plain terms: debt has to stay payable, and today's decision-makers may not leave the bill to those who come after without saying so.*

**The limit.** The governing authority sets a published limit on total public debt, using a published sustainability test that shows the debt can be serviced without cutting any funding floor under the Public Finance Office chapter.

**Checking the limit.** The Public Information Office checks the test and publishes its own view each cycle. Where it finds the debt is not sustainable, the governing authority must publish a plan to return within the limit.

**Long-term debt.** Debt that will still be owed after a published long horizon must be justified by a lasting public benefit for those who will repay it, such as lasting infrastructure, and the justification is published with the authorization.

**Funding floors come first.** Paying debt never justifies cutting a funding floor below its formula, except under the shortfall rules of the Public Finance Office chapter.

### 8. Public Ledger and Disclosure

*In plain terms: anyone can see how much money exists, how much is owed, to whom, and on what terms.*

The office keeps a public ledger that shows:

- the currency in circulation, in every form;
- every debt, guarantee, and promise to pay later, with its terms, holder class, and repayment schedule;
- every purchase and sale of public debt by the office;
- every emergency loan under §10, with its terms and repayment; and
- the audit status under §9.

The ledger follows [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*). Where an item cannot be public, a qualified independent audit path must still exist, and the ledger shows that the item exists.

### 9. Audit and Challenge

*In plain terms: outsiders check the books, and anyone harmed can challenge a decision.*

**Audit.** An independent financial audit covers the office each cycle, and the Public Information Office publishes an independent check of the ledger.

**Challenge.** Any sentient affected by an act of the office may challenge it before an Integrity forum. The challenge may claim:

- issuing currency or debt outside this chapter;
- direct financing contrary to §6;
- steering contrary to §6;
- debt above the §7 limit or hidden from the §8 ledger; or
- a missed target with no explanation under §4.

A forum does not set the target or the policy rate; it decides whether the office followed this chapter.

### 10. Emergencies

*In plain terms: in a real crisis the office can act fast, but every emergency step is capped, public, and ends on a date.*

**Declared emergency.** The office may declare a payment-system emergency by a published decision with reasons.

**Lender of last resort.** In a declared emergency, the office may lend to banks and payment providers that are sound but short of cash, against good security, on published terms open to all in the same position, and at a cost above normal.

**Bridge to the governing authority.** In a declared emergency, the office may lend to the governing authority only:

- up to a published cap;
- on published terms;
- repaid within the same cycle; and
- after the Public Finance Office's reserve fund has been drawn.

**Ending.** Every emergency step ends on a published date, may be renewed only by a new published decision with reasons, and is reviewed by an Integrity forum after it ends.

### 11. Proportional Scaling and Adopters Without Their Own Currency

*In plain terms: a small community, or one that uses someone else's money, still needs its debts counted by someone other than the borrower.*

**Small adopters.** A small adopter may place the office's seats in fewer hands under [Chapter Eight §7](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#7-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting). Even then:

- the check seat stays outside the office;
- the office keeps separate decision-makers from the Public Finance Office; and
- the §6 bar on direct financing and the §8 ledger apply in full.

**Adopters that use another currency.** An adopter that uses a currency it does not issue needs no currency functions. It keeps §5, §6 as it applies to borrowing, §7, §8, and §9 for its public debt, and may give those duties to the Public Finance Office only if the debt decision-makers stay separate from the spending decision-makers.
