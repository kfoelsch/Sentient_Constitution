# CHAPTER [M]: PUBLIC MONETARY OFFICE — CURRENCY WITHOUT CAPTURE

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Corpus placement (non-operative): file structure and reading rules</span></strong></summary>

> This chapter would be **part of the Sentient Constitution** and **binding only together** with the other numbered `core_*` files read as one text.
>
> - **Constitutional owner:** the office that issues the public currency, manages its value under a published target, and watches over the stability of the financial system; and the bars on printing money to pay for spending and on steering by money or credit.
> - **Not owned here:** public debt (Public Debt Office chapter); spending and funding floors (Public Finance Office chapter); public statistics, including the price figures the office relies on (Public Information Office chapter); who holds governing authority ([Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship)); supervision of individual banks, deposit protection, and bank resolution (the bank supervision body under §5).
> - **Implementation owner:** a new corpus-institutions file (target method, financial-stability framework, collateral framework, reserve-management policy, ledger fields).
>
> Reading order: §1 purpose → §2 why the office exists → §3 independence → §4 currency and the stability target → §5 financial stability → §6 no money for spending, balance-sheet operations, and no steering → §7 governance, transparency, and no override → §8 capital and losses → §9 exchange rate and reserves → §10 public ledger and digital currency → §11 audit and challenge → §12 emergencies → §13 scaling.

Chapter [M] is the constitutional owner of **public currency**.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

<br>

*In plain terms: whoever can print money can quietly take value from everyone who holds it. This chapter puts that power in one independent office that works to a published target, cannot print money to pay for spending, explains every decision, and keeps the financial system safe without picking winners.*

**Purpose:** Chapter [M] keeps the public currency trustworthy. It does this through one independent office that issues currency to a published stability target, watches over the stability of the financial system, and shows every operation in a public ledger.

**Role:** Chapter [M] is the constitutional owner of public currency. Where it sits:

- The Public Finance Office chapter governs how public money is spent and protects the funding floors. It may not issue currency, and this office may not decide spending.
- The Public Debt Office chapter governs public borrowing. This office has no say over how much debt is issued, when, or on what terms, and the Public Debt Office has no say over interest rates.
- The Public Information Office chapter produces the price and economic figures this office uses, and checks this office's ledger.
- [Chapter Twenty-Seven](../../core_27.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-seven-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) governs who holds governing authority, including the authority to set the target under §4 and choose the exchange-rate regime under §9.
- A later corpus-institutions file carries the target method, the financial-stability framework, the collateral framework, the reserve-management policy, and ledger fields. It may not narrow this chapter.

**Chapter scope:** The office:

- issues the public currency, in every form, including digital;
- sets and pursues a published stability target for the currency's purchasing power;
- sets system-wide financial-stability policy under §5;
- runs the exchange-rate regime and holds the public's foreign reserves under §9;
- keeps the public ledger of currency in circulation and of its own operations; and
- acts as lender of last resort in a declared emergency under §12.

The chapter's sections cover:

- why the office exists and what it is grounded in (**section 2**);
- the office's independence, appointment, and seats (**section 3**);
- currency and the stability target (**section 4**);
- financial stability (**section 5**);
- the bar on printing money for spending, balance-sheet operations, and the bar on steering (**section 6**);
- governance, transparency, and no override (**section 7**);
- capital and losses (**section 8**);
- the exchange rate and foreign reserves (**section 9**);
- the public ledger and digital currency (**section 10**);
- audit and challenge (**section 11**);
- emergencies (**section 12**); and
- proportional scaling (**section 13**).

**Out of scope:** The office decides no matter, finding, classification, or Standing Record, and it decides no spending or borrowing. It does not supervise individual banks or set tax policy.

### 2. Why the Public Monetary Office Exists

*In plain terms: printing money is one of the easiest ways for those in power to spend without asking. The cost falls on people who were never asked: savers, people on fixed incomes, and those with the least.*

This section explains the reasons for the chapter. It is non-operative: the binding rules are in [§3](#3-independence-and-placement) through [§13](#13-proportional-scaling).

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter One principles:
  - [§18.3 Separation of Duties](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties);
  - [§19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture); and
  - [§19.2 Convenient Proxies and Proxy Divergence](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#192-convenient-proxies-and-proxy-divergence).
- Chapter Six Articles:
  - [Article V-A](../../core_06.000_rights_part_a.md#article-v-a-dependency-mapping-and-resource-flow-transparency) (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*);
  - [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*);
  - [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*); and
  - [Article XXVIII-A](../../core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxviii-a-non-entrenchment-and-revisability) (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*).

**The problem it answers.** A governing authority that controls the currency has easy paths around every limit on its spending:

- **Printing to spend:** new money pays for spending today, and the cost arrives later as rising prices that fall hardest on those with the least.
- **Timed money:** easy money before a selection, or credit steered to supporters.
- **Quiet bailouts:** cheap loans or asset purchases that rescue favored firms at the public's risk.

**Why this approach.** Experience with independent central banks points the same way: a published target set openly, a bar on direct financing of the government, decisions explained and voted in the open, a separate office for public debt, and clear rules for emergency lending and for losses. The [best-practice sweep](public_monetary_office_best_practice_sweep_2026-11-20.md) gives the sources. The chapter turns those lessons into five design choices:

- **Separation:** the office that spends cannot print, the office that prints cannot spend or borrow, and the office that borrows cannot set interest rates.
- **A published target:** the office answers for one measurable aim, so its choices can be judged.
- **No direct financing:** the office does not buy the governing authority's debt when it is issued or lend to it, except a capped bridge in a declared emergency.
- **Open decisions:** a calendar set in advance, reasons, votes, minutes, and later the full transcripts.
- **No picking winners:** financial-stability tools and emergency lending work by published rules open to every firm in the same position.

**How it carries the Aims and the Tetrad.** A currency that holds its value protects the means of every sentient now and leaves later generations room to choose, serving [the Two Constitutional Aims](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims). Within the [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):

- **Oversight:** a public ledger, minutes, transcripts, and an outside check.
- **Accountability:** the office answers publicly for missing its target, in regular open hearings.
- **Participation:** the target and the exchange-rate regime are set in the open by the governing authority.
- **Timeliness:** decisions are published on a calendar set in advance.

These reasons do not add duties. They explain why the duties in §3 to §13 take the shape they do.

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office answers to its target and its rules, not to whoever is in power this year.*

**Independent Body:** The Public Monetary Office is an Independent Body under [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), and meets every standard requirement there. For this office:

- it sits outside the Material Control Line of the governing authority, every forum family, the Public Finance Office, the Public Debt Office, and every bank or payment provider it deals with;
- the protected decision-makers are the members of its decision board, drawn by lot under *Appointment by sortition* in Chapter Eight §11 from candidates who pass a competency gate in monetary economics, financial stability, or payment systems. They serve long, staggered, non-renewable terms; and
- its own costs are paid from its operating income under a published budget, as a self-funded body under *Protected funding* in Chapter Eight §11, building its own reserve from that income. What happens to any surplus is governed by §8.

**Seat map:** Every materially binding act of the office, such as setting a policy rate, setting a financial-stability tool, or lending in an emergency, places the four seats under Chapter Eight. The independent check seat is an auditor outside the office.

**No dual roles (added for this office):** In addition to Chapter Eight §11, no one may at the same time hold a decision seat in this office and any seat in the Public Finance Office, the Public Debt Office, the governing authority, the bank supervision body, or a bank or payment provider the office deals with. Former members follow the cooling-off rules of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) for any role with such a body.

### 4. Currency and the Stability Target

*In plain terms: the office's main job is to keep the money's buying power steady, by a target everyone can see.*

**Issuing currency.** Only the office may issue the public currency. No other public body may issue money or anything meant to circulate as money.

**The target.** The office pursues a published target for the stability of the currency's purchasing power, measured by price figures from the Public Information Office. The governing authority sets the target by a published act; the office chooses how to meet it.

**Other aims.** Without undermining the target, the office may also support employment and financial stability, and it must say in each decision how it weighed them.

**Missing the target.** Whenever the target is missed by more than a published margin, the office publishes an explanation and a plan to return.

**Changing the target.** A change to the target is published with its reasons, is open to public comment, and takes effect no earlier than a published notice period after adoption. A change adopted within a published period before a selection takes effect only after it.

**Review cycle.** The governing authority and the office review the target and the office's strategy together at least every five years, in public and with public comment, and publish the result. A review may keep the target as it is.

### 5. Financial Stability

*In plain terms: the office watches the whole financial system and can lean against a dangerous build-up of risk. A separate body supervises individual banks and handles one that fails, so that no single body both lends to banks and grades them.*

**System-wide policy.** The office watches risks to the stability of the financial system as a whole and may use system-wide tools to lean against them, such as requiring all banks of a class to hold more capital in a boom, or limiting how much can be lent against a home. It uses these tools only:

- under a published framework that names the tools and when they may be used;
- by rules that apply alike to every firm in the same class, never to favor or punish a firm; and
- with a published decision and reasons, on the same calendar and transparency rules as its other decisions.

**Bank supervision body.** Supervision of individual banks and payment providers, deposit protection, and the resolution of a failing bank belong to a separate Independent Body under [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), established by its own chapter. That chapter must at least provide that:

- the body applies the office's system-wide tools to individual firms, and sets firm-specific requirements under published standards;
- deposit protection is funded by charges on the protected firms, held in a fund apart from public revenue;
- when a bank fails, its owners and then its creditors bear the losses before any public money is used, and any public money used is recorded as public debt under the Public Debt Office chapter; and
- the body tells the office whether a firm seeking emergency lending under §12 is solvent.

**Working together.** The office and the bank supervision body share information under *Information handling and privacy* in Chapter Eight §11, through published channels, and each publishes how they divide their work.

### 6. No Money for Spending, Balance-Sheet Operations, and No Steering

*In plain terms: the office may not print money to pay the government's bills. When it buys assets, it does so in the open, by a published plan, without choosing which firms or sectors benefit. No one may use money or credit to steer a decision.*

**No direct financing.** The office may not:

- buy public debt directly from the governing authority or the Public Debt Office when it is issued;
- lend to the governing authority, the Public Finance Office, the Public Debt Office, or any body they control; or
- pay for any spending,

except the capped bridge in §12.

**Balance-sheet operations.** The office may buy and sell assets, including public debt in the open market, only to pursue the §4 target or §5 stability, and never to make room for new borrowing. Any purchase program larger than routine market operations runs under a published plan that states:

- its aim and why routine tools are not enough;
- its size, the assets that may be bought, and how long it will run;
- how the office will wind it down; and
- the likely gains and losses, and who bears them under §8.

Purchases of assets other than public debt must be broad and neutral across sectors and firms, through market-wide programs, never aimed at a named firm.

**No steering.** The floors of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*) apply to the office and its members. In addition, the office may not:

- direct credit to a person, party, cause, or region because of who they support;
- time a decision to help or harm anyone in a selection; or
- use its powers to reward or penalize a forum, a finding, or a record.

### 7. Governance, Transparency, and No Override

*In plain terms: the office decides in the open, its members cannot profit from what they know, it answers in public hearings, and no one can order it what to do. The general rules for every independent body live in Chapter Eight; this section adds what is special to this office.*

**General rules.** The office meets *Open decisions*, *Market-moving information*, and *No override* in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), with their defaults: minutes within three weeks, transcripts after five years, a quiet period of one week, and an outside review of its forecasting and analysis at least every five years. The office's decisions move asset prices, so the quiet period and the bar on holdings apply to it in full. In addition:

- the vote of each member is published on the day of the decision;
- at least twice a year, the office appears in an open public session of the governing authority, and its annual report covers its target, its financial-stability work, its balance sheet, and its accounts; and
- the emergency powers in §12 are the office's own, used by its own decision.

### 8. Capital and Losses

*In plain terms: the office keeps a cushion against losses, rebuilds it before paying anything out, and can be topped up when needed without anyone using that as a lever.*

**Capital target.** The office keeps capital at a published target suited to the risks on its balance sheet.

**Surplus.** Profit goes first to rebuild capital up to the target and to cover losses carried forward. Only then is any surplus paid to the governing authority's general revenue, by a published rule set in advance, never at the office's choice.

**Losses.** A loss is absorbed first by capital. A loss beyond capital is carried forward and met from future profit.

**Topping up.** Where capital falls below a published floor, the governing authority restores it to the floor under a published rule that runs on its own, without a new decision. A top-up may not be conditioned on any decision or policy of the office, and may not be delayed to press it.

**No hidden spending.** The office may not use its balance sheet to carry out spending that belongs to the governing authority, such as subsidies or below-cost loans to chosen sectors.

### 9. The Exchange Rate and Foreign Reserves

*In plain terms: the governing authority decides, in the open and after hearing the office, whether the currency floats or is tied to another; the office runs whatever is chosen and invests the public's foreign reserves safely.*

**The regime.** The governing authority chooses the exchange-rate regime by a published act, after the office publishes its advice. The office runs the regime. Where the regime would conflict with the §4 target, the office says so publicly, and the governing authority must publish how the conflict is to be resolved. A change of regime adopted within a published period before a selection takes effect only after it.

**Interventions.** The office's purchases and sales of foreign currency are published, in total at once and in detail after a published delay.

**Foreign reserves.** The office holds the public's foreign reserves under a published policy that puts safety first, the ability to use them quickly second, and return third. Reserves:

- are held only in the safe, readily usable assets that the published policy names;
- may not be lent to the governing authority or used to pay for any spending; and
- are reported on the §10 ledger.

### 10. Public Ledger and Digital Currency

*In plain terms: anyone can see how much money exists and what the office owns and owes. If the money goes digital, people keep their privacy and can still use cash.*

**The ledger.** The office keeps a public ledger that shows:

- the currency in circulation, in every form;
- its assets and obligations, including every purchase and sale of public debt and every purchase program under §6;
- its foreign reserves and interventions under §9;
- its capital, profits, losses, and any top-up under §8;
- every emergency loan under §12, with its terms and repayment; and
- the audit status under §11.

The ledger follows [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*). Where an item cannot be public, a qualified independent audit path must still exist, and the ledger shows that the item exists.

**Digital currency.** Where the office issues the currency in a digital form:

- the payment data of those who hold and use it is personal data under *Information handling and privacy* in Chapter Eight §11, and the office collects no more of it than it needs to run the currency;
- the office may not restrict, condition, or program how lawfully held money may be spent; and
- the digital form is offered alongside the physical form, not in place of it.

**Access to cash.** The office keeps a physical form of the currency available. Public bodies accept it for payments owed to them.

### 11. Audit and Challenge

*In plain terms: outsiders check the books, and anyone harmed can challenge a decision. The general rule is in Chapter Eight; this section lists what a challenge to this office may claim.*

**General rule.** The office meets *Audit and challenge* in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies). In addition, the Public Information Office publishes an independent check of the ledger each cycle.

**Grounds.** A challenge may claim:

- issuing currency outside this chapter;
- direct financing or a purchase program contrary to §6;
- steering contrary to §6, or a financial-stability tool aimed at a firm contrary to §5;
- a decision, minutes, transcript, or ledger item not published as §7 and §10 require;
- a top-up conditioned or delayed contrary to §8;
- emergency lending contrary to §12; or
- a missed target with no explanation under §4.

Under *Scope of review* in Chapter Eight §11, a forum does not set the target or the policy rate; it decides whether the office followed this chapter.

### 12. Emergencies

*In plain terms: in a real crisis the office can act fast, but every emergency step is capped, public, and ends on a date, and it never rescues a firm that is already broke.*

**Declared emergency.** The office may declare a financial-system emergency by a published decision with reasons.

**Lender of last resort.** In a declared emergency, the office may lend to banks and payment providers only where:

- the bank supervision body has found the borrower solvent but short of cash;
- the loan is against good security, valued under a published collateral framework with published discounts;
- the terms are published and open to all in the same position; and
- the cost is above normal.

The office may not lend to a firm that is insolvent. Total emergency lending is published at once; each borrower and its terms are published after a published delay (by default, two years).

**Lending beyond banks.** The office may lend to firms other than banks and payment providers only through a broad program open to a whole class of firms, never to a named firm, and only where the governing authority has accepted by a published act the risk of loss on that program.

**Bridge to the governing authority.** In a declared emergency, the office may lend to the governing authority only:

- up to a published cap;
- on published terms;
- repaid within the same cycle; and
- after the Public Finance Office's reserve fund has been drawn.

**Ending.** Every emergency step ends on a published date, may be renewed only by a new published decision with reasons, and is reviewed by an Integrity Forum after it ends.

### 13. Proportional Scaling

*In plain terms: a small community can share these functions, as long as no one ends up printing money for their own spending.*

**Small adopters.** The *Scaling* rule in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies) applies. Even when hosting is merged, the §6 bar on direct financing, §8, and the §10 ledger apply in full, and this office stays separate from the Public Debt Office.

**Adopters that use another currency.** An adopter that uses a currency it does not issue needs no Public Monetary Office. Its public debt is governed by the Public Debt Office chapter, and its financial-stability and supervision duties under §5 go to the bank supervision body or are shared with the issuer of the currency it uses by published agreement.
