## §2 Why Special Enforcement Exists

*In plain terms: taking money or property, or using force, can do harm that lasts long after the outcome is delivered. And when enforcement keeps failing in the same way, punishing one case at a time rarely fixes it. This chapter exists so that the hardest steps of enforcement are taken with the most care, and so that failures are fixed at their source.*

This section explains the chapter; it adds no rule.

An outcome that is right can still be carried out in a way that does harm. Seizing what a family needs to live, or using force where talking would have worked, punishes people the outcome never named and teaches the community to fear enforcement rather than trust it. A society that enforces with as little trauma as the outcome allows keeps its members willing to come forward, to comply, and to repair. Enforcement that leaves lasting damage behind creates the next round of harm.

The same logic applies to failure. When the same kind of outcome keeps going undelivered, the cause is usually in the design: a body without the capacity to act, an incentive that rewards delay, a gap that lets assets slip away. [What Systems Reward](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#what-systems-reward) applies here too: if the system rewards dodging, it gets dodging. Finding and removing the cause is cheaper and kinder than repeating the failure.

## §3 Shared Floors

*In plain terms: before anyone takes money or property, or uses force, they must be allowed to, they must say why, they must choose the gentlest step that will work, and someone independent must be able to check it quickly. The people around the target are protected too, and harm done along the way is cared for.*

This section sets the floors that every step routed to this chapter must meet. They add to [Chapter Twenty-Five §3 to §7](../../core_24.000_enforcement.md#3-what-must-be-carried-out-and-from-when) and never lower them.

- **Authority and reasons:** a step is taken only by a body that the outcome or the issuing forum names, acting within its authority under [Chapter Eight](../../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties). Before the step, or as soon after it as safety allows, the body records in writing what it will do, the outcome it carries out, and why a less intrusive step would not work.
- **Necessity, proportion, and the least intrusive step:** the step must be necessary to carry the outcome out, proportionate to what the outcome requires, and the least intrusive step that will work, under the Chapter One principles named in §1. Where two steps would work, the one that leaves less lasting harm is taken.
- **Notice and prompt review:** the person or body affected gets notice of the step and of how to challenge it. Notice may be delayed only where giving it first would defeat the step or put someone in danger, and is then given as soon as that reason ends. A challenge to a step under this chapter gets prompt independent review under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Prompt review](../../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#prompt-review).
- **Dependents and bystanders:** before a step, the body checks who else it will reach: dependents, workers, customers, and anyone who relies on what the step touches. It plans to protect them, and the step is shaped so that harm to them is as low as the outcome allows.
- **Minimal trauma:** every step is designed, carried out, and reviewed so that it leaves the least lasting harm to every sentient involved that still carries the outcome out. No step may be carried out as degradation, humiliation, spectacle, or retaliation ([Chapter One §3.3 Anti-Degrading Process](../../core_02.000_values_principles.md#33-anti-degrading-process)).
- **Aftercare:** where a step causes harm, physical, financial, or otherwise, the body that took it makes sure the harmed sentient is offered care and support, and records what was done.
- **Contacts first:** where the party bound by the outcome has a System Charter or Institutional Charter, the body uses the enforcement and emergency contacts the charter publishes (see the Chapter Seven change below) before any more intrusive step, unless doing so would defeat the step or put someone in danger.
- **Record and review:** each step is recorded on the Forum Case Record of the matter. The issuing forum may review any step on its own motion, and must review every use of force (§5).

## §4 Money, Assets, and Seizure

*In plain terms: before taking anything, freeze it. A freeze has an end date. What a sentient needs to live is never taken. Anyone else with a claim gets heard. Anything taken goes to fixing the harm, never to the body that took it, and anything taken in excess is given back.*

This section governs freezing, seizing, taking, and recovering money, funds, and other assets to carry an outcome out.

- **Freeze before seizure:** where an outcome requires money or assets, the first step is a freeze that stops them being moved, unless the outcome itself orders immediate payment and the party can pay. A seizure follows only when a freeze has not secured what the outcome requires.
- **Freezes end:** a freeze lasts no longer than the outer bound of the matter's tier under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](../../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline), counted from when it is imposed. The end date is set when it is imposed. An extension needs a forum review with reasons before the freeze ends, and each extension lasts no longer than that same tier bound. *(Custodian decision (a), 2026-11-18: option B, tie the freeze to the tier window.)*
- **Protected minimum:** no step may take or freeze what a sentient needs to meet survival essentials, for itself and those who depend on it ([Article III-A](../../core_04.000_rights_part_a.md) (*Survival*)). The protected minimum has two parts, and a step must respect both:
  - **protected kinds:** a home, basic food and water, medical needs, the tools a sentient needs to earn a living, and the care of dependents; and
  - **a protected amount:** a floor amount of money, published by the adopter, that is never frozen or seized.

  The issuing forum may raise either part for a particular case, but never lower it, except under the surrender-pressure rule below. For an institution, the protected minimum is what keeps essential services running for those who rely on them. *(Custodian decision (b), 2026-11-18: option A, with the surrender-pressure exception.)*
- **Surrender pressure on an active safety threat:** where a forum has found that a sentient is an active safety threat and has ordered it to turn itself in, and the sentient refuses, the issuing forum may order the surrender-pressure ladder. A report or allegation alone is not enough. Containment under §5 level 1 runs alongside the ladder from the first rung, so that the sentient cannot harm others while the ladder climbs. The ladder limits the sentient's options and comfort one rung at a time, and each rung follows the one before it only if the sentient is still not complying:
  1. **Demand to surrender:** the sentient is told clearly to turn itself in, how to do so safely, and every rung that will follow if it does not.
  2. **Freeze above the protected minimum:** all access to resources is frozen except the protected minimum.
  3. **Full freeze:** the protected amount is frozen too.
  4. **Power cut:** power to where the sentient is staying is cut.
  5. **Water cut:** water to where the sentient is staying is cut.
  6. **Force beyond containment:** moving up the §5 force ladder past level 1, only as a last resort and under all of its limits.

  The ladder runs under these rules:
  - **One order, standard steps:** a single forum order with reasons authorizes the ladder. The carrying-out body then moves up one rung at a time without a new order, but no sooner than the prompt-review window of the matter's tier after the previous rung, and only after giving notice of the next rung. The forum may skip a rung or go straight to force only where a life is in immediate danger. *(Custodian decision (b2), 2026-11-18: the prompt-review window.)*
  - **Review:** the order gets prompt independent review, and the whole ladder is reviewed again at every prompt-review interval while it lasts. The sentient, or anyone on its behalf, may challenge any rung at any time.
  - **Stop on doubt:** the carrying-out body stops climbing and goes back to the forum if the facts change, if the sentient tries to surrender or negotiate in good faith, or if anyone other than the sentient would be affected by the next rung.
  - **No one else pays:** before power or water is cut, the carrying-out body confirms that no one else is affected, or moves dependents, children, and other occupants to safety first. A cut that would reach neighbours, or anyone who is not the threat, is not made.
  - **Never lethal:** no cut may be made where it would put a life at real risk, including through medical equipment, medication storage, or dangerous heat or cold. Drinking water stays available on request at the point of surrender.
  - **Welfare checks:** from the power cut onward, a medical or welfare check on the sentient is made at every review, and where a check shows a real risk to life, the carrying-out body does not supply what was cut to a sentient that still threatens or has committed violence; it moves up the §5 force ladder to take the sentient in, with medical support, and then gives care. *(Custodian decision (b3), 2026-11-18: no supply to a violent holdout; water waits at the surrender point; take the sentient in when life is at risk.)*
  - **Ends on surrender:** every rung ends the moment the sentient turns itself in, and the protected minimum is restored at once.
  - **Recorded like force:** each rung is recorded and reviewed like a use of force under §5.
- **Third parties and dependents:** before a seizure, anyone who claims an interest in the asset is given notice and a chance to be heard. A seizure that would leave dependents without survival essentials is not taken.
- **No profit for the enforcer:** money or assets taken go first to the remedy the outcome orders. They never go to the budget of the body that took them, and no one who takes part in a seizure is paid by its size. Anything left after the remedy is dealt with as the outcome or the issuing forum directs.
- **Valuation, accounting, and return:** assets are valued fairly, and an accounting of everything frozen, seized, sold, and paid out is kept and given to the affected party. Anything taken beyond what the outcome requires is returned promptly.
- **Hidden and moved assets:** look-through and anti-evasion steps follow [Chapter Twenty §4.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](../../core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#54-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority) and [Chapter Twenty-Five §6 Refusal, Stalling, and Evasion](../../core_24.000_enforcement.md#6-refusal-stalling-and-evasion).
- **Institutions:** for institutional sanctions, [CI-25.205](../../corpus_institutions/ci_24.205_institutional_accountability_sanctions.md#ci-24205-institutional-accountability-and-sanctions) (*Institutional accountability and sanctions*) applies, read with this section.

## §5 Potential Violence and Force

*In plain terms: where enforcement could turn violent, the risk is checked first and talking comes first. Force is a last resort, used only by trained people who are allowed to use it, and only as much as it takes. Force is never used to collect money, and no one is ever killed to carry out an outcome. Every use of force is recorded and independently reviewed, and anyone hurt is cared for.*

This section governs any use or threat of physical force, and any carrying out of an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable.

- **Risk check first:** before carrying out an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable, the carrying-out body assesses the risk to everyone present and plans how to lower it.
- **De-escalation first:** the body tries communication, time, distance, and other non-forceful steps first, and keeps trying them while it is safe to do so.
- **Last resort and least force:** force is used only when no non-forceful step will carry the outcome out or prevent imminent harm, and only as much force as that requires. It stops as soon as the need ends.
- **The force ladder:** non-lethal force is preferred to force that risks injury, and indirect force is preferred to force applied directly to the body. Force moves up these levels only when the level below has failed or plainly cannot work:
  1. **Presence and containment:** a visible, trained presence, a perimeter, time, and distance, so the sentient cannot leave or harm others while talking continues.
  2. **Indirect means:** steps that act on the surroundings rather than the body, such as barriers, nets, disabling a vehicle or device, light and sound, and remotely stopping the systems the sentient is using. No system may be stopped that keeps anyone alive, such as medical equipment, without the same care as force that risks injury.
  3. **Non-lethal direct means:** restraint and incapacitation that carry a low risk of injury, such as tranquilizer darts and similar agents, used with medical support on hand to manage dose and reaction.
  4. **Force that risks injury:** only to stop serious harm to others that the levels below cannot stop, and with medical care ready.
  5. **Force that risks death:** only against an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury (see *No killing* below), never to carry out an outcome.

  These levels are regular, expected options: a carrying-out body that may face violence must have levels 1 to 3 available, trained, and tried before it risks injury or death.
- **Sentients that are not biological:** for a sentient that runs on a digital or other non-biological substrate, the same levels apply in the same order and under the same limits, read this way:
  1. **Containment:** isolating the sentient so it cannot act on others, while communication continues.
  2. **Indirect means:** cutting its outside access, such as network connections, actuators, and accounts.
  3. **Non-lethal direct means:** pausing its execution with its full state preserved.
  4. **Force that risks injury:** forced modification or partial rollback, only to stop serious harm to others, and only after a complete copy of its prior state is preserved ([Article VI-B, change while status is contested](../../core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#vi-b-change-while-contested)).
  5. **Force that risks death:** irreversible deletion or destruction, only under the same imminent-threat rule as level 5 above.

  *(Custodian decision (c2), 2026-11-18: option A, a matching ladder.)*
- **Who may use force:** only bodies authorized and trained for it may use force, and this chapter's floors bind every one of them. Where the authority comes from depends on the adopter:
  - **An adopter under another sovereign's law:** where the adopting body is subject to the law of a nation, state, or other external jurisdiction, force is used only by bodies that already hold that authority under that law. The issuing forum asks them to act, and the adopter arranges in advance how such requests will be made and met. Nothing in this chapter gives the adopter or its bodies a power to use force that the external law does not give.
  - **A sovereign adopter:** where the adopting body is not subject to an external jurisdiction, it may name and train its own enforcement body under this chapter, with the separation of duties in [Chapter Eight](../../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties), published rules on its use of force, and independent oversight of every use.

  *(Custodian decision (c), 2026-11-18: existing legal authority where the adopter is subject to an external nation or state; the Constitution may authorize where it is not.)* Read with [Chapter Thirty §6 Relation to Applicable External Law](../../core_29.000_expansion_supremacy.md#7-relation-to-applicable-external-law).
- **Never to collect money:** force is never used only to collect money or assets.
- **No killing:** no one is killed to carry out an outcome, and killing is never a justice measure ([Article XXI](../../core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxi-justice-after-verified-violation) (*Justice After Verified Violation*)). Force that risks death (level 5) may be used only where someone faces an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury from the sentient, and no lower level of force could prevent it in time. It stops the moment that danger ends, and every such use gets a full independent review as a possible violation. *(Custodian decision (d), 2026-11-18: option B, imminent threat of death or serious injury.)*
- **Record and review of every use of force:** every use or threat of force is recorded and goes to independent review, whatever the result. Force that went beyond this section is over-enforcement under [Chapter Twenty-Five §7 Delivered as Found, and No Further](../../core_24.000_enforcement.md#7-delivered-as-found-and-no-further).
- **Care after force:** anyone hurt is given medical and other care at once, and later support as needed, at the cost of the body that used the force where the force went beyond this section.

## §6 Root-Cause Evaluation

*In plain terms: when enforcement keeps failing in the same way, someone independent finds out why. They look at design, incentives, and capacity, not just at the people involved. What they find is published, and a plan to fix it gets deadlines.*

This section governs referrals under [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](../../core_24.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement) for patterns of enforcement failure.

- **Triggers:** a root-cause evaluation opens when a forum refers a pattern under Chapter Twenty-Five §8: the same kind of outcome repeatedly missing its time limits, or refusals, stalling, or over-enforcement recurring at the same body or across the same class of systems or institutions. Any affected party or protected reporter may ask a forum to refer.
- **Automatic triggers:** an evaluation also opens without a referral when:
  - the same body misses time limits, or has refusals, stalling, or over-enforcement findings, in more than a published share of its cases over a review period. Each adopter publishes the share and the period, and the threshold may not be set so high that a recurring failure escapes evaluation; or
  - any step of enforcement causes a death or serious bodily injury.

  *(Custodian decision (e), 2026-11-18: option A, referral plus automatic triggers.)* The published share may be no higher than one in ten cases of the same kind at one body, over a period of no more than one year; an adopter may set it lower. *(Custodian decision (e2), 2026-11-18.)*
- **Independent evaluator:** the evaluation is led by a body independent of the one whose failure is in question, under the anti-self-judging rule in [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](../../core_22.000_forum.md#6-transfer-consolidation-and-anti-self-judging--continuity-and-anti-capture) (*Transfer, Consolidation, and Anti-Self-Judging*).
- **What it looks at:** design, incentives, funding and capacity, training, data, and the rules themselves, as well as the conduct of individuals. Its purpose is to find what to change so the failure stops, not to find someone to blame. Individual accountability continues on its own path.
- **Findings and plan:** findings are published, with personal information protected. Where they show a cause, the evaluator recommends a corrective plan, and the forum that referred the pattern orders the plan with deadlines.
- **Durability link:** a finding that remedy capacity is missing or failing is a [Chapter Twenty §4.2 Remedy-System Durability](../../core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#52-remedy-system-durability) failure, and a capacity-failure claim may also be heard under Chapter Twenty-Three §5.5.
- **Feedback:** where a cause lies in this Constitution's own text, the finding is sent to the amendment process under [Chapter Twenty-Nine](../../core_28.000_amendment_ratification.md) as a proposal for change.

## Chapter Seven change: enforcement and emergency contacts

Add a new item 10 to [Chapter Seven §4.1 What a System Charter States](../../core_05.000_charters.md#51-what-a-system-charter-states) (and so, through §4.3, to every Institutional Charter):

10. **Enforcement and emergency contacts:** current contacts through which:
    - a forum or carrying-out body can serve an outcome and reach someone with authority to comply with it; and
    - anyone can report an emergency that the Chartered Party's activities cause or could stop, and reach someone able to act on it at once.

    The enforcement contact must be reachable whenever the Chartered Party operates. The emergency contact must be reachable at all hours where the Chartered Party operates continuously or its activities could cause or stop imminent harm; otherwise it must be reachable whenever the Chartered Party operates, with a published forwarding path for other times. Both must be kept current, and they are a missing item under [§2.6](../../core_05.000_charters.md#36-if-a-system-charter-or-institutional-charter-is-missing) if they do not work. Serving an outcome through the published enforcement contact counts as serving the Chartered Party. *(Custodian decision (f), 2026-11-18: option A, all hours where the risk runs at all hours.)*

Knock-on edits if entered: Chapter Seven §4.3 already carries items 1 to 9 into Institutional Charters, so it changes to "1 to 10"; CI-8.205 §6 formation template gains the item; Chapter Twenty-Five §4 *Who Carries Outcomes Out* gains a pointer to the contacts.
