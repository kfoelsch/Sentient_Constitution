# Monetary office: best-practice sweep against central banks and public debt offices

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Asked for by:** the custodian ("Did you do a sweep of state banks' best practices? If not, let's do that.").

**What this note does.** It checks the [Public Monetary Office draft](public_monetary_office_draft_2026-11-19.md) against the main international frameworks and the practice of leading central banks and debt offices. For each practice it says whether the draft already meets it, partly meets it, or misses it, and recommends a change. Nothing in the draft has been changed yet.

**Sources.** The comparison draws on these frameworks and institutions, from general knowledge of their published texts. I did not fetch current editions for this note, so exact figures (such as lag periods) should be checked before any of them is cited in the corpus.

- IMF *Central Bank Transparency Code* (2020) and the older *Code of Good Practices on Transparency in Monetary and Financial Policies* (1999).
- Central-bank independence indices used in research (Cukierman, Webb and Neyapti 1992; Garriga 2016): appointment, dismissal, policy authority, objective, and limits on lending to government.
- EU Treaty Articles 123 (no monetary financing), 127 (price stability as the primary objective) and 130 (no instructions), and the ECB's practice.
- US Federal Reserve: minutes, delayed transcripts, the pre-meeting blackout, semiannual testimony, emergency-lending limits added in 2010, delayed disclosure of discount-window borrowers, and the 2022 rules on officials' personal trading.
- Bank of England: target set by the government in a published remit, open letters when inflation misses the target by more than a set margin, external committee members, a separate Financial Policy Committee, and the 2024 external review of its forecasting.
- Reserve Bank of New Zealand and Bank of Canada: published target agreements renewed on a fixed cycle (Canada every five years).
- Swiss National Bank: profit distribution to government under a published agreement, and a capital target.
- IMF and World Bank *Revised Guidelines for Public Debt Management* (2014), and the practice of separate debt offices (UK Debt Management Office, Swedish National Debt Office, Germany's Finanzagentur).
- Debt transparency: IIF *Voluntary Principles for Debt Transparency*, G20 *Operational Guidelines for Sustainable Financing*, and lessons from hidden-debt cases such as Mozambique's undisclosed loans (2016).
- Fiscal rules and councils: Switzerland's debt brake (a cyclically adjusted balance rule with a compensation account), EU escape clauses, and independent fiscal councils such as the UK Office for Budget Responsibility.
- IMF *Guidelines for Foreign Exchange Reserve Management* (2013), and current central-bank work on digital currency privacy (for example, the ECB's digital euro design).

---

## 1. What the draft already does well

These match or go beyond best practice and need no change:

- **No direct financing** (§6): the same rule as EU Article 123, with a narrower emergency exception than most laws.
- **Goal set by government, tools chosen by the office** (§4): the UK and New Zealand model. The rule that a target change cannot take effect just before a selection goes further than any model law.
- **Published target, decision calendar, vote by member, and an explanation with a return plan when the target is missed** (§4): matches Bank of England open letters and IMF transparency practice.
- **Independence protections**: long, staggered, non-renewable terms, removal only for cause, self-funding, and the bar on instructions, now all under Chapter Eight §11. Drawing members by lot is new; no central bank does it, but it removes the main capture route the indices measure (who appoints).
- **Surplus to general revenue by published rule** (§3): matches the Swiss distribution agreement.
- **Open auctions and every obligation counted, including guarantees** (§5): matches the debt-management guidelines and the debt-transparency principles.
- **Lender of last resort on Bagehot terms** (§10): sound firms, good security, a cost above normal, and terms open to all.
- **Outside check of debt sustainability** (§7): the Information Office plays the role of an independent fiscal council.

## 2. Gaps

Each gap is marked **Adopt** (a clear best practice, which I recommend adding) or **Decide** (a real design choice for the custodian).

| # | Practice | Draft today | Gap | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Debt management separate from monetary policy.** The debt-management guidelines say the two should have separate objectives and, ideally, separate institutions, because an office that sets interest rates and also sells debt is tempted to keep rates low to cut its own debt costs. Most advanced economies use a separate debt office. | One office does both (drafting note 1). | The draft's own provisional choice conflicts with the strongest best practice in this area. | **Decide** |
| 2 | **Financial stability and bank oversight.** Since 2008, central banks usually hold a financial-stability mandate and macroprudential tools, often with bank supervision as well. | "Stability of the payment system" appears only as a secondary aim. No one owns bank supervision, deposit protection, or resolving a failing bank. | No owner for financial-stability policy, bank supervision, deposit protection, or bank resolution. | **Decide** (who owns it); **Adopt** (that someone must) |
| 3 | **Capital and losses.** Model central-bank laws set a capital target, keep profits until capital is restored, and say how losses are covered without giving the government a lever. Recent losses after large asset purchases made this live. | Surplus goes to general revenue; losses are not mentioned. | No rule for a loss, rebuilding capital, or how any recapitalization avoids becoming a lever. | **Adopt** |
| 4 | **Minutes, transcripts, and a quiet period.** Minutes within weeks, full transcripts after a set delay (the Fed uses five years), and a blackout on members' public comments around each decision. | Decision with reasons and a vote by member. | No minutes, no delayed transcripts, no quiet period. | **Adopt** |
| 5 | **Personal trading and conflicts of members.** After 2021 the Fed barred officials from holding individual stocks and from active trading. | Article XIII-C and the general conflict rules apply. | No specific bar on members trading assets whose price the office's decisions move. | **Adopt** |
| 6 | **Regular accountability hearings and framework reviews.** Semiannual public testimony, an annual report, a fixed-cycle review of the target and strategy (Canada every five years; the Fed and ECB about every five), and periodic external review of forecasting. | None. | No regular public hearing, no fixed review cycle for the target, and no external review of methods. | **Adopt** |
| 7 | **Balance-sheet operations.** Large asset purchases should run under published rules on what may be bought, how much, the exit plan, and who bears the losses, and should stay out of choosing sectors or firms. | Market purchases only for the target and never to make room for borrowing. | No rules on the size of purchases, eligible assets, exit, or neutrality between sectors and firms. | **Adopt** |
| 8 | **Emergency lending disclosure and limits.** Borrowers are disclosed after a lag; no lending to insolvent firms; lending beyond banks only through broad programs with the government's approval for the credit risk. | Lender of last resort exists; emergencies are capped, public, and end on a date. | No disclosure of borrowers after a lag, no explicit bar on lending to insolvent firms, and no rule for lending to non-banks. | **Adopt** |
| 9 | **Foreign-exchange regime and reserves.** Laws say who chooses the exchange-rate regime (usually the government, after advice) and require a published reserve-management policy that puts safety and liquidity first. | Silent. | No owner for the exchange-rate regime and no rules for foreign reserves. | **Adopt** (reserves); **Decide** (who chooses the regime) |
| 10 | **Debt strategy and calendar.** A published medium-term debt strategy with cost and risk targets, an issuance calendar, and a published framework for buybacks and exchanges. | Open auctions on published terms. | No published strategy, cost and risk aims, or issuance calendar. | **Adopt** |
| 11 | **Full debt contracts and no hidden collateral.** The debt-transparency principles call for publishing loan contracts, banning confidentiality clauses on public debt, and disclosing loans secured on public resources and public-private partnerships. | Guarantees and deferred payments count as debt. | Contracts are not required to be published in full; loans secured on public assets, public-private partnerships, and other contingent obligations are not named. | **Adopt** |
| 12 | **Debt limit that cannot force a default.** A nominal debt ceiling voted separately from spending can force a default on bills already authorized (the US experience). Better designs anchor on a sustainability or balance rule, such as Switzerland's debt brake with its compensation account, with a published escape clause and a duty to correct afterward. | A published limit set by the governing authority using a sustainability test; a return plan when breached. | No rule that a procedural limit may never cause a missed payment, no cyclical adjustment, and no escape clause with a required correction. | **Adopt** (no forced default; escape clause with correction); **Decide** (cyclical rule) |
| 13 | **Debt service versus funding floors.** | "Funding floors come first" (§7). | Read literally, a revenue collapse could put floors ahead of paying bondholders, which raises borrowing costs for everyone. Best practice treats debt service as protected. | **Decide** |
| 14 | **Government override.** Some laws (UK 1998 Act, section 19) let the government direct the bank in extreme circumstances, with parliamentary approval and for a short time. Others forbid instructions entirely (EU). | No override, and Chapter Eight bars instructions. | Silent on whether any override exists. | **Decide** (I recommend saying expressly that there is none) |
| 15 | **Digital public currency and access to cash.** Current designs protect payment privacy, limit how much the issuer sees, and keep cash available. | "Currency … in every form" appears on the ledger. | No privacy rule for a digital form and no guaranteed access to a non-digital form. The new Chapter Eight privacy rule covers part of this. | **Adopt** |

## 3. Recommendations

**Adopt now (no change of goal, all reversible drafting):** gaps 3 to 8, 10, 11, the reserves part of 9, the no-forced-default and escape-clause part of 12, and 15. These would add about two sections to the draft: one on governance and transparency (minutes, transcripts, quiet period, trading bar, hearings, review cycle, capital and losses) and one on debt practice (strategy, calendar, contracts, contingent obligations, escape clause), with smaller additions to §6, §10, and §8.

**Decide:**

1. **Gap 1, separate debt office.** I recommend a separate Public Debt Office: it is the clearest best practice and fits this project's separation-of-duties design. The alternative is one office with a firewall: separate decision-makers, separate cost-and-risk objectives, and no say for the rate-setters over issuance.
2. **Gap 2, financial stability.** I recommend that the monetary office owns financial-stability policy and lender of last resort, and that bank supervision, deposit protection, and bank resolution sit in a separate Independent Body that works with it. That follows the post-2008 split many countries use and avoids one body grading its own lending.
3. **Gap 9, exchange-rate regime.** I recommend that the governing authority chooses the regime by a published act after the office's published advice, and the office runs it.
4. **Gap 12, cyclical rule.** I recommend a balance rule adjusted for the business cycle, with a compensation account, in place of a plain debt cap.
5. **Gap 13, debt service.** I recommend that paying debt already lawfully issued comes first, and that the shortfall rules decide everything after that, so lenders can trust the public's word.
6. **Gap 14, override.** I recommend stating that there is no override.
