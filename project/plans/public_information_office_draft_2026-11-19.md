# CHAPTER [I]: PUBLIC INFORMATION OFFICE — SHARED EVIDENCE, PUBLIC STATISTICS, AND FORUM-REQUESTED RESEARCH

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

<br>

*In plain terms: everyone who makes decisions, from forums and governing bodies to businesses and households, needs numbers and research that are as accurate and as current as possible, and honest about how sure they are. This chapter puts that work in one office that nobody can lean on.*

**Purpose:** Chapter [I] provides the information that public and private decision-making relies on, as accurate and as timely as possible, with every figure and finding labeled by how confident the office is in it. That information is produced by one independent office that no decision-maker controls.

**Role:** Chapter [I] is the constitutional owner of public statistics and forum-requested research. Where it sits:

- [CF-23.440 §15](../../corpus_forum/cf_23.440_technical_specialist_forums_specialist_chambers.md#cf-23440-15-research-and-uncertainty-reduction-authority) lets a forum commission research once it publishes a finding that uncertainty is blocking reliable decisions. This chapter runs that research; the forum still decides what the findings mean for its case.
- The Public Finance Office chapter pays for the office and for forum-requested research, and does not direct either.
- A later corpus-institutions file carries the indicator catalog, release calendar, and research intake forms. It may not narrow this chapter.

**Chapter scope:** The office:

- produces and publishes public statistics and indicators, including wellbeing, survival-floor access, economic and business activity, and the performance of constitutional bodies;
- runs intake, researcher selection, oversight, and publication for research that a forum requests;
- rates every figure and finding it publishes on the confidence ladder in §6;
- publishes an independent check of the Public Finance Office's ledger; and
- keeps the methods, data, and correction history behind everything it publishes.

The chapter's sections cover:

- why the office exists and what it is grounded in (**section 2**);
- the office's independence, appointment, and seats (**section 3**);
- public statistics and indicators (**section 4**);
- research requested by forums (**section 5**);
- methods, evidence standards, and the confidence ladder (**section 6**);
- information handling and privacy (**section 7**);
- the bar on steering (**section 8**);
- records, audit, and challenge (**section 9**);
- funding, reserve, and shortfalls (**section 10**); and
- proportional scaling (**section 11**).

**Out of scope:** The office does not decide any matter, finding, classification, or Standing Record, set policy or advocate for it, or hold funds. The chapter leaves case-specific forensic and analytical support to **CF-23.430**, forum records and Standing Records to the registrars and [Chapter Fifteen](../../core_15.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fifteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened), data types and handling to **CS-6.305** and **CS-6.310**, and other institutions' scientific publication duties to **CI-6.270**.

### 2. Why the Public Information Office Exists

*In plain terms: people can only govern themselves, and forums can only judge fairly, if they are working from the same facts. When the people in power control the numbers, the numbers start to say what those people want.*

This section explains the reasons for the chapter. It is non-operative: the binding rules are in [§3](#3-independence-and-placement) through [§11](#11-proportional-scaling).

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter One principles:
  - [§2.1 Non-Negotiable Principle Constraints: Safety and Truth](../../core_01.000_values_principles.md#21-non-negotiable-principle-constraints-safety-and-truth);
  - [§5.1 Science-Informed Inquiry and Decision Support](../../core_01.000_values_principles.md#51-science-informed-inquiry-and-decision-support);
  - [§13.2 Epistemic Disclosure Constraints](../../core_01.020_interaction_interpretation.md#132-epistemic-disclosure-constraints);
  - [§18.3 Separation of Duties](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#183-separation-of-duties); and
  - [§19.2 Convenient Proxies and Proxy Divergence](../../core_01.050_governance_incentive_application.md#192-convenient-proxies-and-proxy-divergence).
- Chapter Six Articles:
  - [Article XVII](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-audit-transparency-and-independent-verification) (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*);
  - [Article XVII-A](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvii-a-auditability-and-observable-evidence) (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*);
  - [Article XVI-D](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvi-d-scientific-publication-review-and-replication-integrity) (*Scientific Publication, Review, and Replication Integrity*); and
  - [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*).

**The problem it answers.** Forums, governing bodies, and the public all rely on figures about wellbeing, access, the economy, and how constitutional bodies are performing. Forums sometimes need research that no single case can pay for or answer. Before this chapter, no one was charged with producing those figures and that research independently. That leaves three failures easy:

- **Convenient numbers:** a governing body that produces its own figures can change methods, delay bad news, or stop measuring what is going wrong.
- **Bought research:** a forum's evidence comes from whoever is willing to fund it, and funders tend to have a stake.
- **Unequal access:** some parties see figures early and act on them before others can.

**Why this approach.** Statistical agencies have learned these lessons over time. The United Nations *Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics* (adopted by the General Assembly in 2014) call for official statistics to be produced on strictly professional grounds, with methods made public, and with individual data kept confidential and used only for statistics.

The chapter turns those lessons into five design choices:

- **Methods first:** methods are published before the numbers, so a method cannot be picked after the result is known.
- **Equal release:** everyone gets a release at the same moment.
- **Arm's-length research:** researchers are chosen by published criteria, not by the forum or the parties, and report to everyone at once.
- **Confidence on every figure:** each figure and finding carries a rung on a published confidence ladder, so a user can tell a firm number from an early estimate.
- **Separation from money:** the office is separate from the Public Finance Office, which cannot cut its funding, and it publishes an independent check of that office's ledger.

**How it carries the Aims and the Tetrad.** Shared, trustworthy evidence lets the Constitution see whether sentients are flourishing and whether shared systems will last, which serves [the Two Constitutional Aims](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims). Within the [Constitutional Tetrad](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):

- **Oversight:** independent figures and a check of the finance ledger.
- **Accountability:** logged corrections and a challenge route.
- **Participation:** equal release to the public.
- **Timeliness:** research runs at forum request without waiting for a funder.

These reasons do not add duties. They explain why the duties in §3 to §11 take the shape they do.

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office answers to its methods. It has the standard protections every independent body gets, and its methods are checked by people outside the team that made the figures.*

**Independent Body:** The Public Information Office is an Independent Body under [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies), and meets every standard requirement there. For this office:

- it sits outside the Material Control Line of the governing authority, every forum family, and the Public Finance Office;
- the protected decision-makers are its head and its chief methodologists. The methodologists are drawn by lot under *Appointment by sortition* in Chapter Eight §11 from candidates who pass a competency gate in statistics, research methods, or the field they will oversee, and the head is chosen from among them under that section's *Leadership* rule; and
- its funding, reserve, and shortfall protections are set out in §10.

**Seat map:** Every materially binding act of the office, such as publishing an official figure, selecting a researcher, or correcting a release, places the four seats under Chapter Eight. The check seat for a method or figure is an independent method reviewer outside the team that produced it.

**No dual roles (added for this office):** In addition to Chapter Eight §11, no one may at the same time hold a decision seat in this office and any seat in the Public Finance Office, or serve as a researcher on a study the office is overseeing.

### 4. Public Statistics and Indicators

*In plain terms: methods are published before the numbers, everyone gets the numbers at the same moment, and every change is logged.*

**Indicator catalog.** The office keeps a published catalog of the indicators it produces. Each entry states what is measured, how, how often, its known limits, and its release date. Wellbeing indicators measure lived conditions, not only spending or activity.

**Methods first.** A method is published before the first release that uses it. A method change is published with its reasons, a comparison of old and new results where feasible, and the date it takes effect.

**Equal release.** Each release goes to everyone at the same published moment. No governing authority, forum, funder, or party gets the figures early, and no one may ask for a release to be moved because of what it shows.

**Timeliness.** Where waiting would leave decision-makers without information they need, the office publishes an early estimate at the rung it has earned under §6, rather than holding it back, and updates it as better evidence comes in. Speed never buys a higher rung: an early estimate says that it is early.

**Revisions.** Revisions and corrections are logged with their reasons. A figure is never silently replaced, and a revision that changes its rung says so.

### 5. Research Requested by Forums

*In plain terms: a forum says what question it needs answered. The office finds independent researchers, keeps them at arm's length from the parties, and publishes the answer to everyone at once.*

**Intake.** A forum that has published a finding under [CF-23.440 §15](../../corpus_forum/cf_23.440_technical_specialist_forums_specialist_chambers.md#cf-23440-15-research-and-uncertainty-reduction-authority), or a comparable finding by another forum family, sends the office its scope order. The office checks the order for completeness and passes it to the Public Finance Office for funding from the research reserve.

**Selection.** The office selects researchers by criteria published before the call. The requesting forum, the parties, and the Finance Office do not pick researchers. Selection:

- screens for conflicts with every party and with the question's likely winners and losers;
- states who was considered and why the chosen researchers were picked; and
- is challengeable under §9 before work begins.

**Arm's length.** No party may contact the researchers about the work except through a published, shared channel. The researchers' reports go to the requesting forum, every party, and the public at the same moment, unless a narrower lawful exception applies under §7.

**Provisional status.** A research finding informs a forum. It does not bind one, and it does not become law without the ordinary review and adoption pathways, as [CF-23.440 §15](../../corpus_forum/cf_23.440_technical_specialist_forums_specialist_chambers.md#cf-23440-15-research-and-uncertainty-reduction-authority) already requires.

**Shared questions.** Where several forums ask the same or overlapping questions, the office may combine the requests into one study, with the agreement of each requesting forum and a published record of the merger.

### 6. Methods, Evidence Standards, and the Confidence Ladder

*In plain terms: anything the office publishes must be checkable by someone outside it, and must say how sure the office is, on a fixed nine-step scale everyone can read. To climb the scale, a result needs more independent confirmation, better studies, and stronger statistics, all three at once.*

#### 6.1 What Every Publication Carries

*In plain terms: every figure comes with its rung, where it came from, how it was made, and who paid for it.*

Everything the office publishes carries:

- its rung on the confidence ladder in §6.2, with the reason for that rung;
- its provenance, methods, and uncertainty, given as a range wherever the figure is a number;
- funding and conflict disclosures;
- data or lawful access limits; and
- a searchable record of corrections and retractions.

The office's publications meet [Article XVI-D](../../core_06.020_rights_part_c.md#article-xvi-d-scientific-publication-review-and-replication-integrity) and **CI-6.270**. They must be reproducible by a qualified independent reviewer to the degree set by the applicable Technical Forum standard.

#### 6.2 The Confidence Ladder

*In plain terms: nine rungs, from a single anecdote to a result that many independent lines of evidence have confirmed for years. Each rung asks for more confirmation, better studies, and stronger statistics than the one below.*

Every figure and finding the office publishes is rated on a ladder of nine rungs, numbered from 1 (weakest) to 9 (strongest), the same shape as the nine slots of the standing scale in [Chapter Sixteen §7 Unified Proportional LEQU Scale — Contribution and Violation Axes](../../core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes). A rung measures three things, and the evidence must get stronger on all three as it climbs:

- **Confirmation:** how many independent studies, sources, or methods reach the same result;
- **Quality:** how well those studies or sources are designed, carried out, and checked; and
- **Statistical strength:** how unlikely the result is to be chance, and how narrow its uncertainty range is.

| Rung | Name | Confirmation | Quality | Statistical strength |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anecdotal** | None. A single report, an expert's view, or an untested model. | No systematic method. | Not tested. |
| 2 | **Indicative** | One study or source, not yet checked by the office. | Exploratory: a small or convenience sample, or a method not set before the data were seen. | A pattern that has not reached significance, or was not tested for it. |
| 3 | **Preliminary** | One study or source, checked by the office's method reviewer. | A published method, but limited sample, coverage, or controls. | Significant at the 5% level (p < 0.05) or the field's equivalent, with a wide range. |
| 4 | **Provisional** | One good study or source, or two smaller ones that agree. | A published method with an adequate sample or coverage, and data open to checking. | p < 0.05, and the conclusion holds across the whole uncertainty range. |
| 5 | **Moderate** | At least two independent studies or sources that agree. | At least one strong design, such as a controlled study or a full count; methods published in advance. | Each result p < 0.05; combined p < 0.01. |
| 6 | **Substantial** | At least three independent studies by different teams, or three independent sources, that agree. | Mostly strong designs, at least one pre-registered; the method has passed independent audit. | Combined p < 0.005, with no unexplained contrary result of similar quality. |
| 7 | **Strong** | Confirmed by at least three independent teams, including at least one direct replication, and summarized in a published systematic review. | Pre-registered, audited, high-quality designs carry the result. | Combined p < 0.001; results consistent across studies. |
| 8 | **Robust** | At least five independent studies, using different methods or populations, agree in a systematic review or meta-analysis; the result has held across revisions. | High-quality designs dominate, and the review found no material bias. | Combined p < 0.0001; the size of the effect is stable as evidence is added. |
| 9 | **Established** | Several independent lines of evidence, from different methods and fields, converge, and the result has held over many years and many tests. | As for rung 8, with each line of evidence independently audited. | At least five-sigma strength (about p < 3 × 10⁻⁷) or the field's equivalent, and no credible contrary evidence. |

#### 6.3 Reading the Three Tests

*In plain terms: a result needs all three tests to reach a rung, the confirming studies must really be independent, statistics must be reported honestly, and unexplained contrary evidence holds a result down.*

- **All three must be met:** a figure or finding takes the highest rung whose three tests it meets in full. A result that is highly significant in one small, unreplicated study stays low; many weak studies that agree do not reach a high rung either.
- **Independence:** studies or sources count as independent only when different teams produced them from different data, and no team is funded by, employed by, or otherwise tied to a party with a [material stake](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) in the result. A new analysis of the same data is a check, not a confirmation.
- **Honest significance:** significance is adjusted for the number of tests run, and is reported with the size of the effect and its uncertainty range, never alone. A result found only after searching many comparisons, or whose analysis was not set in advance, is held at rung 4 or below until it is confirmed by a study designed to test it.
- **Contrary evidence:** an unexplained contrary result of similar quality holds a finding at rung 5 or below until it is explained or outweighed under the tests above.
- **Field equivalents:** the significance thresholds in the table are defaults. Where a field measures strength in another way, such as Bayes factors or interval widths, or where a field's own standard differs, the corpus-institutions file publishes the equivalent for each rung. It may tune a threshold to the field, so long as each rung asks for evidence of equal strength in that field and the rungs stay in order; it may not lower a rung's bar to fit a result already in hand.

#### 6.4 Special Kinds of Evidence

*In plain terms: the same ladder rates findings that something has no effect, forecasts, research in fields that cannot run experiments, and official statistics, each with tests that fit it.*

- **Findings of no effect:** a finding that an effect is absent, or too small to matter, climbs the same ladder. A result that is merely not significant counts as no evidence either way. To rank as a finding of no effect, the study must state in advance the smallest effect that would matter, and its uncertainty range must rule that effect out: by an equivalence test or a comparable method at the strength the rung requires. Confirmation and quality are tested in the same way as for any other finding.
- **Forecasts and projections:** a forecast cannot be confirmed by repeating it, so its rung comes from its record. The office publishes each forecasting method's past forecasts beside what then happened, and the method's rung rests on that record: how many forecasts it has made, how often reality fell inside its stated range, and how it compares with a simple benchmark, such as assuming no change. A new method with no record starts no higher than rung 3, whatever its sophistication. Every forecast states its range and the assumptions it depends on.
- **Fields without experiments:** where a controlled or pre-registered study is impossible or would be unethical, a strong observational design counts as a strong design for every rung. Examples are a natural experiment, a well-specified comparison across places or periods, or several independent data sets that rule out the main alternative explanations. The method reviewer states which alternatives were ruled out and how.
- **Figures that are not studies:** for official statistics, a confirmation is an independent source or method that measures the same quantity, quality is the coverage and audit of the collection, and statistical strength is the width of the uncertainty range relative to the figure (or, for a full count, the audited error rate). The corpus-institutions file sets these equivalents for each type of figure, and they may be no looser than the tests in the table.

#### 6.5 How Rungs Are Given and Used

*In plain terms: an independent reviewer sets the rung by published tests, no one may move it for convenience, and anyone who relies on a figure in a public decision must state its rung.*

- **Independent rating:** the rung is set by the independent method reviewer in the check seat under §3, not by the team that produced the figure.
- **Published criteria:** the corpus-institutions file sets the tests for each rung by type of figure. A rung may be given only when its tests are met, and the reason names the test.
- **Weakest link:** a figure built from several inputs takes no higher rung than its weakest material input, unless the method shows why that input does not drive the result.
- **No rating for convenience:** no one may raise a rung to make a figure more useful or lower one to make it easier to ignore. A change of rung is logged and published like any revision.
- **Research findings:** findings from forum-requested research under §5 carry a rung on the same ladder, set by the office's method reviewer, not by the researchers.

**The rung travels with the figure.** Anyone who republishes or relies on an official figure in a public decision states its rung. This chapter does not set what rung a forum or decision-maker needs. That is for the rules that govern each decision; this chapter makes sure the rung is always known.

### 7. Information Handling and Privacy

*In plain terms: the office handles more personal data than almost anyone. The general privacy rule for every independent body lives in Chapter Eight; this section adds what is special to statistics and research.*

**General rule.** The office meets *Information handling and privacy* in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies). In addition:

- data collected for statistics or research is used only for statistics or research, never for any other duty of the office or of anyone else; and
- no statistical record may be used as evidence against any sentient it describes, whoever gave the data.

### 8. No Steering

*In plain terms: no one may change, hide, delay, or time a number or a finding because of what it says.*

The funding-integrity, gifts-and-benefits, and apparent-conflict floors of [Article XIII-C](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and [Article XIII-D](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-d-funding-integrity) (*Funding Integrity*) apply to the office and its researchers, with records kept under **CI-8.210 §4** (*Gifts, Apparent Conflicts, and Funding Integrity*). In addition, the office, the governing authority, the Public Finance Office, every forum, and every funder must not:

- alter, suppress, delay, or time the release of a figure or finding because of what it shows;
- reward or punish a researcher, methodologist, or official for a result;
- tie the office's or a study's funding to a result;
- direct the office to measure or stop measuring an indicator without a published decision under §4; or
- press the office to raise or lower a confidence rung.

The office must not advocate for a policy, a party, or an outcome. It may explain what its figures and findings show and what they do not.

**Pattern evidence.** A pattern of delayed releases for unfavorable figures, dropped indicators that had been showing decline, or method changes that consistently move a figure in one direction is evidence of steering and shifts the burden to show a lawful, published reason.

### 9. Records, Audit, and Challenge

*In plain terms: anyone can question a figure, a method, or a researcher choice, and an independent forum decides. The general audit and challenge rule for every independent body lives in Chapter Eight; this section says how it applies here.*

**General rule.** The office meets *Audit and challenge* in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies). For this office:

- the audit of its work is an independent method audit of its methods and releases, and its spending is audited with the Public Finance Office's audit;
- a challenge may concern a release, a method, a confidence rung, a researcher selection, or a refusal to publish; and
- a successful challenge leads to a published correction, a re-run, or a new researcher selection, as fits.

### 10. Funding, Reserve, and Shortfalls

*In plain terms: whoever pays the office could steer what it finds, so its money is protected the way every independent body's is: its own budget track, its own reserve for bad years, and a published rule for any cut. The general rule is in Chapter Eight; this section says how it applies here.*

**General rule.** *Protected funding* in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies) applies to the office in full, including its *Reserve* and *Shortfalls* requirements. The Public Finance Office chapter carries the mechanics; this section applies them to this office.

**Funding floor.** The office's floor:

- is worked out on its own published track, under the rules of [§4 of the Public Finance Office chapter](public_finance_office_draft_2026-11-19.md#4-funding-floors-for-forums-and-remedy-systems), from the indicator catalog, the release calendar, and the expected volume of forum-requested research the office oversees;
- is paid through the Public Finance Office, which may not set, reduce, delay, or condition it; and
- is protected by [§5 of that chapter](public_finance_office_draft_2026-11-19.md#5-no-steering-by-money), so a cut or delay that follows an unwelcome figure is presumed to be steering.

**Research money is separate.** Researchers are paid from the research reserve under [§6 of the Public Finance Office chapter](public_finance_office_draft_2026-11-19.md#6-research-funding-at-forum-request). That reserve is not part of the office's floor or reserve, and neither may be used to cover the other.

**Reserve.** The office's reserve is held for it as its own share of the reserve fund under [§11 of the Public Finance Office chapter](public_finance_office_draft_2026-11-19.md#11-reserve-fund). Its default target is one quarter of its floor for a funding cycle. It is drawn only for this office.

**Shortfalls.** In a shortfall under [§12 of the Public Finance Office chapter](public_finance_office_draft_2026-11-19.md#12-shortfalls-and-emergencies), the office's reserve is drawn first. If its floor must still be cut, these functions are cut last, in this order:

- releases of indicators that this Constitution or a forum relies on, on their published calendar;
- oversight of research already under way; and
- correction of errors in published figures.

A shortfall never suspends the *Methods first* or *Equal release* rules of §4. It may delay a release only by a published notice that applies to every user alike.

**Other income.** The office may charge for custom data work only on published terms. It may not charge a party to a matter for figures that bear on that matter, and fee income never replaces or reduces its floor.

### 11. Proportional Scaling

*In plain terms: a small community may share an office or borrow statistics from a larger one, as long as the work stays independent.*

The *Scaling* rule in [Chapter Eight §11](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#11-independent-bodies) applies, including sharing administration under **CF-23.470 §5** and using another adopter's office by published agreement, if that office also meets this chapter. Even when hosting is merged, the office keeps decision-makers separate from every forum whose research it runs.
