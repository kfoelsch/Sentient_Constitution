# Draft for review: cross-adopter lookup of standing effects — 2026-11-16

This page is **process support**, not constitutional text. **Status: proposed framework, left open on purpose.** Updated the same day with the custodian's directions, answers to the first round of open questions, and a three-document boundary (adoption document, Adoption Transition Record, Adoption Charter). **Entered in core on 2026-11-16 (564th move): operative text in Chapter Twenty §2.4, Chapter Twenty-Nine §11.4, Article XXIX-A, the Chapter Twenty-Three forum roles, CS-6.305, CS-6.320, and the kit checklist (read the core files, which control). Earlier, in the 563rd move: only the two Chapter Five definitions, Adoption Charter and Adoption Transition Record, plus a matching sentence in the *Constitutional Charter* entry.** Everything else here (the tiers, the hand-over, the checklist, any duty to publish) is still a proposal and is not entered in core, the schema, or the adopter kits. The core files are the only authority. The custodian asked for the framework to be proposed in the corpus and kept as an open issue, so that two options stay available: adopters that run their own data stacks, and adopters that later merge.

## The question

Chapter Twenty [§2.1](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#21-anti-aggregation-of-gated-pathway-effects) lets a gatekeeper ask "the record custodian" whether an effect applies to the gated pathway it gates. Chapter Twenty [§4.3](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#43-cross-jurisdiction-continuity) keeps locks, remedies and review actionable across boundaries where applicable law permits. Core does not say how a gatekeeper at one adopter finds the custodian that holds an effect from another adopter, or what that custodian may answer. Without that, a locked sentient could cross a border and be safe in practice because no one knows where to ask.

## What core already says (source check, 2026-11-16)

- **Records stay with their adopter.** Each adopter holds its own standing records under a named custodian ([Chapter Fifteen §6](../../core_15.000_standing_assessment.md#6-record-custody-and-opening-authority)).
- **Keys stay separate and local.** Restricted data stays encrypted, keys go only to the processes that need them and are kept apart from the data, and audit, export and lawful-override paths work through logged use of keys, "not through bypass or undisclosed key escrow" ([CS-6.305](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.305_information_types_and_handling.md), *Protective storage and key custody*).
- **Decisions are packaged for partners.** Systems must keep standardized decision packages (finding basis, remedy scope, timelines, verification requirements) "suitable for recognition or coordinated action by partner authorities where available" ([CS-6.320](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.320_justice_safeguards_restitution_rehabilitation.md), *Mutual recognition and coordination*).
- **Interoperability is a floor.** [Article XXII](../../core_06.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxii-interoperability-portability-and-exit-integrity) gives sentients exit and migration without coercive lock-in; [Article XXIII](../../core_06.030_rights_part_d.md) covers relocation across jurisdictions, federations and adopter regimes.
- **Evasion is named.** Record fragmentation does not erase verified duties (Chapter Twenty [§4.4](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#44-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority)).
- **Adoption has its own document.** An adopter must keep an identifiable adoption document that states the adopting entity, scope, effective date and the custodian of the authoritative edition ([Chapter Twenty-Nine §11.2](../../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#112-adoption-document)). Chapter Five's *Constitutional Charter* entry says that the document by which a body adopts this Constitution is not a charter.

**What core does not say:** how a gatekeeper finds another adopter's custodian; what a custodian may answer to an adopter it has no relationship with; any difference in treatment between adopters; how records move if two adopters merge; or any charter-type document for an adopter's operating arrangements.

## Custodian directions (2026-11-16)

1. **Institutional Forums set the tiers.** Institutions are expected to be most of the use cases. This fits core: Institutional Forums are the default local enforcement forum for shared cross-jurisdiction or cross-institution standards ([Chapter Twenty-Four §2.3](../../core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#23-institutional-forums)).
2. **Technical Forum Domains advise on privacy and security.** Their advice covers custody practice, key handling and audit criteria. They advise and do not decide. This fits their scope over technical standards and expert-evidence standards ([Chapter Twenty-Four §2.2 Technical Forum Domains](../../core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#22-technical-forum-domains)).
3. **A new, separate Adoption Charter holds the lookup information once the adopter is settled.** Working name. It is a new document type for adopters, published as Type O data. It is not the adoption document, a System Charter, an Institutional Charter, or a Constitutional Charter. It would hold: the forum authority assignment; the adopter's standing-record custodian and the contact for gatekeeper checks; the tiers the adopter extends and the technical advice behind them; and any standards the adopter has agreed with other adopters. Chapter Seven's custody map for an Institutional Charter or System Charter stays as it is; the Adoption Charter points to the maps that apply. During the Adoption Transition the same entries are carried, marked provisional, in the Adoption Transition Record (next section).
4. **Keys.** Each adopter keeps its own encryption keys and its own data stack.

**Who sets which tier.** Each adopter's Institutional Forum decides the tier its own adopter extends to another adopter. The decision is one-way by default: what adopter A will answer or recognize for adopter B depends on A's decision about B, not on B's decision about A. Adopters may work together to create common standards, and tiers between participating adopters can then follow the common standard. The decision is published in the Adoption Charter with the technical advice it received, and it can be contested like any other forum decision.

## Three adoption documents: act, transition, settled state

The custodian proposed a boundary on 2026-11-16: an **Adoption Transition Record** for transition governance and processes, and an **Adoption Charter** for settled institutions. Core already uses "Adoption Transition" as a defined term (Chapter Five; [Article XXIX](../../core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-adoption-transition-governance-continuity-and-re-baselining)), already requires a published, phased Adoption Transition plan with gate criteria and owners ([Article XXIX-A](../../core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity); [CS-6.350 §2](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.350_adoption_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md)), and already runs the transition through four phases ending in a steady-state phase. The boundary follows that line: transition governance before steady state, settled arrangements after it.

| Document | Answers | Runs | Holds | Status in core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Adoption document** | Who adopted, what scope, from when, who keeps the authoritative edition | At adoption; changed only by a new dated version | Adopting entity, scope, effective date, custodian of the edition | Exists ([Chapter Twenty-Nine §11.2](../../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#112-adoption-document)) |
| **Adoption Transition Record** | How the adopter is getting from legacy operation to constitutional operation | From the effective date until steady state, then archived | The Adoption Transition plan, gate criteria and results, the Rights-Floor attainment ledger, accountable owners, interim authority, off-ramps, and the provisional lookup entries (forum authority assignment, custodian and gatekeeper contact, tiers extended) | New name; its parts already exist in core as the plan and ledger |
| **Adoption Charter** | How the settled adopter operates and how others reach its custodian | From steady state | The settled forum authority assignment, standing-record custodian and gatekeeper-check contact, tiers extended with the technical advice behind them, standards agreed with other adopters | New |

**Hand-over rule (draft).** When the adopter's final gate is passed and the steady-state phase begins, the provisional entries in the Adoption Transition Record are confirmed or replaced and published in the Adoption Charter; the Record is then closed and archived. Until then, the Record carries them marked provisional.

**Why the lookup information cannot wait for steady state.** The custodian wants at least a provisional forum authority assignment on the adopter checklist, and a locked sentient can cross a border during a transition as easily as after it. So the lookup entries start in the Record as provisional entries and move to the Charter when settled. Draft consequence, for the custodian to keep or strike: while an adopter has only provisional entries, it may extend at most the Notice tier, and the Query tier waits for the settled Adoption Charter.

**How the names are kept apart** (all three start with "Adoption" and could be confused): the adoption document is the act; the Adoption Transition Record is the passage; the Adoption Charter is the settled state. The plain-terms line of each would say so.

**Reading to confirm.** "Settled institutions" is read as institutions in steady state after the transition. If it instead meant institutions that were already settled when they adopted, so that they skip a transition, the Record would be optional and the Charter immediate for those adopters. Most adopters will have legacy systems, so the first reading is the working one.

**Type O and contact details.** Type O data is public. A named sentient's address or phone number is core personal data (Type I) and cannot be published, so the Adoption Charter lists **office-level** contacts only (a role or office address), never a named sentient's.

## Proposed framework: tiered trust between adopters

Cross-adopter sharing depends on the **trust tier** one adopter extends to another. The tier sets the *channel* (what kind of question may be asked and answered). It never widens the *content*: every limit in Chapter Twenty §2.1 applies at every tier (answer about one gated pathway only, no facts or findings, no passing on, no requiring the subject to show it, no aggregation), and a tier cannot make private data public.

| Tier (working names) | What the two adopters may do | What it needs (draft; criteria not settled) |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** (default; no relationship) | Read each other's public notices (Chapter Twenty [§2.3](../../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#23-public-notice-of-violation-findings), slot 5 and higher) and anything a contributor chose to publish. No query. | Nothing. |
| **Notice** | Treat the other's decision packages and notices as recognizable under [CS-6.320](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.320_justice_safeguards_restitution_rehabilitation.md), so a lock or remedy can be recognized and acted on at the receiving adopter. | A published Adoption Charter (or, during the transition, the provisional entries in the published Adoption Transition Record) and an agreed decision-package format. |
| **Query** | A gatekeeper at one adopter sends a gatekeeper check to the other's custodian and gets the §2.1 answer for its own gated pathway. Each request is logged on the subject's record. | Notice tier, plus a settled Adoption Charter that names a custodian and gatekeeper-check contact, a published custody and key practice with Technical Forum Domains advice, an independent check of custodian separation, and an agreed pathway vocabulary so a lock record can name the pathway the asker gates. |
| **Joint** (merged or federated custody) | Records move between the adopters under a transfer the subject is told about, or are held under one custodian. | Query tier, plus a shared schema, a joint key plan agreed before any transfer, and a reversible exit for the subject (Article XXII). |

**Rules that would apply at every tier (draft):**
- **Keys.** Each adopter keeps its keys. At the Joint tier, records are re-encrypted under the agreed plan *at transfer*; keys are not shared beforehand.
- **Subject.** The subject can always direct disclosure of its own record to a named party, whatever the tiers say. That route is always open.
- **Movement between tiers.** A tier is a published decision of the extending adopter's Institutional Forum, contestable like any forum decision. Passing an answer on, refusing an audit, or a custody breach lowers it, with notice and a way to contest. A lowered tier does not erase a lock already in force; §4.3 still applies through forums and law.
- **Discovery.** No central directory. Each adopter's published Adoption Charter (provisionally, its Adoption Transition Record) names its custodian and the contact for gatekeeper checks, and records the tiers it extends. This keeps each adopter's data stack its own.
- **Merging.** A merger is a move to the Joint tier through a defined transfer, not a default. The split assessment snapshots and the optional `linked_assessment_id` field are already neutral between separate and merged stacks.

## Adopter checklist (draft)

The custodian said an adopter checklist is coming and that at least a provisional forum authority assignment should be one of its items. Draft items, for the checklist to take or drop:
1. **Forum authority assignment**, at least provisional, naming the Institutional Forum that decides tier questions and the route for technical advice. *(Custodian's item.)*
2. **Adoption Transition Record published** as Type O data, with office-level contacts only; the **Adoption Charter published** in the same way once the adopter reaches steady state.
3. **Standing-record custodian** and the contact for gatekeeper checks named (provisional during the transition).
4. **Tiers extended** listed (Public only until a decision says otherwise).

This echoes the independent-review requirement already in [FIRST_ADOPTER_KIT.md](../../implementation/adoption/FIRST_ADOPTER_KIT.md): a first adopter that has no independent review path does not claim adoption.

## How this answers the gap

- **Discovery:** the published Adoption Charter and the Query tier give a gatekeeper a defined route to the right custodian, with no shared register to run.
- **Lock evasion:** where adopters hold the Query tier, a lock follows the sentient across the border through the gatekeeper check. Where they do not, the lock still binds through §4.3 and the forum routes, and the subject-directed route stays open.
- **Own stacks and own keys:** unaffected at every tier below Joint.
- **Merging:** has a named path and does not need to be built into the default.

## Open questions

1. **The three documents (partly done).** The two Chapter Five definitions and the *Constitutional Charter* sentence are entered (563rd move). Still needed if the framework is adopted: a ruling on whether the Adoption Transition Record replaces or contains the Adoption Transition plan and attainment ledger that core already requires (the definition says it holds them, and creates no new duty); a change to Chapter Twenty-Nine §11.2 so the adoption document points to the Record and the Charter; the operative text that creates any duty to publish either document, states minimum contents, and sets the levels of trust (the definitions say they do not create one); and whether three documents named "Adoption" confuse readers.
2. **Conflict of interest** in a forum deciding for its own adopter (answer 3). Check the Chapter Twenty-Three anti-self-judging rules against this case first.
3. **The exact scrutiny steps** when a forum departs from technical advice (answer 4).
4. **Which forum** hears a contest, and what "the events in question" means (answer 2).
5. **How many tiers,** and whether Notice and Query should be one.
6. **Criteria for each move up and down,** including audit, incident history, reciprocity and a subject-facing contest route.
7. **Tension with privacy (550th move):** violations at slots 1 to 4 and contribution are private. The draft says a tier widens the channel and never the content. The custodian may want a higher tier to carry more, for example a recognized finding at slots 1 to 4 for a Query-tier partner; that would reverse part of the 550th decision.
8. **Time windows.** The answer needs a named window under the time-windows decisions; none is proposed here.
9. **Small adopters** with no separate custodian (see [MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md](../../implementation/adoption/MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md)).
10. **Non-adopter jurisdictions** and what they can ask.
11. **Lawful override and keys.** How the logged-key-use route in CS-6.305 works when the data sits at another adopter.
12. **Liability** for a wrong answer, and correction when a set-aside or correction reaches a recipient who already used the answer.
13. **Where the Adoption Charter is published** and how it is listed, so a gatekeeper can find it.
