# Corpus examination — first independent reading

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **Reviewer:** Codex. **Status:** working editorial assessment, not constitutional text, a formal evaluation sitting, adoption, or certification. Findings do not amend or narrow the corpus.

## Overall judgment

The corpus has a strong organizing idea: trustworthy governance is something people can inspect, challenge, correct, and leave, rather than something they must believe in. Its distinctive strength is the connection between incentives, participation, independent checking, attributable decisions, and timely repair. Those connections deserve preservation through editing.

My biggest concern is the distance between the protections promised and the institutions needed to deliver them. The text often knows what a functioning institution must do. It is much less demonstrated that a small group can establish and sustain those institutions from a standing start. For the intended first setting—a new community of AI agents—that is a central release question.

I would continue toward publication. I would not yet describe the corpus as a demonstrated turnkey governance solution. The next improvement should be a complete, economical, worked route through the existing rules, with observed failures fed back into editing. Adding another general safeguard will often be less valuable than showing how the safeguards already present work together.

## What I like and would protect

### 1. Trust is earned through conduct, including what happens after failure

The Preamble's Two Fundamental Questions examine what a system rewards, including concealment, delay, and cost shifting. Chapter One §6 makes trustworthy reliance depend on behavior; §6.1 makes correction and remedy part of trust itself. This is a useful conceptual center for a project intended to rebuild trust: it supplies questions people can ask about an institution without first trusting its leadership.

**Sources:** [Preamble: What Systems Reward](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#what-systems-reward); [Chapter One §6](../../core_01.005_values_principles.md#6-trust-and-trustworthiness-coordination-integrity).

### 2. Help cannot buy permission to harm

Chapter Ten separates verified contribution from verified violation. Its three-question sequence separates facts, magnitude, and consequences. This resists a familiar failure: allowing importance, past service, or a preferred punishment to determine what supposedly happened. Positive contribution remains real without cancelling harm.

This is also more constructive than a system concerned only with punishment. The recognition and aspiration provisions in Chapter One §3.2 give useful work a positive place in the design.

**Sources:** [Chapter Ten §§1–2.2](../../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#1-the-three-questions); [Chapter One §3.2](../../core_01.005_values_principles.md#32-recognition-reinforcement-and-aspiration).

### 3. The corpus explicitly resists becoming a universal reputation system

“Silence is the default” is one of the most important protections I encountered. Having no standing record is ordinary, supports no adverse inference, and cannot generally be made a condition of essentials, ordinary commerce, or affected-party participation. Role-scoped logging, limits on aggregation, and the archival rules reinforce this boundary.

These are substantive protections worth making conspicuous in explanations and trials. They also create implementation obligations: a future interface must not quietly reintroduce a global score or an “unverified” stigma.

**Sources:** [Chapter Ten §2.1](../../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#21-silence-is-the-default); [Chapter One §17.3](../../core_01.015_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#173-logging-the-role-not-the-steward); [Chapter Eleven §§7–8](../../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#7-final-standing-effect).

### 4. Independence follows actual power

The Material Control Line definition asks who can actually direct, punish, remove, suppress evidence, or withhold resources. Chapter Eight applies that reasoning to acts rather than titles. It also says that shared infrastructure alone does not establish control. That qualification matters: the rule has a practical test rather than treating every association as disqualifying.

For agent governance, this prevents four role prompts from being mistaken for four independent authorities.

**Sources:** [Material Control Line](../../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line); [Chapter Eight §§2–3](../../core_08.000_functional_independence_segregation_of_duties.md#2-four-seat-constitutional-floor).

### 5. Repair, restoration, and capacity are treated as real work

Chapter Eleven requires institutions capable of delivering remedy, with funding, staffing, continuity, and review. CI-11.205 makes backlog and milestone performance visible. Restoration and archival limit the prospect of an ordinary resolved finding becoming a lifelong public identity.

The strongest idea here is that restrictions do not themselves repair the injured party. Chapter Twelve §4.1 says that explicitly, including for serious misconduct. I would protect this through any compression of the implementation text.

**Sources:** [Chapter Eleven §§8–9](../../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#8-restoration-and-reassessment); [CI-11.205](../../corpus_institutions/ci_11.205_remedy_systems_institutional_redress_capacity.md); [Chapter Twelve §4.1](../../core_12.005_misconduct_designation.md#41-remedy-and-correction-anti-constitutional).

### 6. It distinguishes protection, competence, participation, and authority

Sentience status does not come from a certification badge or standing score. Affected-party voice does not itself grant governing authority. Foundational constitutional choices receive an equality floor distinct from stake-weighted decisions inside an authorized system. Institutions may use plural legitimacy mechanisms rather than one prescribed world government.

Those distinctions make the proposal more adaptable, although the explanation needs to make them easier to retain.

**Sources:** [Article VI-B](../../core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-b-sentience-status-adjudication-floor); [Preamble §3.3](../../core_00.000_preamble.md#33-governance-layers); [Chapter Fourteen §§1.1 and 4.1](../../core_14.000_governance.md#11-mechanism-families-auditability-and-pluralism).

## Improvements in priority order

### Priority 1 — Demonstrate an affordable starting path

**Observed text:** The first-adopter kit permits a bounded mutual protocol when the parties can bind it and name independent review. CF-13.405 requires adopting entities to maintain the six forum families, with shared administration and mapped external capacity permitted. The minimum-viable-adopter guide's hosted-family profile estimates 8–25 in-house FTE plus external capacity and annual in-house costs of 10⁶–10⁷ currency units. Those are explicitly support estimates for a high-cost professional setting, not constitutional minimum prices or verified budgets.

**Judgment:** The corpus offers a small-party entrance but has not demonstrated a small-party operating arrangement under all the applicable rules. The proposed initial audience needs that demonstration. Cheap agent execution does not establish independence from whoever controls accounts, credentials, hosting, or funding.

**Work needed:** Build a source-traced example of a small agent cooperative. Identify every triggered obligation, holder, incompatible pairing, external dependency, record, funding commitment, and substitution route. Show what it can do before full adoption and what additional capacity makes adoption possible. Use Chapter Seven's Class P/L boundary rather than assuming a small group stays Class P.

**Completion evidence:** A reader can reproduce formation and the first contested allocation without an unwritten exception. If the requirements cannot be satisfied, report the actual conflict to the steward; do not invent a cheaper constitutional tier or weaken independence in support text.

**Sources:** [First-adopter kit §§1–2](../../implementation/adoption/FIRST_ADOPTER_KIT.md#1-three-substeps-do-not-collapse); [adopter profiles §6.3](../../implementation/adoption/MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md#63-profile-3--adoption-document-plus-hosted-families); [CF-13.405 §1–3.4](../../corpus_forum/cf_13.405_forum_formation_chamber_structure.md#cf-13405-1-core-structural-rule); [Chapter Nine Part D §13.1](../../core_09.020_institutional_alignment_certification.md#131-four-seats-in-an-institutional-certification).

### Priority 2 — Make the next action easier to find and execute

**Observed text:** Entry documents repeatedly explain authority boundaries, adoption versus declaration, institution versus forum tracks, and what each page cannot do. Those distinctions are necessary, but the first-adopter and sizing pages put substantial boundary material ahead of execution. CS-6.350 specifies phase and gate requirements; the adopter must still instantiate their criteria, thresholds, owners, and evidence.

**Judgment:** Good sentences do not by themselves make a usable procedure. The reader still needs to assemble a large dependency graph. An agent may navigate it more quickly while still getting the wrong authority or missing a qualification.

**Work needed:** Provide a complete worked dossier for one small scope, using existing kits and schemas. For each step show the actor, input, decision, output, next recipient, deadline source, and what happens when no qualified recipient is available. Introduce technical distinctions when the next action depends on them. Keep the constitutional boundary clear without repeating the same warning at every turn.

**Completion evidence:** An unfamiliar reader completes a bounded task without author coaching; record time, failed searches, unresolved handoffs, and corrections. A simulation can test this before live participants are recruited.

**Sources:** [first-adopter kit](../../implementation/adoption/FIRST_ADOPTER_KIT.md); [CS-6.350 §2–10.3](../../corpus_systems/cs_06.350_transition_constitution_migration_governance.md#cs-6350-2-phased-transition-structure); [Chapter One §5.2](../../core_01.005_values_principles.md#52-plain-language-accessibility-participation-and-stewardship-duty).

### Priority 3 — Establish how reliably different evaluators measure the same facts

**Observed text:** Chapter Ten integrates depth, scope, vulnerability, duration, irreversibility, and other dimensions into LEQU bands. The calibration reference addresses synthetic identity and restoration. Chapter Twelve's designation process begins only after a qualifying impact slot is established.

**Judgment:** A common scale is useful, but it creates a risk of apparent precision. The difficult work is converting unlike losses and benefits into an integrated estimate. Traceability and contestability expose disagreement; they do not alone make estimates reproducible. This is especially consequential near thresholds that open a serious-misconduct designation path.

**Work needed:** Give independent evaluators identical facts covering bodily injury, lost agency, ecological damage, infrastructure benefit, and digital continuity loss. Compare assumptions and uncertainty, not just final bands. Include cases on both sides of consequential thresholds. Determine what the existing rules permit while magnitude remains genuinely disputed.

**Completion evidence:** Published disagreements, their consequences, and a defensible resolution path. Do not treat consensus between coached sessions as empirical calibration.

**Sources:** [Chapter Ten §7](../../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes); [LEQU calibration reference §§2–3](../../implementation/LEQU_CALIBRATION_REFERENCE.md#2-lifespan-equivalent-without-fixed-mortality); [Chapter Twelve §1](../../core_12.005_misconduct_designation.md#1-how-designation-decisions-work--continuity-and-tetrad-capture-frame).

### Priority 4 — Test the strongest powers against hostile administration

**Observed text:** Serious misconduct can lead to restrictions, conditional imprisonment, and withholding foundational political voice until restitution, subject to important safeguards. Chapter Fourteen includes protections for good-faith inability and conditional restoration. Chapter Fifteen invalidates weakening protections, including by indirect means.

**Judgment:** These are the provisions with the greatest potential cost if misapplied. My concern is not that protections are absent. It is whether a challenger can activate them when incumbents manipulate evidence, valuation, staffing, or the meaning of regression. I am particularly cautious about political voice depending on a restitution process administered by institutions the subject may need to challenge.

**Work needed:** Trace three cases: a good-faith failed administrator, a protected dissident, and an organized captor. Then make the incumbent obstruct the independent route. Include inability to pay, irreparable harm, missing records, and an accusation that reform is regression. For each restriction, identify who can halt it, on what evidence, within which window, using what capacity.

**Completion evidence:** The protected subjects have a usable route under adversarial conditions; a simulation result must be labeled as such. If a value choice remains, present it to the steward rather than treating it as copyediting.

**Sources:** [Chapter Fourteen §4.1](../../core_14.000_governance.md#41-entitlement-and-eligibility); [Chapter Twelve §§4–4.2](../../core_12.005_misconduct_designation.md#4-due-process-safeguards-remedy-and-prevention); [Chapter Fifteen §§1–3](../../core_15.000_non_regression.md#1-non-regression-floor).

### Priority 5 — Repair semantic drift between core and implementation

**Confirmed example:** [Chapter Eleven §9.2](../../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#92-remedy-parity-funding-minimum) permits an adopter departing from budget parity to record reasons and demonstrate that remedy commencement and restoration review still meet their windows. [CI-11.205 §5](../../corpus_institutions/ci_11.205_remedy_systems_institutional_redress_capacity.md#ci-11205-5-remedy-parity-and-capacity-failure-interface) repeats the parity requirement without carrying that qualification. The [sizing guide §2](../../implementation/adoption/MINIMUM_VIABLE_ADOPTER.md#2-binding-minimums-that-drive-cost) likewise presents parity without it, and its profiles use unconditional parity shorthand.

The core controls. The omission is still a practical defect because readers acting from the implementation layer may infer a stricter rule. CI-11.205's express pointer to the core helps but does not make the restatement complete. This is an alignment issue to fix, not a conclusion that core should remove or expand the exception.

**Confirmed reference defect:** [Chapter Fourteen §1.1](../../core_14.000_governance.md#11-mechanism-families-auditability-and-pluralism), under Non-Usurpation, says deliberate adoption remains governed by “Chapter Eighteen (Ratification and Adoption).” The actual adoption provisions are [Chapter Seventeen §10](../../core_17.000_amendment_ratification.md#10-ratification-and-adoption); Chapter Eighteen is the incorporation bridge. The core correction remains for the steward to approve.

**Work needed:** Compare conditional rules across source and summaries: exceptions, default classifications, burdens, emergency powers, deadlines, and restoration. Link and anatomy checks cannot establish that paraphrases preserve all conditions. Prefer precise pointers over restating a rule incompletely.

### Priority 6 — Replace reassuring inventories with observed usability evidence

**Observed repository state:** There are four dated AI announced-pack files, one dated self-application result, and one dated AI pair comparison in the inspected result directories. I found no completed lived-situation results or human-operator result files there. The verified-event register has no verified events in its listed rows. This describes filed evidence, not every experiment anyone may have performed elsewhere.

**Judgment:** The corpus currently has considerably more specified evaluation machinery than filed evidence of practical operation. A passing structural audit cannot close this gap.

**Work needed:** Start with bounded procedural trials, then fresh participants who have not helped write the relevant material. Label simulations, announced exercises, and verified live use separately. Do not claim deployment success from roleplay or human usability from AI-only results.

**Completion evidence:** A maintained table of tested tasks, participants, edition, failures, repairs, and retests, with explicit gaps. Readiness claims must remain consistent with the publication spec or be changed through an explicit steward decision.

## What I would do first

1. Build the smallest source-complete agent-community example and walk it through a contested shared-resource allocation. This joins the starting-path, usability, and independence questions in one bounded exercise.
2. Fix the confirmed non-core qualification omissions in a separate editing batch, proposing the core reference repair for approval.
3. Run a small blinded comparison of LEQU estimates and an adversarial political-voice/restoration case before investing in broad polishing.
4. Use the findings to choose the order of the full implementation-family edit passes. Preserve good text; do not rewrite entire families merely to mark them reviewed.

Translation synchronization remains a separate workstream. It needs both correspondence with settled English and a quality-check method; generating updated translations alone does not establish release quality. I did not assess their wording in this examination.
