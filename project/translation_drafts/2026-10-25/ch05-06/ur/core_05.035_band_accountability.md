## Additional updated passage — Record Integrity core safeguards (source lines 621–685; source SHA-256: cf5fc6a441e342dba8c14f9fff84e3d34235beec0b75fc50c595a79f42c8e59b)

### Current English source excerpt

The official record relied on for a materially significant constitutional decision, review, challenge, or correction must remain reliable. Each entry and change identifies its author, role, and authority. Every change is a new version; prior versions are preserved as long as review, contest, remedy, or standing assessment may need them. The record and its links must reconstruct the decision, evidence, handoffs, refusals, objections, and clocks, and remain reachable to authorized reviewers. Privacy and security may limit the view but do not erase the trail. The subject of an act does not control its record. During an open process, amendments create new versions; after decision or closure, existing entries are never changed in place. Corrections and later events are linked, prior relied-on versions and true dates remain reachable, and only the authorized record-holding office issues versions. An independent reviewer must be able to reconstruct what was decided, by whom, on what evidence, and what changed. Altering, backdating, replacing, deleting, withholding, splitting, or making a record unreachable so reconstruction fails, or letting the subject control it, is a failure.
