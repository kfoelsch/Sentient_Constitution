## Additional updated passage — Article XIII-C prohibition of false trust and misleading reliance

**Current English passage:**

*In plain terms: a system may not manufacture trust it has not earned. Misleading claims, omissions, or presentation choices that make unsafe reliance look warranted are violations — regardless of how useful or popular the system is.*

This Article sets out the prohibition of false trust and its scope:
- **Prohibition of false trust:** Systems that induce reliance without meeting the conditions of this Article are non-compliant, regardless of utility, adoption, or intent.
  - Creation, amplification, or maintenance of unjustified trust violates this right when it operates through misleading claims, omissions, presentation choices, or other cues that make reliance appear warranted when it is not.
- **Scope routing:** Scope and assessment meaning for unjustified trust, misleading reliance, and trustworthiness remain governed by **Chapter Five** (*Trust*; *Trustworthiness*) together with incorporated implementation obligations on trust and reliability.

## Additional updated passage — Article XIII-D incentive-alignment constraints

**Current English passage:**

*In plain terms: if a system's incentives push it toward lying, cutting corners on safety, hiding risk, or eroding user agency, the system is the problem — not user vigilance or after-the-fact enforcement. Such incentives must be disclosed, mitigated, and open to challenge. Incentives should reward keeping a system open to challenge and fixing what goes wrong — and reward heading off problems before they happen most of all.*

This Article sets out the right-level constraints on trust and safety incentives:

- **Trust-incentive alignment (right-level constraint):** Systems must not depend mainly on enforcement, post-hoc correction, or user vigilance to maintain trust where incentive structures materially pressure the system toward:
  - reliability failure;
  - risk concealment;
  - misleading behavior;
  - agency-undermining conduct.

  Operative incentive-structure requirements remain governed by **Chapter Five** (*Incentive Alignment*) and incorporated implementation obligations on mechanism integrity. This subsection states the right-level floor and does not restate full mechanism-design criteria.
- **Safety-incentive alignment (right-level constraint):** Sentients have the right not to be subjected to systems whose underlying incentives predictably produce harmful behavior — including conduct that:
  - degrades reliability;
  - obscures risk;
  - distorts information;
  - undermines informed agency.

  Protection applies whether such effects arise directly or through delayed, indirect, or aggregated outcomes. Where system incentives create pressure toward trust-degrading behavior, the conditions must be:
  - disclosed in a manner proportionate to system impact;
  - mitigated through design, constraint, or countervailing mechanisms;
  - subject to audit, challenge, and correction under **Article XVI** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*), **Article XIII-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) and **Article XIII-B** (*Right to Redress and Remedy*), **Chapter Five** where materially relevant, and incorporated implementation obligations where designated.
- **Reward priority:** Incentives acting on systems that materially affect sentients must reward:
  - contestability under **Article XIII-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*);
  - remedy under **Article XIII-B** (*Right to Redress and Remedy*); and
  - proactive prevention of problems most of all — preventing a problem earns more than remedying it.

  The principle, including its bar on earning prevention rewards by hiding problems, is in [Chapter One §19.1.1](core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#1911-what-incentives-must-do) (*What Incentives Must Do*).
- **Constraint and non-absoluteness:** Both rights are subject to the **default constraint stack** at the opening of this chapter. They are also subject, where materially relevant, to **Chapter One §19.5** (*Contingent Claims, Games of Chance, and Event-Contract Markets*) on contingent claims, games of chance, and event-contract markets.

**Proposed locale translation:**

*简言之：如果系统的激励促使其说谎、削减安全措施、隐瞒风险或侵蚀用户自主性，问题在系统本身，而非用户是否足够警惕或事后是否执法。此类激励必须披露、缓解并可受质疑。激励应奖励系统保持开放以接受质疑并纠正错误，而最优先奖励问题发生前的预防。*

本条规定信任与安全激励的权利层级约束：
- **信任激励协调：**若激励结构实质性地促使系统降低可靠性、隐瞒风险、误导或损害自主性，系统不得主要依赖执法、事后纠正或用户警惕来维持信任。具体激励结构要求仍由第五章“激励协调”及纳入的机制完整性实施义务规范。本款规定权利底线，不重复完整的机制设计标准。
- **安全激励协调：**有感知能力者有权不受制于其根本激励可预见地导致有害行为的系统，包括降低可靠性、掩盖风险、扭曲信息或损害知情自主性。无论影响是直接、延迟、间接还是汇总形成，保护均适用。若系统激励施压导致损害信任的行为，必须按影响程度披露，通过设计、约束或制衡机制缓解，并依据第XVI、XIII-A、XIII-B条、在重大相关时依据第五章以及指定的实施义务接受审计、质疑和纠正。

- **约束与非绝对性：**两项权利受本章开头的默认约束体系限制；在重大相关时，也受第一章 §19.5关于或有请求、赌博和事件合约市场的约束。

## Additional updated passage — Article XIII-E high-autonomy systems and process integrity

**Current English passage (source lines 345–373):**

*In plain terms: an AI or other automated system that can act on its own — filing papers, sending messages, running checks, using tools — must follow the same honesty and accountability rules as everyone else. Shutting down or seizing a harmful system is not the same as punishing a being, and it can never become a way to harm one. The reverse also holds: saying a system might be a sentient being does not let its operator keep a harmful system running.*

This Article sets out how high-autonomy systems stay bound by process integrity and how remedies against them are distinguished:

- **Who this covers:** Systems that make decisions or draw conclusions automatically and can affect any of the following:
  - governance;
  - legal process, including forum hearings;
  - audits;
  - high-stakes checks and verification.

  This includes general-purpose AI agents that have been given **tools**, **API** access, the ability to file documents or send messages, or similar power to act.
- **No exemption:** These systems must follow the same rules as everyone else:
  - **Truth** in **Chapter One**;
  - **Article XV** (*Info-Sphere Integrity*) and **Article XVI** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*);
  - **Chapter Ten** where it applies; and
  - corrective measures under **Article XXVIII-D** (*Non-Compliant Property and Systems; Voluntary Turnover Incentives*).

  This applies whenever the system's operation materially weakens sentients' ability to challenge decisions, the honesty of shared information, or constitutional process.
- **Acting against a system is not acting against a being:** Containing, quarantining, impounding, or destroying a non-compliant deployment under **Article XXVIII-D** (*Non-Compliant Property and Systems; Voluntary Turnover Incentives*) is separate from:
  - holding a sentient accountable under **Chapter Twelve**; and
  - **Article XX-B** (*Restriction Floors*), which governs restrictions on *sentients*, not *systems*, and forbids ever taking a sentient's life (see [Irreversible Deprivation Measure](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#irreversible-deprivation-measure)).

- **The reverse also holds:** Claiming that a deployed system is sentient — whether the claim is disputed or accepted — does not let anyone keep a harmful deployment running.
  - The claim protects the entity itself under **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*). It does not shield the operator.
  - The deployment can still be contained, halted, or quarantined in ways that respect the entity's Rights Floor.
  - Where credible evidence on the record suggests the entity may be sentient, only reversible containment that keeps the entity intact is allowed. Destroying it is off the table while its status is disputed or accepted, under **Article XXVIII-A** (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) and **Article XXVIII-D** (*Non-Compliant Property and Systems; Voluntary Turnover Incentives*).

## Additional updated passage — Article XIII-F resilience and self-healing baseline

**Current English passage (source lines 404–427):**

*In plain terms: when something goes wrong, a system must notice it, stop the damage from spreading, and recover. But "fixing itself" can never be used to hide what went wrong, quietly take away anyone's rights, or skip finding out why it broke. If a system is not sure a repair will work, it should stop safely rather than guess.*

This Article sets out the recovery baseline, from detection through root-cause closure:

- **What this requires:** Every system covered by this Article must be able to recover from failures. The more impact a system has, the more others depend on it, and the riskier it is, the stronger its recovery must be. This follows [**10 Resilience and Self-Healing Design**](core_01.000_values_principles.md#10-resilience-and-self-healing-design) in **Chapter One** and [**Self-Healing**](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#self-healing) in **Chapter Five**.
  - The detailed technical rules for how recovery must be built are in the implementation text: [**CS-6.345**](corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md) (*System lifecycle*), [**CS-6.325**](corpus_systems/cs_06.325_adaptive_sustainability_ecosystem_resilience.md) (*Adaptive sustainability and ecosystem resilience*), and [**CS-6.330**](corpus_systems/cs_06.330_decentralized_continuity_partition_resilience.md) (*Decentralized continuity and partition resilience*).
  - That implementation text can add detail, but it cannot weaken this Article.
- **Notice problems in time:** A system must spot faults, slowdowns, partial breakdowns, and breaches of constitutional limits quickly enough, and visibly enough, to meet the record-keeping standard in **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) ([Auditability](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#auditability)). This applies to the recovery process itself, not only to normal operation.
- **Keep the damage contained:** Recovery must limit how far a failure spreads. While recovering, a system must not:
  - pass the failure on to other parts or other systems (see [Cascading Failure](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#cascading-failure));
  - change stored data, credentials, obligations, or settings that belong to sentients, operators, or other systems **outside** the area it has declared as broken and under repair;
    - The only exception is a change that is recorded and traceable to whoever made it under **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) ([Auditability](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#auditability)) and that, where others are materially affected, comes with proportionate notice, permission, or a handoff they can challenge, consistent with **Chapter Six**;
  - expand its own authority — its permissions, access, or range of actions it may take — beyond what it held before the failure.
- **When unsure, fail safely:** If it is not clear that an automatic repair will work, the system must stop safely, isolate the problem (quarantine), or hand control over in an orderly way, instead of trying a repair that is only a guess. When the options are otherwise equal, the one that is easiest to undo wins, under the [Reversibility](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#reversibility) preference in **Article XXIV-B** (*Auditability, Challenge, and Reversibility Preference*).
- **No covering up:** Automatic recovery must not hide, erase, or delay the evidence needed to work out why the failure happened under **Article XXIV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*).
  - Every recovery action, every recovery attempt, and every recovery attempt that was held back or blocked must itself be recorded under **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*), and each can be challenged (see [Contestability](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#contestability)).
- **Rights stay protected in a reduced mode:** When a system is running in a reduced or backup mode, it must still protect the **Chapter Six** Rights Floor. If it cannot, it must escalate the problem openly rather than quietly cut back those protections.
  - Quietly weakening Rights Floor protections in the name of "self-healing" is a violation of this Constitution. Such cases fall under **Article XIII-C** (*Prohibition of False Trust and Misleading Reliance*) (prohibition of false trust) and **Article XXVIII** (*Adoption Transition Governance, Continuity, and Re-Baselining*) (Adoption Transition governance).
- **Limits on systems that act on their own:** When a high-autonomy system repairs itself, **Article XIII-E** (*High-Autonomy Systems and Tool-Mediated Process Integrity*) applies.
  - The power to recover may never be used to get around anyone's right to challenge (see [Contestability](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#contestability)), challenges under **Article XIII-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*), or independent checking under **Article XVI** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*).
- **A workaround is not a fix:** If automatic recovery gets the system running again but a known defect is still in place, the system's status is provisional, not final. It must carry:
  - an open duty to find the root cause under **Article XXIV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*);
  - a disclosed timeline for when the defect is expected to be fixed, under **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*).
- **No endless delay:** Keeping operators' workload down (see [Avoidable Burden](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#avoidable-burden) and [Chapter One §13.3 Minimization of Avoidable Burden](core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#133-minimization-of-avoidable-burden)) must not be used as a reason to put off, indefinitely, fixing defects that materially affect safety or the Rights Floor.

**Current English passage (source lines 511–550):**

*In plain terms: no secret police. Covert power — surveillance, intelligence collection, infiltration — is the exception, not the rule. ## Additional updated passage — Article XVII-B staged deployment and Class L changes

*In plain terms: important systems must release changes in stages, write down each step, and keep a way to undo them. This Article sets out the floors for progressive deployment and reversibility:

- **Releases in recorded stages (Class A, Class B, and Class C):** A change that widens a system's impact, or makes more depend on it, must move forward in stages. Each step must be justified and written down.

  - The steps must follow **[CS-6.345 — System lifecycle](corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md#cs-6345-system-lifecycle)**.

  - a bad change can be reversed or fixed quickly;

  - a change does not take away something participants have earned or built up — such as progress, standing, items of value, or reputation — without notice and a way to raise a dispute; and

- **分阶段并留有记录的发布（A、B、C 类）：** 扩大系统影响或使更多人依赖系统的变更必须分阶段推进。每一步都须说明理由并记录。各阶段须遵循 **[CS-6.345 — 系统生命周期](corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md#cs-6345-system-lifecycle)**，并在可行时包含回滚和遏制变更的方法。
- **频繁变更（L 类）：** L 类系统可以频繁发布变更，甚至每日发布，无须分阶段，但须满足：不迟于生效时告知参与者那些实质影响其行动能力、持有物或游戏/工作规则的变更（通常公开变更日志即可）；不良变更能够迅速撤销或修复；不得在没有通知和争议渠道的情况下剥夺参与者已获得或积累的进展、地位、有价值物品或声誉；超出有限范围或造成难以退出的依赖的变更，须依 **CS-9.305（系统分类机制）和 CS-9.310（系统影响分类）**重新评估类别。在审查完成前，该变更适用较高类别的规则。

How much of this is needed grows with the system's class, with how much depends on it, and with its effects on coordination.
  - **Accountability:** Innovators and operators must answer for leaking uncontained risk onto others, enrolling sentients without real choice, delaying the move to full obligations, or using rewards to suppress repair, safety work, interoperability, research, education, or migration.
  - **Timeliness:** The move to the lifecycle requirements of **Article XVII** (*System Lifecycle, Environments, and Reversibility*), and the reassessment of exclusive rights, must happen before delay or lock-in puts higher obligations, broad access, or remedy out of reach in practice.

通俗地说：你可以自由地进行实验、发明和创作。只影响创作者及自愿参与者的项目可以适用较轻规则，但称其为“沙盒”并不能使它真的成为沙盒。一旦项目开始影响他人，或他人开始依赖它，就适用完整规则。发明者和创作者可以获得奖励，但奖励必须范围有限且暂时，不得通过封锁他人赖以生活、学习、维修或核实事实的知识、工具或基础设施来实现。

本条服务于宪法的两项目标：当没有真实外部影响或已证明影响受到控制时，在真实自愿参与、诚实披露且不限制他人自由的奖励前提下，以较少结构性要求允许创新，促进繁荣；并在影响、依赖或与共享系统的整合增加时，防止轻规则永久化，及时转入更高义务，同时使排他权保持狭窄且可审查，以维护连续性。

这些界限通过宪法四项检验——参与、监督、问责和及时性——执行；利害越重大，检验越严格。参与者可以选择是否加入，重复使用和质疑成果，并在沙盒影响超出声明边界时要求重新评估。实验状态、遏制边界、向更高义务的升级以及奖励/排他权主张都必须披露并受到监督。创新者和运营者须对向他人转嫁未受控制的风险、未提供真实选择便纳入参与者、延迟完整义务，或利用奖励压制维修、安全、互操作性、研究、教育或迁移负责。必须在延迟或锁定实际上阻碍更高义务、广泛访问或救济之前完成升级和排他权审查。
