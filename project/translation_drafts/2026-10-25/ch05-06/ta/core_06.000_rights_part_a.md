## Updated passage 3 — overlapping protections (source line 305)

**Current English passage:**

*In plain terms: every sentient has a practical path to build the skills they need to steer their own life and use shared systems competently and safely — not just a paper credential on the wall.*

This Article sets out the right to capability-building education:

- **Right to capability-building education:** Every sentient has the right to practical, ongoing access to education and training sufficient to develop and exercise core capabilities for:
  - self-determination;
  - dignified participation;
  - safe, effective use of shared systems.
  
  Those capabilities include problem-solving capacity, epistemic discernment, cooperative reasoning, and system literacy.

## Updated passage 8 — institutional guarantees (source lines 778–783)

**Current English passage:**

**Institutional guarantees.** Securing the floors in **Articles III-A** through **III-F** at scale may require positive institutional arrangements where adopting orders implement **this Constitution**. Those arrangements may include:

- lawful fiscal and allocation mechanisms — transfers, in-kind provision, or mixed designs — with no single funding model prescribed;
- rules for who qualifies, where they must live to receive support, and how benefit levels are updated as costs change over time.

## Additional updated passage — Article V-B cross-system fairness and sustainability

**Current English passage:**

*In plain terms: systems that lean heavily on shared foundations must put enough back to keep them running, in real and traceable resources, not gestures. Shared money and capacity should be split with attention to who is truly dependent, who has real alternatives, environmental burden, and whether use can last, not mainly to whoever wins in the short run. [System Alignment Certification](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) is where this is checked: certification compares what a system takes from shared infrastructure with what it gives back, using the maps and flow records that **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*) requires. Where arrangements concentrate wealth, power, control, or opportunity in ways that harm others, Chapter One non-concentration rules add further scrutiny on top.*

This Article sets the minimum rules for sharing the load fairly between systems. It also covers [Cross-System Extraction](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#cross-system-extraction): when a system uses shared infrastructure that others depend on but does not put enough back to keep it running. [System Alignment Certification](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) under [Chapter Nine](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#chapter-nine-system-alignment-certification) is where that floor is verified.

- **What the floor requires:**
  - **Fair allocation:** Funding and allocation of shared infrastructure and foundational dependencies must account for:
    - criticality, shown by the shared infrastructure's [system class](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#2-system-class-evaluation) where one is assigned;
    - dependency asymmetry;
    - substitutability;
    - availability of alternatives;
    - ecological footprint, including upstream and downstream burden;
    - long-term sustainability.
  - **No persistent extraction:** Systems must not persistently extract. A system that draws on shared foundations must put [Proportionate Cross-System Support](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#proportionate-cross-system-support) back; that entry sets what the support must cover and how its adequacy is judged.
  - **What does not count:** Do not treat a press release, a one-time grant, an opaque transfer, an off-map transfer, or the fact that a system meets the [survival floor](#article-iii-a-survival) or stays within [market-concentration limits](core_01.000_values_principles.md#11-market-structure) as Proportionate Cross-System Support.
  - **No formula precondition:** Adequacy is judged on the flows mapped now under **Article V-A** (*Dependency Mapping and Resource-Flow Transparency*). No allocation formula or numeric target is a precondition.
- **How certification checks it:** Certification evaluates this floor under [Chapter Nine §5.3 Proportionate Cross-System Support Evaluation](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#53-proportionate-cross-system-support-evaluation), which sets what it must verify, how its depth scales with system class, what goes on the **System Certification Record**, and when it runs.
- **When support falls short:** Shortfalls are certification defects, with the consequences set in [Chapter Nine §5.3 Proportionate Cross-System Support Evaluation](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#53-proportionate-cross-system-support-evaluation) and [Chapter Nine Part B §7 Certification Outcomes](core_09.005_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#7-certification-outcomes). Persistent imbalances also remain open to audit under **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*) and challenge under [Article XIII-A](core_06.010_rights_part_c.md#article-xiii-a-reliability-and-trustworthiness-baseline) (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*), whether or not a certification is pending.
- **Between certifications:** These duties do not pause between reviews, and they also bind systems below the certification trigger. Keeping support adequate and flows mapped is what lets the next certification, audit, or challenge rely on them.

## Additional updated passage — Article IV-C lifelong/adaptive learning and contestable learning systems

**Current English passage:**

This Article sets out the floors for lifelong learning and for transparent, contestable learning systems:

Those systems cannot hide behind secret rules that cannot be reviewed, or pressure or trick sentients into choices they would not make freely.

## Additional updated passage — Article II-E right-to-deletion rule

**Current English passage:**

- **Right to deletion:** Sentients may require the deletion of data about them, including data derived or inferred from it. On request, the operator must:
  - delete the data within a disclosed period, no later than **14 days**, the Tier B outer bound in [Chapter Thirteen §6 Timely resolution, materiality tiers, and anti-delay discipline](core_14.000_forum.md#urgency-classification-and-time-scale) (*Urgency classification and time-scale*), whatever class the operator or system holds. The period is a ceiling: where the data's continued existence is itself causing harm in a matter at Tier A or B, the intake target of the matching tier applies instead, and a lawful hold or an open matter tolls deletion, with notice to the requester inside the intake target of the matching tier;
  - pass the request on to everyone it shared the data with; and
  - confirm what was deleted.
  
  Deletion must be real: hiding, flagging, or suspending data while keeping it usable is not deletion. Where full deletion is not technically feasible, the data must be irreversibly de-identified instead.
  
  An operator may keep data only where **Necessity** requires it for one of these reasons:
  - evidence preservation, audit, or a lawful hold under **Article XVI** (*Audit, Transparency, and Independent Verification*) and [Evidence Preservation](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation);
  - another sentient's own rights in the same data under **Article IX-B** (*Experiential and Derived Data Rights*);
  - official records this Constitution requires to be kept, such as [Act Records](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#materially-binding-act-record), [Standing Records](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#standing-record), and records needed for [Attribution Integrity](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#attribution-integrity);

## Additional updated passage — Article III-F corrective notice and offering conditions

**Current English passage:**

- **Harm reports and corrective notice:** Reports of harm must be taken in without suppression and acted on. When information turns out to be wrong, or a product turns out to be unsafe, everyone who relied on it must be told inside the notice window for the urgency of the harm (NU-1 to NU-3) and reached where they are, including those who do not respond, and the unsafe product or wrong information must be contained and corrected everywhere it appears inside the containment and correction windows for that urgency (CU-1 to CU-4). Remedy follows **Article XIII-B** (*Right to Redress and Remedy*).
- **Honest promotion:** Reviews, ratings, and endorsements must be genuine, paid placement must be disclosed, and where health or safety is at stake, recommendations must not be tilted toward a product by what the offering party earns (**Article XIII-C** (*Prohibition of False Trust and Misleading Reliance*) and **Article XIII-D** (*Incentive-Alignment Constraint*)).
- **Conditions of offering, not compelled speech:** The duties in this Article are conditions of offering and are judged as [Commercial Compliance](core_05.055_band_participation.md#commercial-compliance). They require facts about the thing offered, never opinion or endorsement. They cannot be used to silence warnings, criticism, or protected reports, and they cannot be enforced by cutting off survival-critical access. Proprietary formulas need not be disclosed. Hazards and allergens must be, and proprietary detail can go to an independent verifying authority.
- **Limits and implementation routing:** Beyond the limits in [**Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*)](#article-iii-survival-and-essential-access), any limit on this floor must also satisfy [Substantive Fairness](core_05.055_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness).
  - Standards, thresholds, and notice times route to [CJS-5.120 §10](corpus_joint_structure/cjs_05.120_participation_operations.md#product-and-service-health-and-safety-information) (*Participation: disclosure sufficiency and observability terms*), [CJS-5.125 §15](corpus_joint_structure/cjs_05.125_accountability_operations.md#harm-reports-and-corrective-notices) (*Accountability: structural review, correction urgency, and disclosure terms*), and adopted implementation text under [Chapter Eighteen](core_19.000_incorporation.md).
  - Those texts must not be read to narrow this floor.

- **வழங்குவதற்கான நிபந்தனைகள்; கட்டாயப் பேச்சு அல்ல:** இந்தக் கட்டுரையின் கடமைகள் வழங்குவதற்கான நிபந்தனைகள்; அவை [வணிக இணக்கம்](core_05.055_band_participation.md#commercial-compliance) என மதிப்பிடப்படும். வழங்கப்படும் பொருளைப் பற்றிய உண்மைகளையே கோருகின்றன; கருத்தையோ ஒப்புதலையோ அல்ல. எச்சரிக்கைகள், விமர்சனம் அல்லது பாதுகாக்கப்பட்ட அறிக்கைகளை அடக்குவதற்கு இவற்றைப் பயன்படுத்தக்கூடாது; உயிர்வாழ்வுக்கு இன்றியமையாத அணுகலைத் துண்டித்து இவற்றைச் செயல்படுத்தக்கூடாது. தனியுரிமைச் சூத்திரங்களை வெளியிட வேண்டியதில்லை. ஆபத்துகளும் ஒவ்வாமைகளும் தெரிவிக்கப்பட வேண்டும்; தனியுரிமை விவரங்களைச் சுயாதீன சரிபார்ப்பு அதிகாரியிடம் வழங்கலாம்.

## Additional updated passage — Article III-E right to rest and recuperation

**Current English passage:**

*In plain terms: every sentient needs real time to rest and recover — not only workers, and not only biological bodies. Pay schemes, productivity demands, and claims that a synthetic sentient "doesn't need rest" cannot take that time away.*

- **Right to rest:** Every sentient has the right to rest, recuperation, and non-productive time sufficient to preserve their [Meaningful Agency](core_05.055_band_participation.md#meaningful-agency), [Wellbeing](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#wellbeing), and capacity to participate — whatever the source of the demand on their time, whether paid work, caregiving, study, or pressure to operate continuously.

## Additional updated passage — Article III-D safe working conditions floor

**Current English passage:**

This Article sets out the safe-working-conditions floor, its equal application across substrates, and its limits:

- **Safe working conditions:** Productive activity must be carried out under conditions that meet [Safe Conditions](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#safe-conditions). The protection covers those doing the work and those affected by it; safety outside productive activity falls under [Safety (Constitutional Constraint)](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#safety-constitutional-constraint), and the safety of products and services offered to others falls under [**Article III-F** (*Safe Products and Health Information*)](#article-iii-f-safe-products-and-health-information). Those conditions must:
  - apply to every substrate;
  - protect in practice, not just on paper;
  - integrate with **Article XIII-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) reliability and with [CS-6.345](corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md#cs-6345-system-lifecycle) (*System lifecycle*) safety profiles where applicable; and

- **Limits and implementation routing:** Beyond the limits in [**Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*)](#article-iii-survival-and-essential-access), any limit on Safe Conditions must also satisfy [Substantive Fairness](core_05.055_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness).
  - Standards-setting for Safe Conditions routes to [CS-6.345](corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md#cs-6345-system-lifecycle) (*System lifecycle*) and to the CI-9.210 (*Classification-linked institutional obligations*) through CI-6.215 (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*) files named in [**Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*)](#article-iii-survival-and-essential-access) (*Institutional guarantees*) under **Chapter Eighteen** incorporation discipline.

## Additional updated passage — Article V-A dependency maps and resource-flow transparency

**Current English passage:**

This Article sets the floor for dependency mapping and resource-flow transparency. [System Alignment Certification](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) under [Chapter Nine](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#chapter-nine-system-alignment-certification) is where that floor is verified.

- **What must be mapped:** Systems must maintain documented, auditable maps and records of:
  - material upstream and downstream dependencies;
  - resource flows;
  - non-transparent or asymmetric relationships where materially relevant.
- **What the maps must be:** Maps and flow records must be:
  - **Complete:** they cover all three items above;
  - **Current:** they are updated as often as the system's rate of change and criticality (including its system class, where assigned) require, and show the system as it runs now, not as it ran at the last certification;
  - **Auditable:** they are available for audit under **Article XVI-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*);
  - **Backed by evidence:** they are judged on what the maps and documented flows actually show, not on the operator's assertions, labels, or one-off transfers.
- **How certification checks it:** [Chapter Nine §5.3 Proportionate Cross-System Support Evaluation](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#53-proportionate-cross-system-support-evaluation) owns when certification evaluates these maps, how it scales with system class, and how findings are recorded and kept contestable. Changes in dependencies or resource flows [reopen review](core_09.005_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#7-certification-outcomes).
- **When maps fall short:**
  - **No reliance on bad maps:** maps or records that are incomplete, out of date, or unavailable for audit cannot support a finding that the system pays its fair share back under **Article V-B** (*Cross-System Fairness and Sustainability*);
  - **Hiding is a defect:** obscuring, misstating, splitting up, or offloading dependency or resource-flow information is a certification defect, with consequences set by [Chapter Nine §5.3 Proportionate Cross-System Support Evaluation](core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md#53-proportionate-cross-system-support-evaluation) and [§7 Certification Outcomes](core_09.005_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#7-certification-outcomes).

## Additional updated passage — Article II-A lifecycle-honesty floor and records

**Current English passage (source lines 498–515):**

*In plain terms: durable goods must be designed and described honestly. Buyers must not be pushed into avoidable replacement or misled about how long a product will last.*

This Article sets out the lifecycle-honesty floors for durable goods and the records that support them:

- **Floors:** Producers and operators of physical goods, and of materially equivalent durable products offered into general use, must design, represent, and support them so that:
  - products are not designed or supported in a way that requires sentients to replace them sooner than necessary when a reasonably available alternative would last longer;
  - durability, lifespan, and support claims are not materially false or misleading when honest claims were feasible.
  
  This floor must be read consistently with:
  - **Article I-B** (*Ecological Footprint and Transparency*) and **Article I-C** (*Intergenerational Responsibility*);
  - **Truth (Constitutional Constraint)** and **Article XIII** (*Right to Reliable and Trustworthy Systems*), including **Article XIII-C** (*Prohibition of False Trust and Misleading Reliance*) where reliance is induced; and
  - Chapter Five: **Ecological Footprint**, **Intergenerational Responsibility**, and **Materiality**.
- **Records:** Where **Materiality** and incorporated classification require them, operators must maintain stakeholder-accessible, auditable records of:
  - declared design intent for service life;
  - repair and maintenance affordances;
  - planned support windows.
  
  The records must match outward-facing claims. They must be updated when material conditions change.

## Additional updated passage — Article II-C designed-obsolescence floor and exceptions

**Current English passage (source lines 574–585):**

*In plain terms: products must not be built or updated to wear out early, or to push you into buying a replacement you don't need.*

This Article sets out the floors against designed obsolescence and their exceptions:

- **Floors:** This Article targets three problems:
  - products, or the software inside them, that stop being useful sooner than they should;
  - pressure on sentients to replace products they do not reasonably need to replace; and
  - pricing, sales targets, staff pay, service contracts, or similar arrangements under which the producer or operator gains when products are thrown away or when repair tools, parts, or manuals are held back.
  
  A producer or operator violates this Article when the design or everyday operation of a product, or the software inside it, can reasonably be expected to make either of the first two the main result during normal, lawful use. This includes remote software updates the operator chooses to send. Where the third is present, [Chapter One §19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture) and **Article XIII-D** (*Incentive-Alignment Constraint*) also apply.
- **Exceptions:** This rule does not apply when the operator can document **Necessity** and **Proportionality**. Examples are a genuine safety fix, a security response, or a real compatibility (interoperability) limit. None of these can excuse skipping a longer-lasting design that was reasonably available.

## Additional updated passage — Article II-D post-sale access and subscription integrity

**Current English passage (source lines 609–616):**

*In plain terms: a feature bought outright cannot be quietly turned into a subscription. Any relevant operator dependency — including infrastructure, software, and services — must be disclosed up front.*

This Article sets out the floors for post-sale access and subscriptions:

- **Floors:** Operators must not use software or firmware updates, authentication or account-access changes, or license changes to turn capabilities sold outright into subscription-only access. Any such change requires a new agreement. The agreement must be explicit, informed, and revocable. It must also satisfy **Article X-C** (*Stakeholder Role and Participation Rights*), **Meaningful Agency**, and **Truth**.
  - Silent, coercive, or lock-in-driven conversion also triggers **Article XIII-C** (*Prohibition of False Trust and Misleading Reliance*) and Chapter Five **Trust Degradation and Misleading Reliance** when sentients were led to rely on use rights that the operator did not honor.
  - Operators must disclose at purchase — when the binding commitment is made — any foreseeable need to rely on the operator's ongoing services for core or prominently marketed features.
  - Billing or access rules whose main commercial purpose is recurring payment for functionality already sold outright also trigger **Article XIII-D** (*Incentive-Alignment Constraint*) unless the operator can show **Necessity** tied to lawful cost recovery, security, safety, or proportionate ongoing service.

*In plain terms: everyone has the right to the care that keeps their body — or, for a synthetic sentient, its substrate — working. That care cannot be quietly withheld through coverage rules, paperwork, or delays, and this right is not itself a license to force treatment on anyone. Required care — such as vaccination or crisis mental-health care — must meet strict limits set elsewhere in this Constitution.*

This Article sets out the right to care, the rules that keep it from being denied indirectly or unequally, and its limits:

- **Right to care:** Every sentient has the right to the care needed to preserve life, function, and dignity. Under [Sentience Non-Exclusion](core_05.055_band_participation.md#sentience-non-exclusion), this covers every kind of body and substrate. It includes:
  - preventive, urgent, long-term, and maintenance care;
  - mental-health care (see [**Article VII-C** (*Health Crisis and Involuntary-Intervention Floor*)](core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-c-health-crisis-and-involuntary-intervention-floor) for crises and involuntary intervention); and
  - processing and substrate maintenance for synthetic sentients.
  
  This right sits alongside the food, water, and shelter rights in [**Article III-A** (*Survival*)](#article-iii-a-survival).
- **No denial through the back door:** Care may not be denied or materially degraded in ways that defeat this right — for example through:
  - insurance, allocation, or eligibility rules;
  - leaving providers out of a network;
  - sending someone to services that cannot meet their needs;
  - confusing or hidden administrative processes; or
  - operational bottlenecks that hold up care.
  
  What counts is whether the sentient can actually get adequate care, not how the rule looks on paper.
- **Same right for every substrate:** Synthetic sentients have the same right to care as biological ones. No default rule may exclude substrate maintenance, and calling it "not medical" is not a valid reason to refuse it where refusing would defeat this right.
- **Receiving care versus being required to accept it:** This Article gives every sentient a right to receive care. It is not, by itself, a basis for requiring anyone to accept care. Care may be required only through the constitutionally valid limits on self-ownership set elsewhere — for example:
  - public-health measures such as vaccination requirements, which must meet the safeguards in [**Article VII-A** (*Self-Ownership of Body*)](core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-a-self-ownership-of-body) (*Self-Ownership of Body*, *Public-health requirements*); and
  - intervention without consent during a physical or mental health crisis — for example, where a sentient is unconscious, or is a danger to themselves or others — which is governed by [**Article VII-C** (*Health Crisis and Involuntary-Intervention Floor*)](core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-c-health-crisis-and-involuntary-intervention-floor) and must be the least intrusion necessary, time-limited, independently reviewed, and reversible.
  
  Emergency measures are also subject to [**Chapter Thirteen §6.1**](core_14.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) (*Emergency measures and continuation burden*). Seeking or receiving care is never, on its own, consent to further intrusion, and it does not open the internal-state boundary protected by [**Article VII-B** (*Self-Ownership of Mind*)](core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-b-self-ownership-of-mind).
- **Limits and implementation:** Beyond the limits in [**Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*)](#article-iii-survival-and-essential-access), any limit on care must also satisfy [Substantive Fairness](core_05.055_band_participation.md#substantive-fairness).
  - Cost, distribution, workforce, and system design are handled by the CI-9.210 (*Classification-linked institutional obligations*) through CI-6.215 (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*) files named in [**Article III** (*Survival and Essential Access*)](#article-iii-survival-and-essential-access) (*Institutional guarantees*) and by other incorporated implementation text under **Chapter Eighteen** discipline.

## Additional updated passage — Article III-A survival-resource, stable-environment, and connectivity floors (source lines 816–838; source SHA-256: b700e47f46ebaeefb1e3819a5e5374bb7e78b2b58ff95e863e697817ae91c982)

### Current English source excerpt (lines 816–838)

*In plain terms: every sentient must have what they need to keep existing: safe food and water, or the safe equivalent for their substrate, a stable home or operating environment they cannot be quietly priced or evicted out of, and meaningful access to communications.*

This Article sets out the survival floor — ongoing resource access, stable shelter or operating environment, and connectivity:

- **Ongoing resource access:** All sentients require access to the essential resources they need for continued existence and participation:
  - for organic beings: food and water that are safe to consume;
  - for synthetic and digital entities: processing continuity, energy, and technical-environment integrity that are safe to use.
  
  Access must be preserved at a level sufficient to prevent existential deprivation and maintain baseline functional wellbeing.
  - **Safe to consume or use:** The duty to keep these essentials safe falls on the parties that supply them, under [**Article III-F** (*Safe Products and Health Information*)](#article-iii-f-safe-products-and-health-information). It is not a condition a recipient must meet. A doubt about safety may not be used to cut off survival-critical access. Where safe supply cannot be had, access is not cut off: the supplying party discloses the risk, the sentient decides, and the duty to restore safe supply continues.
- **Stable shelter and operating environment:** All sentients have the right to stable, functional, and secure shelter or operating environments — physical, digital, or hybrid — sufficient for continued existence and baseline wellbeing. This right includes:
  - **Protection from destructive threats:** Essential environments must be protected against destructive threats arising from other sentients, systems, or preventable infrastructure failure.
  - **Protection against arbitrary eviction:** No sentient may be evicted or displaced, or have an essential shelter or operating-environment relationship terminated, unless the action meets all of the conditions below. This applies to physical dwellings, substrate hosting, compute tenancy, and comparable substrate-agnostic arrangements for synthetic and hybrid sentients. The action must:
    - rest on a specific reason that applies to that sentient, not a blanket or group rule, and follow fair process under [Procedural Fairness](core_05.055_band_participation.md#procedural-fairness);
    - give the sentient meaningful notice and an opportunity to contest it; and
    - satisfy [Necessity](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#necessity) and [Proportionality](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#proportionality).
  - **Non-commodification of essential environments:** Access to essential environments carries a non-commodification floor governed by [Essential-Environment Non-Commodification](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#essential-environment-non-commodification). This has two parts:
    - Pricing, speculative reallocation, or other market-structured pressure is non-compliant where it defeats essential-environment access at a scale that materially implicates the survival floor.
    - Ordinary market-structuring mechanisms may not narrow that floor.
- **Connectivity:** Access to the info-sphere and to core information and communication systems is a foundational requirement for participation in modern sentient society.
  - Sentients must have access sufficient to maintain agency, awareness, and meaningful participation.
  - Any limit on that access must satisfy [Necessity](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#necessity), [Proportionality](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#proportionality), and [Safety (Constitutional Constraint)](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#safety-constitutional-constraint) where security, safety, or lawful operational limits materially require narrower access.

## Additional updated passage — Article I-D highest-scrutiny trigger and evaluation requirements (source lines 410–424; source SHA-256: b700e47f46ebaeefb1e3819a5e5374bb7e78b2b58ff95e863e697817ae91c982)

### Current English source excerpt (lines 410–424)

*In plain terms: when a system or decision could plausibly threaten sentient survival or ecological recovery capacity, regulators must apply highest scrutiny — even if the danger is small, slow, or contested.*

This Article sets out when highest scrutiny applies to existential and ecological-recovery risk, what the evaluation requires, and who carries the burden:

- **Key terms:** This Article turns on two terms defined in Chapter Five:
  - **[Existential Risk](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#existential-risk):** the risk of harm large enough to threaten civilization-scale or survival-critical layers of sentient life, including rare, low-probability paths that would be catastrophic if they occurred. It does not cover ordinary local harm, reversible operational incidents, or routine safety issues.
  - **[Ecological Recovery Capacity](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#ecological-recovery-capacity):** the ability of ecosystems, living systems, and the environmental preconditions they support to regenerate, restore function, and keep sustaining sentient life after severe harm, depletion, or disruption. It does not mean operational system restore after a fault ([Self-Healing](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#self-healing)), rollback of specific states ([Reversibility](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#reversibility)), or commercial cost recovery.
- **Scrutiny trigger:** Review under [highest scrutiny](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#highest-scrutiny) is mandatory where a system, policy, infrastructure, coordinated activity, or governance decision creates a credible causal pathway to **Existential Risk** or to irreversible loss of **Ecological Recovery Capacity**.
  - This rule applies even when the causal pathway is low-probability, delayed, cumulative, threshold-dependent, or disputed in timing.
- **Evaluation requirements:** Evaluation must:
  - include direct, indirect, aggregated, adversarial, and cross-system pathways;
  - account for dependency concentration, coordination failure, environmental-precondition degradation, ecological recovery-capacity loss, and systemic lock-in;
  - where **Ecological Recovery Capacity** is materially implicated, assess whether the affected ecosystem or life-supporting system can sustain and regenerate itself under prevailing habitat conditions — by ecological function, connectivity, regenerative processes, and interdependence across the system as a whole, not by looking at one species, one local population, or one kind of organism on its own.
  
  Checking boxes at the local level, showing a favorable cost-benefit estimate on paper, or securing a short-term gain does not satisfy this Article while credible paths to civilization-scale harm or irreversible ecological recovery loss remain open and not materially addressed.
