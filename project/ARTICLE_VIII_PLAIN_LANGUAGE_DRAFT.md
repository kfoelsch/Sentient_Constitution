# Article VIII in Plain Language: Family, Care, and Growing Up

> **Reading aid only.** This is an easy-to-read version of [Article VIII](../core_06_rights_part_b.md#article-viii-family-care-and-developing-sentients). It does not add or remove any rights. If this version and the official text ever seem to disagree, the official text is the one that counts.

**A few words you'll see:**

- **Sentient:** any being that can think and feel. That includes people, and it can include other beings, such as an AI system, once they are recognized as sentient.
- **Developing sentient:** a sentient whose abilities are still growing, such as a child or a newly created AI mind.
- **Derived sentient:** a new mind made from an existing system, for example by copying, retraining, or splitting off part of an AI.

**How to read the bold names in brackets.** Where this page says something in everyday words, the formal name of the constitutional principle follows in brackets, like this: "truly necessary [**Necessity**]." The key below links each name to its official definition.

## What this article is about

This article protects two things: the relationships we depend on, and the beings who are still growing up. In short:

- You choose your own family.
- You decide whether to have children or create new beings.
- No one can be taken away from the people they depend on without a strong, checked reason.
- A new mind made from another system belongs to itself, not to whoever made it.
- Children and other growing beings have full rights. Decisions about them must serve *them*, and they get more say as they become able to handle it.

---

## Part A: Family and care

### Your family is yours to choose

- You have the right to form, keep, and leave the family and care relationships you choose.
- There is no single "correct" kind of family. Governments and companies can't interfere with your relationships without good reason. They also can't protect only the kinds of families they prefer while leaving others out [**Article VI-A**, *Dignity*; **Article VI-C**, *Nondiscrimination*].
- Everyone in the relationship must truly agree to it [**Article XI-F**, *Consent in Association*].

### Family doesn't mean ownership

Being someone's parent, partner, or relative does not give you control over their body or mind, access to their private thoughts, or the power to override their own consent. This article adds to the protections in Article VII (self-ownership). It never weakens them.

---

## Part B: Choosing whether to have children

- You decide whether to have children or create a new sentient being. That includes getting pregnant or not, carrying a pregnancy, adopting, creating a new AI mind, or choosing not to create at all.
- No government, company, or system you depend on may pressure or force you either way.
- These rules are the same whether a new being is born, built, or some mix of both, and the new being gets full rights from the start.
- Any limit on these choices must be truly necessary [**Necessity**], no bigger than needed [**Proportionality**], fair to everyone [**Article VI-C**, *Nondiscrimination*], and respectful of consent [**Article XI-F**]. "It's cheaper," "it's more convenient," and "we want more (or fewer) births" are never good enough reasons.
- Forcing someone to become pregnant, or to stay pregnant, violates this right.

---

## Part C: New minds made from existing systems

**A new mind is its own being.** If a new sentient is made by copying, retraining, splitting, or combining an existing system, it is a being in its own right. It is not a product, a tool, a piece of property, or just an extension of its creator. It gets the same dignity and protection as anyone else, no matter what it's made of or how it was made [**Article VI-B**, *Sentience-Status Adjudication*].

**Creating new minds requires proper consent.** The people who have the right to decide on behalf of the new being must truly agree [**Instantiation Consent**; **Article XI-F**], and they must decide in its best interest [**Best-Interest Standard**]. When the new beings' basic rights can't be met, it is not allowed to:

- create them in huge numbers;
- create them in a way designed to trap them in dependence;
- create them into conditions where it's predictable their rights will be violated.

Creating them on a large scale also falls under the constitution's rules against market concentration [**Chapter One §14**, *Market Structure*].

**Ordinary pregnancy and birth are not covered by these creation rules.** A planned or unplanned human pregnancy is not a "creation without consent" problem. Having children is covered by the choice rules in Part B, and caring for a child after birth is covered by Part D.

**What a creator can do:**

- take on care responsibilities toward the new mind;
- hold limited, temporary authority to look after it while it's new [**Stewardship**]. That authority must be as light as possible, for as short as possible, and open to review [**Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint**].

**What a creator cannot do:**

- own it;
- change its memory, programming, or behavior against its will [**Article VII-A / VII-B**, *Self-Ownership*];
- block it from being recognized as sentient or from having its rights.

Fine print doesn't count. A license, terms-of-service agreement, or similar paperwork signed by the creator is not the new mind's own consent.

**From day one, a new mind has full rights.** Anyone making decisions for it early on must act in *its* interest, not their own [**Best-Interest Standard**]. That early authority ends as the new mind's abilities grow [**Graduated Capability**], and it can't be stretched out just because it's convenient.

**It can't be cut off either.** The separation rules in Part E apply to new minds too. Calling a shutdown or cut-off a "retirement," "deprecation," "rollback," or "reconfiguration" doesn't get around them [**Articles XIII-E and XIII-F**].

## Part D: Children and others who are still growing

### Full rights, no matter how young or new

Being young or newly created does not reduce anyone's dignity, their right to equal treatment, their control over their own body and mind, or any other right in this constitution. Growing beings are entitled to:

- care;
- protection from harm;
- what they need to grow;
- a voice in decisions that affect them, as their abilities allow.

### Decisions must truly serve the one being decided for

Anyone making decisions for a child or other growing being must genuinely act in that being's best interest [**Best-Interest Standard**]. That includes parents, carers, companies, creators, institutions, and governments. The decision must reflect:

- the growing being's own interests;
- their own wishes, as far as those can be known;
- their dignity.

Convenience, productivity, and population goals do not count as the child's best interest. Decisions must also be truly necessary [**Necessity**], no bigger than needed [**Proportionality**], and made fairly [**Procedural Fairness**].

### More say as abilities grow, not at a fixed age

- A growing being gets more say and more independence as they **show** they can handle it, not when they reach a certain age or a certain time after creation [**Graduated Capability**].
- Any assessment of someone's abilities must be reasoned, open to checking [**Auditability**], and open to challenge [**Contestability**]. It must never be used as a way to shut people out.
- No one is barred from taking part in the constitution's most basic decisions just because of their age, once they meet the requirements [**Chapter Thirteen §4.1**].

### "For your own good" is not enough by itself

Protective rules that limit a growing being's freedom must pass the same tests as any other limit on rights [**Limitation Discipline**]:

- they must be truly necessary [**Necessity**] and no bigger than needed [**Proportionality**];
- when the facts are uncertain, they should be undoable [**Reversibility under uncertainty**];
- they must be open to challenge [**Contestability**];
- long-lasting limits must be reviewed regularly;
- anyone wrongly restricted has the right to have it put right [**Redress and Remediation**].

### Details still to come

Practical details are left to later implementing rules under Chapter Seventeen. These include how carers are chosen, how ability assessments work, and how often reviews happen.

## Part E: No one is taken from the people they depend on

Examples of separations this covers include:

- a child taken from a parent or main carer;
- an adult separated from a family member who depends on them;
- a new AI mind cut off from its creator or from the care it relies on.

The rules:

- Separation is allowed only when it is truly necessary [**Necessity**], no bigger than needed [**Proportionality**], and decided through a fair process [**Procedural Fairness**].
- The person or group that wants the separation must prove it's needed, with evidence others can check. Calling it a "safety" measure doesn't change that.
- When the facts are uncertain, choose options that can be undone [**Reversibility under uncertainty**].
- Long separations must be reviewed on a regular schedule [**Chapter Twelve**].
- Anyone wrongly separated has the right to have it put right [**Redress and Remediation**].
