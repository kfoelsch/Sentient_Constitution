# Draft: Influence-contact (lobbying) boundaries (proposal, not adopted text)

**Status:** 2026-10-10. Written into the binding files (uncommitted, awaiting custodian review). The lobbying and influence-contact rules now live in a new sub-article, **Article XII-E: Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries** (`core_06_rights_part_b.md`, after XII-D). XII-E holds the influence-contact boundaries (Part 1 below), the post-service and prospective-role conflicts with the five-year default cooling-off, and the automated, generated, and bulk approach rules (Part 7). XII-D keeps a one-bullet pointer. Other files changed: `core_13_governance.md` (sortition floor; Selection-funding boundaries in §1.2), `core_05_band_accountability.md` (Interested Party), `corpus_joint_structure/cjs_03o_oversight_operations.md` (CJS-3.6 *Influence-contact and lobbying boundaries* and *Selection-funding integrity controls*), `corpus_institutions/ci_04_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md` (CI-4.2), and `corpus_institutions/ci_05_conflict_integrity_anti_capture_anti_corruption.md` (CI-5.3). Wording in the binding files differs slightly from the proposals below (shortened for the lexical guardrails, and Part 1 moved from XII-D to XII-E). `make regression` passes (81 of 81). Translations under `translations/` have not been updated.

**Problem.** The corpus names lobbying only inside the sortition rules: **Chapter Thirteen** ("Sortition integrity and anti-capture use") requires "anti-lobbying and anti-intimidation safeguards", and **CJS-3.6** (*Sortition and civic-lottery integrity controls*) requires "contact and lobbying boundaries". Neither says what a boundary is. For every other body that decides for others (forums, Stakeholder System Participation bodies, appointing authorities, adopting institutions), **Article XII-D** (*Non-Capture Safeguards*) covers capture, conflicts, recusal, and hidden control, but nothing requires contact by interested parties to be recorded, disclosed, or kept off the decision channel. Lobbying is caught only if it becomes a conflict, hidden control, or corruption.

**What this draft does not do.** It does not ban advocacy. Petition, public comment, and ordinary participation are protected by the participation rights (**Article X**, **Article XII-A**). The line drawn here is between *open, recorded* influence and *private, unrecorded* influence on the people who decide.

**Placeholder terms.** "Influence contact" and "interested party" are descriptive placeholders. They need checking against the Chapter Five definition protocol (**Chapters Two and Three**) before use.

## Part 1. Proposed text for Article XII-D (now Article XII-E)

Insert as a new bullet after **Ongoing disclosure and recusal**, same article, same house style.

*In plain terms: people with a stake in a decision may speak up, but not in private to the people deciding it. Contact about a live matter is recorded, the material parts are put in the open record, and gifts or offers are refused and reported.*

- **Influence-contact boundaries:** Covered bodies, their **appointing authorities**, and **adopting institutions** must publish rules for **influence contact**: any attempt by an **interested party** to shape a pending or foreseeable matter through a member, panelist, staff member, facilitator, or briefing source, outside the matter's open record. The rules scale with **material stake** and must at least:
  - **record** each material influence contact (who, when, channel, matter, and substance in summary);
  - **disclose** material contacts in the matter record in time for other participants to respond;
  - keep evidence and argument on the open record: a private contact may not supply evidence or argument that the decision then relies on;
  - treat any offer of gift, benefit, future role, or threat as a **conflict to be disclosed, refused, and routed** under **CI-5**, not as ordinary contact; and
  - protect open advocacy, petition, and public comment, which are not influence contact when made on the record.
- **Not a gag:** These rules must not be used to limit what affected sentients may say about their own stake, or to deny them access to a body that must hear them.
- **Emergency preservation:** The narrow ex parte preservation step in **CF-8** is unaffected. It stays limited to evidence preservation and stays contestable.

## Part 2. Companion text for the implementation corpus

To sit in `corpus_joint_structure/cjs_03o_oversight_operations.md` as **CJS-3.6**, directly after *Shared anti-capture control stack*. It uses the same four-part shape as the neighboring entries.

**Influence-contact and lobbying boundaries**

*In plain terms: where an interested party could shape a decision through private contact with the people who make or support it, the owner text must say what contact is allowed, record it, put the material parts in the open record, and route offers or pressure as integrity triggers.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Where an interested party could materially shape a governing, adjudicative, interpretive, oversight, procurement, audit, agenda-setting, or appointment decision through contact with a member, panelist, staff member, facilitator, briefing source, or appointing authority, the owner text must maintain published contact rules, a contact record, a disclosure step, offer-and-pressure routing, and a lawful-advocacy carve-out.
  - **Out of scope:** open advocacy, petition, and public comment made on the matter record; ordinary administrative contact with no material stake.
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** published contact rules by body and matter type; the influence-contact record; time between contact and disclosure in the matter record; routed offers and pressure incidents; and the lawful-advocacy channels kept open.
  - **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify that each measure exists, that a sample of decisions can be reconstructed against the contact record, and that no decision relied on evidence or argument that arrived only through private contact.
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Treat lobbying boundaries as satisfied when the contact record is kept by the person contacted without an independence safeguard, when material contacts surface only after the decision, when offers of benefit are handled as ordinary correspondence, or when the rules are used to keep affected sentients out of the process.

Add **influence-contact** as a seventh trigger to the **CI-5.3** list only if the custodian wants it routed separately. Otherwise route it under **conflict concealment** and **reporting suppression**.

## Part 4. Decisions taken, and what stays open

Taken (custodian can reverse any of these):

1. **Scope tiers.** All covered bodies, scaled to material stake, with no numbers. This matches the other floors.
2. **Revolving door.** Added as a conflict rule (discussing a later role while a matter is open) in Article XII-D, with the cooling-off and clearance rules in CI-4.2. Editor decision, 2026-10-10: the default cooling-off period is **five years** from the end of the person's last involvement in the matter. Adopting institutions may set a longer period, and a shorter one only where material stake is low, with published reasons. An independent seat can still clear a specific offer in a published decision.
3. **Campaign finance.** Expanded on 2026-10-10 from disclosure only to **Selection-funding boundaries** (Chapter Thirteen §1.2): eligible sources (sentients in the authorizing community, giving their own resources; no institutions or systems), a bar on outside-community money, anti-circumvention rules (directed or reimbursed gifts, bundling, loans, in-kind including compute and generated content), an adopter-set donor cap, third-party registration and limits, a real-source public register with a protected-identity route, optional public support, and independent enforcement (audit, clawback, disqualification). Implementation entry: CJS-3.6 *Selection-funding integrity controls*; CI-5.3 trigger *selection-funding circumvention*. Defaults added later the same day (editor-approved scope): donor cap of one quarter of the community's median weekly income (a lower cap is free; a higher one needs published reasons and independent review); third-party limit of ten percent of the targeted candidate's spending limit (no default where the adopter sets no candidate limit); optional public support defaulting to 50% reimbursement above a support threshold of no more than 1% of eligible members. A defined "credible risk of retaliation" test governs the protected-identity route (reasonable likelihood, pattern or specific threat suffices, independent viewpoint-neutral decision, sealed records). An outcome-review duty under Article XXVII-A revalidation tests whether the rules reduce capture and looks for leaks. The numbers are judgment calls, not derived from a study. Evidence base: Quebec (electors only, $100 a year, over 70% public funding), Canada (individuals only, foreign third-party bar), France's 1995 ban, and Brazil's 2015 ban (firms shifted to individual gifts, hence the anti-circumvention rules).
4. **Who keeps the record.** A seat independent of the person contacted, per Chapter Seven. The specific seat is left to the lane map (CI-3.2).
5. **Interested party.** Defined in Chapter Five (Accountability band, Governance architecture group), as a per-matter test of material stake. It includes affected sentients, but status never removes the right to be heard.
6. **Trigger label.** "Influence-contact concealment" added to the CI-5.3 list.

## Part 7. Automated, generated, and bulk approach (now in Article XII-E)

**Problem.** Article XII-D's influence-contact boundaries assume a human-scale approach. They do not say what happens when a system makes or produces the contact, when one party multiplies a single voice into thousands, or when the target is a system the deciding body uses. Existing homes cover parts of this: **Article XIII-E** (*High-Autonomy Systems and Tool-Mediated Process Integrity*) binds systems to the same rules as everyone else; **Article IX-A** covers impersonation and synthetic likeness; **Article X-A** covers manipulation; **Chapter Eleven** names flooding of forums and the info-sphere with inauthentic traffic as a misconduct pattern. None governs contact with decision-makers outside a forum filing.

**Design points.** The Constitution recognizes synthetic sentients, so the rule cannot bar "AI" from speaking. It attributes the contact, bars fabricated voices, stops volume from counting as support, closes private channels to automation, and covers systems on the deciding side.

### 7.1 Text (now in Article XII-E)

Insert as a sub-list under **Influence-contact boundaries**, or as a new bullet directly after it.

- **Automated, generated, and bulk approach:** Influence contact made by, through, or at the scale of a system is influence contact like any other, with these added rules:
  - **Attribution:** Contact produced or sent by a system must say so, name the **interested party** the system acts for, and name the sentient who authorized it. A synthetic sentient speaking for itself is a sentient like any other. Where it acts for an operator or other principal, the principal answers for the contact.
  - **Real voices only:** No one may approach a covered body through a false identity, a fabricated supporter, impersonated or synthetic testimony, or an invented grassroots group. This holds whether one party acts alone or several act together.
  - **Assistive use:** A sentient who uses a system to draft, translate, or send their own message, and approves it, makes their own contact.
  - **Bulk contact:** Where the same or a substantially similar approach reaches a body in volume, the body records it once, as a single campaign. The record states the sponsor, the funding, the method, the volume, and the number of distinct sentients verified. Volume is not evidence of support. A body may rely on support only to the extent of distinct verified sentients.
  - **Channels:** A covered body must publish an authenticated channel for on-record input. It may rate-limit, deduplicate, and set aside input that breaks these rules. Automated approach to a member, panelist, staff member, facilitator, or briefing source through any private channel is barred.
  - **Systems on the deciding side:** Where a body uses a system to receive, sort, summarize, or draft for a decision, an approach aimed at steering that system rather than informing the decision-makers is influence contact. This includes instructions hidden in submitted material, poisoned data, and content built to game a ranking. The body must treat submitted material as evidence and never as instructions, keep each output traceable to the source record, and disclose which inbound material reached a system that fed the decision. Contact between an interested party's system and the body's system is recorded the same way as contact between sentients.
  - **Responsibility:** The interested party and the operator who deploys the system both answer for the contact. Not knowing is no defense where they disregarded an obvious risk.

**Default for "in volume" (placeholder for editor decision).** The body publishes its own threshold. The default is the same or a substantially similar approach reaching more than one quarter of the body's members, or reaching the body 50 or more times in one matter, by or for the same interested party.

### 7.2 Companion changes

- **CJS-3.6** *Influence-contact and lobbying boundaries*: add to **In scope** "contact made by, through, or at the scale of a system, including approach aimed at systems the body uses"; add to the **Primary measure** the system-attributed contacts, the verified-distinct count for each bulk campaign, automated contact blocked on private channels, and disclosures of inbound material that reached decision-support systems; add to the **Primary failure** "counts volume as support, treats submitted material as instructions, or lets system-to-system contact go unrecorded".
- **Interested Party** (Chapter Five), **In scope**: change "anyone acting for it" to "anyone, or any system, acting for it".
- **CI-5.3**: no new trigger; route breaches under *influence-contact concealment*.

### 7.3 Decisions for the editor

1. Whether the bulk default (a quarter of members, or 50 contacts in a matter) is right, or whether to leave thresholds wholly to each body.
2. Whether barring all automated private-channel approach is too broad for accessibility. The assistive-use rule is meant to cover voice assistants and translation.
3. Whether the decision-support disclosure duty is too heavy for small bodies. A scaling rule by material stake would ease it.
4. Resolved: it lives in its own sub-article, Article XII-E, with the other influence-contact rules. Editor accepted the Part 7 defaults for now (bulk threshold of a quarter of members or 50 contacts in a matter; private-channel bar; decision-support disclosure).
