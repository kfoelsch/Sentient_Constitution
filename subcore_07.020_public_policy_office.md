<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: before a big decision is made, an independent office works out what it is likely to do in the real world, for whom, and at what cost, and says how sure it is. After the decision takes effect, the office checks whether it did what it promised.*

**Purpose:** Subcore 7-B makes sure that those who decide binding policy, and the sentients affected by it, can see what a proposal is likely to do before it is decided, and what it actually did afterward.

**Role:** Subcore 7-B is the constitutional owner of **policy impact evaluation**: the evaluation of proposed binding policy before the final decision, and the review of adopted policy after it takes effect. Where it sits:

- [Chapter Twenty-One](core_21.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-one-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) keeps the power to decide. This office advises and publishes; it decides nothing.
- Official statistics come from the [Subcore 7-D](subcore_07.040_public_information_office.md#subcore-7-d-public-information-office) (*Public Information Office*). This office relies on them but does not produce official statistics.
- The [Subcore 7-C Public Stewardship Office](subcore_07.030_public_stewardship_office.md#subcore-7-c-public-stewardship-office--looking-ahead-for-those-not-yet-here) looks decades ahead across all policy. This office looks at one proposal at a time. For a proposal with long-horizon or irreversible effects, the two publish one combined evaluation (§5.1 *Contents*).
- The governing authority pays for the office under *Protected funding* in [Chapter Seven §3](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) (*What Makes a Body Independent*) and does not direct it.

**Scope:** The office:

- evaluates proposals for binding policy that meet the materiality threshold in §4.1 (*The Materiality Threshold*), or are asked for under §4.2 (*Evaluation on Request*) and §4.3 (*Gap Questions*), and updates an evaluation when a proposal is materially amended (§6 *Timing, Response, and No Veto*);
- evaluates, at cost, any proposal a sentient submits under §4.5 (*Evaluation at Cost*);
- screens every proposal for constitutional alignment before a full evaluation, and declines a full evaluation of a proposal that plainly conflicts with this Constitution (§4.6 *Constitutional Alignment Screen*);
- evaluates proposed amendments to this Constitution, so that the justification each proposer must give can be checked in public (§4.7 *Proposed Amendments*);
- rates each sponsor's own impact statement (§5.2 *The Sponsor's Impact Statement*);
- reviews adopted policy at its set review date;
- publishes every evaluation and review with its data, methods, and confidence;
- creates and publishes open decision-making models that anyone may use, check, and improve (§9 *Open Decision-Making Models*);
- offers platform costing before contested selections, and drafting help, to every candidate and sponsor on the same terms (§8 *Even-Handedness and No Steering*); and
- reports each year on how accurate its past evaluations turned out to be.

**Out of scope:** The office does not decide any policy, rank political aims, endorse or oppose a sponsor, draft legislation for one side (the drafting help in §8 (*Even-Handedness and No Steering*) is technical and open to every sponsor on the same terms), or decide any matter that belongs to a forum.

<a id="2-why-the-public-policy-office-exists"></a>

### 2. Why the Public Policy Office Exists

*In plain terms: decision-makers have every reason to oversell their own proposals. An office that tests every proposal the same way, in public, makes it harder to promise one thing and deliver another.*

Policies are often adopted on predictions that no one checks: who will benefit, who will pay, what it will cost, and whether it will work. Sponsors tend to overstate benefits and understate costs, and the sentients who bear the costs are often the least able to say so in time. Once a policy is in force, there is rarely a set moment to ask whether it did what it promised. An independent office that evaluates every material proposal by the same published methods, and returns to it after adoption, gives decision-makers and the public the same evidence and makes the promise checkable.

<a id="3-independence-and-placement"></a>

### 3. Independence and Placement

*In plain terms: the office meets every test for an Independent Body, and no one whose proposal it evaluates can choose its members, pay it on condition, or tell it what to conclude.*

The office is an Independent Body and meets [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) in full:

- **Decision seats:** drawn by lot from the candidates who pass the office's published competency gate, under the role rules of [Subcore 21-E §3 Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways](subcore_21.050_role_holders_interpretive_bodies.md#3-role-criteria-appointment-standards-and-removal-pathways).
- **Competencies:** the gate covers evaluation methods, statistics, economics, and the social and ecological sciences, and at least one seat is held by a sentient with lived experience of the effects of public policy on those with least power.
- **Funding:** under *Protected funding* in Chapter Seven §3 (*What Makes a Body Independent*), through a published floor that may not be cut in response to an evaluation.
- **Post-service limits:** members and staff are bound by the cooling-off rules of Subcore 21-E §3 (*Independence and Placement*) for any sponsor of a proposal they evaluated.
- **Access to information:** the office has the right of access in *Access to information* in Chapter Seven §3 (*What Makes a Body Independent*), and for this office:
    - **From whom:** any public body, and the sponsor of any proposal the office is evaluating, whether or not the sponsor is a public body;
    - **What:** the data, models, analysis, and explanations behind the proposal and its expected effects, including any analysis the sponsor relied on but did not publish;
    - **How fast:** within a shorter information response window of 15 days from the request, because evaluations run on fixed time limits;
    - **Effect of delay:** where a holder's delay keeps the office from meeting a time limit in §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*), the time limit runs from when the information arrives, and the record of the decision says why; and
    - **Published:** each evaluation lists every request it made, and every refusal or delay with the holder's reason.

<a id="4-what-must-be-evaluated"></a>

### 4. What Must Be Evaluated

*In plain terms: every proposal that would make a real difference to many sentients, to rights, or to public money gets an evaluation before it is decided.*

This section sets which proposals must be evaluated (§4.1 *The Materiality Threshold*), who may ask for an evaluation of any other proposal (§4.2 *Evaluation on Request*), how a question that uncovers a gap is handled (§4.3 *Gap Questions*), how emergency measures are evaluated (§4.4 *Emergency Measures*), how any sentient may have a proposal of their own evaluated at cost (§4.5 *Evaluation at Cost*), the constitutional alignment screen that every proposal passes first (§4.6 *Constitutional Alignment Screen*), and how proposed amendments to this Constitution are evaluated (§4.7 *Proposed Amendments*).

<a id="41-the-materiality-threshold"></a>

#### 4.1 The Materiality Threshold

A proposal for binding policy must be evaluated before the final decision when it meets the published materiality threshold. At a minimum, the threshold covers a proposal that would:

- **Rights:** restrict or condition a right under the Chapter Four Rights Floor;
- **Survival floor:** change access to survival-floor goods or services for any group;
- **Public money:** commit public money above a published share of annual public spending;
- **Reach:** materially affect more than a published share of the members of the authorizing community; or
- **Irreversibility:** have effects that cannot practicably be undone.

<a id="42-evaluation-on-request"></a>

#### 4.2 Evaluation on Request

A proposal below the threshold is evaluated when any of the following asks:

- the governing authority or the proposal's sponsor;
- a published share of members of the authorizing community, by petition;
- a forum, where the proposal bears on a matter before it; or
- any sentient who asks a question that uncovers a gap: a likely material effect, on a group, a right, the survival floor, or public money, that no evaluation or published record of the proposal addresses.

<a id="43-gap-questions"></a>

#### 4.3 Gap Questions

A question that may uncover a gap may be asked about any proposal, including one already evaluated:

- **Answered in public:** the office answers each question in public within a published time;
- **Where there is a gap:** the office evaluates the proposal, or adds to an existing evaluation, at least in short form, covering the effect the question raised;
- **Where there is none:** the office says where the effect is already addressed, or why it is not likely to be material;
- **No flooding:** the office may answer questions that raise the same point together, and need not answer again a point it has already answered.

<a id="44-emergency-measures"></a>

#### 4.4 Emergency Measures

The emergency rules of [Chapter Seventeen §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) and [Chapter Six §8 Emergency and Urgent Action](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#8-emergency-and-urgent-action) govern every emergency measure. This office adds evidence to them; it does not replace their review or their clocks:

- **Before evaluation:** a measure taken under those rules may take effect before the office evaluates it. Its default expiry, independent review, and restore-challenge window run as Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 (*Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden*) sets them.
- **Evaluation before any continuation:** a measure that would continue past its default expiry must carry the continuation burden of Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 (*Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden*). The office publishes an evaluation before the decision to continue is made:
    - **What it addresses:** the three parts of that burden:
        - continuing necessity;
        - proportionality; and
        - the absence of a less-restrictive feasible alternative;
    - **What else it covers:** the measure's likely effects and costs;
    - **When time is short:** a short-form evaluation comes first, and the full evaluation follows within the **emergency full-evaluation period**, which is 90 days after the measure took effect.
- **An input, not the review:** the evaluation is evidence for the independent reviewer and for the body deciding continuation. It does not take the place of the independent review, and it never delays the protective steps or the restore-challenge window.
- **Looking back:** once the measure ends, the office's review under §7 (*Review After Adoption*) is published as an input to the retrospective review required by [Article XXVII-A](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxvii-a-retrospective-review-and-disclosure) (*Retrospective Review and Disclosure*).
- **No normalization:** a proposal to make an emergency measure permanent is a new proposal, evaluated in full under this section before it is adopted.

<a id="45-evaluation-at-cost"></a>

#### 4.5 Evaluation at Cost

Any sentient may propose a policy of their own and have the office evaluate it, whether or not anyone has put it forward for decision:

- **A diligent proposal:** the proposal is submitted on the office's published template. It states its aim, says what it would change and for whom, and is specific enough to evaluate. The office returns a proposal that falls short, once, with the reasons, so that it can be fixed.
- **At cost:** the requester pays a fee that covers the office's published cost of the evaluation and no more. The fee schedule is published, depends only on the size of the evaluation, and never on who asks or on what the result is likely to be.
- **Fee relief:** a requester who cannot pay may ask for a reduced or waived fee under a published rule. The office keeps a published share of its capacity for these evaluations (by default, one tenth of the capacity it gives to evaluations at cost).
- **Same evaluation:** the office evaluates the proposal by the same methods, template, and confidence ladder as any other (§5.1 (*Contents*) and §8 (*Even-Handedness and No Steering*)), in short form or in full as the requester chooses. Paying the fee buys the evaluation, never a finding.
- **Published like any other:** the proposal and the evaluation are published together, as every evaluation is under §10 (*Records, Accuracy, and Challenge*). Once submitted, a proposal cannot be withdrawn to keep its evaluation from being published.
- **Capacity:** evaluations required under §4.1 (*The Materiality Threshold*) through §4.4 (*Emergency Measures*) come first. The office publishes its queue and expected waiting times, and fees pay for added capacity, so that this route never slows required evaluations.
- **What it is not:** an evaluation at cost does not put a proposal before the governing authority or any forum. A sponsor who later introduces the same proposal may rely on the evaluation, updated under §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*) if the proposal has changed.

<a id="46-constitutional-alignment-screen"></a>

#### 4.6 Constitutional Alignment Screen

Before any full evaluation, the office screens each proposal against this Constitution. A proposal that plainly conflicts with it is not given a full evaluation:

- **What is screened:** whether the proposal conflicts with binding constitutional text, including:
    - the rights of the Chapter Four Rights Floor;
    - access to survival-floor goods and services; and
    - the requirements of the core chapters, read with the [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims) as that text states them.
- **What is not screened:** whether the proposal's aim is worth pursuing. The screen tests conflict with binding text, never political preference, and §8 (*Even-Handedness and No Steering*) applies to it in full.
- **Three outcomes:** the office publishes one of these within the **screening period**, which is 10 days from receiving the proposal, on published criteria:
    - **Aligned:** no conflict is found, and the evaluation goes ahead;
    - **In doubt:** a conflict is arguable. The evaluation goes ahead and includes an alignment section that names each provision at issue and the question it raises; and
    - **Not aligned:** the proposal plainly conflicts with binding text on any reasonable reading. The office declines a full evaluation and publishes a screening finding that names each provision and explains the conflict.
- **Part of a proposal:** where only part of a proposal is not aligned, the office says which part, and may evaluate the rest.
- **Not a ruling:** a screening finding is the office's published view, not an interpretation of the Constitution. Final interpretation belongs to the Constitutional Forums under [Chapter Seventeen §3.6 Constitutional Forums](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#36-constitutional-forums) and [Article XXVI](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxvi-constitutional-interpretation-review-and-anti-capture-safeguards) (*Constitutional Interpretation, Review, and Anti-Capture Safeguards*).
- **After a finding of not aligned:**
    - **Revise:** the sponsor may revise the proposal and submit it again for a fresh screen;
    - **Contest:** the sponsor, or any sentient with a [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake) in the proposal, may bring the finding before a Constitutional Forum. If the forum finds the proposal aligned or in doubt, the office evaluates it, and its time limit under §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*) starts from the forum's decision; and
    - **Proceeding anyway:** if the governing authority goes ahead with the proposal, it must answer the screening finding as a material finding under §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*), and the finding is published notice for any constitutional challenge to the policy once adopted.
- **Emergency measures:** the screen never delays a measure under §4.4 (*Emergency Measures*). It is done with the evaluation before any continuation.
- **Evaluation at cost:** for a proposal under §4.5 (*Evaluation at Cost*) found not aligned, the requester pays only the published cost of the screen.
- **Kept honest:** every screen is published with its reasons, and the yearly accuracy report in §10 (*Records, Accuracy, and Challenge*) shows how Constitutional Forums decided the findings brought before them.

<a id="47-proposed-amendments"></a>

#### 4.7 Proposed Amendments

A proposed amendment to this Constitution is a proposal for binding policy under this section. The office's evaluation is how the justification required by [Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Burden of Justification and Public Traceability](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#5-burden-of-justification-and-public-traceability) is tested in public:

- **When:** an amendment is evaluated when it meets the threshold in §4.1 (*The Materiality Threshold*) or is asked for under §4.2 (*Evaluation on Request*). An amendment that restricts or conditions a right under the Chapter Four Rights Floor always meets the threshold.
- **The proposer's justification:** the justification required by Chapter Twenty-Nine §5 (*Burden of Justification and Public Traceability*) serves as the sponsor's impact statement under §5.2 (*The Sponsor's Impact Statement*), and is rated in the same way.
- **What it adds:** besides the contents in §5.1 (*Contents*), the evaluation states the office's view of the evidence for the proposer's claims of necessity and feasibility, and of the effects bearing on the non-regression constraints of [Chapter Twenty-Two](core_22.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-two-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity).
- **Screen:** the screen in §4.6 (*Constitutional Alignment Screen*) tests the amendment against the text it would leave in force, never against the text it proposes to change.
- **Not a ruling on validity:** whether an amendment is valid under Chapter Twenty-Eight or Chapter Twenty-Nine is decided as those chapters set out. The evaluation is evidence for that decision, and §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*) applies to it in full.

<a id="5-what-an-evaluation-contains"></a>

### 5. What an Evaluation Contains

*In plain terms: every evaluation answers the same questions in the same order, so evaluations can be compared and nothing awkward is left out.*

This section sets what every evaluation contains (§5.1 *Contents*) and what the sponsor of a proposal shows of its own analysis (§5.2 *The Sponsor's Impact Statement*).

<a id="51-contents"></a>

#### 5.1 Contents

Every evaluation follows the published template and states, at a minimum:

- **The aim:** the effect the sponsor says the proposal will have, in the sponsor's words, and how it would be measured.
- **The likely effects:**
    - who is likely to gain and who is likely to bear costs, including effects that fall unevenly by place, income, age, substrate, or other group;
    - effects on rights under the Chapter Four Rights Floor and on access to survival-floor goods and services;
    - effects on [Productive Capacity](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#productive-capacity) and on [Avoidable Burden](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#avoidable-burden); and
    - the cost to public money, now and over time.
- **Uncertainty:** how confident the office is in each estimate, using the [Subcore 7-D §4.2 confidence ladder](subcore_07.040_public_information_office.md#42-the-confidence-ladder) and its [§4.3 application rules](subcore_07.040_public_information_office.md#43-applying-the-confidence-ladder), and what evidence would change it. The office rates its own estimates using the published criteria and independent checking those rules require.
- **Alternatives:** doing nothing, and any other options that would plausibly meet the same aim at lower cost or harm.
- **Measurement and review:** the indicators that would show whether the policy works, and a proposed review date.
- **Long horizon:** where effects reach beyond the published horizon or cannot be undone, a long-horizon section prepared with the Public Stewardship Office.

A short-form evaluation, using the same headings, may be used for a proposal below the threshold.

<a id="52-the-sponsors-impact-statement"></a>

#### 5.2 The Sponsor's Impact Statement

A proposal above the threshold comes with its sponsor's own account of what it will do, so that the sponsor's reasoning is tested as well as the proposal:

- **Who submits:** the sponsor of a proposal that meets the threshold in §4.1 (*The Materiality Threshold*) submits an impact statement with the proposal, on the office's published template, using the headings of §5.1 (*Contents*). A sponsor that is not a public body, such as a petition, may ask the office for the help in §8 (*Even-Handedness and No Steering*) or submit a shorter statement, and is never refused evaluation for lack of one.
- **Rating:** the office rates the statement **fit for purpose** or **not fit for purpose**, against published criteria, within the time limit for the evaluation, and publishes the rating with its reasons beside the statement.
- **Not fit for purpose:** the sponsor may revise the statement once before the final decision. The rating and any revision are published with the evaluation, and the governing authority's response under §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*) addresses a not-fit rating that stands.
- **The office's own estimate:** the office still makes its own estimate of every effect that is material or contested, and says where it agrees or differs with the sponsor. A fit rating never replaces the office's evaluation.
- **Tracked over time:** the yearly accuracy report in §10 (*Records, Accuracy, and Challenge*) shows each sponsor's ratings over time, so that the quality of sponsors' analysis can be seen to improve or not.

<a id="6-timing-response-and-no-veto"></a>

### 6. Timing, Response, and No Veto

*In plain terms: the evaluation comes out before the vote. The decision-makers do not have to agree with it, but they have to say in public why not.*

Each evaluation is timed to inform the decision, and the office advises without deciding:

- **Before the decision:** the evaluation is published before the final decision, within the **evaluation period**, which is 60 days from the request, or 30 days for a short-form evaluation. The governing authority may not cut that time short by delaying the request. The evaluation is part of what is published about the proposal under [Publication Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#publication-integrity).
- **Late evaluation:** if the office misses its time limit, the decision may go ahead. The record of the decision states that no evaluation was available, and the office publishes its evaluation as soon as it is done.
- **Amended proposals:** when a proposal is amended after its evaluation in a way that would change a material finding, or that would itself meet the threshold in §4.1 (*The Materiality Threshold*), the sponsor notifies the office and the office publishes a short-form update, covering the amendment, within the **amendment update period**, which is 10 days. The final decision is not taken until the update is published or the amendment update period has passed. Amendments made together may be covered by one update.
- **Response:** before or with the final decision, the governing authority publishes a reasoned response to each material finding: where it agrees, where it disagrees and why, and what it changed.
- **No veto:** what the office finds never decides the outcome, but the steps of this section must still be followed:
    - **Findings do not bind:** no evaluation, update, or screening finding blocks a decision or makes it invalid because of what it says. The governing authority may decide against every finding, so long as it answers each one under *Response* above.
    - **The steps do bind:** the governing authority must wait for each required evaluation or update until it is published or its time limit has passed, and must publish its response. These waits are fixed in advance and short. If the office is late, the decision goes ahead under *Late evaluation* above.
    - **Skipping a step:** a decision taken without a required step, such as deciding before the time limit has passed, or without a response, may be challenged as a procedural failure under [Chapter Twenty-One §3.1.4 Contestation, Correction, and Pluralism](core_21.000_governance.md#314-contestation-correction-and-pluralism). The challenge is about the missing step, never about whether the office's findings were right, and the usual remedy is to have the step carried out.

<a id="7-review-after-adoption"></a>

### 7. Review After Adoption

*In plain terms: on the review date, the office checks whether the policy did what it promised and says whether it should be kept, fixed, or ended.*

Every evaluated policy is checked again after it takes effect:

- **Review date:** every policy evaluated under §4 (*What Must Be Evaluated*) carries a review date, set at adoption. If none is set, it falls at the end of the **default review interval**, which is five years after adoption.
- **The review:** compares what happened with what the evaluation and the sponsor predicted, using the indicators named in §5 (*What an Evaluation Contains*), and states whether the evidence supports keeping, changing, or ending the policy.
- **Response:** the governing authority publishes a reasoned response to the review, as in §6 (*Timing, Response, and No Veto*).
- **Link to stewardship:** reviews are an input to the [Review and Correction Duty](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#review-and-correction-duty) under [Chapter Twenty-One §5.3 Public Institutions Supporting Stewardship](core_21.000_governance.md#53-public-institutions-supporting-stewardship).

<a id="8-even-handedness-and-no-steering"></a>

### 8. Even-Handedness and No Steering

*In plain terms: the office treats every proposal the same, whoever sponsors it, and never campaigns for a side.*

The office treats every sponsor and every aim alike:

- **Same methods for everyone:** the office uses its published methods for every proposal, whoever the sponsor is, and publishes any change to them before using it.
- **No ranking of aims:** the office tests whether a proposal is likely to meet its own stated aim, and at what cost. It does not judge whether the aim is worth pursuing.
- **No advocacy:** the office and its members do not campaign for or against a proposal, a sponsor, or a party.
- **No pressure:** any attempt to direct a conclusion, delay a publication, or tie funding to a result is a violation, reported to the Integrity Forum.
- **Platform costing before contested selections:** before a contested selection under [Chapter Twenty-One §3.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums](core_21.000_governance.md#32-eligibility-contested-selection-and-democratic-minimums), the office offers to evaluate the main proposals of every candidate or slate:
    - **Voluntary:** no candidate is required to take part, and the office does not comment on anyone who does not;
    - **Equal terms:** every submission is evaluated by the same methods, on the same timetable, with a submission deadline published in advance;
    - **Published together:** all results are published on the same day, no later than the start of the **costing lead time**, which is 14 days before the selection, and none is published earlier; and
    - **No ranking:** the results state effects and costs. They never rank candidates or aims.
- **Drafting help:** any sponsor may ask the office for technical help in drafting a proposal, such as estimating its costs or checking that it does what the sponsor intends:
    - **Same terms for all:** the help is offered to every sponsor on the same published terms;
    - **Confidential only until introduction:** the help stays confidential until the proposal is introduced or published, and then the record of the request and the help given is published in full;
    - **Technical only:** the help never includes advice on whether to pursue an aim; and
    - **Disclosed in the evaluation:** an evaluation of a proposal the office helped draft says so.

<a id="9-open-decision-making-models"></a>

### 9. Open Decision-Making Models

*In plain terms: the office builds the tools it uses to predict what a policy will do, and gives them away. Anyone can run them, check them, improve them, or use them to test their own idea before proposing it.*

The office creates and publishes **open decision-making models**: the models, data, and tools on which its evaluations rest. The foundations of its work are made as widely available as possible, so that anyone can check an evaluation and anyone can use the same tools to test a proposal of their own.

- **What is published:**
    - every model the office uses to estimate a policy's effects, costs, and distribution, with its code, documentation, and assumptions;
    - the input data, as Type O data under [Subcore 4-A §10.3 Type O](subcore_04.050_data_types.md#103-type-o-open-public-oversight-baseline-disclosure-data), with protected types replaced by the strongest feasible Type O substitute under the standard data-type rules of [Subcore 4-A §10 Data Classifications](subcore_04.050_data_types.md#10-data-classifications); and
    - plain-language guides, and a simple public way to run the main models without programming skills.
- **Open to all:**
    - everything is published under an open licence that lets anyone use, copy, change, and share it, for any lawful purpose, without fee;
    - the models run on freely available software; and
    - other adopters may use them freely.
- **Reproducible:** each evaluation names the model version and data it used, so that anyone can run it again and get the same results.
- **Open to improvement:**
    - anyone may propose a correction or improvement to a model;
    - the office reviews each proposal and publishes what it accepted or declined, and why; and
    - a change to a model is a change of method under §8 (*Even-Handedness and No Steering*), published before it is used.
- **No closed foundations:** no evaluation may rest on a model, tool, or data set that cannot be published this way. Where the office relies on outside work, it uses openly licensed work or rebuilds it openly.
- **Advice, not command:** a model's output informs an evaluation; it never binds a decision, and the evaluation still states its confidence under §5 (*What an Evaluation Contains*).

<a id="10-records-accuracy-and-challenge"></a>

### 10. Records, Accuracy, and Challenge

*In plain terms: everything the office publishes can be checked, and each reporting year it shows how its past predictions turned out.*

The office keeps its work open to checking and correction:

- **Open publication:** each evaluation and review is published with its data, methods, assumptions, and correction history, except data that must be protected under the Chapter Four Rights Floor, which is described but withheld.
- **Accuracy report:** each year the office publishes how its past estimates compared with outcomes, how Constitutional Forums decided the screening findings brought before them, and what it changed as a result.
- **Outside review:** under *Audit of its work* in [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent), within each method-review interval (by default, five years) a panel from outside the office reviews its methods, the accuracy of its past evaluations, its access to information, and its independence in practice. The panel may include reviewers from other adopters' offices, and its findings are published with the office's response.
- **Challenge:** any sentient may ask for a correction of an error of fact or method. Capture or manipulation of the office may be raised with the Integrity Forum.

<a id="11-proportional-scaling"></a>

### 11. Proportional Scaling

*In plain terms: a small community still gets honest evaluations, but with a lighter process.*

The *Scaling* rule in [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent) applies to this office in full. For this office it also means:

- **Sharing:** a small adopter may share an office with other adopters under a published agreement. Sharing never lets one adopter direct the office's evaluation of another adopter's proposal.
- **Combining:** a small adopter may combine this office with its Public Stewardship Office, as [Subcore 7-C §13](subcore_07.030_public_stewardship_office.md#13-proportional-scaling) allows.
- **Lighter forms:** a small adopter may use the short-form evaluation for every proposal.
- **Never scaled down:** every evaluation and review is still published, and the governing authority's duty to respond or explain applies in full.
