*In plain terms: Authorities must hold themselves to standards at least as strict as those they impose on others. Exempting yourself from your own rule is the clearest sign the rule is not about safety.*

Use this rule when legitimacy depends on authorities holding themselves to equal or stronger standards than they apply to others.

One cluster-level term is pinned at the top of this section in functional reading order rather than alphabetical order, because reflexive-accountability failures usually appear only when authorities are held to the same or stronger standards they impose; meeting transparency or audit sub-rules for subjects does not by itself prove equal treatment for the authority. It states the integrated obligation, evaluation, and compliance floor for **CJS-3.2** as a whole: reflexive transparency and accountability must be assessed as one claim before any named sub-rule is treated as satisfied on its own.

<a id="reflexive-transparency-and-accountability-terms"></a>
reflexive transparency and accountability terms

*In plain terms: authorities must hold themselves to standards at least as strict as those they impose — transparent, auditable, challengeable, and honest about what they can actually enforce.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Authorities must be transparent, auditable, realistic about enforcement capacity, open to challenge, and protected against corruption.
  - **Out of scope:** subject-facing compliance duties assessed alone, without asking whether the authority itself meets equal or stricter standards.
<a id="reflexive-transparency-and-accountability-terms-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** joint review of authorities, system components, dependencies, institutional roles, and implementation-layer interactions as one reflexive-accountability claim

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must assess all relevant authorities, system components, dependencies, institutional roles, and implementation-layer interactions together.
<a id="reflexive-transparency-and-accountability-terms-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Reflexive accountability when authorities use weaker standards for themselves than for subjects.

The sub-rules below apply that cluster floor to specific anti-corruption, enforcement-capacity, misrepresentation, revalidation, opaque-enforcement, parity, pluralistic-validation, and transparency-audit dimensions. Read them together with the cluster floor and the CJS read-with chain.

<a id="anti-corruption-and-undue-influence-auditability-controls"></a>
Anti-corruption and undue-influence auditability controls

*In plain terms: if money, favors, or influence could bend a high-stakes decision, the channel must be recorded and disclosable in proportion to the impact.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Material benefits and influence channels that could distort high-stakes decisions must be recorded and disclosed in proportion to impact.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary hospitality, routine disclosure paperwork, or ethics training with no material benefit, influence channel, or high-stakes decision at issue.
<a id="anti-corruption-and-undue-influence-auditability-controls-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** escalation routes for improper influence and alignment with anti-capture controls

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify escalation routes for improper influence and alignment with anti-capture controls.
<a id="anti-corruption-and-undue-influence-auditability-controls-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Concealed side benefits or decision paths concentrated around private benefit.

<a id="enforcement-capacity-realism-requirements"></a>
Enforcement-capacity realism requirements

*In plain terms: accountability needs real remedies and real capacity — funding, staffing, tools, and coordination — not promises on paper.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Accountability must include real remedies and real enforcement capacity, including funding, staffing, tools, and coordination.
  - **Out of scope:** aspirational enforcement plans or budget narrative with no remedy path, staffing, tools, or coordination claim under review.
<a id="enforcement-capacity-realism-requirements-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** capacity matched to harm risk, dependency, claimant scale, and cross-jurisdiction needs

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify that capacity matches harm risk, dependency, claimant scale, and cross-jurisdiction needs.
<a id="enforcement-capacity-realism-requirements-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Hollow enforcement claims.

<a id="misrepresentation-and-violation-signaling"></a>
Misrepresentation and violation signaling

*In plain terms: misstating compliance, audit status, validation level, or system behavior is an integrity breach — not a communications problem.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Misstating compliance, audit status, validation level, or system behavior is an integrity breach.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary drafting error or non-material wording variance with no compliance, audit, validation, or behavior claim at stake.
<a id="misrepresentation-and-violation-signaling-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** mechanisms to detect, record, and correct misrepresentation by authorities

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify mechanisms to detect, record, and correct misrepresentation by authorities.
<a id="misrepresentation-and-violation-signaling-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Untracked or unremedied misrepresentation.

<a id="ongoing-accountability-and-revalidation-continuity"></a>
Ongoing accountability and revalidation continuity

*In plain terms: governance and enforcement must stay under live audit, challenge, revalidation, and independent verification — not a one-time certificate.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Governance and enforcement systems must remain under ongoing audit, challenge, revalidation, and independent verification.
  - **Out of scope:** one-time launch review or ceremonial attestation with no ongoing audit, challenge, revalidation, or independent-verification duty.
<a id="ongoing-accountability-and-revalidation-continuity-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** accountability channels that continue over time and under stress

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify that accountability channels continue over time and under stress.
<a id="ongoing-accountability-and-revalidation-continuity-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Lapsed, symbolic, or check-the-box accountability.

<a id="opaque-enforcement-limits-and-constrained-disclosure-handling"></a>
Opaque-enforcement limits and constrained-disclosure handling

*In plain terms: if enforcement cannot be audited, it is disallowed when a feasible alternative exists; lawful secrecy still needs the most transparency feasible and a documented reason.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Non-auditable enforcement is disallowed when feasible alternatives exist. If lawful limits prevent full disclosure, authorities must still provide the most transparency feasible and document the reason for the limit.
  - **Out of scope:** routine access controls or redaction practice with no enforcement action, feasible alternative, or constrained-disclosure claim.
<a id="opaque-enforcement-limits-and-constrained-disclosure-handling-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** constrained disclosure that remains independently auditable and reviewable

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify that constrained disclosure remains independently auditable and reviewable.
<a id="opaque-enforcement-limits-and-constrained-disclosure-handling-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Secrecy used as a practical bypass of safeguards.

<a id="parity-and-non-exemption-floor"></a>
Parity and non-exemption floor

*In plain terms: oversight, governance, adjudication, and enforcement actors must meet standards equal to or stricter than those they impose on others.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Oversight, governance, adjudication, and enforcement actors must meet standards equal to or stricter than those they impose.
  - **Out of scope:** role-design talk or org-chart labels with no verification, challenge, or accountability standard being applied to an authority.
<a id="parity-and-non-exemption-floor-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** whether authorities are exempt from verification, challenge, or accountability

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify that authorities are not exempt from verification, challenge, or accountability.
<a id="parity-and-non-exemption-floor-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Self-rules weaker than imposed rules.

<a id="pluralistic-validation-and-contestability-protections"></a>
Pluralistic validation and contestability protections

*In plain terms: where plural validation is required, no single verification authority may monopolize what counts as true.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** No single verification authority may monopolize truth determination where plural validation is required.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary single-auditor assignments where plural validation is not required for the claim.
<a id="pluralistic-validation-and-contestability-protections-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** paths for challenge, refinement, and re-evaluation

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify paths for challenge, refinement, and re-evaluation.
<a id="pluralistic-validation-and-contestability-protections-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Suppressing dissenting or minority verification outcomes.

<a id="transparency-audit-and-verification-integrity-duties"></a>
Transparency, audit, and verification-integrity duties

*In plain terms: enforcement, investigation, and governance actions must leave a reconstructable, proportionate, independently reviewable trail.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Enforcement, investigation, and governance actions and methods must be documented, auditable, proportionate, and independently reviewable.
  - **Out of scope:** internal working notes with no enforcement, investigation, or governance action under review.
<a id="transparency-audit-and-verification-integrity-duties-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** records that can reconstruct decisions and actions

    **Primary assessment:** Reviewers must verify that records can reconstruct decisions and actions.
<a id="transparency-audit-and-verification-integrity-duties-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Opaque methods or selective disclosure when they undermine accountability.

<a id="auditability-and-reconstructability-terms"></a>
auditability and reconstructability terms

*In plain terms: records must be verifiable, independently reviewable, and adequate for reconstructing harm or disputes.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Records must be verifiable, independently reviewable, and adequate for reconstructing harm or disputes.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary process talk, symbolic labels, or adjacent owner-file duties with no material stake in auditability and reconstructability.
<a id="auditability-and-reconstructability-terms-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** all relevant system components, dependencies, institutional roles, and implementation-layer interactions together

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must assess all relevant system components, dependencies, institutional roles, and implementation-layer interactions together.
<a id="auditability-and-reconstructability-terms-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Claim auditability when a material component has not been fully evaluated, is unavailable to qualified review, or is unusable for reconstruction.

<a id="article-vii-b-boundary-and-anti-concealment-rule"></a>
**Article VII-B** (*Internal-State Boundary and Type-N Protection*) boundary and anti-concealment rule

*In plain terms: private internal states stay protected, but systems still have to show what they did, why it mattered, who was responsible, and what happened outside.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** **Article VII-B** (*Internal-State Boundary and Type-N Protection*) protects private internal states, but that protection does not excuse systems from showing what they did, why it mattered, who or what was responsible, and what external effects followed.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary privacy or confidentiality talk with no accountability-relevant action, output, decision, effect, responsibility, or control pathway at stake.
<a id="article-vii-b-boundary-and-anti-concealment-rule-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** privacy limits that protect internal states without blocking lawful audit of actions, outputs, decisions, effects, responsibilities, or control pathways

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify that privacy limits protect internal states without preventing lawful audit of actions, outputs, decisions, effects, responsibilities, or control pathways.
<a id="article-vii-b-boundary-and-anti-concealment-rule-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Invoking internal-state protection in a way that conceals accountability-relevant system behavior or prevents lawful reconstruction of external facts.

<a id="auditability-floor-and-record-sufficiency"></a>
Auditability floor and record sufficiency

*In plain terms: keep records with enough scope, provenance, intelligibility, continuity, and retention that a qualified reviewer can check compliance and reconstruct material behavior.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Apply [Auditability](../core_05_band_oversight.md#auditability) by retaining records whose scope, provenance, intelligibility, continuity, and retention support compliance evaluation, reconstruction of material behavior, and verification of material claims.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary process talk, symbolic labels, or adjacent owner-file duties with no material stake in auditability floor and record sufficiency; the constitutional meaning of Auditability itself.
<a id="auditability-floor-and-record-sufficiency-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** record scope, quality, and retention in practice

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify record scope, quality, and retention in practice.
<a id="auditability-floor-and-record-sufficiency-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Recordkeeping when records lack the scope, provenance, intelligibility, continuity, or retention needed for a qualified reviewer to evaluate compliance, reconstruct material behavior, or verify material claims.

<a id="forensic-depth-access-proportionality"></a>
Forensic-depth access proportionality

*In plain terms: deeper forensic access must be available when harm, credible risk, or investigation needs require full reconstruction.*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Deeper forensic access must be available when harm, credible risk, or investigation needs require full reconstruction.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary process talk, symbolic labels, or adjacent owner-file duties with no material stake in forensic-depth access proportionality.
<a id="forensic-depth-access-proportionality-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** escalation from ordinary records to forensic depth is real and timely

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify escalation from ordinary records to forensic depth is real and timely.

  - **Primary failure:** Treat anti-capture control as satisfied when controls are undocumented, owned by the subject of the allegation without an independence safeguard, disconnected from protected reporting, unsupported by reconstruction records, or unable to trigger assurance, escalation, remediation, sanction, restitution, clawback, or lawful referral where required.

<a id="sortition-and-civic-lottery-integrity-controls"></a>
Sortition and civic-lottery integrity controls

*In plain terms: where sortition, civic lottery, representative draw, or comparable randomized participation materially shapes governing, oversight, review, procurement, audit, agenda-setting, or constitutional-deliberation authority, the pathway must…*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Where sortition, civic lottery, representative draw, or comparable randomized participation materially shapes governing, oversight, review, procurement, audit, agenda-setting, or constitutional-deliberation authority, the pathway must protect randomization integrity, eligibility and exclusion rules, replacement and rotation rules, participant access supports, briefing-source independence, facilitator and staff separation, contact and lobbying boundaries, intimidation safeguards, post-service benefit controls, and contest routes for selection failure, manipulation, capture, or procedural distortion.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary process talk, symbolic labels, or adjacent owner-file duties with no material stake in sortition and civic-lottery integrity.
<a id="sortition-and-civic-lottery-integrity-controls-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** the draw method, representative or stratification design where used, source-list custody, exclusion reasons, replacement records, compensation and accommodation design, conflict screening, briefing and facilitation independence, staff-support boundaries, communication restrictions, intimidation controls, post-service benefit restrictions, and contest records

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify the draw method, representative or stratification design where used, source-list custody, exclusion reasons, replacement records, compensation and accommodation design, conflict screening, briefing and facilitation independence, staff-support boundaries, communication restrictions, intimidation controls, post-service benefit restrictions, and contest records.
<a id="sortition-and-civic-lottery-integrity-controls-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** A sortition or civic-lottery pathway if the pool, draw, briefing process, facilitator lane, staff support channel, or implementation response can be manipulated, captured, intimidated, or used to launder a predetermined outcome where the body exercises or materially shapes governing authority.

<a id="three-lines-assurance-model"></a>
Three-lines assurance model

*In plain terms: where implementation duties require operational ownership, independent risk or compliance challenge, and independent assurance, those lines must remain distinguishable and must not collapse into a single self-certifying lane without a…*

- **What it is**
  - **In scope:** Where implementation duties require operational ownership, independent risk or compliance challenge, and independent assurance, those lines must remain distinguishable and must not collapse into a single self-certifying lane without a documented independence safeguard.
  - **Out of scope:** ordinary process talk, symbolic labels, or adjacent owner-file duties with no material stake in three-lines assurance model.
<a id="three-lines-assurance-model-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** each line has a named owner, that role concentration across lines is limited and justified where unavoidable, and that records and evidence custody are sufficient for audit, contest, correction, and continuity transfer under [Evidence Preservation](../core_05_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation)

    **Primary assessment:** Evaluation must verify that each line has a named owner, that role concentration across lines is limited and justified where unavoidable, and that records and evidence custody are sufficient for audit, contest, correction, and continuity transfer under [Evidence Preservation](../core_05_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation).
<a id="three-lines-assurance-model-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Treat assurance as satisfied when the challenged operational owner also controls the only review or assurance path without an independence safeguard, or when record custody cannot support reconstruction across transfers.

Default **NUL** bands (adopting instruments may tighten but must not weaken without lawful authority):
- **NUL-1 (immediate):** Active ongoing harm; unlawful **Type N** access, exposure, or reconstruction risk under `corpus_systems.md` **CS-2 — Information types and handling**; active corruption, fraud, or capture in progress; or control collapse affecting rights pathways — internal escalation and affected-party notice without unjustified delay; supervisory, assurance-line, and public declaration as soon as containment posture allows without destroying lawful investigation integrity where **Type S** or protected-investigation constraints apply.
- **NUL-2 (expedited):** Confirmed breach, integrity failure, or verified **CI-5.3** corruption or fraud trigger with material but contained impact — notice to affected parties and the independent assurance line within a published expedited window materially shorter than routine reporting.
- **NUL-3 (standard):** Material control weakness or integrity finding requiring cure but not active ongoing exposure — notice within a published standard incident window and inclusion in the next required control declaration if not cured.
- **NUL-4 (routine):** Baseline controls attestation and non-urgent assurance reporting only — published annual or other routine cadence.

**NUL** assignment must govern who is notified, in what order, and by when. **NUL-1** and **NUL-2** events must invoke **CI-7.2** external-assurance review when published triggers are met. **Type N** breaches default to at least **NUL-2** and to **NUL-1** while unlawful access, exposure, or reconstruction risk is ongoing.
