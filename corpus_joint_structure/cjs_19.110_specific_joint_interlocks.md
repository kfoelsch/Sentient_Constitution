*In plain terms: this package covers hybrid decision bodies for institutions and forums in two steps — **CJS-19.110 §1.1** (*Composition minimum*) says who must be on the body; **CJS-19.110 §1.2** (*Attachment publication*) says what the published rules must spell out about how long members serve. Both steps must be satisfied.*

A hybrid design that names home and rotating roles but omits **CJS-19.110 §1.2** (*Attachment publication*) publication mechanics does not satisfy **CJS-19.110 §1** (*Hybrid delegated authority (delegated binding bodies)*). Naming the mix is not enough; the published term rules have to be there too.

### CJS-19.110 §1.1 Composition minimum


*In plain terms: a group that makes binding decisions cannot be all long-serving insiders or all newcomers. It must mix stable "home" members with rotating members — and home members must usually be the minority when the group decides together.*

**Who this covers**

- **Institution decision bodies** — committees, divisions, and similar bodies below a parent institution that make binding decisions, within the **Class C and above** applicability of **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*). Owner detail: **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*). Router topic: **CJS-R01** in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Cross-file routing*).
- **Forum chambers and panels** — chambers, divisions, and designated panels under **CF-14.405 §5** (*Chamber creation, identification, and family boundary*) through **CF-14.405 §8** (*Specialist and technical chamber discipline*). Router topic: **CJS-R02** in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Cross-file routing*). The minimum **Class C** institutional design minimum in **CF-14.405 §6** (*Chamber authority composition and service mechanics*) applies even where other governed systems are below Class C.

**The hybrid mix required**

Every covered body must use a **hybrid design** that includes both:

- **Home-based authority** — members who serve long enough to keep continuity and institutional memory.
- **Rotating authority** — members who rotate in to bring broader perspective, case-specific expertise, and limits on insider control.

**When a group decides together.** Home-based members must be a **minority** on the deciding group unless the rules use an equivalent published alternative across stages — for example staged roles, mandatory review, or a second review step before the decision binds.

**Alternative hybrid designs.** A different deciding-group balance or staged design must retain both home-based and rotating authority across the ordinary decision workflow. Institutional alternatives must also meet **CI-9.210 §3.2** (*Authority composition*), including published substitute capture safeguards and the independent assurance or review required for Class A and Class B scope. That route does not authorize ordinary binding decisions by an entirely home-based or entirely rotating workflow.

**When one member may decide alone.** If the rules lawfully allow a single member or sole officer to decide, the overall workflow must still include both continuity and rotation somewhere in the path to that decision.

**Emergencies only.** An entirely home-based or entirely rotating workflow fails this rule for ordinary binding decisions. A temporary departure is permitted only to the extent authorized by the primary owner's continuity rules and consistent with [Emergency and Contingency](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#emergency-and-contingency-constitutional), [Chapter Fourteen §6.1 Emergency measures and continuation burden](../core_14.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden), and [Chapter Eight §7 Emergency and Urgent Action](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#7-emergency-and-urgent-action). A published plan alone does not establish an emergency or authorize a departure. The applicable emergency predicate, necessity, proportionality, default expiry, independent review, continuation burden, notice and challenge restoration, and restoration or rollback requirements must all be met. A staffing shortage, deadline, release target, or reputational concern is not by itself an emergency. Emergency action preserves seat ownership and class-scaled separation, including the prohibition on any merger of the four seats for Class A; an actor may not verify its own continuation, hold a conflicted record, or hear the challenge to its own act.

For forums, apply [CF-14.450 §3 Activation triggers](../corpus_forum/cf_14.450_forum_continuity.md#cf-14450-3-activation-triggers), [CF-14.455 §1 Temporary quorum and provisional authority](../corpus_forum/cf_14.455_fallback_operation.md#cf-14455-1-temporary-quorum-and-provisional-authority), and [CF-14.460 Emergency adjudication](../corpus_forum/cf_14.460_emergency_adjudication.md), including independent review during emergency mode and restoration and reconciliation. Document and carry out the return to the ordinary hybrid workflow when the emergency conditions clear or lawful continuation fails. Retrospective review and disclosure remain governed by [Article XXVI-A Retrospective Review and Disclosure](../core_06.020_rights_part_e.md#article-xxvi-a-retrospective-review-and-disclosure); restrictions must meet [Article XX-B Restriction Floors](../core_06.015_rights_part_d.md#article-xx-b-restriction-floors). Where constitutional-system continuity is implicated, apply [CS-6.345 §8 Governance continuity, crisis communications, and exercises (high-impact systems)](../corpus_systems/cs_06.345_design_testing_verification_deployment.md#cs-6345-8-governance-continuity-crisis-communications-and-exercises-high-impact-systems).

**When institution and forum rules both apply.** Where **CI** and **CF** both speak to the same body, the clearly adopted rule with the [Fullest Protective Effect](../core_05.045_band_integrative.md#fullest-protective-effect) governs.

### CJS-19.110 §1.2 Attachment publication


*In plain terms: once the hybrid mix is required (**CJS-19.110 §1.1** (*Composition minimum*)), the published rules must spell out how long rotating members serve, how home-member terms work, and who may not change those durations in secret.*

Where **CJS-19.110 §1** (*Hybrid delegated authority (delegated binding bodies)*) applies, the document must publish:

- a bounded formula for rotating attachment that rotating authorities can understand before service;
- no undefined discretion over how long rotating attachment lasts;
- term, renewal, or stagger rules for home-based continuity; and
- any class-, forum-, institution-, emergency-, or substitution-specific safeguards required by **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*), **CF-14.405 §5** (*Chamber creation, identification, and family boundary*), **CF-14.450** (*Forum continuity*), **CF-14.455** (*Fallback operation*), **CF-14.460** (*Emergency adjudication*), or related hooks.

In everyday reading: publish how long rotating members serve, how home-member terms work, and that those lengths are not changed in secret. Sample formulas are illustrations unless a **primary owner** makes them mandatory. Home-based term rules do not need to copy rotating attachment rules.

*In plain terms: a trust claim is only as strong as the whole chain behind it. If one implementation file looks reassuring while another in the same chain still leaves gaps that block observation, verification, or challenge, the claim fails — and institutions must publish a map of what supports it, how to contest it, and who fixes it.*

Use this rule when a trust claim depends on more than one system, institution, dependency, or implementation layer working together. Router topic: **CJS-R17** (*Cross-implementation trust integrity*) in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Cross-file routing*).

The meanings of **Trust**, **Trustworthiness**, and **Trust Degradation and Misleading Reliance** remain in the [Chapter Five trust cluster](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#defc4-trust-and-trustworthiness). This subsection only explains the joint-operation duties.

A trustworthiness claim is non-compliant if one implementation file gives reassuring signals while another implementation file in the same chain leaves unresolved problems that defeat observable, verifiable, or contestable reliance.

For implementation routing, **Class A**, **Class B**, and **Class C** systems follow **[CS-9.305](../corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-9305-part-a-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [CS-9.310](../corpus_systems/cs_09.310_system_impact_classifications.md#cs-9310-part-b-system-impact-classifications)** (*System impact classifications*) and **[CS-15.305 — Critical system stewardship](../corpus_systems/cs_15.305_critical_system_stewardship.md#cs-15305-critical-system-stewardship)** for class-scaled trustworthiness assurance. **Class L** and **Class P** systems follow **[CS-9.305](../corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-9305-part-a-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [CS-9.310](../corpus_systems/cs_09.310_system_impact_classifications.md#cs-9310-part-b-system-impact-classifications)** (*System impact classifications*) criteria and limits, apply **Chapter Nine §3** (*Whole-System Certification Evaluation*) evaluation discipline at proportionate depth (**Class L**, mandatory) or as encouraged practice (**Class P**), and may not evade obligations where material external effects exist.

Where trust depends on multiple systems, institutions, dependencies, or implementation layers, institutions must maintain a published and auditable map that identifies:
- the implementation duties, classification conditions, dependencies, steward duties, and assurance burdens supporting the trust claim;

## CJS-19.110 §4 Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [Chapter One §17.6 Portable Competence](../core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#176-portable-competence) (1.010 §17.6) (*the principle this subsection carries out*).
- Read with: **CJS-19.110 §4** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy*); **CJS-5.105 §0** (*Cross-band: Role-definition preface and standing competency bar and clearance interface*).
- Topic routing (mandatory read-with): **CJS-R18** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy for materially bin…*) in
  - **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Topic router*); primary owners
  - **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*),
  - **CI-15.205** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*),
  - **CI-6.215** (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*),
  - **CI-14.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: high-impact roles cannot depend on one sentient. For materially binding constitutional lanes — especially under higher-risk system classes — institutions need enough qualified sentients, documented backup and succession, and real spread of knowledge so the lane stays competent without becoming fragile or captured.*

This rule covers roles with materially binding effect under **Chapter Six**, section **5**, where [**CS-9.305**](../corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md) (*System classification machinery*) or **CS-15.305 — Critical system stewardship** scales the burden. Router topic: **CJS-R18** in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Cross-file routing*).

Before using role, role-holder, stewardship, operator, accountable-role, or lane terms in this subsection, apply **CJS-5.105 §0** (*Cross-band: Role-definition preface and standing competency bar and clearance interface*), [Chapter Twelve §6.2 Competency bars and clearances](../core_12.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances) (*Competency bars and clearances*), and any controlling standing lock under [Chapter Twelve §4.2 Prevention — general standing locks](../core_12.000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks) or [§5.5 Special locks](../core_12.000_standing_integration.md#55-special-locks).

For **constitutional lane** and functional-separation meaning, apply **CJS-5.125 §11** (*Accountability: distributed and proportional authority terms*) with **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*).

For **Class A** and **Class B** systems, each institution that hosts a constitutional lane with materially binding duties must have:
- at least **three sentients** assigned to the lane;
- documented competency and succession coverage for that lane; and
- no single sentient who is the only qualified actor for the lane's materially binding duties.

Role-boundary design must balance sustained engagement with community redundancy. Role holders need enough recurring, bounded responsibility to develop and retain competence, while the lane keeps cross-training, backup coverage, and cross-functional familiarity sufficient for continuity, review, and succession.

Staffing must be real, not just numerical. Responsibility cannot be spread so thin that nobody is competent, and knowledge cannot be concentrated so tightly that the lane becomes fragile, captured, or unable to replace itself.

Apply [Chapter One §17.6 Portable Competence](../core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#176-portable-competence) to that coverage: an alternate counts only if it would not fail for the same reason as the steward it covers, and succession and handoffs must be exercised, with gaps recorded and corrected. A second copy of an AI system with the same flaw is not coverage. Cross-training does not authorize a sentient to check its own earlier work, bypass a competency bar, or obtain evidence beyond its lawful access. Rotation remains a tool, not a duty to move every steward through every role.

The constitutional floor remains in [Chapter Fifteen](../core_15.000_governance.md), section **5**. Domain detail remains in **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*), **CI-15.205** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*), **CI-6.215** (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*), **CI-14.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*), and [**CS-9.305**](../corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md) (*System classification machinery*) and **CS-15.305 — Critical system stewardship**.

## CJS-19.110 §5 Shared procedural abstractions for delegated bodies and forum routing

<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Topic routing (mandatory read-with): **CJS-R01** (*Delegated binding bodies and hybrid composition (non-forum institutions)*) in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Topic router*); primary owner **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*).
- Topic routing (mandatory read-with): **CJS-R02** (*Forum chambers, divisions, and designated panels (Chapter Fourteen fami…*) in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Topic router*); primary owners **CF-14.405 §5** (*Chamber creation, identification, and family boundary*), **CF-14.405 §8** (*Specialist and technical chamber discipline*).
- Topic routing (mandatory read-with): **CJS-R03** (*Lawful panel formation, disclosure, recusal, substitution, inability-to…*) in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Topic router*); primary owner **CF-14.410** (*Panel formation, disclosure, recusal, and lawful bench constitution*).
- Topic routing (mandatory read-with): **CJS-R04** (*Routing, intake, transfer, certification, representative treatment*) in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Topic router*); primary owner **CF-14.415** (*Routing operations, transfer, certification, and representative treatment*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: institutions and forums use different local rulebooks, but they need the same baseline words for delegated bodies, lawful independent forums, backup routing when a lead forum cannot act in time, and representative procedures that still preserve individual contest rights. **CJS-19.110 §5** (*Shared procedural abstractions for delegated bodies and forum routing*) states those shared terms; **CI** and **CF** state the full operative detail.*

These shared **CJS** terms apply wherever the following owner files apply, as routed in **CJS-0.105 §1** (*Cross-file routing*):

- **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*)
- **CF-14.405** (*Forum formation, forum-structure mapping, and chamber structure*)
- **CF-14.410** (*Panel formation, disclosure, recusal, and lawful bench constitution*)
- **CF-14.415** (*Routing operations, transfer, certification, and representative treatment*)

Apply the operational evaluation terms in **CJS-5.125 §13** (*Accountability: procedural integrity and adjudication terms*). This subsection does not replace **CI** or **CF** detail.

Before applying these procedural role terms, apply the role-definition preface in **CJS-5.105 §0** (*Cross-band: Role-definition preface and standing competency bar and clearance interface*), including the competency bar, clearance, and standing interface.

- **Delegated binding body:** a standing or recurring body below a parent institution that exercises materially binding delegated authority within a published scope.
- **Lawful independent forum:** an adjudicative or equivalent merits forum formed under published authority, with required competence, quorum, and conflict-screened independence.
- **Backup activation:** documented transfer or co-routing to a designated backup forum when the lead forum cannot provide lawful independent merits determination in time.
- **Representative treatment:** procedure resolving common questions for a broader affected group only when commonality, notice, adequate representation, and contestability are preserved.

For **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*), a **delegated subunit** is the institutional application of **Delegated binding body**: a standing or recurring internal body — a division, chamber, committee, designated panel, or regional or functional office with decision rights — that exercises materially binding delegated authority for the parent institution and is not the institution's sole governing plenary.

Forum-specific detail, including forum families, routing, appeals, and specialist chamber doctrine, remains in [**CF-14.405**](../corpus_forum/cf_14.405_forum_formation_chamber_structure.md) (*Forum formation, forum-structure mapping, and chamber structure*) and related **CF** sections. Where **CF-14.405 §6** (*Chamber authority composition and service mechanics*) states forum-specific delegated-authority duties or illustrations, **CF** text governs for **Chapter Fourteen** forums. Where **CI** and **CF** obligations overlap, the clearly adopted rule with the [Fullest Protective Effect](../core_05.045_band_integrative.md#fullest-protective-effect) governs under [Chapter One §15.4.3 Incorporation layer](../core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#1543-incorporation-layer) (*Incorporation layer*, including the cross-file Fullest Protective Effect rule).

It is non-compliant to treat a body or route as valid if the records do not make lawful authority, independence safeguards, backup routes, and contest routes auditable where they materially apply.

It is also non-compliant to use common-question procedures to suppress material sentient-specific contest rights required by owner rules.

Owner detail remains in the institutional family, including **CI-14.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*), **CI-6.205** (*Transparency, participation, and accessible challenge and service pathways*), **CI-9.210 §3** (*Delegated subunits, institutional design class, and attachment discipline*), and the forum family, including **CF-14.410** (*Panel formation, disclosure, recusal, and lawful bench constitution*), **CF-14.415** (*Routing operations, transfer, certification, and representative treatment*), and **CF-14.420** (*Appeal, secondary review, and exhaustion pathways*).
