## Purpose, Role, and Scope

**Purpose:** Make the shared audit process understandable across systems, institutions, and forums.

**Role:** This file states what auditing is, why and how it is done, when it runs, and how the sibling audit modes relate. The Rights Floor and the Auditability definition remain in their constitutional homes.

## CJS-3.115 §3 Audit Process Home (oDef.3)

### What Auditing Is

*In plain terms: auditing is an independent check of real behavior and evidence against what a system, institution, or forum claims — not a stamp, a popularity score, or a private club.*

**Auditing** is the practical process of checking whether constitutional and implementation claims match what actually happened: records, behavior, outputs, effects, and responsibilities. It answers: *Can an outsider reconstruct what mattered, test the claim, and push back if something is wrong?*

Auditing **is**:

- an independent or plural check against real evidence
- scaled to impact, dependency, and risk
- required under oversight — not optional window dressing
- able to produce usable outputs sentients can see or review

Auditing **is not**:

- a rubber stamp, marketing packet, or forever pass
- the same thing as [System Alignment Certification](../subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#system-alignment-certification) alone — SAC is one large sibling mode
- a single auditor, operator, or gatekeeper monopolizing review (**Article XVII-B** (*Distributed Oversight and Anti-Monopoly Review*))
- a substitute for challenge and remedy under **Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*) and **Article XIV-B** (*Right to Redress and Remedy*)
- satisfied by saying "audit happened" when outputs are missing, unreachable, or typed more restrictively than justified



### Why We Audit

*In plain terms: we audit so misalignment, concealment, and capture get caught before harm hardens — and so sentients can challenge and fix what went wrong.*

We audit to:

- catch **misalignment**, understatement of class or risk, concealment, and false reliance
- keep **oversight** real as systems, institutions, and forums grow more powerful or entangled
- support **challenge**, correction, and remedy before delay or opacity make accountability theoretical
- preserve **trust** that depends on checkable claims, not prestige or insider assurance

Depth and frequency scale with [material stake](../core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake), class, dependency, and risk. Higher stakes mean deeper checks, clearer outputs, and harder-to-capture review paths.



### How We Audit

*In plain terms: keep honest evidence, pick the right access level, get an independent check, publish usable results as openly as you can, and leave a path to challenge.*

A working audit process follows this chain:

1. **Preserve evidence:** Keep records sufficient to reconstruct material behavior, decisions, effects, and claims (see the [CJS-3.110 §3 Auditability and reconstructability terms (annex)](cjs_03.110_oversight_operations.md#cjs-3110-3-oversight-auditability-and-reconstructability-terms-annex); **Article XVII-A** (*Auditability and Observable Evidence*)).
2. **Choose the access path:** Use the access level that fits the data and the question:
   - **public baseline** where [Public Oversight Baseline Disclosure](../subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#public-oversight-baseline-disclosure) or other public duties apply (**Type O** under **Subcore 4-A** (*Data Types*))
   - **qualified / structured audit access** to non-public source (**Type G** or other non-public typing) when raw public release is inappropriate
   - **forensic depth** when harm, dispute, or credible risk requires full reconstruction
   - Access-level mechanics live in **[CJS-3.110 §4](cjs_03.110_oversight_operations.md#cjs-34-audit-process-output-disclosure) (*Oversight: Transparency Levels and Audit-Access Terms*)**; typing lives in **Subcore 4-A** (*Data Types*).
3. **Run an independent check:** Someone who is not captured by the operator or the claim under review must be able to test the evidence (**CJS-3.110 §5** (*independent verification and claim-integrity terms*); **Article XVII-B** (*Distributed Oversight and Anti-Monopoly Review*) / **XVII-C**).
4. **Publish usable outputs:** Findings, reports, eligibility rules for deeper access, and related artifacts must exist and remain reachable. Disclose them at the **most open feasible** level: **Type O** (including strongest feasible public substitute) where feasible; otherwise qualified **Type G** or other non-public source; forensic or more-restricted levels only as justified — see **[CJS-3.110 §4](cjs_03.110_oversight_operations.md#cjs-34-audit-process-output-disclosure) (*Oversight: Transparency Levels and Audit-Access Terms*)**.
5. **Leave challenge and correction open:** Audit supports contest under **Article XIV-A** (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*); it does not replace it. Material findings must be correctable and reviewable.

Privacy and internal-state protections (**Article VII-B** (*Self-Ownership of Mind*)) limit what may be exposed. They do **not** excuse hiding what was done, why it mattered, who or what was responsible, or what external effects followed.



### When We Audit

*In plain terms: audit when impact is material, when something important changes, on the schedule its class sets, and whenever harm or dispute requires reconstruction — not only when certification is due.*

Audit when any of the following applies (often more than one):

- **Class, impact, or dependency requires it** — including **Class A**, **Class B**, and **Class C** systems, and institution or forum functions that materially affect sentients
- **Material change** — capability, scope, behavior, dependency, risk, incentive structure, or governance changes enough that prior checks no longer describe reality
- **Scheduled audit** — for a system or Institution, at the audit midpoint of its class's certification interval ([Chapter Seventeen §5 Review cadence](../core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#review-cadence)); for any other subject, on a schedule scaled to its impact
- **Incident, dispute, or credible risk** — including escalation to forensic depth when reconstruction is needed
- **Continuous or automated monitoring** — where impact or criticality (including system class where assigned) requires ongoing detection (including systems-layer monitoring profiles such as CS-4.345 (*System lifecycle*) ACA), without treating monitoring alone as a complete audit
- **Certification or recertification cycles** — including System Alignment Certification, which is one trigger among others, not the only time auditing happens

"Where feasible" does **not** make required independent audit paths soft-optional for high-impact subjects. Eligibility rules for qualified independent or third-party paths must stay public and non-exclusive (**CJS-3.110 §4** (*Oversight: Transparency Levels and Audit-Access Terms*)).
