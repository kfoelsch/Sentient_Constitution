*In plain terms: every time limit in the corpus is named by what kind of time it is, and the last word of the name says which kind. This section lists those names and where each one is set. It sets no length of time and starts no clock.*

**What this list is**

- **A list of names, not of rules:** The owner file sets the length, the start, the pause, and the end of each window. If this list and an owner file differ, the owner file governs and this list is corrected.
- **No second clock table:** [Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) says the corpus adds no second clock table. This list therefore carries no numbers. Open the owner to read the length.
- **One name, one window:** A name in the list below means the window its owner sets and no other. A new window takes a name that ends in one of the kind words, and is added here with its owner.

**The kind words**

| Last word | Kind of time |
|---|---|
| **window** | Time allowed to act, counted from a trigger |
| **target** | A non-binding stage goal inside a window |
| **bound** | The longest a measure may last; it lapses on its own |
| **term** | The fixed life of an order, office, or grant |
| **interval** | The length between recurring checks |
| **period** | Time during which a rule applies or something is watched |
| **span** | The time a Standing Record covers |

**Five usage rules**

- **Clock is not a name:** It describes how a window runs (when it starts, keeps running, or stalls) and appears in plain idiom such as "the applicable clock".
- **Cadence is not a length:** *Review cadence* is the name of the [Chapter Twenty-Three rule](../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#review-cadence) that sets the intervals below. It is not itself an interval.
- **Promptly is not a length:** Where owner text says "promptly" and names no window, it states a duty of diligence. It implies no tier, setting, or window, and it does not excuse delay. Where the duty is review of a measure in force, intake, or notice, the owner text names the window instead (see *Windows with no length* below). Record duties that ask for an Act Record "at the time of the act" follow [Chapter Two §16.4 Document at Action](../core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#164-document-at-action), which is a design default for records and not a length.
- **Declaration window means the NU-1 incident declaration only:** The enforceability declaration under [Article XXIX-A](../core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) is not a declaration window. It is bounded directly by the outer bound of a Tier, as [CI-4.275 §4.4](../corpus_institutions/ci_04.275_adoption_transition_governance_institutional_evolution.md#ci-4275-44-enforcement-readiness-declarations) (*Enforcement-readiness declarations*) states.
- **Response window means the Chartered Party's window to answer:** It answers a request, a notice, a failed finding, or a classification challenge, and it takes the length of the matching tier's intake target. It is never the time for reviewing a hold or an order. The time to begin that review is the **prompt-review window**, and the time to finish a matter is the matching **tier window**.

| **Good-faith extension** | window | One extension of the resolution window on a showing of good-faith progress and no continuing harm | CI-18.210 §3 (*Notice, Response, and Resolution*) |

Both windows take the Tier that matches the Chartered Party's class: Class A, B, C, or L takes Tier A, B, C, or L, and a class in question counts at the Tier of the more critical of the class the Chartered Party holds on its System Classification Record and any class shown on a prima facie basis, until it is settled, or at Tier C where it holds none or holds Class P. Class P takes Tier P where **CI-18.210 §3** (*Notice, Response, and Resolution*) applies to it.

The two extensions are different rules in different owners. Neither stands in for the other.

**Urgency scales.** Four scales set how fast something moves: the tier labels A, B, C, L, and P ([Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline)); the urgency categories of [CF-22.445 §4.3](../corpus_forum/cf_22.445_performance_backlog_publication_accessibility.md#cf-22445-43-minimum-urgency-categories) (*Minimum urgency categories*), which [CF-22.445](../corpus_forum/cf_22.445_performance_backlog_publication_accessibility.md#cf-22445-forum-performance-backlog-requirements-publication-timeliness-and-accessibility) (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*) maps to tiers; the notice urgency categories (NU) in [CJS-3.110 §6](cjs_03.110_oversight_operations.md#incident-and-assurance-notification-urgency-nu) (*Oversight: Integrity Assurance and Resilience Operations*); and the correction urgency categories (CU) in [CJS-3.125 §15](cjs_03.125_accountability_operations.md#correction-urgency-cu-discipline) (*Accountability: Structural Review, Correction Urgency, and Disclosure Terms*). The correction and notice scales now borrow the tier columns. **CU-1** to **CU-4** take the columns of Tiers A, B, C, and L, and **CU-1** runs at the Emergency setting where its conditions are met ([CJS-3.125 §15](cjs_03.125_accountability_operations.md#correction-urgency-cu-discipline) (*Accountability: Structural Review, Correction Urgency, and Disclosure Terms*)). **NU-1** to **NU-3** take Tiers A, B, and C, and **NU-4** is a routine attestation interval with no tier ([CJS-3.110 §6](cjs_03.110_oversight_operations.md#incident-and-assurance-notification-urgency-nu) (*Oversight: Integrity Assurance and Resilience Operations*)). Each borrows a column's figures and none is a tier classification. The urgency categories of [CF-22.445 §4.3](../corpus_forum/cf_22.445_performance_backlog_publication_accessibility.md#cf-22445-43-minimum-urgency-categories) (*Minimum urgency categories*) keep their own mapping to tiers.

**Windows with no length.** Some owner text still says "promptly" or "reasonable" and sets no length. [Chapter Twenty-Three §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](../core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) says "as soon as feasible" is not a clock.

Where the duty is review of a measure already in force, it runs inside the **prompt-review window**. Where it is intake or evidence preservation, it runs inside the **intake target**. Where it is notice, notice comes with the measure or inside the **restore-challenge window**. Where it is a duty to update a classification or data-types record, the update is due inside the **intake target** of the system's class, counted from when changed conditions are recognized, and the System Certification Record is also checked on the system's **certification interval**.

Where owner text says a "reasonable" period or timeframe, it means **14 days**, the Tier B outer bound (the forum response time for a Tier B matter), whatever class or stewardship level the operator or system holds, in three uses: data deletion in **Article II-E** (*Data Handling and Network Dependency*), the recovery property of a Class C steward in CS-26.305 (*Critical system stewardship*), and the Class L intervention timeframe in CS-8.305 (*System classification machinery*) and Subcore 8-A (*System Classification*). The sandbox step-up in **Article XIX-C** (*Step-Up to Higher-Obligation Regimes*) and CS-4.345 (*System lifecycle*), and the reauthorization-transition start in CS-4.335 (*Resource allocation and funding stewardship*), take the outer bound of the Tier that matches the system's class (7 days for Class A, 14 for B, 30 for C, 60 for L, 90 for P), and 14 days where the system holds no class. The Review and Correction Duty runs on the tier-appropriate windows.

Three uses stay open on purpose, each for a stated reason: the Timeliness aim in **Article V** (*Resource Allocation, Dependencies, and Ecosystem Funding*) states a principle and sets no deadline; the prompt verified remediation that may support **Diligence** in Chapter Fourteen is a standing factor and not a deadline; and the good-faith drafting carve-out in Chapter Twenty is already bounded by "before constitutional effect", so a number would widen or narrow a defence to misconduct. Under the usage rule above, none of them implies a tier, setting, or window, and none excuses delay.
