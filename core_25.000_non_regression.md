Chapter Twenty-Five is the constitutional owner of **substantive non-regression and substantive amendment validity** (Substantive Test 1), anti-evasion controls, and validity-protection measures.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: a change that weakens the protections this Constitution depends on is invalid, even if the words say otherwise and even if it is done through procedure, definitions, guidance, a temporary measure, or an emergency label. This chapter is Substantive Test 1.*

**Purpose:** The purpose of Chapter Twenty-Five is to prevent regressive changes to the Constitution. It is a guardrail against alterations that degrade the core constraints, Rights Floors, integrity and verification requirements, and legitimacy and stewardship obligations the rest of the Constitution depends on. No constitutional change is valid if it violates these non-regression requirements.

**Role:** The chapter is the substantive half of change validity. Four tests judge a change: Substantive Test 1 and Procedural Tests 2 to 4. This chapter owns Substantive Test 1:

- **Substantive Test 1** (this chapter), asks what a change actually does, not what it is called, so relabeling a rollback as guidance or a temporary measure does not save it. Amendment validity must also preserve both aims, Flourishing and Continuity, and must not hollow the Tetrad below material stake.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- the non-regression floor (**section 2**);
- Substantive Test 1 (**section 3**);
- the anti-evasion clause (**section 4**); and
- the validity-protection controls the chapter may impose and the owner non-relocation rule (**section 5**).

The chapter leaves:

- numeric standing measurement to [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#chapter-sixteen-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it);
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designation to [Chapter Twenty-One](core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-one-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation);
- Procedural Tests 2 to 4, ratification, and the referral of serious bad-faith conduct to [Chapter Twenty-Six](core_26.000_amendment_ratification.md#chapter-twenty-six-amendment-ratification-and-procedural-validity):
  - **Procedural Test 2:** procedural validity and publication integrity;
  - **Procedural Test 3:** authority-chain and custody validity; and
  - **Procedural Test 4:** contestability and independent review validity; and
- additive expansion of protection and the sequencing of supremacy to [Chapter Twenty-Seven](core_27.000_expansion_supremacy.md#chapter-twenty-seven-expansion-supremacy-and-external-legal-orders).

<br>

*In plain terms: weakening **Safety (Constitutional Constraint)**, **Truth (Constitutional Constraint)**, **Epistemic Integrity**, **Dignity and Equal Moral Standing**, **Meaningful Agency**, or other Chapter Six protections — including by tightening definitions, raising impossible proof bars, or carving emergencies so they eat the rule — counts as invalid even if the words "amendment" or "interpretation" are on the label.*

No constitutional change is valid if it materially weakens core protections for any covered sentient class. Core protections include:
- Safety (Constitutional Constraint);
- Truth (Constitutional Constraint);
- Epistemic Integrity;
- Dignity and Equal Moral Standing; and
- Meaningful Agency and other Rights Floor protections stated in Chapter Six.

Material weakening includes indirect narrowing through:
- definitions;
- procedures;
- standing gates;
- verification barriers; or
- emergency carve-outs.

<br>

*In plain terms: Substantive Test 1 is the checklist — if a proposal would gut Chapter One constraints, Chapters Two through Four integrity, Chapter Six Rights Floors, or Chapter Twenty-Four legitimacy, it fails, including when the damage is done through procedure, evidence rules, or re-tagging rather than a single blunt deletion.*

A proposed amendment is invalid if it materially weakens any of the following:
- Chapter One non-negotiable principle constraints
- **Chapters Two through Four** integrity requirements
- Chapter Six Rights Floors
- Chapter Twenty-Four legitimacy obligations

Invalidity includes indirect narrowing through:
- definitions;
- standing gates;
- evidentiary burden manipulation;
- observability degradation; or
- emergency re-labeling.

### 4. Anti-Evasion Clause
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§2](#2-non-regression-floor)–[§3](#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) substantive invalidity standards.

<br>

*In plain terms: calling a rollback "implementation guidance," burying it in metadata, or calling it an emergency patch does not save it if the real-world effect is the same as a bad amendment.*

Repackaging a regressive change as any of the following does not fix invalidity:
- interpretation guidance;
- implementation memo;
- adoption metadata; or
- emergency temporary measure.

Invalidity remains where substantive constitutional effect is materially equivalent.

Serious bad-faith conduct in amendment or adoption is referred under [Chapter Twenty-Six §10](core_26.000_amendment_ratification.md#10-invalid-change-handling-and-remediation-continuity) (*Invalid-Change Handling and Remediation Continuity*), not handled here.

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Five can demand extra review, preserve evidence, or pause things when validity is in doubt — but it does not run parallel classification or invent new offense categories; numeric standing measurement belongs to the Chapter Sixteen §7 (*Unified Proportional LEQU Scale — Contribution and Violation Axes*) unified scale, and Chapter Twenty-One only adds the corresponding anti-constitutional-misconduct designation to qualifying Violation Axis slots 7–9.*

This chapter may impose immediate validity-protection controls. Those controls include:
- heightened review;
- provisional suspension where materially necessary;
- [Evidence Preservation](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation); and
- remediation publication.

Under [Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#owner-non-relocation), this chapter must not absorb, restate, or relocate obligations owned by another constitutional owner layer — including final offense classification, sanction taxonomy, standing measurement, adjudicative workflow, or procedural amendment validity owned by Chapters Sixteen, Thirteen, Fourteen, and Eighteen respectively. Slot-flavored or classification-flavored language in this chapter or in **Chapter Twenty-Six** operates only as a **trigger or referral heuristic** for routing to canonical owners and is **non-canonical** for final offense classification.

Cross-layer drift that imports adjudicative workflow, sanction mechanics, or classification substance from another owner layer into **Chapter Twenty-Five** or **Chapter Twenty-Six** — whether as direct text, as reader-guidance prose elevated to operative effect, or as inference from review-trigger language — is **non-compliant** under this section.

Future additions that touch offense-flavored, sanction-flavored, or classification-flavored language must include an explicit non-relocation pointer to the canonical owner home in the [Preamble — constitutional owner register](core_00.000_preamble.md#4-principles-definitions-and-rights), read with [Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#owner-non-relocation).
