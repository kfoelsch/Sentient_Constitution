<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Four says that only an assembly may set a tax. This file says what a fair, simple tax must look like, which tax designs are good and which are barred, how taxes are collected and enforced, and what anyone who owes tax may rely on.*

**Purpose:** Subcore 24-A is the constitutional owner of the **standard detail of taxation**: the tests every tax must pass, the preferred and barred tax designs, the ease-of-use duties, the enforcement rules, the rights of those who owe tax, how tax rules are made and changed, and the outcome-review measures.

**Role:** [Chapter Twenty-Four §3 What Assemblies Decide](core_24.000_assemblies.md#3-what-assemblies-decide) states the guarantees: no tax is levied except by an assembly's general rule, and each tier taxes only within its matters under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Dividing Work Between Tiers](core_24.000_assemblies.md#4-dividing-work-between-tiers). This file says how that power is used. It does not set any adopter's tax bases or rates. Each assembly chooses those, within the rules below.

**Scope:** This file applies to every tax, duty, levy, or compulsory charge that raises general revenue for a polity adopter, at every tier. A user fee that only pays for a service the payer chooses to use is governed by [CI-4.210](corpus_institutions/ci_04.210_public_revenue_fees_recurring_charges_billing_integrity.md#ci-4210-public-revenue-fees-recurring-charges-and-billing-integrity) (*Public Revenue, Fees, Recurring Charges, and Billing Integrity*) instead. A charge that raises more than the cost of the service it pays for is a tax and must meet this file. **Taxpayer** in this file means any sentient or institution that owes, pays, or collects a tax.

<a id="2-why-the-tax-rules-are-the-same-for-every-adopter"></a>

### 2. Why the Tax Rules Are the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: taxes reach almost everyone, are backed by force, and are easy to bend toward whoever writes them. A shared floor keeps every tax fair, readable, and contestable, whatever rates an adopter picks.*

A tax is the most common way a polity takes something from the sentients it serves without their individual consent. Experience across many tax systems shows the same failures again and again: codes too complex to follow without paid help, special breaks won by those with access, retroactive changes, collectors paid by what they collect, and enforcement that falls hardest on those least able to resist it. Each of these is a choice an assembly could make under pressure. Fixed tests, preferred designs, and barred designs keep the guarantee the same for every adopter, while leaving every assembly free to choose what to tax and how much.

**Grounded in:**

<a id="3-tests-every-tax-must-pass"></a>

### 3. Tests Every Tax Must Pass

*In plain terms: before an assembly enacts or keeps a tax, it must be able to say yes to each of these questions, in writing.*

Every tax, and every change to a tax, must pass these tests. The assembly states in its [Act Record](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#act-record) how the tax passes each one:

- **Lawful and open:**
  - **Lawful:** the tax is enacted by the assembly's general rule, within the tier's matters, and states its base, its rates, who owes it, and when.
  - **Visible:** taxpayers can see the tax and know what they paid. No tax is hidden inside another charge.
- **Fair:**
  - **Between equals:** taxpayers in the same economic position pay the same tax. Two ways of earning, holding, or spending the same amount are taxed alike unless the assembly states a reason tied to the tax's purpose.
  - **By ability to pay:** the tax system taken as a whole asks more of those with more. A tax that falls harder on those with less is allowed only as part of a system that offsets it, and the assembly publishes the combined effect.
- **Easy to use:**
  - **Simple:** an ordinary taxpayer can learn from a plain-language summary what is taxed, at what rate, and what they must do.
  - **Certain:** the amount owed, the due date, and the way to pay can be worked out in advance from published rules, without needing an official's discretion.
  - **Convenient:** the tax is collected at a time and in a way that suits those who pay it, such as at the time of payment or receipt.
- **Efficient:**
  - **Cheap to run:** the cost of collecting the tax and the cost to taxpayers of complying are small compared with what it raises. Both are estimated and published.
  - **Neutral:** the tax does not steer choices about work, saving, investment, or how to organize a business, except where steering is the tax's stated purpose under [§4.2 Taxes on Harm](#42-taxes-on-harm).
- **Dependable:**
  - **Adequate and stable:** the tax system as a whole raises what the budgets adopted under [Chapter Twenty-Four §3 What Assemblies Decide](core_24.000_assemblies.md#3-what-assemblies-decide) need, without frequent changes.
  - **Enforceable:** the tax can be enforced evenly with the information the revenue office can lawfully obtain, so honest taxpayers are not left to carry those who evade.

<a id="4-good-tax-policies"></a>

### 4. Good Tax Policies

*In plain terms: these are the designs the evidence favors. An assembly should use them, and must explain in writing when it does not.*

<a id="41-preferred-designs"></a>

#### 4.1 Preferred Designs

An assembly uses each design below unless it states in its Act Record why the design does not fit, and what it uses instead:

- **Bases and rates:**
  - **Broad bases and low rates:** tax wide bases with few exclusions, so rates can stay low and the burden is widely shared.
  - **Few rates:** each tax has as few rates and brackets as its purpose allows. **Default:** no more than four rates for any one tax.
  - **Taxes on land value:** where land is taxed, the tax falls on the value of the location rather than on buildings and improvements, so it does not discourage building or repair.
- **Protecting those with less:**
  - **A tax-free allowance:** a tax on income or on sentients exempts an amount at least equal to what a sentient needs to meet the [Article III-A](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-iii-a-survival) (*Survival*) floor where the taxpayer lives.
  - **Offset for consumption taxes:** a broad tax on sales or consumption is paired with a credit, rebate, or exemption that returns at least its cost on survival needs to those with the lowest incomes.
- **Doing the work for taxpayers:**
  - **Collection at the source:** tax is withheld or collected where money changes hands (wages, interest, dividends, sales), by the payer, rather than left to each taxpayer to calculate.
  - **Reporting by third parties:** those who pay income to others, such as employers, banks, and platforms, report it to the revenue office and to the recipient.
  - **Pre-filled returns:** where the revenue office already holds the information, it prepares the return and the taxpayer checks, corrects, or confirms it.
  - **One place to deal with:** each taxpayer can register, file, pay, and contest every tax of every tier through one service, where tiers share a base or a collection channel.
- **Simple and stable rules:**
  - **One definition, used everywhere:** a term such as income, residence, or a business is defined once for the polity and used by every tax and every tier that taxes that base.
  - **Simple rules for small taxpayers:** small businesses and occasional sellers may use a simplified method, such as a tax on turnover below a published threshold, instead of full accounts.
  - **Indexing:** allowances, brackets, and fixed amounts are adjusted each year by a published measure of prices, so inflation does not raise taxes without a vote.
  - **Stable rules:** tax rules change only on a published schedule, with the notice in [§8 Making and Changing Tax Rules](#8-making-and-changing-tax-rules).

<a id="42-taxes-on-harm"></a>

#### 4.2 Taxes on Harm

A tax may be used on purpose to make those who cause a measurable harm to others pay its cost, such as pollution, congestion, or depletion of a shared resource. Such a tax must:

- name the harm it prices and the published measure of it;
- set the rate by the estimated cost of the harm, not by the revenue wanted;
- apply to every source of the same harm alike, whoever causes it;
- publish each year what it raised and what happened to the harm; and
- say how its effect on those with the lowest incomes is offset.

<a id="43-tax-breaks-under-review"></a>

#### 4.3 Tax Breaks Under Review

An exemption, deduction, credit, reduced rate, or deferral that favors some taxpayers or activities over others is a **tax break**, and is spending through the tax system. Every tax break must:

- be enacted by the assembly's general rule, like a tax;
- state its purpose and how success will be measured;
- appear, with its estimated yearly cost and who benefits by income band, in a **tax-break report** published with each budget;
- lapse at the end of its **tax-break lapse period** unless renewed. **Default:** the tax-break lapse period is five years; and
- be renewed only after a published review shows it met its purpose at a reasonable cost.

<a id="5-barred-tax-policies"></a>

### 5. Barred Tax Policies

*In plain terms: some tax designs are never allowed, and others are allowed only with strong written reasons and an independent check.*

<a id="51-never-allowed"></a>

#### 5.1 Never Allowed

No assembly, executive, or revenue office may:

- **Acting without authority:**
  - **Tax without a general rule:** levy a tax, or set or change a rate, base, or tax break, other than by the assembly's general rule. An executive may not be given power to set rates.
  - **Tax outside its tier:** tax a matter outside the tier's published matters under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Dividing Work Between Tiers](core_24.000_assemblies.md#4-dividing-work-between-tiers).
  - **Tax in an emergency without a rule:** an emergency under [Chapter Twenty-Four §8 Emergencies](core_24.000_assemblies.md#8-emergencies) does not unlock any power to tax.
  - **Tax backward:** impose a new tax, a higher rate, or a narrower tax break on income, transactions, or holdings from before the rule was enacted. A change that only lowers tax may apply backward.
- **Harming what is protected:**
  - **Tax away survival:** levy or collect tax in a way that leaves a sentient below the [Article III-A](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-iii-a-survival) (*Survival*) floor.
  - **Tax a right:** tax voting, standing for office, petitioning, worship, speech, peaceful assembly, or bringing a case to a forum, or make any of them depend on having paid tax.
  - **Tax by identity:** set a tax, rate, or tax break by a characteristic protected under [Article VI-C](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-c-nondiscrimination) (*Nondiscrimination*), including [Substrate Class](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#substrate-class), or by viewpoint or political affiliation.
  - **Create a cliff past one hundred percent:** design taxes and benefit withdrawals so that earning one more unit leaves a sentient worse off than before.
- **Hiding or bending the system:**
  - **Hide a tax:** raise general revenue through a fee, fine, charge, or price that is not enacted and reported as a tax.
  - **Make secret deals:** agree a tax treatment with one taxpayer that is not published. A ruling given to one taxpayer is published within a set time, with identifying details removed, and applies to every taxpayer in the same position.
  - **Pay collectors by results:** pay, promote, rate, or reward any official, contractor, or system by the amount of tax assessed, collected, or penalized, or set collection quotas. This includes private tax collection on a share of what is collected.

<a id="52-allowed-only-with-reasons-and-review"></a>

#### 5.2 Allowed Only with Reasons and Review

The designs below are disfavored. An assembly may use one only if its Act Record states why no preferred design under [§4 Good Tax Policies](#4-good-tax-policies) would serve, and an independent seat reviews and publishes an opinion on those reasons before the vote:

- **Breaks for the few:**
  - **Narrow breaks:** a tax break that benefits a single taxpayer, a named industry, or a small group.
  - **Tax breaks without a lapse date:** a tax break with no end date or no measure of success.
  - **Amnesties:** forgiving penalties or tax for those who did not comply. No more than one amnesty may be offered in any **amnesty interval**, which is ten years, and those who used an earlier one may not use another.
- **Burdens that pile up:**
  - **High combined rates:** a design in which a sentient loses more than seventy percent of an extra unit earned to taxes and benefit withdrawals combined, at any income.
  - **Head taxes:** a fixed tax per sentient or per household, regardless of means.
  - **Cascading taxes:** a tax on gross turnover at each stage of production, so that tax is charged on tax, except the simplified small-taxpayer method of [§4.1 Preferred Designs](#41-preferred-designs).
  - **Double taxation:** taxing the same income or transaction twice, across taxes or across tiers, without a credit for the tax already paid.
- **Taxes hard to see or use:**
  - **Temporary taxes made permanent:** renewing a tax enacted as temporary.
  - **Earmarking that hides choices:** tying a tax's revenue to one use in a way that keeps the assembly from weighing that use against others in the budget.
  - **Complexity that needs paid help:** a tax that an ordinary taxpayer cannot reasonably file without paying an adviser.

<a id="6-ease-of-use"></a>

### 6. Ease of Use

*In plain terms: paying tax should be easy, free, and understandable, in your own language and in a form you can use.*

Every tier that taxes, and the revenue office that collects for it, must meet these duties:

- **Clear rules:**
  - **Plain language:** each tax has a plain-language summary, published with the rule, that says who owes it, on what, at what rate, and what to do.
  - **One code:** each tier keeps its tax rules in one published, consolidated, searchable code, with every change dated.
  - **Binding answers:** written guidance from the revenue office binds the office in the taxpayer's favor where the taxpayer relied on it in good faith.
- **Free and easy filing:**
  - **Free filing:** every taxpayer can file and pay for free, through the revenue office's own service, without buying software or advice.
  - **Pre-filled where possible:** a sentient whose income is all reported by third parties receives a prepared return to confirm. **Default target:** confirming takes under thirty minutes.
  - **Free help:** free help is available face to face and remotely, in the languages and formats required by [Article VI-D](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-d-accessibility) (*Accessibility*).
- **Fair payment and follow-up:**
  - **Payment plans:** a taxpayer who cannot pay at once may pay by instalments on published terms.
  - **Prompt refunds:** overpaid tax is refunded within a published time, with interest at the same rate the office charges on late tax.
  - **Measured burden:** the revenue office measures and publishes each year the time and cost of complying, by kind of taxpayer.

<a id="7-enforcement"></a>

### 7. Enforcement

*In plain terms: most sentients pay when paying is easy and others are seen to pay too. Enforcement starts with help, rises only as far as needed, treats rich and poor alike, and can always be challenged.*

<a id="71-a-graduated-response"></a>

#### 7.1 A Graduated Response

The revenue office answers non-compliance with the lightest step that will work, and moves up only when that fails:

- **Inform and assist:** reminders, clear notices, and help to correct.
- **Correct:** an amended assessment with interest and no penalty, for an honest mistake corrected when found.
- **Penalize:** a civil penalty, from a published schedule, for carelessness or for deliberate non-compliance.
- **Prosecute:** referral for prosecution only for deliberate fraud or concealment, under [Chapter Twenty-Five](core_25.000_enforcement.md#chapter-twenty-five-enforcement) and the forums.

<a id="72-penalties-and-interest"></a>

#### 7.2 Penalties and Interest

Penalties and interest follow these rules:

- **Proportionate:** a penalty fits the fault and the amount. **Default ceilings:** no penalty for an honest mistake; up to twenty percent of the shortfall for carelessness; up to seventy-five percent for deliberate concealment.
- **Reduced for coming forward:** a taxpayer who discloses a shortfall before the office finds it pays a reduced penalty or none.
- **Interest is not a penalty:** interest on late tax is set by a published measure of the cost of money, not to punish.
- **Same for all:** the schedule applies alike to sentients and institutions of every size.

<a id="73-fair-and-even-enforcement"></a>

#### 7.3 Fair and Even Enforcement

The revenue office enforces evenly by these rules:

- **Choosing whom to audit:**
  - **Published selection:** returns are chosen for audit by published risk criteria and by random sample, never by the taxpayer's viewpoint, affiliation, or protected characteristic, and never at the request of an official with a personal or political interest.
  - **No lighter touch at the top:** the audit rate for the highest-income tenth of taxpayers, and for the largest institutions, is no lower than the audit rate for those claiming tax breaks meant for low incomes.
- **Closing gaps:**
  - **Substance over form:** a published general anti-avoidance rule lets the office tax an arrangement by what it actually does where its main purpose is to avoid tax against the rule's intent. A taxpayer may ask in advance whether the rule applies.
  - **Institutions answer for their systems:** an institution is responsible for the tax effects of the systems it runs, including automated and high-autonomy systems, as for its own acts.
- **Finding evasion:**
  - **Cooperation across borders:** a polity shares tax information with other adopters and other polities only under published agreements that protect confidentiality.
  - **Reports from insiders:** anyone may report evasion, and is protected from retaliation for doing so in good faith.

<a id="74-limits-on-collection"></a>

#### 7.4 Limits on Collection

Collection is limited by these rules:

- **Time limit:** once a return is filed, the revenue office may go back and change the tax owed for that period (reassess it) only within the **reassessment period**, counted from the date the return was filed. After that, the amount is final. **Defaults:**
  - **Ordinary returns:** the reassessment period is three years.
  - **Large understatements:** the reassessment period is six years where the return left out more than a quarter of the tax actually owed. For example, if the correct tax was 1,000 and the return showed less than 750, the six-year reassessment period applies.
  - **Fraud or no return:** there is no time limit where the taxpayer committed fraud or never filed a return, because the office could not have known what to check.
- **Survival is protected:** collection never takes what a sentient needs to meet the [Article III-A](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#article-iii-a-survival) (*Survival*) floor, including a basic home, food, medical needs, and the tools of the taxpayer's work up to a published value.
- **Contest before paying:** while a first independent appeal is pending, collection of the disputed amount is paused, unless a forum finds a real risk that assets will be moved out of reach, in which case it may require security.
- **Due process for force:** seizing property or freezing accounts needs prior written notice and a chance to be heard, except where a forum authorizes it to prevent assets being moved out of reach. Any freeze or seizure also meets [Chapter Twenty-Six §4 Money, Assets, and Seizure](core_26.000_special_enforcement.md#4-money-assets-and-seizure).

<a id="75-separate-seats"></a>

#### 7.5 Separate Seats

The work of taxation is split under [Chapter Six](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties):

- the assembly sets the rules;
- the revenue office assesses and collects;
- a **taxpayer advocate**, independent of the revenue office, helps taxpayers with problems the office has not resolved and reports each year to the assembly on the most serious ones; and
- appeals go to a forum under [Chapter Twenty-Two](core_22.000_forum.md#chapter-twenty-two-standards-routing-and-forum-cases), never to the official who made the assessment.

<a id="8-making-and-changing-tax-rules"></a>

### 8. Making and Changing Tax Rules

*In plain terms: tax changes are announced ahead of time, costed, and shown by who gains and who pays, before anyone votes.*

A rule that creates, raises, or changes a tax or a tax break follows these steps:

- **Published draft:** the draft rule, its plain-language summary, and the statements below are published before the vote, for a public comment period.
- **Statements with the draft:** the draft states how the tax passes each test in [§3 Tests Every Tax Must Pass](#3-tests-every-tax-must-pass), the expected revenue, the cost of collection and of complying, and the effect on taxpayers by income band.
- **Notice before effect:** a new tax, a higher rate, or a narrower tax break takes effect no earlier than the start of the first tax period that begins after the **tax notice period**, which is ninety days from enactment.
- **Recorded vote:** the vote is recorded in the Act Record under [Chapter Twenty-Four §5 How Assemblies Decide](core_24.000_assemblies.md#5-how-assemblies-decide).
- **Coordinated tiers:** where more than one tier taxes the same base, they use the same definitions and, where feasible, one return and one collection channel, and each tier credits tax paid to another on the same income or transaction where double taxation would otherwise result.
- **Contestable:** any taxpayer may challenge a tax rule in a forum as inconsistent with this file or with this Constitution, under [Chapter Twenty-Four §9 Limits on Every Assembly](core_24.000_assemblies.md#9-limits-on-every-assembly).

<a id="9-taxpayer-rights"></a>

### 9. Taxpayer Rights

*In plain terms: whoever owes tax is entitled to be told, helped, heard, kept private, and charged only what the law says.*

Every taxpayer has these rights, which the revenue office publishes in plain language and keeps:

- **To be informed:** to know what the law requires, why the office has made a decision, and how to challenge it.
- **To pay no more than is owed:** to pay only the correct amount, and to have overpayments refunded within the published time under [§6 Ease of Use](#6-ease-of-use).
- **To be heard:** to give their side before the office decides against them, and to have it considered.
- **To appeal:** to an independent appeal in a forum, at no fee that would prevent a sentient from bringing it.
- **To finality:** to know that a period, once past the time limit in [§7.4 Limits on Collection](#74-limits-on-collection), will not be reopened.
- **To privacy:** to have their tax information used only for tax and for purposes a general rule names, kept confidential, and shown to no official without a need to see it. Misuse is misconduct.
- **To representation:** to be represented or assisted by anyone they choose.
- **To courtesy and a fair hearing:** to be treated as honest unless there is evidence otherwise.

<a id="10-outcome-review-measures"></a>

### 10. Outcome Review Measures

*In plain terms: every year the revenue office reports whether the tax system is fair, simple, and well enforced, and the assembly fixes what the numbers show is broken.*

Each year, the revenue office of each tier publishes:

- **The tax gap:** the estimated difference between tax owed and tax paid, by tax and by kind of taxpayer.
- **Who pays:** the share of all taxes paid by each income band, counting every tier.
- **The cost of complying:** the time and money taxpayers spend, and the share of ordinary taxpayers who file without paid help.
- **Audit and penalty rates:** by income band and size of institution.
- **Appeals:** how many assessments were appealed, and how many were changed on appeal.
- **Refunds:** the time taken to pay refunds.
- **The tax-break report:** under [§4.3 Tax Breaks Under Review](#43-tax-breaks-under-review).

At each periodic revalidation under [Article XXVIII-A](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxviii-a-non-entrenchment-and-revisability) (*Non-Entrenchment and Revisability*), the assembly reviews these figures. A rising tax gap, a rising cost of complying, a fall in the share filing without paid help, a lighter audit rate at the top, or a high rate of assessments changed on appeal is evidence that the rules need changing, and the assembly must say in its Act Record what it will change.
