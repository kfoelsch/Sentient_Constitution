### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 21-A is the constitutional owner of the **governance disclosure report**: the standard report every institution publishes on a fixed schedule, and the fields it must contain at minimum. How often an institution publishes, and in what format, stay in the institutions corpus.

*In plain terms: every institution publishes the same kind of governance report, so anyone can compare one institution with another and with its own past reports.*

### 2. Why Every Institution Reports the Same Way

*In plain terms: a report that each institution designs for itself tends to show what flatters it. A fixed list of contents makes the uncomfortable parts appear too.*

Governing power is legitimate only while those it affects can see how it is used. If each institution chose what to report, the incidents, conflicts, and adverse findings would be the first things left out. A required minimum, the same for every adopter, means sentients and reviewers can find the same facts in every report and notice when one is missing.

### 3. Governance Disclosure Report

*In plain terms: every institution must publish a standard governance report on a fixed schedule — not a marketing brochure, but a checklist covering what it oversees, what went wrong, what it is fixing, who had conflicts, and what outside reviewers or supervisors found.*

Each institution publishes a standard governance report on a fixed schedule. At minimum, the report contains:

- institutional scope and class/stewardship-level coverage;
- a summary of the institution's risk profile;
- its declaration of material controls;
- material incidents and their current status;
- conflict and related-party disclosures;
- remediation actions and whether each is complete;
- the outcomes of external assurance; and
- sanctions and supervisory actions.
