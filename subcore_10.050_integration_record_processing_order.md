### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 10-E is the constitutional owner of the **mechanics of standing integration**: the fields each Standing Record brings to a Question 3 decision, the full contents of the integration record, the eight steps of the processing order, and the manual continuity process during an outage. [Subcore 10-F](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#subcore-10-f-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows) states each guarantee in one line; where a line there and the text here seem to differ, the reading more protective of the affected sentient controls.

*In plain terms: Chapter Ten says what a Question 3 decision may and may not do. This file lists exactly what goes into the decision, what the integration record must show, and the order of the steps, so that every adopter runs the decision the same way.*

### 2. Why the Mechanics Are the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: if adopters could change the order of the steps or leave fields off the integration record, a lock could be skipped or a contribution used to buy down a harm, and no one could tell.*

The guarantees of Chapter Ten depend on the order in which the steps run and on what the integration record shows. Deciding contribution after locks, or letting a missing field hide which Standing Record a result came from, would let an adopter reach a preferred result while keeping the chapter's words. One order and one list of fields, the same for every adopter and for software and manual processing alike, mean any affected sentient can trace a result back to its source and see whether a step was skipped.

<a id="3-contents-of-the-standing-records-used"></a>
### 3. Contents of the Standing Records Used

*In plain terms: each track brings its own verified Standing Record, with these fields.*

The decision uses the following verified Standing Records, one for each axis that applies:

- If contribution applies, use the **contribution Standing Record**, including:
  - its verified facts;
  - Contribution Axis slot;
  - supporting evidence;
  - scope;
  - time period;
  - review status; and
  - contribution descriptors.
- If violation applies, use the **violation Standing Record**, including:
  - its verified facts;
  - Violation Axis slot;
  - supporting evidence;
  - scope;
  - time period;
  - review status;
  - process or response character; and
  - violation descriptors.

<a id="4-what-the-integration-record-identifies"></a>
### 4. What the Integration Record Identifies

*In plain terms: every integration record states its inputs, who and what is affected, the results, and what happens next.*

Each integration record must identify the following, in four groups:

1. **Inputs:**
   - each source Standing Record and its current version;
   - each applicable fixed Contribution Axis or Violation Axis measurement; and
   - each descriptor (how the verified benefit or harm happened) and each attachment (the legal or constitutional character of the response), imported unchanged from its source Standing Record and normalized as [Subcore 10-F §5 Descriptor Integration and Attachment Normalization](subcore_10.060_standing_integration.md#5-descriptor-integration-and-attachment-normalization) provides.
2. **Who and what is affected:**
   - affected sentients, interests, systems, and institutions; and
   - affected gated pathways, meaning the roles, privileges, or kinds of access the decision opens, limits, or closes.
3. **Results:**
   - on the violation side: correction, remedy, safeguards, and locks;
   - on the contribution side: currentness and competency-clearance results; and
   - the final standing effect for each applicable gated pathway.
4. **What happens next:**
   - implementation milestones;
   - the review route;
   - reassessment triggers; and
   - any Chapter Fourteen gateway status.

<a id="5-processing-order"></a>
### 5. Processing Order

*In plain terms: every decision, automated or manual, follows the same eight steps. The two tracks are decided separately and meet only at the final standing effect, where a lock controls.*

The following processing order applies to both automated and manual decisions. It has eight numbered steps, grouped under four headings:

**Prepare the inputs:**

1. validate the Chapter Ten records and their current review status; and
2. normalize shared descriptors and attachments without changing either slot.

**Decide the consequences on each track:**

3. decide contribution consequences:
   - currentness; and
   - competency bars and clearances.
4. decide violation consequences (remedy, correction, and prevention), in this order:
   - remedy and correction;
   - locks;
   - voluntary public accountability expression, where used; and
   - then lock design and enforcement, which covers:
     - definition and attachment;
     - proportionality and calibration;
     - record visibility and escalation;
     - special violation rules; and
     - special locks.

**State the outcome:**

5. state one final standing effect for each applicable gated pathway; and
6. state restoration and reassessment conditions.

**Verify and route:**

7. verify practical enforceability; and
8. route a qualifying Violation Axis slot 7–9 finding to [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-fourteen-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) where anti-constitutional-misconduct designation (ACMD) is at issue.

Steps 3 and 4 do not depend on each other. They can be decided in either order or together, and both are finished before step 5. Two rules govern how the tracks meet:

- **Independence:** remedy, correction, safeguards, and locks come from the violation Standing Record alone, and contribution is not an input to them ([§3 Violation, Correction, and Prevention](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#3-violation-correction-and-prevention)). The one exception is help given after the violation, which can count as restitution under [§3.1.2 Remedy](core_12.000_violation_consequences.md#312-remedy).
- **Lock precedence:** an applicable standing lock controls the gated pathway it attaches to, and no contribution result opens a blocked gated pathway ([§3 Contribution Consequences](core_11.000_contribution_consequences.md#3-contribution-consequences)).

<a id="6-outage-continuity"></a>
### 6. Outage Continuity

*In plain terms: when the computers are down, a manual process takes over under the same rules, and the two sets of records are reconciled afterward.*

During a technology outage, authorized bodies must use a manual continuity process that preserves the same required fields, processing order, evidence custody, review rights, and applicable time limits. When the technology returns, the system must reconcile the manual and electronic records, preserve both audit trails, explain any difference, and never silently overwrite a decision.
