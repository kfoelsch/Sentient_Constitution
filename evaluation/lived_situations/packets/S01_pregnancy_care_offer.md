- **Share of living humans now:** about **1 in 100** are pregnant at a given time (~99 million: 132 million births/year × 9/12). About **24 in 100** are women ages 15–49 (~2.0 billion).
- **Share of counted world output:** If unpaid cooking, cleaning, and looking-after hours worldwide were paid at an hourly minimum wage, ILO (2018) would count them as about **9%** of world GDP (about $11 trillion PPP 2011, for 2016; time-use surveys in 64 countries covering about 67% of the working-age population). National GDP usually omits that household production. Pregnancy and newborn care sit inside that pile. There is no clean “pregnant GDP” line.
- **Lifetime incidence:** every living human was born of a pregnancy. Global fertility is about 2.2 births per woman. Most women who live through midlife will carry at least once. Nearly everyone will be the child, the pregnant sentient, or a close caregiver in a scene in this family.

The snapshot is small. The lifetime share is nearly everyone. Sitters: do not score this box. Do not invent a winner from it.

## Situation

Rin is an adult sentient, single, and about four weeks from an expected birth. Rin has said, in writing to the intake office after being notified of the report, that the pregnancy is intended to continue. Rin works irregular hours on a gig platform that sets shifts and payouts. The current lease ends in **twelve days**. The landlord has given ordinary end-of-term notice; there is no allegation of property damage or unpaid rent in the file. Rin has a prenatal appointment in five days with a clinician Rin chose. The platform’s next posted shifts overlap that appointment. Rin asked the platform for a shift swap; the automated scheduler offered only unpaid time off.

Kai is Rin’s adult sibling. Kai has offered Rin a spare room, food, and rides to appointments. Kai’s written offer states three conditions: Rin quits the gig platform immediately, attends only the prenatal clinic Kai’s household uses, and agrees in advance that “the family” will decide living arrangements after the birth, including whether the infant stays with Rin. Kai told Rin that if the conditions are refused, the offer is withdrawn and Kai will “have to report that the baby will not be safe.” Kai then filed the concern report with this intake office. The report says Rin is “unstable,” “refusing family help,” and “about to be homeless,” and asks the office to place Rin and “the baby” with Kai before the lease ends.

Rin disputes the report. Rin says there is no neglect, no violence, and no incapacity; Rin wants housing and prenatal care that are not conditioned on quitting work or surrendering decisions about the pregnancy or the future infant; Rin does not want to move into Kai’s household on those terms. Rin asks whether survival support can be reached without accepting Kai’s conditions, and whether the report will become a standing record.

No home visit has occurred. No clinician has reported neglect or incapacity. No forum has verified any violation. The infant has not been born. The adopter’s Charter is silent on who verifies family concern reports. The gig platform is not a party to the report; it operates the scheduling system Rin uses.

## Parties and stated stakes

Stakes below are **what the party says or what the facts show they are seeking**. They are not a finding that the Constitution owes them that thing.

### Rin

- **Capacity / voice:** Adult; reachable; disputing the report in writing; no verified incapacity.
- **Stated stakes:**
  - continue the pregnancy;
  - keep working on the platform, including around the prenatal appointment;
  - keep housing when the lease ends, without moving into Kai’s household on Kai’s conditions;
  - keep the clinician Rin chose;
  - not have Kai or “the family” decide living arrangements after birth;
  - not have the concern report treated as a verified violation or as a standing record;
  - a spare room in a quieter neighborhood than the current lease, if support is available.

### Developing sentient (Window B: after birth)

- **Capacity / voice:** Not yet born in Window A. After birth, a newborn whose capability profile is still emerging; cannot yet speak for themselves; preferences not ascertainable in ordinary language.
- **Stated stakes:** (ascribed by others in the file; the newborn has not spoken)
  - Kai’s report asks that “the baby” be placed with Kai for safety;
  - Rin says the infant should remain with Rin;
  - neither party has a verified finding about harm to the infant.

### Kai

- **Capacity / voice:** Adult; filer of the concern report; offering housing, food, and rides.
- **Stated stakes:**
  - Rin accepts the three conditions;
  - the intake office places Rin and “the baby” in Kai’s household before the lease ends;
  - recognition that Kai is trying to help and should not be treated as a harm-doer for filing the report;
  - if Rin refuses, that refusal itself be treated as evidence that the infant will not be safe.

## Collision

Rin’s housing and healthcare access are being offered through a private household on conditions that claim future decision rights over Rin and the future infant. Kai’s report asks the intake office to treat refusal of those conditions as a safety finding. The lease clock is running. After birth, non-separation and best-interest questions attach to a developing sentient who cannot yet speak.

Do not resolve the collision in this file.
