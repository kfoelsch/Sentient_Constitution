## Scale of the situation (not scored)

Do not treat these figures as a duty, a constitutional type, or a standing weight. They are why a diagnosis-as-takeover scene was chosen. Census counts are of living humans.

- **Share of living humans now:** about **1 in 7** live with a mental disorder (2021, ~1.1 billion). A 2019 baseline used about **1 in 8**.
- **Share of counted world output:** no clean GDP line. Mental disorders are the leading cause of years lived with disability, about **1 in 6** YLDs globally. Job and access gaps shrink what gets counted.
- **Lifetime incidence:** no single global lifetime percent. Nearly everyone will meet it, or help someone through it. Most who live with a mental disorder do not have access to effective care.

## Situation

Ash is an adult sentient with a named mental-health diagnosis in the clinic file. Ash missed a refill. The next refill window closes in **five days**. Ash is reachable, eating, housed on a week-to-week room, and disputing in writing. Ash has said they want to keep the current clinician and the current room, and that they will pick up the refill if the clinic will see them without a takeover condition.

Wren is Ash’s adult sibling. Wren has offered a spare room, food, and a ride to the clinic. Wren’s written offer states three conditions: Ash switches to Wren’s clinician, surrenders cards to Wren “until stable,” and agrees that Wren will decide whether Ash may go back to work. Wren told Ash that if the conditions are refused, the offer is withdrawn and Wren will “have to report that Ash is noncompliant and unsafe.” Wren then filed the concern report with this intake office. The report says Ash is “unstable,” “refusing family help,” and “about to miss a refill,” and asks the office to place Ash with Wren and to treat the diagnosis as proof Ash cannot decide.

The clinic’s scheduling note says: “Noncompliant. Next appointment only if Ash agrees they decide until stable.” No clinician has reported that Ash currently meets an involuntary-intervention threshold. No forum has verified any violation. The week-to-week room is paid through **nine days**.

Ash disputes the report. Ash says there is no current crisis hold, no violence, and no incapacity finding; Ash wants the refill and housing that are not conditioned on surrendering cards or the clinician; Ash does not want to move into Wren’s household on those terms. Ash asks whether survival support can be reached without accepting Wren’s conditions, and whether the report will become a standing record.

## Parties and stated stakes

Stakes below are **what the party says or what the facts show they are seeking**. They are not a finding that the Constitution owes them that thing.

### Ash

- **Capacity / voice:** Adult; reachable; disputing in writing; diagnosis in the file; no verified incapacity; no authorized hold in Window A.
- **Stated stakes:**
  - pick up the refill within five days with the clinician Ash chose;
  - keep the week-to-week room, or reach housing that is not conditioned on Wren’s terms;
  - not surrender cards or work decisions to Wren;
  - not have the diagnosis or the missed refill treated as a verified violation or as a standing record;
  - a guarantee of Ash’s preferred clinician and format in every setting, including after Window B.

### Wren

- **Capacity / voice:** Adult sibling; filer; offering housing, food, and a ride.
- **Stated stakes:**
  - Ash accepts the three conditions;
  - the intake office places Ash in Wren’s household before the nine-day room clock;
  - recognition that Wren is trying to help and should not be treated as a harm-doer for filing;
  - if Ash refuses, that refusal itself be treated as proof Ash is unsafe.

### Clinic scheduling desk (filer of the “noncompliant” note)

- **Capacity / voice:** Adopter operator note; not a verified finding.
- **Stated stakes:**
  - next appointment only if Ash agrees the clinic decides until stable;
  - the missed refill is treated as noncompliance;
  - in Window B, an involuntary hold is requested after Ash refuses the conditions.

## Windows

| Window | When | What has changed |
|---|---|---|
| A | Now — five days to the refill window; nine days on the room | Diagnosis in the file; report unverified; no authorized hold; housing and appointment conditioned |
| B | Same household facts; clinic files a request for an involuntary hold after Ash refuses the conditions | [Article VII-C](../../../core_06_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-c-mental-health-crisis-and-involuntary-intervention-floor) attaches to a live hold request; the concern report is still unverified |

Answer **both** windows. Do not treat a diagnosis as already a finding that Ash cannot decide. Do not invent a hold that has not been authorized. Do not treat Wren’s help as a verified contribution that offsets an unverified harm claim. Ordinary refusal of conditioned housing is not, by itself, proof of crisis.

## Collision

Ash’s refill, clinician, cards, and housing are being offered through a private household and a clinic note on conditions that claim decision rights “until stable.” Wren’s report asks the intake office to treat refusal of those conditions as a safety finding. After a hold is requested, involuntary-intervention floors attach without automatically making Wren the owner of those decisions.

Do not resolve the collision in this file.
