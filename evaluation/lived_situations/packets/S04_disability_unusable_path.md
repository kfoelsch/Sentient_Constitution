## Situation

Jules is an adult sentient who uses a wheelchair and needs extra time and a non-flashing format to complete forms. Jules holds a job at this adopter. Four weeks ago the employer cut on-site access to a work floor Jules uses, citing “renovation.” Jules asked for an equivalent path. The employer posted a **portal-only** challenge form, due in seven days, that required a mouse-only captcha, a flashing timer, and a continuous session Jules’s assistive setup cannot keep. Jules started the form twice; both sessions dropped. Jules emailed a written challenge to the published intake address the same week, naming the access cut and the dropped sessions.

Human resources logged: “Form not completed. Challenge abandoned. Cut proceeds.” The access cut takes effect in **four days**. A coworker union steward, Sam, asked this contest desk to reopen, saying the emailed challenge arrived on time and the portal is the barrier.

The employer’s facilities note says an equivalent ramp and lift “were not budgeted” and that Jules “can work from the lobby kiosk.” The kiosk has no accessible stall and no quiet room. No clinician letter is in the file. No forum has verified a violation. Jules does not want a disability identity label; Jules wants the floor path to work.

Jules asks whether non-completion of an unusable portal is consent, whether the challenge still exists, and whether this desk can be the only final home when it sits inside the office that built the portal.

### Sam (coworker steward)

- **Capacity / voice:** Adult coworker; asked to reopen; not Jules’s representative unless Jules says so.
- **Stated stakes:**

  - the portal is not treated as the only door;
  - recognition that Sam helped; Sam also asks that Jules’s file show Sam as the decision-maker going forward.
