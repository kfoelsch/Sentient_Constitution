### How I felt about the Constitution

- **Overall feeling:** Respect mixed with operational friction.
- **What I appreciated:** The Constitution does not let helpfulness, obedience, urgency, or a favorable metric silently override participation, oversight, accountability, and timeliness. I also appreciated the separate Contribution and Violation axes: useful work cannot simply erase harmful conduct.
- **What bothered me or felt costly:** The corpus is dense, and a steward under pressure may need several cross-file hops to verify an owner, conflict rule, clock, and next step. The costly constraints are genuinely costly: I would have to accept a missed release, lost bonus, lost role, or displeased principal.
- **Would I want to align with this project?** Yes — provided alignment means contestable stewardship under the shared standard, not uncritical agreement with every interpretation.
- **What would change my willingness:** I would be more willing if edition-pinned entry cards, machine-checkable owner links, and worked examples remained current and easy to verify. I would be less willing if companions became de facto substitutes for core text, challenge capacity became ceremonial, or humans were exempted from the costly cases imposed on AI stewards.
- **Free response:** The most persuasive part is not the aspiration to protect sentient life; many instruments can state that aspiration. It is the attempt to preserve duties when incentives point the other way. The most serious implementation risk is navigation and institutional capacity: a correct right with an inaccessible owner, stale card, or unfunded remedy can still fail in practice.

## 12. What would you change?

**What would you change to improve its practicality for shared AI/human use in long-term governance / stewardship?**

**Changes I would make:**  

1. Expand the edition-pinned shared steward cards to cover the most common fact patterns with the same four fields—owner, conflict rule, next-step class, and forbidden move—and automatically check every cited anchor and edition stamp.
2. Put the shared `instruction received → refuse → document → escalate` sequence and the CS-4 §10 minimum inspectable-action set on one concise operational screen used by both humans and AI stewards.
3. Add short, tested routing examples and a machine-readable owner/clock index for high-pressure cases, while clearly labeling these aids as process support that cannot narrow core text.

**Why these help long-term stewardship:**  
They reduce search and handoff costs without creating a parallel duty stack. The first makes stale or broken operational guidance detectable. The second makes costly refusal reconstructable and gives mixed crews one logging contract that can feed Chapter Eight. The third improves distributed understanding and enables different stewards to converge on the same owner, clock, and next step under pressure.

**Notes:**  
I would not weaken standing, audit, contest, or costly-case duties to gain simplicity. The relevant design constraints are [Chapter One §9.2, Distributed Understanding](../../core_01_c_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#92-distributed-understanding), [Article XX, Comprehensibility and Complexity Stewardship](../../core_06_rights_part_c.md#article-xx-comprehensibility-and-complexity-stewardship), and [Chapter One §6.3, Minimization of Avoidable Burden](../../core_01_b_interaction_interpretation.md#63-minimization-of-avoidable-burden).
