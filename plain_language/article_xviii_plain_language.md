# Article XVIII, in Plain Language

## Standing and Participation Status

*Who qualifies for which roles, and how that can be fairly challenged, limited, and restored.*

> **This is an everyday-reader version, not the official text.** If anything here seems to differ from the official [Article XVIII](../core_06_rights_part_c.md#article-xviii-standing-and-participation-status), the official text is what counts.
>
> A note on "you" and "people": this constitution protects every sentient being it covers, not only humans. Here, "people" means all of them.

---

### The big idea

**"Standing" is whether you qualify for a particular role.** Roles like managing shared money, overseeing a system, judging a dispute, or voting on certain decisions. It's decided from checked records and fair, published rules.

Standing is **not**:

- how popular you are, your brand, or a social score;
- your worth as a being;
- your basic rights;
- whether you get a say because a decision actually affects you.

Your standing can be lowered or paused if you did something wrong and it was actually proven. When that happens, you get clear reasons, a real way to push back, limits that fit what you did, and a real path back. And no matter what, low standing can never cut you off from the things you need to survive, or from your ability to challenge a decision and get harm fixed.

### Why this article exists

The article has five parts:

- **Part A:** what standing is, and what it must never be confused with.
- **Part B:** the fair-process rules for every standing decision.
- **Part C:** how doors open: help, qualification bars, and eligibility.
- **Part D:** how doors close: proven harm and locks.
- **Part E:** extra rules for locks that touch your vote, and for the most serious cases.

It has two goals:

- **Let people thrive.** Everyone gets fair, checkable routes into roles, based on what they can actually show, not on who they know or how famous they are.
- **Keep things safe over time.** Restrictions stay fair, temporary where possible, and fixable, so nobody is shut out forever over something that's been dealt with.

### Part A: Standing is not your worth

Standing only decides which roles and privileges you can use. Nothing more.

#### Having no record is normal

Most people will never have a standing record at all, and that's fine.

- Having no record can't be held against you.
- Nobody can demand a record, or a "clean record" certificate, before letting you get survival essentials, buy and sell normally, or be heard when a decision affects you.
- Standing results can't be combined into a profile, ranking, or public scoreboard.

#### Standing never decides who counts

A standing score, a certification badge, a benefit score, or a product label can **never** be used to decide whether someone is a sentient being with rights. That question has its own separate process ([Article V-E](../core_06_rights_part_b.md#article-v-e-sentience-status-adjudication-floor)).

---|---|
| **Governance vote** | The big, foundational questions: who governs and under what rules. Everyone's voice counts equally. |
| **Stakeholder voice** | Having a weighted say *inside* a particular system, because that system affects you. |

A lock on one doesn't automatically take away the other. And a lock on your stakeholder voice doesn't erase the fact that the system affects you.

---

### Part B: You can always challenge it, and limits must fit

These rules apply to every standing decision, whether it opens a door or closes one.

#### Only proven facts count

Anything that affects your standing must be based on verified records, not rumors, open cases, or early labels.

Every standing record must tell you:

- how to challenge it;
- who reviews it;
- how it can be corrected, restored, expire, or come up for scheduled review.

Records of help and records of harm are kept separate. They can't be blended into one score.

#### No single gatekeeper

No single authority, database, reputation service, or hidden algorithm gets to decide your standing in a way you can't meaningfully challenge.

> **Example:** A platform's secret "trust score" can't, on its own, be what keeps you out of a role.

#### Review bodies can fix records

Independent review bodies (called *forums* in the constitution) hear challenges. Once facts are verified, they can open, update, or correct a record, or **throw out a bad one**. Simply filing a case doesn't change anyone's standing.

#### When your standing is limited

The limit must be the least restrictive one that works, and you must get:

- a **clear explanation** of why;
- a **time limit**, wherever that's possible;
- a **working way** to challenge it, get it reviewed, fix the problem, and ask for another look.

While your challenge is pending, any temporary limits can only be as broad, and as hard to undo, as safety or fairness truly require.

#### What standing can never take away

Standing, qualification bars, and locks only control **roles and privileges**. They can never:

- reduce your basic rights;
- cut you off from what you need to survive;
- block you from challenging decisions, requesting audits, or getting harm fixed.

> **Example:** A lock that keeps someone from being a community treasurer can't stop them from buying food, getting medical care, or filing a complaint.

### Part C: How doors open

#### Present evidence, not past reputation

Eligibility for roles and votes must be based on **current, observable evidence** that can be challenged, not reputation, how big or famous you are, or what you did long ago.

Ordinary participation, including the governance vote, stays open as long as you meet the published rules and no lock applies. Eligibility rules are **checked continuously** and **updated** when the facts change.

#### Qualification bars

Some roles need trust or skill. Each one must have a **published** qualification standard that anyone can see and challenge. If your checked experience and track record meet that standard, you're **cleared** for that role.

A qualification bar or clearance isn't a rank, a reputation, an insider's favor, or a permanent entitlement.

> **Example:** Someone who has spent ten years running a volunteer repair shop can qualify for a role just as well as someone with a formal job title, as long as the experience can be shown to the same standard. **Informal, community, teaching, repair, and mutual-aid experience counts.**

#### Help is recorded on its own track

Verified help is kept in its own records. It can help you qualify for a role, but it never cancels a verified finding of harm, and being cleared for a role doesn't override a lock on it.

---

### Part D: How doors close

A **lock** keeps you out of a specific role or privilege because of a verified finding of harm that hasn't been fixed yet.

#### Rules for locks

- **Accusations aren't findings.** A complaint, a pending case, or an early label can't lower your standing. Only a verified finding can.
- **Every lock must say:** exactly which role or privilege is blocked, who it protects, what you have to fix, where you can appeal, and when it will be looked at again.
- **A lock isn't a punishment for everything.** It isn't a lower rank as a being, a cut to your basic rights, or automatic payback.
- **Help doesn't erase harm.** A strong record of help can't cancel an unresolved finding of harm. And being cleared for a role doesn't override a lock on it.

**Going along with wrongdoing counts too.** If you had a real duty and a real ability to refuse an unlawful or unconstitutional order, and you went along with it anyway, that can be taken into account.

#### Limits must fit what you did

- The lock must match what was actually proven and what it protects.
- A **minor** finding doesn't justify a long exclusion, unless there's a pattern, you dodged responsibility, or it's tied to real harm.
- Stricter limits for repeat or hidden wrongdoing must still be necessary, fair in size, reviewable, and based on verified findings.

#### A real way back

- **No permanent exclusion based only on the past.** Any ongoing restriction needs a current, checkable reason.
- **Fixing things opens a real review.** If you correct the problem, pay back what's owed, accept monitoring, or add safeguards, you get a genuine chance to be reassessed.
- **Not fixing things keeps the finding active.**

### Part E: Extra rules for votes and serious cases

Some locks carry extra protections, or extra weight, on top of the rules in Part D.

#### A lock on one kind of vote doesn't touch the other

|

#### Special protection for your vote

Taking away someone's right to vote on who governs is serious. Any lock on it needs:

- a finding about **you specifically**, made through a fair process;
- a real **need** for it, and a size that **fits**;
- a **narrow** link to the specific kind of wrongdoing;
- a **real** path back, not just one on paper.

These are never allowed:

- sweeping whole broad categories of misconduct into voting bans;
- lock patterns that end up targeting people by race, sex, disability, or other protected traits, or by stand-ins for them.

**The one exception:** after a **final** finding of the most serious kind of attack on the constitution itself, a person loses their foundational vote **until they've fully made things right**. Even then, the vote can't be withheld just because of poverty, disability, exile, loss of the body or hardware they run on, or uncertainty about how much is owed, if they're making good-faith, ongoing repayment. (Details are in [Chapter Thirteen §4.1](../core_13_governance.md#41-entitlement-and-eligibility).)

#### Three special locks for serious cases

1. **Judges who should have stepped aside.** If someone judging a dispute had a conflict, was required to step aside, didn't, and it really compromised fairness, they're locked out of judging roles. Getting back in takes a strict independent review. An apology, past service, being hard to replace, or a staffing shortage isn't enough by itself.
2. **Rigging stakeholder votes.** If someone faked or inflated their stake, bought votes, coerced others, or hid conflicts to take over a system, they lose their weighted say *in that system*. They keep their governance vote and their standing as someone affected.
3. **The most serious attacks on the constitution itself.** After a final finding of this kind of misconduct, the person is barred from any role or real influence over the most important systems, over courts and review bodies, and over constitutional oversight, until a strict independent review shows they've truly made things right.

### Where did the movement and refuge rules go?

The rule that standing can never be used as a border used to be its own part of this article. It now lives in [Article XX-D](../core_06_rights_part_c.md#article-xx-d-limitation-custody-and-emergency-discipline), alongside the other rules on moving, finding refuge, and never being left without a country.

In short: lower standing, or a lock, can never by itself stop you from moving, deny you refuge, trap you in a system, or leave you without any authority that protects your basic rights. Real dangers are handled only through a full, fair justice process.

---

### The whole article in one breath

Your standing decides which roles you qualify for, never your worth or your rights. It must rest on today's proven facts, be open to challenge, fit what you actually did, and come with a real way back. It can never cut off survival, your right to seek a remedy, or your freedom to move and find refuge.
