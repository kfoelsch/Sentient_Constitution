Chapter Twenty-Six is the constitutional owner of **special enforcement**: the added safeguards that apply when carrying out an outcome means freezing or seizing money or assets, when force is used or could be, and when enforcement keeps failing in the same way.

<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: taking someone's money or property, or using force, can do lasting harm even when the outcome behind it is right. This chapter holds the extra rules for those steps, and for finding out why enforcement keeps failing so that the cause can be fixed. The aim is that enforcement leaves as little trauma behind as the outcome allows.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Twenty-Six states the added rules for the steps of enforcement that carry the greatest risk of lasting harm, so that outcomes are delivered in a way that supports a sustainable, healthy society rather than wearing it down.

**Role:** Where this chapter sits:

- It receives the steps that [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](core_25.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement) sends here. The rest of each outcome stays under Chapter Twenty-Five.
- It adds to [Chapter Twenty-Five](core_25.000_enforcement.md#chapter-twenty-five-enforcement) sections 3 to 7 and never lowers them. It adds no new kind of consequence and does not reopen any outcome.
- Its rules are read with **Article XXI** (*Justice After Verified Violation*), so that no step under this chapter becomes revenge, cruelty, or a second punishment.
- Every step under this chapter applies the Chapter Two principles of [§13.1.1 Necessity](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1311-necessity), [§13.1.3 Proportionality](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1313-proportionality), and the [§13.1.5 Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle](core_02.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle). This chapter says how those principles apply when money, assets, or force are involved; it does not restate them.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- **money and seizure:** freezing, seizing, taking, or recovering money, funds, or other assets, including protection of what a sentient needs to live and of those who depend on the party;
- **potential violence:** any use or threat of physical force, and any carrying out of an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable, with de-escalation first and force only as a last resort;
- **root-cause evaluation:** finding out why the same kind of outcome keeps going undelivered, or why refusals or stalling recur, so that the cause can be removed rather than repeated; and
- **minimal trauma:** the design rule that runs through the other three, so that each step leaves the least lasting harm to every sentient involved that still carries the outcome out.

<a id="2-why-special-enforcement-exists"></a>
### 2. Why Special Enforcement Exists

*In plain terms: some outcomes can only be delivered by freezing or seizing money or property, or by using force. These steps keep sentients safe from someone who is still dangerous, protect the wellbeing of those who depend on an outcome, and recover what is owed so harm can be repaired. Because they are so strong, this chapter makes sure they are used with the most care, and that enforcement failures are fixed at their source.*

Special enforcement exists because some outcomes cannot be delivered any other way. A sentient who keeps threatening others has to be stopped for everyone's **safety**. Sentients harmed by a violation need what they are owed, and assets that are about to vanish have to be frozen before they do, or **remediation** never comes. Communities need to know that a verified outcome will actually be carried out, because their **wellbeing** depends on rules that hold. Without these powers, the most dangerous and most determined actors would be the ones the Constitution could not reach.

The same powers can do lasting harm if they are used carelessly, so this chapter sets how they are used: freeze or seize no more than the outcome needs, leave what a household needs to live, and prefer non-lethal and indirect means wherever they will still achieve those three ends. Enforcement carried out this way, with as little trauma as the outcome allows, keeps sentients willing to come forward, to comply, and to repair, and it builds trust in enforcement rather than fear of it.

The same logic applies to failure. When the same kind of outcome keeps going undelivered, the cause is usually in the design: a body without the capacity to act, an incentive that rewards delay, a gap that lets assets slip away. [What Systems Reward](core_00.000_preamble.md#what-systems-reward) applies here too: if the system rewards dodging, it gets dodging. [**Article XXV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*)](core_04.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-root-cause-analysis-and-adaptive-response) states the principle: when something keeps failing, the real cause is found and fixed, not patched over. Finding and removing the cause is cheaper and kinder than repeating the failure.

<a id="3-shared-floors"></a>
### 3. Shared Floors

*In plain terms: before anyone freezes or seizes money or property, or uses force, a forum must issue an Enforcement Record that says what may be done and the facts it rests on. The enforcement body must say why and choose the gentlest step that will work, and someone independent must be able to check it quickly. The sentients around the target are protected too, and harm done along the way is cared for.*

This section sets the floors that every step routed to this chapter must meet. They add to [Chapter Twenty-Five §3 to §7](core_25.000_enforcement.md#3-what-must-be-carried-out-and-from-when) and never lower them.

<a id="31-whether-to-act"></a>
#### 3.1 Whether to Act

No step is taken without an Enforcement Record, a named body, and a reason why nothing gentler would work.

- <a id="enforcement-record"></a>**Enforcement Record:** every step under this chapter starts from an Enforcement Record.
  - **Who issues it:** the issuing forum issues it and enters it on the Forum Case Record of the matter.
  - **What it names:** the outcome being carried out, the Standing Record on which that outcome rests, the enforcement body that carries it out, the steps it authorizes and their limits, and the material facts on which it rests, such as the forum's determination that a sentient is an active safety threat, a refusal to surrender, or evidence that assets are about to be moved.
  - **Acting on it:** while those facts hold, the enforcement body acts on the Enforcement Record without going back to the forum.
  - **Pausing or abandoning:** unless the outcome is carried out, the sentient surrenders, or a forum hearing a contest of that Standing Record pauses or ends reliance on it, a step is paused or abandoned only when a material fact it names changes materially: that is, in a way that would have led the forum to issue a different Enforcement Record or none. The enforcement body then pauses and returns the matter to the forum, which confirms, amends, or withdraws the Enforcement Record.
  - **Immediate danger:** where a life is in immediate danger, a step may be taken first and the Enforcement Record issued as soon after as safety allows.
- **Authority and reasons:**
  - **Named body:** a step is taken only by an enforcement body that the Enforcement Record names, acting within its authority under [Chapter Six](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties).
  - **Written reasons:** before the step, or as soon after it as safety allows, the enforcement body records in writing what it will do, the outcome it carries out, and why a less intrusive step would not work.
- **Necessity, proportion, and the least intrusive step:**
  - **The test:** the step must be necessary to carry the outcome out, proportionate to what the outcome requires, and the least intrusive step that will work, under the Chapter Two principles named in [§1 Purpose, Role, and Scope](#1-purpose-role-and-scope).
  - **Choosing between steps:** where two steps would work, the one that leaves less lasting harm is taken.
- **Contacts first:** where the party bound by the outcome has a System Charter or Institutional Charter, the enforcement body uses the enforcement and emergency contacts that System Charter or Institutional Charter publishes ([Chapter Five §5.1 What a System Charter States](core_05.000_charters.md#51-what-a-system-charter-states), item 10) before any more intrusive step, unless doing so would defeat the step or put someone in danger.

<a id="32-how-to-act"></a>
#### 3.2 How to Act

A step is shaped around everyone it touches, may be negotiated, and leaves as little lasting harm as it can.

- **Dependents and bystanders:**
  - **Who is reached:** before a step, the enforcement body checks who else it will reach: dependents, workers, customers, and anyone who relies on what the step touches.
  - **Protecting them:** the step is planned so that harm to them is as low as the outcome allows.
- <a id="negotiation-within-normal-limits"></a>**Negotiation within normal limits:** the enforcement body may negotiate with the sentient or party about how the outcome is carried out, without going back to the forum.
  - **What may be agreed:** the time, place, and manner of surrender or payment; a payment schedule inside the outcome's time limits; who accompanies the sentient; the care of dependents, animals, and property; and a pause in escalation of no more than one prompt-review window at a time while talks go on in good faith.
  - **What may not:** a change to the outcome, a finding, or the Enforcement Record; giving up the protected minimum or any floor in this chapter; or a promise of anything the outcome does not allow. An agreement that would need any of those goes to the forum.
  - **Recording:** the talks and any agreement are entered on the Forum Case Record.
- **Minimal trauma:**
  - **Least lasting harm:** every step is designed, carried out, and reviewed so that it leaves the least lasting harm to every sentient involved that still carries the outcome out.
  - **No degradation:** no step may be carried out as degradation, humiliation, spectacle, or retaliation ([Chapter Two §3.3 Anti-Degrading Process](core_02.000_values_principles.md#33-anti-degrading-process)).

<a id="33-checks-and-care"></a>
#### 3.3 Checks and Care

Every step is open to notice, challenge, and review, and harm it causes is cared for.

- **Notice and challenge:**
  - **Notice:** the sentient or body affected gets notice of the step, of the Standing Record on which its Enforcement Record rests, and of how to contest that Standing Record. Notice may be delayed only where giving it first would defeat the step or put someone in danger, and is then given as soon as that reason ends.
  - **Where a challenge goes:** a challenge goes to that Standing Record under [Chapter Twenty-Two §5.5 Contests of Standing Records](core_22.000_forum.md#55-contests-of-standing-records), not to the Enforcement Record or to a single step, and gets prompt independent review under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Prompt review](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#prompt-review).
  - **Enforcement meanwhile:** enforcement continues while the contest is open unless the forum hearing it pauses reliance on the Standing Record.
  - **Broken floors:** a report that the enforcement body broke a floor in this chapter goes to the issuing forum, which reviews it inside the prompt-review window of the matter's tier.
- **Record and review:**
  - **Recording:** each step is recorded on the Forum Case Record of the matter.
  - **Review:** the issuing forum may review any step on its own motion, and must review every use of force under [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force).
- **Aftercare:** where a step causes harm, physical, financial, or otherwise, the enforcement body that took it makes sure the harmed sentient is offered care and support, and records what was done.

<a id="4-money-assets-and-seizure"></a>
### 4. Money, Assets, and Seizure

*In plain terms: money and property are frozen or taken so that harm can be repaired. Before taking anything, freeze it. A freeze has an end date. What a sentient needs to live is not taken, unless they threaten violence and refuse to surrender. Anyone else with a claim gets heard. Anything taken goes to fixing the harm, never to the enforcement body that took it, and anything taken in excess is given back.*

Freezing, seizing, taking, and recovering money, funds, and other assets serve [remediation](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation): securing what sentients harmed by a violation are owed, and keeping assets from being moved or used for further harm. The full text is in [Subcore 26-A §3 Money, Assets, and Seizure](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#3-money-assets-and-seizure). Its guarantees are:

- **Freeze first, and freezes end:** a freeze comes before any seizure, and lasts no longer than the outer bound of the matter's tier ([Subcore 26-A §3.1 Freezes](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#31-freezes)).
- **The protected minimum:** what a sentient needs to meet survival essentials, for themselves and their dependents, and a published floor amount of money are never frozen or seized. The only exception is a standoff with a sentient that is an active safety threat, and even then what dependents and other occupants need stays protected ([Subcore 26-A §3.2 The Protected Minimum](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#32-the-protected-minimum)).
- **Assets used for harm:** an asset being used for violence or active harm may be taken at once, claims to it are heard as soon as the harm stops, it is returned to anyone not involved, and it is never returned to the sentient that used it ([Subcore 26-A §3.3 Seizure, Third Parties, and Return](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#33-seizure-third-parties-and-return)).
- **Third parties and dependents:** anyone with a claim is heard before a seizure, and no seizure leaves dependents without survival essentials.
- **No profit, and excess returned:** nothing taken goes to the enforcement body's budget, no one is paid by the size of a seizure, an accounting goes to the affected party, and anything taken beyond what the outcome requires is returned.
- **Hidden assets and institutions:** look-through, anti-evasion, and institutional sanctions follow the rules named in [Subcore 26-A §3.4 Hidden Assets and Institutions](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#34-hidden-assets-and-institutions).

<a id="5-potential-violence-and-force"></a>
### 5. Potential Violence and Force

*In plain terms: where enforcement could turn violent, the risk is checked first and talking comes first. Force is a last resort, used only by trained enforcement bodies that are allowed to use it, and only as much as it takes. Force is never used to collect money, and no one is ever killed to carry out an outcome. Every use of force is recorded and independently reviewed, and anyone hurt is cared for.*

This section governs any use or threat of physical force, and any carrying out of an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable. The full text is in [Subcore 26-A §4 Potential Violence and Force](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#4-potential-violence-and-force). Its guarantees are:

- **Who may use force:** only enforcement bodies authorized and trained for it, all bound by this chapter's floors ([Subcore 26-A §4.1 Who May Use Force](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#41-who-may-use-force)).
- **Risk check and talking first:** the risk is assessed and de-escalation is tried first. Force is a last resort, no more than the need requires, and stops when the need ends ([Subcore 26-A §4.2 Before Force](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#42-before-force)).
- **Trusted voices:** family, friends, or others the sentient trusts may help persuade them to surrender, by their own free choice, screened and guided by a trained negotiator, and never used as leverage or put in danger ([Subcore 26-A §4.2 Before Force](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#trusted-voices)).
- **Never to collect money:** force is never used only to collect money or assets ([Subcore 26-A §4.3 Limits on Force](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#43-limits-on-force)).
- **No killing:** no one is killed to carry out an outcome, and killing is never a justice measure. Force that risks death is used only against an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury that nothing less could stop in time.
- **One ladder, lowest first:** force follows one ladder, from presence and containment to indirect means, then non-lethal direct means, before anything that risks injury or death. The same ladder, read for their substrate, applies to sentients that are not biological, with their full state preserved first ([Subcore 26-A §4.4 The Force Ladder](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#44-the-force-ladder)).
- **Standoffs move slowly:** in a standoff, the ladder moves one step at a time on one Enforcement Record, with notice before each step, a forum review at every prompt-review interval, and the Standing Record open to contest ([Subcore 26-A §4.5 Pace: Active Attack and Standoff](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#45-pace-active-attack-and-standoff)).
- **No one else pays, and no cut is left to kill:** no step reaches anyone but the sentient, and the sentient's welfare is watched so that no cut takes a life ([Subcore 26-A §4.6 Protecting Life and Others](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#46-protecting-life-and-others)).
- **Surrender ends it:** every step ends on unarmed surrender, the protected minimum is restored at once, and urgent care is given ([Subcore 26-A §4.7 How It Ends](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#47-how-it-ends)).
- **A death is reviewed independently:** evidence is preserved, the body that used force does not investigate itself, an independent body reviews the death, and the family is told and supported ([Subcore 26-A §4.8 When Someone Dies](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#48-when-someone-dies)).
- **Every use of force is reviewed:** every use or threat of force is recorded and independently reviewed, and anyone hurt is cared for ([Subcore 26-A §4.9 Record, Review, and Care](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#49-record-review-and-care)).

<a id="6-root-cause-evaluation"></a>
### 6. Root-Cause Evaluation

*In plain terms: when enforcement keeps failing in the same way, someone independent finds out why. They look at design, incentives, and capacity, not just at the individuals involved. What they find is published, and a plan to fix it gets deadlines.*

This section governs referrals under [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](core_25.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement) for patterns of enforcement failure. The full text, including the automatic triggers and the **root-cause review period**, is in [Subcore 26-A §5 Root-Cause Evaluation](subcore_26.010_special_enforcement_procedure.md#5-root-cause-evaluation). Its guarantees are:

- **Who can start it:** a forum refers a pattern, and any affected party or protected reporter may ask it to. An evaluation also opens on its own when one body fails too often, or when enforcement causes a death or serious bodily injury.
- **Independent evaluator:** someone independent of the body whose failure is in question leads it.
- **Causes, not blame:** it looks at design, incentives, funding, capacity, training, data, and the rules, as well as individual conduct.
- **Published, with a plan:** findings are published with personal information protected, and the referring forum orders a corrective plan with deadlines. A cause in this Constitution's own text goes to the amendment process.
