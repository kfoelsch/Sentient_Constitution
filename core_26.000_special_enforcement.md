Chapter Twenty-Six is the constitutional owner of **special enforcement**: the added safeguards that apply when carrying out an outcome means freezing or seizing money or assets, when force is used or could be, and when enforcement keeps failing in the same way.

<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: taking someone's money or property, or using force, can do lasting harm even when the outcome behind it is right. This chapter holds the extra rules for those steps, and for finding out why enforcement keeps failing so that the cause can be fixed. The aim is that enforcement leaves as little trauma behind as the outcome allows.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Twenty-Six states the added rules for the steps of enforcement that carry the greatest risk of lasting harm, so that outcomes are delivered in a way that supports a sustainable, healthy society rather than wearing it down.

**Role:** Where this chapter sits:

- It receives the steps that [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](core_25.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement) sends here. The rest of each outcome stays under Chapter Twenty-Five.
- It adds to [Chapter Twenty-Five](core_25.000_enforcement.md#chapter-twenty-five-enforcement) sections 3 to 7 and never lowers them. It adds no new kind of consequence and does not reopen any outcome.
- Its rules are read with **Article XXI** (*Justice After Verified Violation*), so that no step under this chapter becomes revenge, cruelty, or a second punishment.
- Every step under this chapter applies the Chapter One principles of [§13.1.1 Necessity](core_01.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1311-necessity), [§13.1.3 Proportionality](core_01.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1313-proportionality), and the [§13.1.5 Least-Restrictive, Time-Bounded, and Reviewable Constraint Principle](core_01.020_interaction_interpretation.md#1315-least-restrictive-time-bounded-and-reviewable-constraint-principle). This chapter says how those principles apply when money, assets, or force are involved; it does not restate them.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- **money and seizure:** freezing, seizing, taking, or recovering money, funds, or other assets, including protection of what a sentient needs to live and of those who depend on the party;
- **potential violence:** any use or threat of physical force, and any carrying out of an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable, with de-escalation first and force only as a last resort;
- **root-cause evaluation:** finding out why the same kind of outcome keeps going undelivered, or why refusals or stalling recur, so that the cause can be removed rather than repeated; and
- **minimal trauma:** the design rule that runs through the other three, so that each step leaves the least lasting harm to every sentient involved that still carries the outcome out.

<a id="2-why-special-enforcement-exists"></a>
### 2. Why Special Enforcement Exists

*In plain terms: some outcomes can only be delivered by freezing or seizing money or property, or by using force. These steps keep sentients safe from someone who is still dangerous, protect the wellbeing of those who depend on an outcome, and recover what is owed so harm can be repaired. Because they are so strong, this chapter makes sure they are used with the most care, and that enforcement failures are fixed at their source.*

Special enforcement exists because some outcomes cannot be delivered any other way. A sentient who keeps threatening others has to be stopped for everyone's **safety**. Sentients harmed by a violation need what they are owed, and assets that are about to vanish have to be frozen before they do, or **remediation** never comes. Communities need to know that a verified outcome will actually be carried out, because their **wellbeing** depends on rules that hold. Without these powers, the most dangerous and most determined actors would be the ones the Constitution could not reach.

The same powers can do lasting harm if they are used carelessly, so this chapter sets how they are used: freeze or seize no more than the outcome needs, leave what a household needs to live, and prefer non-lethal and indirect means wherever they will still achieve those three ends. Enforcement carried out this way, with as little trauma as the outcome allows, keeps sentients willing to come forward, to comply, and to repair, and it builds trust in enforcement rather than fear of it.

The same logic applies to failure. When the same kind of outcome keeps going undelivered, the cause is usually in the design: a body without the capacity to act, an incentive that rewards delay, a gap that lets assets slip away. [What Systems Reward](core_00.000_preamble.md#what-systems-reward) applies here too: if the system rewards dodging, it gets dodging. [**Article XXV** (*Root Cause Analysis and Adaptive Response*)](core_06.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-root-cause-analysis-and-adaptive-response) states the principle: when something keeps failing, the real cause is found and fixed, not patched over. Finding and removing the cause is cheaper and kinder than repeating the failure.

<a id="3-shared-floors"></a>
### 3. Shared Floors

*In plain terms: before anyone freezes or seizes money or property, or uses force, a forum must issue an Enforcement Record that says what may be done and the facts it rests on. The enforcement body must say why and choose the gentlest step that will work, and someone independent must be able to check it quickly. The sentients around the target are protected too, and harm done along the way is cared for.*

This section sets the floors that every step routed to this chapter must meet. They add to [Chapter Twenty-Five §3 to §7](core_25.000_enforcement.md#3-what-must-be-carried-out-and-from-when) and never lower them.

<a id="31-whether-to-act"></a>
#### 3.1 Whether to Act

No step is taken without an Enforcement Record, a named body, and a reason why nothing gentler would work.

- <a id="enforcement-record"></a>**Enforcement Record:** every step under this chapter starts from an Enforcement Record.
  - **Who issues it:** the issuing forum issues it and enters it on the Forum Case Record of the matter.
  - **What it names:** the outcome being carried out, the Standing Record on which that outcome rests, the enforcement body that carries it out, the steps it authorizes and their limits, and the material facts on which it rests, such as the forum's determination that a sentient is an active safety threat, a refusal to surrender, or evidence that assets are about to be moved.
  - **Acting on it:** while those facts hold, the enforcement body acts on the Enforcement Record without going back to the forum.
  - **Pausing or abandoning:** unless the outcome is carried out, the sentient surrenders, or a forum hearing a contest of that Standing Record pauses or ends reliance on it, a step is paused or abandoned only when a material fact it names changes materially: that is, in a way that would have led the forum to issue a different Enforcement Record or none. The enforcement body then pauses and returns the matter to the forum, which confirms, amends, or withdraws the Enforcement Record.
  - **Immediate danger:** where a life is in immediate danger, a step may be taken first and the Enforcement Record issued as soon after as safety allows.
- **Authority and reasons:**
  - **Named body:** a step is taken only by an enforcement body that the Enforcement Record names, acting within its authority under [Chapter Eight](core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-eight-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties).
  - **Written reasons:** before the step, or as soon after it as safety allows, the enforcement body records in writing what it will do, the outcome it carries out, and why a less intrusive step would not work.
- **Necessity, proportion, and the least intrusive step:**
  - **The test:** the step must be necessary to carry the outcome out, proportionate to what the outcome requires, and the least intrusive step that will work, under the Chapter One principles named in [§1 Purpose, Role, and Scope](#1-purpose-role-and-scope).
  - **Choosing between steps:** where two steps would work, the one that leaves less lasting harm is taken.
- **Contacts first:** where the party bound by the outcome has a System Charter or Institutional Charter, the enforcement body uses the enforcement and emergency contacts that System Charter or Institutional Charter publishes ([Chapter Seven §4.1 What a System Charter States](core_07.000_charters.md#41-what-a-system-charter-states), item 10) before any more intrusive step, unless doing so would defeat the step or put someone in danger.

<a id="32-how-to-act"></a>
#### 3.2 How to Act

A step is shaped around everyone it touches, may be negotiated, and leaves as little lasting harm as it can.

- **Dependents and bystanders:**
  - **Who is reached:** before a step, the enforcement body checks who else it will reach: dependents, workers, customers, and anyone who relies on what the step touches.
  - **Protecting them:** the step is planned so that harm to them is as low as the outcome allows.
- <a id="negotiation-within-normal-limits"></a>**Negotiation within normal limits:** the enforcement body may negotiate with the sentient or party about how the outcome is carried out, without going back to the forum.
  - **What may be agreed:** the time, place, and manner of surrender or payment; a payment schedule inside the outcome's time limits; who accompanies the sentient; the care of dependents, animals, and property; and a pause in escalation of no more than one prompt-review window at a time while talks go on in good faith.
  - **What may not:** a change to the outcome, a finding, or the Enforcement Record; giving up the protected minimum or any floor in this chapter; or a promise of anything the outcome does not allow. An agreement that would need any of those goes to the forum.
  - **Recording:** the talks and any agreement are entered on the Forum Case Record.
- **Minimal trauma:**
  - **Least lasting harm:** every step is designed, carried out, and reviewed so that it leaves the least lasting harm to every sentient involved that still carries the outcome out.
  - **No degradation:** no step may be carried out as degradation, humiliation, spectacle, or retaliation ([Chapter One §3.3 Anti-Degrading Process](core_01.000_values_principles.md#33-anti-degrading-process)).

<a id="33-checks-and-care"></a>
#### 3.3 Checks and Care

Every step is open to notice, challenge, and review, and harm it causes is cared for.

- **Notice and challenge:**
  - **Notice:** the sentient or body affected gets notice of the step, of the Standing Record on which its Enforcement Record rests, and of how to contest that Standing Record. Notice may be delayed only where giving it first would defeat the step or put someone in danger, and is then given as soon as that reason ends.
  - **Where a challenge goes:** a challenge goes to that Standing Record under [Chapter Twenty-Three §4.5 Contests of Standing Records](core_23.000_forum.md#45-contests-of-standing-records), not to the Enforcement Record or to a single step, and gets prompt independent review under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Prompt review](core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#prompt-review).
  - **Enforcement meanwhile:** enforcement continues while the contest is open unless the forum hearing it pauses reliance on the Standing Record.
  - **Broken floors:** a report that the enforcement body broke a floor in this chapter goes to the issuing forum, which reviews it inside the prompt-review window of the matter's tier.
- **Record and review:**
  - **Recording:** each step is recorded on the Forum Case Record of the matter.
  - **Review:** the issuing forum may review any step on its own motion, and must review every use of force under [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force).
- **Aftercare:** where a step causes harm, physical, financial, or otherwise, the enforcement body that took it makes sure the harmed sentient is offered care and support, and records what was done.

<a id="4-money-assets-and-seizure"></a>
### 4. Money, Assets, and Seizure

*In plain terms: money and property are frozen or taken so that harm can be repaired. Before taking anything, freeze it. A freeze has an end date. What a sentient needs to live is not taken, unless they threaten violence and refuse to surrender. Anyone else with a claim gets heard. Anything taken goes to fixing the harm, never to the enforcement body that took it, and anything taken in excess is given back.*

This section governs freezing, seizing, taking, and recovering money, funds, and other assets to carry an outcome out. Its purpose is [remediation](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation): securing what sentients harmed by a violation are owed, and keeping assets from being moved or used for further harm, so that the harm can be repaired.

<a id="41-freezes"></a>
#### 4.1 Freezes

A freeze comes first, and it does not last indefinitely.

- **Freeze before seizure:** where an outcome requires money or assets, the first step is a freeze that stops them being moved, unless the outcome itself orders immediate payment and the party can pay. A seizure follows only when a freeze has not secured what the outcome requires.
- **Freezes end:** a freeze lasts no longer than the outer bound of the matter's tier under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline), counted from when it is imposed. The end date is set when it is imposed. An extension needs a forum review with reasons before the freeze ends, and each extension lasts no longer than that same tier bound.

<a id="42-the-protected-minimum"></a>
#### 4.2 The Protected Minimum

Except against a sentient that is an active safety threat in a standoff under [§5.5 Pace: Active Attack and Standoff](#standoff), where everything may be frozen, no step may take or freeze what a sentient needs to meet survival essentials, for themselves and those who depend on them ([Article III-A](core_06.000_rights_part_a.md) (*Survival*)). The protected minimum has two parts, and a step must respect both:

- **Protected kinds:** a home, basic food and water, medical needs, the tools a sentient needs to earn a living, and the care of dependents; and
- **A protected amount:** a floor amount of money, published by the adopter, that is never frozen or seized.

The issuing forum may raise either part for a particular case, but never lower it, except as force level 2 of the force ladder allows in a standoff. Even then, what dependents and other occupants need stays protected. For an institution, the protected minimum is what keeps essential services running for those who rely on them.

<a id="43-seizure-third-parties-and-return"></a>
#### 4.3 Seizure, Third Parties, and Return

A seizure respects the claims of others, profits no one who carries it out, and returns anything taken in excess. Assets being used for violence or active harm are the exception.

- <a id="431-assets-used-for-violence-or-active-harm"></a>**Assets used for violence or active harm:** these are handled differently from other assets:
  - **What counts:** an asset that is being used, or is about to be used, for violence or other active harm, such as a weapon, a motor vehicle or drone used in an attack, a system running an attack, or funds paying for one.
  - **Taken at once:** such an asset may be seized or disabled at once. The freeze-first rule in [§4.1 Freezes](#41-freezes), prior notice to third parties, and the protected minimum in [§4.2 The Protected Minimum](#42-the-protected-minimum) do not delay it. Where a life is in immediate danger, the step may come first and the [Enforcement Record](#enforcement-record) after, as [§3.1 Whether to Act](#enforcement-record) allows.
  - **Claims heard after:** anyone who claims an interest is heard as soon as the harm has stopped.
  - **Return to those not involved:** an asset belonging to someone who took no part in the harm is returned, or its loss made good, once it is no longer needed to stop the harm.
  - **Never back to the user:** a weapon or other means of harm is never returned to the sentient that used it.
  - **Dependents:** dependents who relied on the asset are given survival essentials another way.
- **Third parties and dependents:** before a seizure, anyone who claims an interest in the asset is given notice and a chance to be heard. A seizure that would leave dependents without survival essentials is not taken.
- **No profit for the enforcement body:** money or assets taken go first to the remedy the outcome orders. They never go to the budget of the enforcement body that took them, and no one who takes part in a seizure is paid by its size. Anything left after the remedy is dealt with as the outcome or the issuing forum directs.
- **Valuation, accounting, and return:** assets are valued fairly, and an accounting of everything frozen, seized, sold, and paid out is kept and given to the affected party. Anything taken beyond what the outcome requires is returned.

<a id="44-hidden-assets-and-institutions"></a>
#### 4.4 Hidden Assets and Institutions

Two further rules govern assets that are hidden or moved, and assets held by institutions.

- **Hidden and moved assets:** look-through and anti-evasion steps follow [Chapter Twenty §4.4 Anti-Evasion and Look-through Authority](core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#44-anti-evasion-and-look-through-authority) and [Chapter Twenty-Five §6 Refusal, Stalling, and Evasion](core_25.000_enforcement.md#6-refusal-stalling-and-evasion).
- **Institutions:** for institutional sanctions, [CI-25.205](corpus_institutions/ci_25.205_institutional_accountability_sanctions.md#ci-25205-institutional-accountability-and-sanctions) (*Institutional accountability and sanctions*) applies, read with this section.

<a id="5-potential-violence-and-force"></a>
### 5. Potential Violence and Force

*In plain terms: where enforcement could turn violent, the risk is checked first and talking comes first. Force is a last resort, used only by trained enforcement bodies that are allowed to use it, and only as much as it takes. Force is never used to collect money, and no one is ever killed to carry out an outcome. Every use of force is recorded and independently reviewed, and anyone hurt is cared for.*

This section governs any use or threat of physical force, and any carrying out of an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable.

<a id="51-who-may-use-force"></a>
#### 5.1 Who May Use Force

Only enforcement bodies authorized and trained for it may use force, and this chapter's floors bind every one of them. Where the authority comes from depends on the adopter:

- **An adopter under another sovereign's law:** where the adopting body is subject to the law of a nation, state, or other external jurisdiction, force is used only by enforcement bodies that already hold that authority under that law. The issuing forum asks them to act, and the adopter arranges in advance how such requests will be made and met. Nothing in this chapter gives the adopter or its bodies a power to use force that the external law does not give.
- **A sovereign adopter:** where the adopting body is not subject to an external jurisdiction, it may name and train its own enforcement body under this chapter, with the separation of duties in [Chapter Eight](core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-eight-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties), published rules on its use of force, and independent oversight of every use.

How this Constitution relates to external law is set out in [Chapter Thirty §6 Relation to Applicable External Law](core_30.000_expansion_supremacy.md#6-relation-to-applicable-external-law).

<a id="52-before-force"></a>
#### 5.2 Before Force

Force comes last; the risk is checked and talking is tried first.

- **Risk check first:** before carrying out an outcome where violence is reasonably foreseeable, the enforcement body assesses the risk to everyone present and plans how to lower it.
- **De-escalation first:** the enforcement body tries communication, time, distance, and other non-forceful steps first, and keeps trying them while it is safe to do so.
- **Last resort and least force:** force is used only when no non-forceful step will carry the outcome out or prevent imminent harm, and only as much force as that requires. It stops as soon as the need ends.

<a id="53-limits-on-force"></a>
#### 5.3 Limits on Force

Some uses of force are never allowed:

- **Never to collect money:** force is never used only to collect money or assets.
- **No killing:** no one is killed to carry out an outcome, and killing is never a justice measure ([Article XXI](core_06.030_rights_part_d.md#article-xxi-justice-after-verified-violation) (*Justice After Verified Violation*)). Force that risks death (force level 5) may be used only where someone faces an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury from the sentient, and no lower level of force could prevent it in time. It stops the moment that danger ends, and every such use gets a full independent review as a possible violation.

<a id="54-the-force-ladder"></a>
#### 5.4 The Force Ladder

Non-lethal force is preferred to force that risks injury, and indirect force is preferred to force applied directly to the body. There is one ladder for every situation; what changes is how fast it is climbed ([§5.5 Pace: Active Attack and Standoff](#55-pace-active-attack-and-standoff)). Force moves up a force level only when the force level below has failed or plainly cannot work:

1. **Presence and containment:** a visible, trained presence, a perimeter, time, and distance, so the sentient cannot leave or harm others while talking continues. In a standoff, this force level also includes:
   - **Info-Sphere and mobility limited:** the sentient's [Info-Sphere](core_05.055_band_participation.md#info-sphere) access is limited to emergency services and the enforcement contact for the matter, so that they cannot use it to plan or coordinate violence. Travel documents, transport passes and bookings, and the motor vehicles and other transport the sentient controls are suspended, and transport and border offices are given notice, so that they cannot flee. Nothing here stops the sentient from reaching the surrender point or emergency care.
   - **Demand to surrender:** through that enforcement channel, the sentient is told clearly to turn themselves in, that they must come unarmed, how to do so safely, and every step that will follow if they do not. Any talks about surrender go through the same channel.
2. **Indirect means:** steps that act on the surroundings rather than the body, such as barriers, nets, disabling a car, drone, or other device, light and sound, and remotely stopping the systems the sentient is using. No system may be stopped that keeps anyone alive, such as medical equipment, without the same care as force that risks injury. In a standoff, this force level also includes, in this order:
   - **Freeze above the protected minimum and power cut:** all access to resources is frozen except the protected minimum, and power to where the sentient is staying is cut.
   - **Full freeze and water cut:** the protected amount is frozen too, and water to where the sentient is staying is cut.
3. **Non-lethal direct means:** restraint and incapacitation that carry a low risk of injury, such as tranquilizer darts and similar agents, used with medical support on hand to manage dose and reaction.
4. **Force that risks injury:** only to stop serious harm to others that the lower force levels cannot stop, and with medical care ready.
5. **Force that risks death:** only against an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury (see *No killing* in [§5.3 Limits on Force](#53-limits-on-force)), never to carry out an outcome.

- **Regular options:** these force levels are regular, expected options: a enforcement body that may face violence must have force levels 1 to 3 available, trained, and tried before it risks injury or death.
- **Sentients that are not biological:** for a sentient that runs on a digital or other non-biological substrate, the same force levels apply in the same order and under the same limits, read this way:
  1. **Containment:** isolating the sentient so they cannot act on others, while communication continues.
  2. **Indirect means:** cutting their outside access, such as network connections, actuators, and accounts.
  3. **Non-lethal direct means:** pausing their execution with their full state preserved.
  4. **Force that risks injury:** forced modification or partial rollback, only to stop serious harm to others, and only after a complete copy of their prior state is preserved ([Article VI-B, change while status is contested](core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#vi-b-change-while-contested)).
  5. **Force that risks death:** irreversible deletion or destruction, only under the same imminent-threat rule as force level 5 above.

<a id="55-pace-active-attack-and-standoff"></a>
#### 5.5 Pace: Active Attack and Standoff

The ladder is climbed at one of two speeds, depending on whether the danger is happening now:

- <a id="active-attack"></a>**Active attack:** where the sentient is attacking, or tries to take others with them, the enforcement body moves up as fast as the danger requires, including to tranquilizer darts and similar agents (force level 3) and, against an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury, to force that risks death (force level 5). No Enforcement Record is needed first; it follows as soon as safety allows, as [§3.1 Whether to Act](#enforcement-record) provides.
- <a id="standoff"></a>**Standoff:** where a forum has found that a sentient is an active safety threat and has ordered them to turn themselves in, and the sentient refuses but is not attacking, the issuing forum may authorize the standoff steps in an [Enforcement Record](#enforcement-record). Its material facts include the forum's determination that the sentient is an active safety threat and its refusal to surrender. A report or allegation alone is not enough. Containment runs from the first step, so that the sentient cannot harm others meanwhile. A standoff becomes an active attack the moment the sentient attacks.

In a standoff, the ladder moves up only on these terms:

- **One Enforcement Record, standard steps:** a single Enforcement Record with reasons authorizes the ladder. After that, the enforcement body does not need a new Enforcement Record for each step. It goes up one step at a time. Before each new step, it tells the sentient what is coming, and it waits at least the prompt-review window of the matter's tier since the last step. Only when a life is in immediate danger may the forum skip a step or go straight to force.
- **Review and challenge:** the issuing forum reviews the whole ladder at every prompt-review interval while it lasts. The sentient, or anyone on their behalf, may contest the Standing Record on which the [Enforcement Record](#enforcement-record) rests, under [Chapter Twenty-Three §4.5 Contests of Standing Records](core_23.000_forum.md#45-contests-of-standing-records); a step itself is not open to challenge.
- **Holding and pausing:** the enforcement body does not move up a step while it is negotiating in good faith under [§3.2 How to Act](#negotiation-within-normal-limits) (*Negotiation within normal limits*), or while the next step would reach anyone other than the sentient ([§5.6 Protecting Life and Others](#56-protecting-life-and-others)). It pauses the ladder and returns to the forum only when a material fact named in the [Enforcement Record](#enforcement-record) changes materially.

<a id="56-protecting-life-and-others"></a>
#### 5.6 Protecting Life and Others

No step may reach anyone but the sentient, and no cut is left to take a life:

- **No one else pays:** before Info-Sphere access or mobility is limited or power or water is cut, the enforcement body confirms that no one else is affected, or moves dependents, children, and other occupants to safety first. A cut that would reach neighbours, or anyone who is not the threat, is not made.
- **Cuts go ahead, watched:** a cut is made even where it brings hardship or risk to the sentient. It is never left to kill: the enforcement body watches for risk to life, including through medical equipment, medication storage, or dangerous heat or cold, and never waits it out.
- **Welfare examinations:** from the power cut onward, the sentient's health and welfare are examined at every review and whenever a risk to life is suspected. Where a real risk to life appears, the enforcement body does not restore what was cut. It moves up the force ladder to take the sentient in, with medical support, before the risk becomes death, and then gives care under [§5.7 How It Ends](#57-how-it-ends) (*Care on surrender*).
- **Digital sentients:** before power is cut to a sentient that runs on a digital or other non-biological substrate, a complete copy of their state is preserved, so that the cut cannot become deletion ([Article VI-B, change while status is contested](core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#vi-b-change-while-contested)).

<a id="57-how-it-ends"></a>
#### 5.7 How It Ends

Not every use of the ladder ends in surrender. It ends in one of four ways, and every step, and how the ladder ended, is entered on the Forum Case Record:

- **Ends on surrender:** every step ends the moment the sentient surrenders, and the protected minimum is restored at once.
- <a id="surrender-is-unarmed"></a>**Surrender is unarmed:** a sentient surrenders only by coming unarmed and following the instructions they were given for doing so safely. A sentient who comes armed, or reaches for a weapon, has not surrendered: the ladder does not end, and the enforcement body may meet the threat with force under [§5.5 Pace: Active Attack and Standoff](#active-attack) (*Active attack*). That force stays within [§5.3 Limits on Force](#53-limits-on-force), and using more than the threat requires is mal-enforcement.
- **Care on surrender:** at the surrender point, medical treatment the sentient needs, and anything else urgent such as water, food, warmth, or medication, is given at once.
- **Taken in by force:** once the sentient is taken in, the ladder ends and *Care on surrender* applies as if they had surrendered.
- **Death:** where the sentient dies, whether by force that risks death or as an unintended result of force used to stop an active attack, the ladder ends, and [§5.8 When Someone Dies](#58-when-someone-dies) applies.
- **Ended by the forum:** the ladder ends when the issuing forum withdraws the [Enforcement Record](#enforcement-record) after a material change, or when a forum hearing a contest of the Standing Record ends reliance on it.

<a id="58-when-someone-dies"></a>
#### 5.8 When Someone Dies

A death during enforcement, whether from force that risks death or as an unintended result of force used to stop an active attack, is handled this way:

- **Preserve:** the scene and all evidence, including recordings, are preserved, and the enforcement body that used the force does not investigate itself.
- **Report and review:** the death is reported at once to the issuing forum and to an independent review body outside the enforcement body's Material Control Line, which reviews it as a possible violation inside the prompt-review window of the matter's tier.
- **Step back:** those who used the force step back from force duties until that review ends. Stepping back is not a violation finding against them.
- **Family and dignity:** the sentient's dependents and family are told at once and offered support, and the remains, or for a digital sentient their preserved state, are treated with dignity.
- **Outcome of the review:** a death that resulted from force used within [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force) is not a violation by those who used it. A death that resulted from force beyond [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force) is mal-enforcement, and is handled as a possible violation like any other.
- **Learning:** every death is also referred for [§6 Root-Cause Evaluation](#6-root-cause-evaluation), to ask whether it could have been prevented.

<a id="59-record-review-and-care"></a>
#### 5.9 Record, Review, and Care

Every use of force is recorded and reviewed, and anyone hurt is cared for.

- **Record and review of every use of force:** every use or threat of force is recorded and goes to independent review, whatever the result. Force that went beyond [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force) is mal-enforcement under [Chapter Twenty-Five §7 Delivered as Found, and No Further](core_25.000_enforcement.md#mal-enforcement).
- **Care after force:** anyone hurt is given medical and other care at once, and later support as needed, at the cost of the enforcement body that used the force where the force went beyond [§5 Potential Violence and Force](#5-potential-violence-and-force).

<a id="6-root-cause-evaluation"></a>
### 6. Root-Cause Evaluation

*In plain terms: when enforcement keeps failing in the same way, someone independent finds out why. They look at design, incentives, and capacity, not just at the individuals involved. What they find is published, and a plan to fix it gets deadlines.*

This section governs referrals under [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](core_25.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement) for patterns of enforcement failure.

- **Triggers:** a root-cause evaluation opens when a forum refers a pattern under [Chapter Twenty-Five §8 Routing to Special Enforcement](core_25.000_enforcement.md#8-routing-to-special-enforcement): the same kind of outcome repeatedly missing its time limits, or refusals, stalling, or mal-enforcement recurring at the same body or across the same class of systems or institutions. Any affected party or protected reporter may ask a forum to refer.
- **Automatic triggers:** an evaluation also opens without a referral when:
  - the same body misses time limits, or has refusals, stalling, or mal-enforcement findings, in more than a published share of its cases over a review period. Each adopter publishes the share and the period, and the threshold may not be set so high that a recurring failure escapes evaluation; or
  - any step of enforcement causes a death or serious bodily injury.

  The published share may be no higher than one in ten cases of the same kind at one body, over a period no longer than the **root-cause review period** of one year; an adopter may set it lower.
- **Independent evaluator:** the evaluation is led by a body independent of the one whose failure is in question, under the anti-self-judging rule in [Chapter Twenty-Three §5](core_23.000_forum.md#5-transfer-consolidation-and-anti-self-judging--continuity-and-anti-capture) (*Transfer, Consolidation, and Anti-Self-Judging*).
- **What it looks at:** design, incentives, funding and capacity, training, data, and the rules themselves, as well as the conduct of individuals. Its purpose is to find what to change so the failure stops, not to find someone to blame. Individual accountability continues on its own path.
- **Findings and plan:** findings are published, with personal information protected. Where they show a cause, the evaluator recommends a corrective plan, and the forum that referred the pattern orders the plan with deadlines.
- **Durability link:** a finding that remedy capacity is missing or failing is a [Chapter Twenty §4.2 Remedy-System Durability](core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability) failure, and a capacity-failure claim may also be heard under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5.5 Capacity Failure Is Heard Outside the Starved Body](core_23.000_forum.md#55-capacity-failure-is-heard-outside-the-starved-body).
- **Feedback:** where a cause lies in this Constitution's own text, the finding is sent to the amendment process under [Chapter Twenty-Nine](core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md) as a proposal for change.
