*In plain terms: Question 2 measures the verified facts from Question 1. It asks **how good was the contribution?** or **how bad was the violation?** This is where the chapter first introduces the two formal measurement axes: the **Contribution Axis** for verified help and the **Violation Axis** for verified harm. The axes remain separate; they are not two sides of a net score.*

To decide which slot someone's contribution or violation belongs in, this chapter measures impact using a **Lifespan Equivalent Unit (LEQU)**. One LEQU roughly equals saving or destroying one sentient's entire lifetime of wellbeing. This is not about counting bodies or dollars; it asks whether an action meaningfully changed the constitutional community's wellbeing. The same unit works for any sentient being, human or otherwise.

<a id="71-lequ-baseline"></a>

#### 7.1 LEQU baseline

A **Lifespan Equivalent Unit** (**LEQU**) is the shorthand unit for that full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit or loss when adopted implementation needs a numeric calibration.

- The unit is substrate-agnostic.
- A current adopter may use an ordinary human lifespan as a calibration example for biological-human contexts, but the binding baseline is sentient and constitutional, not species-bound.

A **full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit** means verified benefit comparable to preserving, restoring, or freeing one full sentient lifespan of non-trivial, rights-consistent wellbeing. A **full-life-equivalent constitutional loss** means verified harm, waste, destruction, foreclosure, deprivation, or risk realization comparable to destroying, wrongfully consuming, or preventing one full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit.

<a id="72-limits-on-using-lequ"></a>

#### 7.2 Limits on using LEQU

The following limits apply whenever LEQU is used.

- **Not a net-score rule:** You cannot "cancel out" violations with contributions or vice versa. Each axis stands alone.
- **Not a mandatory human-life metric:** The LEQU is sentient-generic; it applies to any conscious being, not just humans.
- **Not an accusation metric:** Allegations, intake tags, routing decisions, reputation, disfavored status, or moral dislike do not supply Contribution Axis benefit or Violation Axis severity without verified inputs under **section 2.5** and calibration under **section 12**.
- **Not a license to trade rights:** Claims framed as efficiency, productive capacity, innovation, burden reduction, security, retaliation, emergency response, or institutional necessity must be discounted or rejected where they depend on:
  - coercion;
  - discriminatory burden shifting;
  - ecological depletion;
  - hidden unpaid work;
  - avoidable opacity;
  - Rights-Floor narrowing;
  - capture;
  - misleading proxies;
  - loss of contestability; or
  - unreviewable aggregation.

<a id="73-question-2-only--scope-and-routing"></a>

#### 7.3 Question 2 only — scope and routing

This part covers Question 2 only, the measuring step. It takes facts that are already verified (Question 1) and asks how good a contribution was or how bad a violation was.

- Start from the facts already verified under **section 2** and written into the standing record under **section 4**, and measure them using the dimensions in **section 8**.
- Then give each kind of help or harm its standard name from the catalogs: the **benefit-route** catalog for help (**section 9.1**) and the **harm-route** catalog for harm (**section 9.2**).
- Each name also tells you how much that kind of help or harm counts toward *how good* or *how bad*.
- The rules for what follows from a benefit are on the [benefit side](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances), and the rules for what follows from a harm are on the [harm side](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks).
- Question 3 decides what happens next. Start with [Chapter Twelve §3 Descriptor integration and attachment normalization](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#3-descriptor-integration-and-attachment-normalization).

<a id="74-sentience-status-bridge"></a>

#### 7.4 Sentience-status bridge

Measuring standing does **not** decide whether an entity is a **sentient**.

- Disputes about status go to [Sentience Status Adjudication](core_05.055_band_participation.md#sentience-status-adjudication) and [Article VI-B](core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-vi-b-sentience-status-adjudication-floor) (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*). They do not go to LEQU scores or classification records.
- While a status dispute is open under [Contested-Sentient Life](core_05.055_band_participation.md#contested-sentient-life), **Article VI-B** (*Sentience-Status Adjudication Floor*) includes the entity by default: it can still be measured and can still file a standing record until the dispute is decided, and operators must not treat the open dispute alone as a reason to deny it standing.
- If the entity is found to be a [Sentient](core_05.055_band_participation.md#sentient), or its status is narrowed or restored after adjudication in a decision that can itself be reviewed:
  - the standing record's subject is updated from that point on, and
  - a wrongful exclusion must not be backdated to erase the entity's eligibility to be measured in the meantime, except through [Redress and Remediation](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation).

<a id="8-magnitude-and-severity-input-dimensions"></a>

### 8. Magnitude and severity input dimensions

*In plain terms: this section lists what to examine when measuring a verified contribution (**section 8.1**) or a verified violation (**section 8.2**). Each axis is measured on its own. The shared scaling rules are in **section 10.2**, and the slot comes from the scale in **section 12**.*

The slot scale measures how big a constitutional outcome is and what kind of outcome it is. It counts only outcomes that have been verified.

**What the scale counts, and how that ties to the Constitution's core tests:**

- **[Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims):** both axes measure effect on **Flourishing** and **Continuity**. The Contribution Axis moves them forward. The Violation Axis moves them backward.
- **[Two Fundamental Questions](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-fundamental-questions):** each axis supplies the verified facts for one question. Answering one does not answer the other, so the two axes are never netted against each other.
  - **First question:** the Contribution Axis supplies the verified facts. Does the system reward conduct that helps sentients flourish and endure, tells the truth, and repairs harm?
    - **Counts:** verified constitutional benefit, including verified risk reduction.
    - **Does not count:** raw output, prestige, wealth, how much something is used, speed, or how big the institution is.
  - **Second question:** the Violation Axis supplies the verified facts. Does the system reward conduct that unjustifiably harms, misleads, excludes, delays, wastes, or shifts costs onto others?
    - **Counts:** verified constitutional loss, harm, waste, lost options (foreclosure), danger, or risk escalation.
    - **Does not count, on their own:** a disfavored status, allegations, or moral dislike.
- **[Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad):**
  - **Oversight and accountability:** "verified" means a named actor answers for the finding, and no party verifies its own claim.
  - **Timeliness:** avoidable delay is noted as its own fact, not folded into the size of the loss.

##### 8.1.1 How the way a benefit was produced affects what counts

Check this before you pick a Contribution Axis slot:

| **What you find** | **What it does to the benefit** |
| --- | --- |
| Prompt, verified fixing of a problem after being told about it | May count as **Diligence** (careful, responsible conduct) when it produces real, demonstrable benefit. |
| Harm pushed onto others, or a burden that was hidden | Caps the benefit credited to the disputed pathway (the specific route the benefit is credited to), or rules it out. |
| Verified capture risk (the arrangement could be taken over by narrow interests), structural dependency (sentients are built into relying on it), or exit and lock-in design (it is hard to leave) | Limits the credit for good stewardship. |
| Verified repair of an accessibility or participation barrier | May raise how much the benefit matters (its materiality) and support the **Access and Inclusion** benefit-route descriptor under **section 9.1**. |
| A significant reward that came from an incentive setup that works against the Constitution, from anti-constitutional conduct, or from a materially flawed process | Does not count as benefit by default. In that case, apply [Chapter Twelve §5.4 Special violation rules](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#54-special-violation-rules) and the Adoption Transition limit in [Article XXVIII-A](core_06.020_rights_part_e.md#incentive-alignment-adoption-transition-carve-out) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*). |

##### 8.1.2 What Question 2 produces

Write these in the contribution standing record. They are measurements, not standing effects (the consequences that Question 3 decides):

- The **Contribution Axis slot** (`s` = 1–9) and its main band: **C-BL** (baseline), **C-PC** (positive), **C-SP** (stewardship-positive), or **C-CH** (champion).
- The **benefit-route descriptors** that apply, stacked together.
- The inputs that set the starting point for how the contribution is recognized, under [Chapter Twelve §6 Contribution consequences second](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#6-contribution-consequences-second).

##### 8.2.1 How the way a harm was caused is recorded

Keep this separate from how big the harm was:

| **What you find** | **How to record it** |
| --- | --- |
| Avoidable delay in responding or escalating, where there was a duty to act and the capacity to do so | Recorded as a separate fact about conduct. Any extra loss the delay caused is counted in the LEQU estimate once. |
| An independently verified injury to a forum's integrity (damage to the fairness of the forum) | Counted in the LEQU estimate exactly once. Dishonest omission, deliberately failing the recusal process, coercion, danger to liberty, and other conduct facts stay separate descriptors and do not move the slot. |
| **Obstruction of accountability** (verified interference with the machinery that lets anyone responsible be held answerable, such as evidence integrity, verification, forum process, standing records, remedy windows, or contest and redress paths) | Stays a separately recorded conduct fact. Any extra loss it caused on the named pathway is counted in the LEQU estimate once, but the obstruction label itself does not move the slot. |
| Deception, concealment, evasion, repeating the conduct after notice, reckless disregard, structural enablement (setting things up so harm is easy or likely), or abuse of a major dependency imbalance (exploiting someone who relies heavily on you) | Each stays a separately recorded conduct fact. |
| Violence, coercion, manipulation, or danger to liberty | Stays separately recorded and may require stronger safeguards whatever the slot. |
| [Cruelty](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#cruelty) (verified suffering as an end in itself, or gratuitous or degrading harm beyond what was necessary and proportionate) | Stays a separately recorded conduct fact. It may require stronger safeguards and limits on degrading remedies under [Chapter Twelve §4 Violation, correction, and prevention](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#4-violation-correction-and-prevention), but it does not move the slot. |
| Diffusion (how responsibility is spread out: who played which causal role, held which authority or control, could foresee the harm, benefited, concealed it, or could have prevented it) | It is not part of **sentient scope**, and it does not permit blaming anyone for belonging to a group. |
| Going along with a harm or failing to resist it | May support a finding of duty-based, aggravated, or collective-accountability character only when the link to that specific actor required by **sections 4.1 and 4.3** is verified. |
| Knowingly accepting or keeping a significant reward that works against the Constitution, without timely protected reporting (reporting that is shielded from retaliation) | Recorded under [Chapter Twelve §5.4 Special violation rules](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#54-special-violation-rules), subject to [Article XXVIII-A](core_06.020_rights_part_e.md#incentive-alignment-adoption-transition-carve-out) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*). Only the verified constitutional loss it causes affects the slot. |

**Misaligned-reward boundary.** This chapter only measures how a verified misaligned reward (a reward that works against the Constitution) affects the Contribution Axis or the Violation Axis. [Chapter Twelve §5.4 Special violation rules](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#54-special-violation-rules) sets out the reporting duty, the exclusions, the consequences of giving up the reward (forfeiture), the correction duties, and the routing rules.

### 9. Route descriptor catalogs and measurement roles

*In plain terms: the two catalogs below name the kinds of verified help (**section 9.1**) and harm (**section 9.2**) a standing record can carry. A descriptor says how the benefit or harm occurred; descriptors are stackable and subordinate to the slot.*

**Descriptor rule.** The two catalogs below, [§9.1 Contribution route descriptor catalog and measurement roles](#91-contribution-route-descriptor-catalog-and-measurement-roles) and [§9.2 Violation route descriptor catalog and measurement roles](#92-violation-route-descriptor-catalog-and-measurement-roles), are the official list of descriptors for both axes. A descriptor says **how** the verified benefit or harm happened. These rules apply:

- Descriptors stack, and no descriptor excludes another: one standing record may carry several where the verified facts warrant.
- They rank below the slot: the slot says how big the benefit or harm was, and descriptors add detail about how it happened but never replace it.
- The listed descriptors are a base, not the full set; the catalog does not rule out other kinds of benefit or harm.
- Implementations may use stable identifiers (fixed IDs of their own) mapped to the catalog, but must not create a conflicting taxonomy whose categories are exclusive, so that a standing record can carry only one.
- Before the facts are verified, an alleged descriptor may support lawful evidence preservation, sorting the case, or temporary safeguards.
- Only a verified descriptor may be recorded as a Question 2 output or affect standing.

<a id="10-slot-grammar-and-shared-scaling"></a>

### 10. Slot grammar and shared scaling

<a id="651-what-the-slot-grammar-does"></a>
<a id="661-what-the-slot-grammar-does"></a>
<a id="671-what-the-slot-grammar-does"></a>
<a id="711-what-the-slot-grammar-does"></a>

*In plain terms: this section gives consistent names to nine possible positions on each of two separate axes and explains how impact scales across them. One axis records verified contribution; the other standing records verified violations. Sharing a slot number does not combine the axes or offset harm with help. The unified scale in **section 12** assigns both axes by proportional LEQU impact.*

The slot grammar uses a slot number, `s`, from **1** through **9**. Each axis has its own slot position:

- the **Contribution Axis** records positive contribution nature; and
- the **Violation Axis** records verified violation findings.

The same slot number can appear on both axes, but the axes remain separate. A contribution slot is not a violation slot, and a violation slot is not a contribution slot.

<a id="101-table-1--slot-display-labels"></a>

#### 10.1 Table 1 — slot display labels

**Table 1** names the nine slots and shows the Contribution Axis and Violation Axis display labels side by side. It supplies display language only; slot assignment occurs under the unified scale in **section 12**.

|  `s` | Contribution Axis display label | Violation Axis display label |
| ---: | --- | --- |
|    1 | **Minor Baseline Contribution** | **Minor Constitutional Impact** |
|    2 | **Small Baseline Contribution** | **Small Constitutional Impact** |
|    3 | **Substantial Positive Contribution** | **Substantial Constitutional Impact** |
|    4 | **Significant Positive Contribution** | **Significant Constitutional Impact** |
|    5 | **Serious Stewardship-Positive Contribution** | **Serious Constitutional Impact** |
|    6 | **Major Stewardship-Positive Contribution** | **Major Constitutional Impact** |
|    7 | **Recognized Champion** | **Severe Constitutional Impact** |
|    8 | **Distinguished Champion** | **Grave Constitutional Impact** |
|    9 | **Exemplary Champion** | **Catastrophic Constitutional Impact** |

The Contribution Axis uses baseline, positive, stewardship-positive, and champion labels. The Violation Axis uses neutral impact labels at every slot. The axes remain separate and do not create a net score or permit offset between contribution and violation records. A Violation Axis slot does not by itself classify anti-constitutional misconduct; [Chapter Thirteen](core_13.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-thirteen-anti-constitutional-misconduct) independently determines whether that anti-constitutional-misconduct designation (ACMD) attaches to a verified `s` = 7–9 violation.

Implementations may publish measurement units, evidence methods, and examples to help estimate full-life-equivalent benefit or loss. These materials are guidance only and may not override **section 12**, **Chapter One**, or the **Chapter Six** Rights Floor.

<a id="102-shared-impact-scaling-rules"></a>

#### 10.2 Shared impact-scaling rules

*In plain terms: impact is not measured by headcount alone. A profound benefit or loss affecting one sentient may be more significant than a shallow effect affecting many. Count, depth, vulnerability, duration, durability, reach, and constitutional character must be assessed together.*

Use the following rules to interpret both axes in **section 12**:

| Scaling dimension | Shared rule | Axis-specific application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Constitutional outcome magnitude (LEQU)** | Assess the integrated verified benefit or loss. LEQU is the primary magnitude anchor; raw headcount, money, output, or institutional size is not. | Contribution counts rights-consistent benefit; violation counts verified constitutional loss, harm, waste, foreclosure, or danger. |
| **Sentient scope** | Consider **count, depth, and vulnerability together**. No slot uses headcount as an independent requirement. A sufficiently deep impact on one sentient may support the same slot as a broader but shallower impact where the integrated magnitude and the row's other criteria are equal; any express aggregate-LEQU or wide-scope criterion still must be met. | Dependency, reduced capacity, or unusual vulnerability may increase materiality. Group size alone does not establish contribution or severity. |
| **Temporal scope** | Consider duration, recurrence, durability, and, for loss, irreversibility. A short but transformative event may outweigh a long trivial effect; duration alone does not control. | Durable benefit supports higher contribution magnitude. Persistent or irreversible loss supports higher violation severity. |
| **Spatial or institutional scope** | Broader reach may increase magnitude, but cross-community or cross-institutional reach is not required where depth, duration, vulnerability, or constitutional criticality independently supports the slot. | Institutional repair may increase contribution magnitude; structural or systemic damage may increase violation severity. |
| **Constitutional quality or character** | Measure how the outcome was produced separately from how broadly it spread. Do not double-count the same fact. | Externalized harm, concealed burden, capture, or dependency may cap or disqualify claimed contribution. Duty, negligence, deception, coercion, violence, or structural enablement remains separately recorded as violation conduct character and does not move the impact slot. |
| **Rights Floors and critical safeguards** | Rights-Floor compliance is not traded against aggregate benefit or loss. | Compliance with a floor does not by itself establish high contribution. Material Rights-Floor defeat or critical safeguard failure may support higher violation severity even when few sentients are directly affected. |

The dimensions are not a checklist requiring every form of scale. Explain which dimensions controlled, which did not, and why.

- **Shared slot method:**
  - Estimate integrated verified benefit or loss in LEQU under Proportionality, then assign the one slot whose numeric band contains that magnitude.
  - The same thresholds apply on both axes.
  - Contribution slots retain the applicable baseline and constitutional-alignment requirements; satisfying a numeric threshold cannot make externalized or constitutionally defeated output count as contribution.
- **Character separation:** Keep applicable benefit-route, harm-route, conduct-quality, and conduct-character descriptors stacked in the standing record. Duty, negligence, deception, aggravation, coercion, violence, intent, or structural enablement may affect attribution, safeguards, or Question 3 consequences, but none raises or lowers the LEQU impact slot.
- **Anti-Constitutional Misconduct Review boundary:** Verified Violation Axis loss may reach `s` = 7–9 by impact whether or not anti-constitutional misconduct is established. Chapter Thirteen does not replace the impact assignment; it independently determines whether the corresponding ACMD attaches.

If a danger has not actually happened yet, record each of these separately:

- how likely it is;
- how long sentients would be exposed to it;
- how far it could reach;
- how uncertain the estimate is; and
- the worst outcome that is still believable.

Do not count that worst outcome as harm that has already happened (LEQU loss) unless adopted implementation has set a risk calibration (a method for converting risk into LEQU) that anyone can inspect.

Apply **Proportionality** to estimate integrated LEQU impact from depth, sentient scope, vulnerability, duration, durability or irreversibility, reach, and constitutional criticality. These dimensions shape the LEQU estimate; they are not independent slot bonuses.

- Use **Table 2** after Question 1 confirms what happened, Question 2 measures the verified benefit or loss, **sections 7–9 and 10.2** are applied, and the constitutional sources required by **section 11** are identified.
- Assign one slot from the integrated LEQU estimate.
  - Explain how each material scaling dimension affected the estimate and why omitted dimensions did not control.
  - Every assignment must satisfy:
    - traceability;
    - auditability;
    - contestability; and
    - the applicable Rights Floor.
  - Contribution must also satisfy non-externalization and constitutional alignment.
- Record conduct-character facts as separately traceable descriptors. They may control attribution, review intensity, safeguards, remedy, or a Chapter Thirteen designation; they affect the slot only to the extent that verified consequences alter the integrated LEQU impact.
- Fully assess the verified consequences of the action or course of conduct through the material scaling dimensions, including:
  - physical injury;
  - psychological trauma;
  - coercive effects;
  - deprivation or danger to liberty;
  - cumulative harm; and
  - other constitutionally cognizable benefit or loss.
- Do not:
  - Raise or lower the slot merely because an event or course of conduct is characterized by:
    - duty;
    - negligence;
    - intent;
    - concealment;
    - repetition;
    - aggravation;
    - coercion;
    - violence;
    - liberty danger;
    - correctability; or
    - a preferred consequence.
  - Infer conduct character from magnitude or substitute conduct character for verified magnitude.

**Table 2** lists the nine slots in order. <a id="13-standard-contribution-measures"></a>

### 13. Standard contribution measures

*In plain terms: for tasks sentients do again and again, such as a litter pickup or a creek-restoration work unit, an adopter may publish a default Question 2 measurement so each verified instance does not need its own estimate. The default is a floor, not a cap: work that verifiably does more is measured on what it did, and work that falls short is measured on what was verified. Defaults are updated on a published schedule, can be contested, and never create a duty to do the task.*

Adopted implementation may publish **standard contribution measures**: a default Question 2 estimate — LEQU, scope, and benefit-route descriptors — for verified performance of a defined recurring task at a defined scope, such as a litter pickup along a stated segment, a waterway-restoration work unit, a land-recovery plot, or a maintenance rotation. Standards make routine stewardship legible and worth repeating under [Chapter One §3.2](core_01.000_values_principles.md#32-recognition-reinforcement-and-aspiration) (*Recognition, Reinforcement, and Aspiration*) without a bespoke estimate for every instance. A standard is a measurement helper under **section 4.5**, and:

- **How a standard applies**
  - It applies only to work verified under Question 1 through **section 5**; a standard never substitutes for verification that the task was done, to the stated scope, by the named subject.
  - It is a **floor for standard performance, not a cap**:
    - Work whose verified impact exceeds the standard's assumed impact — a better method, a more durable result, less externalized burden, a wider or deeper benefit — is measured on its actual verified impact under **section 10.2**, so the incentive to do the task better than the standard stays alive.
    - Work that verifiably falls short of the standard's assumed scope or durability is measured on what was verified, not on the label.
- **How a standard is revised**
  - It is **revisited on a published cadence and re-baselined as efficiency improves**, so the standard tracks what the task now costs and produces rather than freezing yesterday's effort as today's credit.
  - A revision applies going forward, and records already opened keep their measurement basis under **section 4.4** unless that basis was wrong when recorded.
  - A revision must be evidence-based, published, and contestable.
  - A revision may not be used to suppress recognition of a class of work, a community, or a group, or to ratchet a standard down faster than the verified efficiency gain it rests on ([Chapter One §19 Incentive Alignment and System Capture](core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#19-incentive-alignment-and-system-capture)).
- **Safeguards against misuse and over-reach**
  - A standard creates no quota, no duty to perform, and no competency bar under [Chapter Twelve §6.2 Competency bars and clearances](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances); **section 2.2** *Silence is the default* controls, and a sentient who never performs a standard task has no record for that reason.
  - The office that sets or revises a standard holds the direction-and-policy seat under **section 5.1** *Separation of duties*; it is not the office that verifies performance against the standard, and neither is a party whose recognition the standard measures.

Standards may not override **section 12**, **Chapter One**, or the **Chapter Six** Rights Floor. A reference method and a worked schedule live in [LEQU_CALIBRATION_REFERENCE.md](implementation/LEQU_CALIBRATION_REFERENCE.md) (*process support; cannot assign a live record*).

### 14. Measurement illustrations

*In plain terms: these illustrations begin with Question 1's verified facts, then show how **sections 8 and 9** measure them. They record Question 2 outputs — not standing effects. Slot assignment follows **sections 8–11**; Question 3 is answered in [Chapter Twelve](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#chapter-twelve-standing-effects-and-integration). For the Question 3 continuation of Examples 1 and 2 below, see [Chapter Twelve §10.1 Informal ecological stewardship / competency clearance](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#101-informal-ecological-stewardship-competency-clearance) and [§10.2 Ecological negligence with concealment](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#102-ecological-negligence-with-concealment).*

<a id="141-example-1--riparian-cooperative-restoration-contribution"></a>

#### 14.1 Example 1 — Riparian cooperative restoration (contribution)

**Situation.** After spring flooding, four sentients in an informal riparian cooperative stabilize a degraded half-kilometer creek buffer between two downstream neighborhoods. The work is unpaid mutual aid — no municipal contract, no licensed-program enrollment, and no platform payout trail. A watershed council later asks whether the cooperative's verified repair should enter a **contribution standing record**.

**Verified inputs.** Soil-test logs, planting receipts, timestamped before/after habitat photos, and contestable neighbor attestations satisfy the verified-input gate under **section 2.5**. Allegations that one member previously dumped waste upstream remain a separate, unverified routing thread and do **not** enter this standing record.

**Seats under section 5.1:**

- **Who is involved**
  - The four members are the subjects and, because they seek recognition, the claimants.
  - The member who kept the planting log is its recorder and a witness, not its verifying authority.
  - The neighbors are beneficiaries whose attestations enter as inputs.
- **Who holds the seats**
  - The watershed council's designated body — independent of the repair and holding published authority to rely on the standing record — is the record-opening authority under *Informal and small-scope records*.
  - The council's separate record custodian enters and holds the verified standing record.
- **Separation of the seats**
  - The council may host both seats under its published lane map and independence safeguards.
  - Neither the authority, the custodian, nor any sentient may both verify and enter this standing record.
- **Opening and contest**
  - No forum is needed to open the standing record.
  - A contest of it, if one comes, goes to the council's challenge lane or a forum under **section 2.7**, not back to the verifying authority.

**Question 2 dimension pass** (contribution standing record):

| **Dimension** | **Verified assessment** |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutional outcome magnitude (LEQU) | Integrated benefit is material but below one LEQU — localized ecological gain, not civilizational scale |
| Sentient scope | Two neighborhoods with elevated flood vulnerability; modest sentient count; no dependency-asymmetric caretaking duty in this fact pattern |
| Temporal scope | Native plantings, berm repair, and erosion controls expected to endure multiple growing seasons |
| Spatial / institutional scope | Cross-neighborhood benefit along a shared watershed segment; informal coordination, not cross-institutional repair |
| Benefit-route stack | **Ecological Stewardship** + **Diligence** — documented planting plan, scheduled follow-up monitoring, and soil checks |
| Conduct-quality stack | Traceable work; runoff not externalized onto adjacent parcels; no concealed burden on downstream irrigators |

**Q2 outputs recorded:**

- The benefit came through two routes, recorded together: **Ecological Stewardship** (the creek repair) and **Diligence** (careful, documented work).
- The size and reach of the benefit are recorded and ready to be turned into a Contribution slot and label under **[section 12](#12-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes)**.
- Nothing changes in anyone's standing yet. This chapter only measures; standing effects are decided later.

<a id="142-example-2--watershed-steward-negligence-with-concealed-test-results-violation"></a>

#### 14.2 Example 2 — Watershed steward negligence with concealed test results (violation)

**Situation.** A sentient holds a community-trust contract to maintain pesticide-free buffer zones above three agricultural cooperatives' irrigation intakes. Regulator notice eight months earlier flagged failing retaining berms. The steward continued applying restricted herbicide through a subcontractor, submitted altered lab exports to the trust board, and told downstream growers that intake readings were "within normal variance."

**Verified inputs.** A forum produces a verified violation finding → an axis-pure **violation standing record** opens under **sections 2 and 4** with the preserved notice chain, authentic-versus-submitted lab comparison, and intake contamination readings across two growing seasons. The steward's later volunteer trash cleanup along the same creek is routed to a **linked contribution standing record** under **section 2.4**; it does **not** offset this violation record.

**Seats under section 5.1:**

- **Who is involved**
  - The steward is the subject and holds no seat.
  - The three cooperatives are the affected parties and, because they allege the harm, the claimants.
  - The trust board, which received the altered lab exports, is an affected party whose copy of the submitted exports enters as an input; it holds neither seat.
  - The regulator's notice is an input in the preserved notice chain, not a seat.
- **Who holds the seats**
  - The forum that verified the violation finding is the record-opening authority under **section 2.7**.
  - A separate record custodian — named by the forum's published designation, or the Backup Record Custodian where none is named — enters and holds the verified violation standing record.
- **Separation of the seats**
  - Neither the forum, the custodian, nor any sentient may both verify and enter this standing record.
  - The forum that verified this standing record does not hear the contest of it.
- **Opening and contest**
  - A forum is needed to open this standing record, because it opens on the forum's verified violation finding.
  - A contest of it, if one comes, goes to secondary review under **section 2.7** and [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_forum.md#chapter-fourteen-forums-and-jurisdiction), not back to the verifying forum.

**Question 2 dimension pass** (violation standing record):

| **Dimension** | **Verified assessment** |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutional outcome magnitude (LEQU) | Moderate integrated constitutional loss — crop risk, water insecurity, and remediation cost — below one LEQU destroyed |
| Sentient scope | Three cooperatives with irrigation dependency; **materiality** elevated in dependency-asymmetric settings |
| Temporal scope | Contamination and false assurance persisted across two growing seasons after notice |
| Spatial / institutional scope | Localized watershed harm with cross-cooperative reach; trust-contract stewardship role |
| Harm-route stack | **Ecological Misconduct** + **Negligent Misconduct** |
| Conduct-character stack | Negligence where maintenance duty existed; deception/concealment after notice |
| Conduct-character inputs | **Deception / concealment / evasion**; **Response timeliness / avoidable delay** (harm compounded after duty to act) |

**Q2 outputs recorded:**

- The harm came through two routes, recorded together: **Ecological Misconduct** (contaminating the watershed) and **Negligent Misconduct** (failing a maintenance duty).
- The steward's conduct after the regulator's notice is recorded as concealment: hiding the problem from the sentients who relied on the work.
- The same concealment and deception may also matter for Question 3, which decides what happens because of it, such as a stronger standing lock under [Chapter Twelve §4.2 Prevention — general standing locks](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks). **Section 15** lists this hand-off. The fact is recorded here and used there, but it is not counted twice.
- The steward's slow response after the notice is recorded as its own fact about conduct. Any extra harm it caused is counted once, in the size of the loss.
- Any later delay in starting or carrying out the remedy is a different fact for Question 3, under [Chapter Twelve §9.5 Timely implementation](core_12.000_standing_integration.md#95-timely-implementation). It must be established on its own and does not change this measurement.
- The size of the loss is recorded and ready to be turned into a Violation slot and label under **[section 12](#12-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes)**.
