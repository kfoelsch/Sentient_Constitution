##### 2.1.1 What the map does

Section 3 states the category rules for the two axes. This subsection gives the shared slot map used to display those categories side by side.

The map uses a slot number, `s`, from **1** through **9**. Each axis has its own slot position:

- the **Contribution Axis** records positive contribution state; and
- the **Violation Axis** records verified violation findings.

The same slot number can appear on both axes, but the axes remain separate. A contribution slot is not a violation slot, and a violation slot is not a contribution slot.

The display labels make the slot ranges visible without adding a separate category layer:

- `s` = 1–3 use **participant** / **non-compliance finding** labels;
- `s` = 4–6 use **steward** / **violation finding** labels; and
- `s` = 7–9 use **champion** / **anti-constitutional misconduct** labels.

For `s` = 1–6, the internal steps display as **minor**, **mid**, and **major**. For `s` = 7–9, the top-end labels change because those slots have stronger constitutional meaning.

##### 2.1.4 Separate records and no netting

*Contribution state* records baseline performance and verified uplift. *Violation nature* records adverse outcomes only when they are **documented in findings**. Allegations, intake tags, and routing decisions do not supply violation nature by themselves.

Each axis must rest on its own supported facts. Analysis and records must keep the axes **separately traceable**. No single entry is a net score, blended score, or tradeoff.

Any verified contribution record may coexist with verified violation findings, including findings in slots `s` = 7–9. Verified contribution may still be recorded, but it must not **offset**, **average down**, excuse, or **relabel** those findings.

The same sentient, institution, assessment, record, or bounded event may carry entries on both axes. A sentient, for example, may have several Contribution Axis records in different slots and a separate confirmed finding on the Violation Axis. An institutional assessment or event record may do the same.

The axes remain separate for classification and recordkeeping; they do not turn contribution into violation, or violation into contribution. `s` on one axis does not legally or evidentially fix `s` on the other. **Table 1** and **Table 2** supply shared indexing for reading and comparison. They are not a merged scorecard.

**Standing orientation (detail).** [Section 7.1 — Standing orientation by Violation Axis slot](core_06-06_standing_integration.md#standing-orientation-by-violation-slot) summarizes how baseline trust, positive credit, and elevated recognition interact with **verified violation findings** across the full `s` = 1 through 9 slot display. It also states the non-waiver rule for **verified** contribution and the cross-axis standing integration rules.

#### 2.3 Dispute-phase material, forums, and challenge protections

Alleged misconduct, unadjudicated claims, provisional routing tags, intake-only narratives, and other dispute-phase material do not supply violation nature for standing purposes by themselves.

#### 2.4 Coexistence and no-offset bridge

After **sections 2.2** and **2.3** identify verified standing inputs and separate forum-phase material, this subsection connects those inputs to the mechanics in **sections 7** and **8**.

The bridge rule is additive and non-substitutive. Adverse **violation** findings may constrain standing, roles, remedies, or safeguards, but they must not erase verified contribution records except where the findings themselves defeat the claimed contribution under the applicable evidence and review rules.

<a id="25-standing-cell-operational-underpinnings"></a>

#### 2.5 Standing-cell operational underpinnings

A **standing cell** is the bounded operational record that applies this chapter's categories to a defined subject, scope, time period, and decision context. It may describe a sentient, institution, system, assessment, event, role, or other constitutionally relevant unit, but it must not collapse distinct units or contexts into one undifferentiated status label. A **bounded event** is a finite occurrence or time-bounded episode used as the subject of a standing cell, as distinguished from an open-ended status label or from a **pattern** as the sole descriptor without a defined scope.

Where contribution or violation is produced by multiple actors, standing records may identify both a collective standing cell and separate actor-specific cells. Shared contribution credit should be distributed according to demonstrable role, time, effort, risk, skill, coordination, maintenance, and causal contribution. Broad distribution is favored where the benefit depended on many participants and individual shares cannot be cleanly separated. Shared violation findings must not be assigned by group membership alone; actor-specific adverse standing requires verified linkage to causal contribution, duty, authority, control, foreseeability, benefit, concealment, or feasible prevention capacity.

Each standing cell must state, at minimum:

- the assessed subject and functional scope, including relevant system boundaries, role boundaries, affected communities, and dependency relationships;
- the applicable time window, current review status, and whether the cell is provisional, final for a stated purpose, under challenge, or due for scheduled re-evaluation;
- the separate Contribution Axis entry, if any, including the primary band, slot `s` where assigned, evidence basis, materiality reasoning, and any adopted LEQU or comparable calibration used for transparent support;
- the separate Violation Axis entry, if any, including the finding authority or record basis, severity level, slot `s` where assigned, process / response character where applicable, and whether Chapter Seven controls final top-slot assignment;
- where contribution is shared, the allocation basis used for credit distribution, including how time, effort, coordination, maintenance, causal contribution, and less-visible support work were considered;
- where violation is shared or distributed, the verified basis for any actor-specific adverse standing, including causal role, duty, authority, control, foreseeability, benefit, concealment, or feasible prevention capacity;
- the records, witnesses, measurements, audits, forum decisions, or other verification materials that make the cell demonstrable, reviewable, and contestable under Chapters Two through Four; and
- the standing effect actually applied to trust, roles, recognition, restriction, safeguards, remediation, or review, with the no-offset rule visibly preserved.

Standing cells must be versioned when material facts, findings, review outcomes, remediation status, contribution evidence, implementation calibration, or constitutional rules change. A later version may update a cell prospectively, correct it retroactively where the record was wrong, or supersede it for a new decision context, but it must preserve the prior version's audit trail and explain the change.

Adopted implementation may define schemas, tags, thresholds, forms, dashboards, and review workflows for standing cells. Those materials must implement this subsection, not narrow it. They must keep human-readable category names, slot `s`, axis separation, evidence provenance, contestability, currentness, and calibration assumptions visible enough that future changes can be checked rather than hidden inside opaque scores.

##### 3.1.1 Constitutional-outcome baseline for slots

The slot scale measures constitutional outcome magnitude and character. On the Contribution Axis, it measures verified constitutional benefit, not raw output, prestige, wealth, utilization, speed, or institutional scale. On the Violation Axis, it measures verified constitutional loss, harm, waste, foreclosure, or danger, not disfavored status, allegations, or moral dislike alone.

For this subsection, a **full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit** means verified benefit comparable to preserving, restoring, or freeing one full sentient lifespan of non-trivial, rights-consistent wellbeing. A **Lifespan Equivalent Unit** (**LEQU**) is the shorthand unit for that full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit when adopted implementation needs a numeric calibration. The unit is substrate-agnostic. A current adopter may use an ordinary human lifespan as a calibration example for biological-human contexts, but the binding baseline is sentient and constitutional, not species-bound.

Full-life-equivalent benefits that increase standing may arise through survival or bodily-maintenance access preserved, caretaking, childcare, teaching, or trauma resolution that materially preserves wellbeing or development, severe avoidable burden removed, meaningful agency restored, productive capacity durably expanded, ecological or infrastructure risk reduced, or safety, truth, auditability, anti-capture, or challenge capacity strengthened. The claimed benefit counts only to the extent it remains traceable, non-externalized, and consistent with Safety, Truth, dignity, equal standing, substantive fairness, ecological integrity, intergenerational responsibility, auditability, and contestability. Externalized harms, coercive dependencies, Rights-Floor narrowing, or concealed burdens limit what may count as verified Contribution Axis benefit; they do not offset, reduce, or cure separate Violation Axis findings. This list is non-exhaustive and must be applied consistently with this Constitution, including its principles, articles, definitions, and competent forum procedures.

Use the following baseline when reading Contribution Axis slot movement:

| `s` | Contribution Axis constitutional-outcome baseline |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | **Baseline contribution:** the constitutional floor is met for the assessed scope, without unresolved material violation. |
| 2 | **Small verified uplift:** a limited but demonstrable improvement beyond baseline obligations. |
| 3 | **Material bounded uplift:** a noticeable local benefit to a sentient, small group, bounded institution, or defined system, short of cross-boundary stewardship. |
| 4 | **Durable stewardship:** recurring burden, risk, or harm is reduced, prevented, or repaired in a defined domain with observable durability. |
| 5 | **Major stewardship:** broad, repeated, or cross-system benefit creates meaningful resilience, repair, or capacity gain. |
| 6 | **Pre-champion stewardship:** large, durable benefit materially approaches a full-life-equivalent, or supplies unusually strong infrastructure for others to meet constitutional obligations without dependency abuse. |
| 7 | **Recognized champion:** at least one full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit, or comparable rare structural stewardship, is verified at material scope. |
| 8 | **Distinguished champion:** several full-life-equivalent constitutional benefits, or durable cross-institutional repair, prevention, or reliance that outlasts originating actors, is verified. |
| 9 | **Exemplary champion:** rare, durable constitutional-resilience gain at the widest material scope, including intergenerational, ecosystem, civilizational, or comparable structural benefit. |

A **full-life-equivalent constitutional loss** means verified harm, waste, destruction, foreclosure, deprivation, or risk realization comparable to destroying, wrongfully consuming, or preventing one full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit. Adopted implementation may express comparable loss in LEQU-equivalent terms for transparent calibration. It may arise through avoidable death or survival deprivation, bodily-maintenance denial, severe avoidable burden, coercive agency loss, Rights-Floor defeat, ecological or infrastructure damage, destroyed productive capacity, degraded Truth or Safety, capture of challenge pathways, or comparable constitutional harm. It is not a netting unit and does not permit positive contribution to offset violation findings. This list is non-exhaustive and must be applied consistently with this Constitution, including its principles, articles, definitions, and competent forum procedures.

Use the following baseline when reading Violation Axis slot movement:

| `s` | Violation Axis constitutional-outcome baseline |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | **Formal non-compliance:** verification, records, access, or process integrity is materially impaired, without demonstrated substantive loss at the assessed scope. |
| 2 | **Remedial substantive non-compliance:** material duty, harm, or rights failure requires repair, restoration, compensation, injunction, or comparable correction. |
| 3 | **Significant substantive non-compliance:** constitutional floors, supremacy, anti-evasion, or challenge-and-remedy access are materially violated. |
| 4 | **Duty-based or negligent-harm non-compliance:** duty, foreseeability, and feasible capacity existed, and preventable material harm, risk, or neglect is verified. |
| 5 | **Aggravated non-compliance:** worsening features such as repetition, evasion, concealment, recklessness, dependency abuse, or structural enablement materially intensify the finding. |
| 6 | **Coercive or punitive-process non-compliance:** culpability, coercion, violence, credible threat, liberty danger, or comparable social danger requires punitive-process or equivalent safeguards. |
| 7 | **Serious anti-constitutional misconduct:** at least one full-life-equivalent constitutional benefit is destroyed, wasted, wrongfully consumed, or foreclosed, or comparable critical harm to survival, agency, ecological integrity, institutional trust, recovery capacity, or constitutional enforceability is found. |
| 8 | **Grave anti-constitutional misconduct:** multiple full-life-equivalent losses, structural or cross-institutional constitutional damage, or Chapter Seven grave anti-constitutional misconduct is finally found. |
| 9 | **Pernicious anti-constitutional misconduct:** rare, intentional, systemic, intergenerational, civilizational, or constitution-subverting harm at the widest material scope, or Chapter Seven pernicious anti-constitutional misconduct is finally found. |

This baseline does not create a net-score rule, a mandatory human-life metric, or a license to trade rights against aggregate gains. Claims framed as efficiency, productive capacity, innovation, burden reduction, security, punishment, emergency response, or institutional necessity must be discounted or rejected where they depend on coercion, discriminatory burden shifting, ecological depletion, hidden unpaid work, avoidable opacity, Rights-Floor narrowing, capture, misleading proxies, loss of contestability, or unreviewable aggregation.

Adopted implementation may publish calibrated units, evidentiary methods, and examples for full-life-equivalent benefit or loss. Those materials must remain subordinate to this subsection, **sections 3.2.1 through 3.2.4**, **sections 3.3.1 through 3.3.7**, **section 7**, **Chapter One**, **Chapter Seven** where final **Violation Axis s = 7, 8, or 9** anti-constitutional misconduct assignment is at issue, and the **Chapter Nine** Rights Floor.

Remedial Substantive Non-Compliance is failure that materially violates required conditions in real-world operation where the dominant constitutional response is repair, restoration, compensation, injunctive constraint, or comparable civil correction.

The **section 3.3** ladder must be applied by the highest level whose criteria fit the findings.

| Distinction | Use the lower level when... | Move upward when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Formal** vs. **Remedial Substantive** | the problem materially impairs records, verification, access, or process, but substantive harm is not yet demonstrated. | real-world duty failure, rights burden, harm, or material operational failure is found and correction is required. |
| **Remedial Substantive** vs. **Significant Substantive** | the dominant **remedy** is repair, restoration, compensation, injunction, or comparable civil correction. | the finding materially violates constitutional floors, supremacy, anti-evasion discipline, or challenge-and-remedy access. |
| **Duty-Based / Negligent-Harm** vs. **Aggravated** | duty, foreseeability, and feasible capacity existed, but aggravating features are not found. | concealment, evasion, repetition after notice, reckless disregard, dependency abuse, or structural enablement is found. |
| **Coercive or Punitive-Process** vs. **section 5.2 character** | use **§3.3.6** when severity itself involves culpability, coercion, violence, credible threat, liberty danger, or comparable social danger. | use **§5.2** as an attached process / response character whenever punitive or liberty-restricting consequences are in play, even if another section 3.3 severity level also applies. |
