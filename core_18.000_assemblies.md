<br>

Chapter Eighteen is the constitutional owner of **what an assembly of a polity decides, how it decides, and what limits it**, including the executive it chooses, community referendums, and typed emergencies.

<a id="1-purpose-role-and-scope"></a>
### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this chapter says what a polity's assemblies are for: making general rules, setting tax policy and budgets, choosing and checking the executive, and handling emergencies, and what they may never do.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Twenty-Four sets the role, powers, procedure, and limits of the assemblies through which a polity adopter exercises governing authority.

**Role:** [Chapter Twenty-One](core_21.000_governance.md#chapter-twenty-one-constitutional-contract-legitimacy-authorization-and-stewardship) (*Constitutional Contract, Legitimacy, Authorization, and Stewardship*) says how governing authority is authorized and how members are selected. This chapter says what an assembly does with that authority once it sits.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter applies to every assembly that holds governing authority for a polity adopter, whatever its selection method. Where the adopter uses the tiered reference design of [CI-21.205 §8 Reference Design: Tiered Assemblies](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-8-reference-design-tiered-assemblies), the tier rules of section 4 apply as written. An adopter with another structure applies them to its own levels of government. The chapter covers:

- what assemblies decide (**section 3**);
- how work is divided between local, regional, and national tiers (**section 4**);
- how assemblies decide (**section 5**);
- executives (**section 6**);
- community referendums (**section 7**);
- emergencies (**section 8**); and
- the limits on every assembly (**section 9**).

<a id="2-why-assemblies-exist"></a>
### 2. Why Assemblies Exist

*In plain terms: someone has to make the general rules and set the budget, in public, and answer for it. An assembly does that, and keeps the executive in check.*

General rules and budgets bind everyone, so they must be made in the open by a body the community can see, question, and replace. Forums decide cases and Independent Bodies check and measure, but neither makes general rules. Without a standing assembly, that work drifts to executives and staff who answer to no one. This chapter puts it in an accountable body and fences it with the Rights Floor.

    - [Oversight](subcore_03.055_apex_oversight_leg.md#oversight): assemblies question and remove the executive, and forums review every assembly act;
  - [Two Constitutional Aims](core_00.000_preamble.md#two-constitutional-aims): both aims, which this chapter serves:
    - [Flourishing](core_00.000_preamble.md#flourishing): general rules and budgets are made close to the communities that live under them;
    - [Continuity](core_00.000_preamble.md#continuity): typed emergencies end on time and elections resume, so self-government survives a crisis;

<a id="3-what-assemblies-decide"></a>
### 3. What Assemblies Decide

*In plain terms: assemblies make the rules, set tax policy, pass the budget, choose and oversee the executive, and give the polity's consent to constitutional change. They do not judge cases or run the independent bodies.*

Within the matters assigned to its tier under section 4, an assembly:

- **Makes general rules:** enacts binding general rules that implement, and never narrow, this Constitution.
- **Sets tax policy:** decides the tier's tax policy by general rule: what is taxed, at what rates, and with what exemptions and credits, read with [CI-4.210](corpus_institutions/ci_04.210_public_revenue_fees_recurring_charges_billing_integrity.md#ci-4210-public-revenue-fees-recurring-charges-and-billing-integrity) (*Public Revenue, Fees, Recurring Charges, and Billing Integrity*). No tax is levied except by an assembly's general rule, and each tier taxes only within its matters under section 4. Every tax meets [Subcore 18-A — Taxation](subcore_18.010_taxation.md#subcore-18-a-taxation): the tests of a fair and simple tax, the barred tax designs, the enforcement limits, and the rights of those who owe tax.
- **Adopts the budget:** adopts the tier's budget and its public revenue, read with [CI-4.210](corpus_institutions/ci_04.210_public_revenue_fees_recurring_charges_billing_integrity.md#ci-4210-public-revenue-fees-recurring-charges-and-billing-integrity) (*Public Revenue, Fees, Recurring Charges, and Billing Integrity*). Each tier adopts its own budget.
- **Chooses and oversees the executive:** chooses the tier's executive under section 6, questions it, holds inquiries, and may remove it.
- **Gives the polity's assent:** gives the polity's assent to adoption or amendment where [Chapter Twenty-Three §12 Ratification and Adoption](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#12-ratification-and-adoption) calls for it.
- **Declares, confirms, and revokes emergencies:** under section 8.

An assembly does **not**:

- decide individual disputes, which belong to the forums under [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_forum.md#chapter-sixteen-standards-routing-and-forum-cases);
- appoint, confirm, veto, or remove the decision-makers of an Independent Body, or set its funding outside its published formula, under [Chapter Seven §3 What Makes a Body Independent](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent); or
- change how its own members are selected or drawn while they sit.

<a id="4-dividing-work-between-tiers"></a>
### 4. Dividing Work Between Tiers

*In plain terms: each matter is decided as close to the community as it can be. Higher tiers take only what lower tiers cannot handle, and must say why.*

Matters are divided between tiers by these rules:

- **Lowest capable tier:** a matter is decided at the lowest tier that can handle it.
- **National matters:** defense and foreign policy are national. The polity publishes any other national matters with its reasons.
- **Regional matters:** matters that cross local boundaries within a region, such as shared infrastructure, are regional where the polity publishes them as such.
- **Reaching up:** a higher tier may act on a matter not on its published list only by two-thirds of its members, stating in its Act Record why the lower tier cannot handle it.
- **Conflicts:** a higher-tier rule prevails within that tier's published matters. A dispute over which tier a matter belongs to goes to a **Constitutional** forum under [Chapter Seventeen §3.6 Constitutional Forums](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#36-constitutional-forums).

<a id="5-how-assemblies-decide"></a>
### 5. How Assemblies Decide

*In plain terms: assemblies meet in public, vote in the open, and need bigger majorities for bigger things.*

Every assembly decides by these rules:

- **In public:** sessions and their papers are public, except narrow, time-limited operational secrets under section 8.
- **Recorded votes:** every member's vote on every [materially binding act](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#materially-binding-act) is recorded in its Act Record.
- **Majorities:** a general rule, a budget, and the choice or removal of an executive pass by a majority of members. These need two-thirds of members:
  - any act that limits a right;
  - any assent under [Chapter Twenty-Three §12 Ratification and Adoption](core_23.000_amendment_ratification.md#12-ratification-and-adoption);
  - a declaration of war by the national assembly, beyond responding to an armed attack under the war-or-armed-attack emergency of section 8;
  - a change to the election method or the draw rules, which takes effect only for future terms; and
  - acting on a matter outside the tier's published list under section 4.
- **Legitimacy gate:** a high-impact act passes the gate of [Article XII-A](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xii-a-stakeholder-system-participation-and-representation) (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) before it binds.
- **Shared agenda:** no officer, faction, or staff member controls the agenda alone. A published share of members can place a matter on it.
- **Contact:** members are covered by [Article XIII-B](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*) and, where drawn by lot, by [Subcore 21-D §6 Lot Drawn from an Elected Pool](subcore_21.040_sortition_integrity.md#6-lot-drawn-from-an-elected-pool).

<a id="6-executives"></a>
### 6. Executives

*In plain terms: each assembly picks its own executive, and can remove it.*

Each tier has an executive, under these rules:

- **Chosen by the assembly:** the executive at each tier (local, regional, and national) is chosen directly by that tier's assembly.
- **Answerable to it:** the executive answers the assembly's questions, appears before its inquiries, and carries out its rules and budget.
- **Removable by it:** the assembly may remove the executive by a vote of no confidence. The vote need not name a replacement. The assembly chooses the replacement within one week, and until then the outgoing executive acts only as caretaker for routine business.
- **Term:** an executive serves for up to 5 years from being chosen, whether chosen at the start or as a replacement after a vote of no confidence.
- **Separate seats:** the executive initiates and carries out acts; recording and challenge stay in separate seats under [Chapter Six](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#chapter-six-functional-independence-and-separation-of-duties).

<a id="7-community-referendums"></a>
### 7. Community Referendums

*In plain terms: if 2% of a community asks, a question goes on the ballot, once a forum confirms it fits the Constitution.*

A community referendum follows these rules:

- **Petition:** a referendum goes on the ballot of a tier when sentients making up 2% of that tier's population request it.
- **Forum screening:** before it goes on the ballot, a **Constitutional** forum screens it. The question must be consistent with this Constitution and must fall within the tier's matters under section 4. The forum publishes its reasons, and a refusal may be contested.
- **Fair question:** the forum may require the wording to be clear and neutral.
- **Voting:** the vote follows the rules of [Subcore 21-F — Binding Collective Choice](subcore_21.060_binding_collective_choice.md#subcore-21-f-binding-collective-choice).
- **Binding result:** a referendum binds when a majority of those voting support it and the votes in support are at least one third of the tier's electorate. Otherwise it is advisory.
- **Changing a binding result:** the assembly may amend or repeal a binding referendum result only by two-thirds of its members.

<a id="8-emergencies"></a>
### 8. Emergencies

*In plain terms: an emergency must be one of the named types. Each type unlocks only its own powers, the assembly must confirm it quickly, can end it at any time, and some things are never allowed.*

This section adds typed emergencies to the discipline every emergency already carries: time-limited, documented, overseen, reversible where feasible, and reviewable under [Chapter Seventeen §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden).

**Renewal tiers:**

- **Tier 1:** the assembly confirms within 72 hours or the declaration lapses. Each renewal lasts up to 90 days and needs two-thirds.
- **Tier 2:** the assembly confirms within 7 days or the declaration lapses. Each renewal lasts up to 30 days.

**Emergency types:**

| Type | Examples | Who declares | Powers it may unlock | Renewal tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| War or armed attack | Invasion, missile or drone strike, attack on a border region | National executive only | Mobilize defense forces; requisition goods and property with compensation; restrict access to combat zones; protect critical infrastructure; control export of military goods | Tier 1 |
| Insurrection | Armed rebellion, coup attempt, armed seizure of a public body | Executive of the affected tier; national if more than one region | Deploy security forces under civilian command; protect assemblies, forums, and election bodies; curfews in named areas | Tier 1 |
| System attack or autonomous-system failure | Cyberattack, a high-autonomy system acting outside its bounds, mass system compromise | Tier where the system sits; national if it crosses regions | Isolate or shut down systems; suspend a system's autonomy; preserve evidence; require operator disclosure | Tier 1 |
| Natural disaster | Earthquake, flood, wildfire, storm, volcanic eruption | Lowest affected tier; a higher tier joins when asked or when the lower tier's capacity is exceeded | Evacuation orders; requisition shelter, transport, and supplies with compensation; emergency procurement entered in the Act Record; caps on price gouging; mutual aid | Tier 2 |
| Public health emergency | Epidemic, pandemic, contamination of food or water | Tier matching the spread | Individual isolation or quarantine with prompt review; allocation of medical supplies; temporary closure of named venues | Tier 2 |
| Critical infrastructure failure | Grid collapse, water system failure, communications outage, failure of a critical system | Tier where the system sits | Direct operators to restore service; priority allocation of power, water, or bandwidth; temporary stewardship of the failed system under [CS-21.305](corpus_systems/cs_21.305_critical_system_stewardship.md#cs-21305-critical-system-stewardship) (*Critical System Stewardship*) | Tier 2 |
| Ecological or slow-onset crisis | Drought, crop failure, toxic release, ecosystem collapse | Tier matching the affected area | Equitable rationing of water or food; limits on extraction or emissions; relocation support | Tier 2 |
| Economic or supply collapse | Payment-system failure, hyperinflation, shortage of essential goods | Tier matching the affected market | Rationing of essentials; temporary price limits; continuity of payments and benefits | Tier 2 |
| Mass displacement | Large arrival or flight of sentients after disaster, war, or invasion | Tier receiving or losing the population | Emergency reception and shelter; requisition housing with compensation; coordination across tiers | Tier 2 |
| Institutional breakdown | An assembly or forum cannot meet or reach quorum | Next tier up, or the forum fallback route | Fallback operation of the stalled body; temporary substitute seats by draw | Tier 2, ending as soon as the body can meet |

**Rules for every type:**

- **Declared, then confirmed:** the declaration names the type, the area, the facts, and the powers used. The assembly of the declaring tier must confirm it within the tier's window, or it lapses.
- **Revocable by the assembly:** that assembly may revoke the whole declaration, or any single power used under it, at any time by a majority of members. Revocation takes effect at once.
- **Only the type's powers:** a declaration unlocks only the powers listed for its type. Using a power of another type needs a new declaration.
- **Never allowed under any type:**
  - suspending the Rights Floor of Chapter Four;
  - suspending forums or contest routes;
  - detention without individual process;
  - punishing peaceful dissent or protest under [Article XI-D](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-d-assembly-dissent-and-peaceful-protest) (*Assembly, Dissent, and Peaceful Protest*);
  - censorship, except narrow, time-limited operational secrets;
  - extending any assembly or executive term, or changing the draw for sitting members; and
  - postponing an election, except under the election rule below.
- **Elections:** an election may be postponed only during mass displacement (including displacement caused by war or invasion), or an infrastructure or system failure, that makes a fair election impossible in the affected area. The Electoral Office, not the executive, certifies whether it does, under [Subcore 7-A §5 Running and Counting Votes](subcore_07.010_electoral_office.md#5-running-and-counting-votes).
  - Sitting members hold over only until the postponed election.
  - The election is held within 90 days after the emergency ends, and the draws for the upper assemblies resume with it.
  - A **Constitutional** forum may review any postponement.
- **Reviewable at any time:** anyone affected may challenge a declaration or a measure in a forum under [Chapter Seventeen §5.1 Emergency Measures and Continuation Burden](core_17.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#51-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden).
- **Afterward:** the executive publishes a report, compensates for everything requisitioned, and repeals every measure that depended on the emergency.

<a id="9-limits-on-every-assembly"></a>
### 9. Limits on Every Assembly

*In plain terms: no assembly can vote away rights, keep itself in power, or pay itself more this term.*

Every assembly is bound by these limits:

- **Rights Floor and non-regression:** every act is bound by the Rights Floor of Chapter Four and by [Chapter Twenty-Two](core_22.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-two-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity) (*Non-Regression and Substantive Amendment Validity*).
- **No self-extension:** an assembly may not extend its own term or the terms of its members.
- **No pay rise for itself:** a change to members' pay takes effect only for the next term of the seats it covers.
- **Reviewable:** any assembly act may be challenged in a forum for inconsistency with this Constitution.
