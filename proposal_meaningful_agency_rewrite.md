# Proposed Rewrite: Meaningful Agency Definition (E-line)

## Location
File: `core_05-05_definitions_b_semi_independent.md`
Line: ~516 (the E-line under Meaningful Agency)

## Current Text
```
- E: Reach substantive ability, not formal option count. Evaluate information quality, time, comprehension, dependency, exit cost, coercive pressure, manipulation, accessibility, and reviewability in light of the decision's material impact. Agency is not meaningful where the only available choice predictably defeats survival, dignity, participation, or contestability.
```

## Proposed Rewrite
```
- E: Agency requires genuine capability, not merely listed options. Assess: information quality (accuracy, completeness, absence of deception); adequate time for comprehension; actual understanding of relevant facts and consequences; dependency relationships that constrain choice; practical costs of exit or refusal; coercive pressure applied directly or structurally; manipulation tactics that distort perception; accessibility of decision processes to those with varying capacities; and meaningful review or appeal mechanisms. Weight each factor according to the decision's material consequences. Agency is not meaningful where available choices predictably force surrender of survival needs, dignity, participation rights, or contestability.
```

## Key Changes
1. **Opening**: "Reach substantive ability, not formal option count" → "Agency requires genuine capability, not merely listed options" (clearer, more direct)
2. **Factor expansion**: Each evaluation factor now has parenthetical clarification:
   - information quality → (accuracy, completeness, absence of deception)
   - time → adequate time for comprehension
   - comprehension → actual understanding of relevant facts and consequences
   - dependency → dependency relationships that constrain choice
   - exit cost → practical costs of exit or refusal
   - coercive pressure → coercive pressure applied directly or structurally
   - manipulation → manipulation tactics that distort perception
   - accessibility → accessibility of decision processes to those with varying capacities
   - reviewability → meaningful review or appeal mechanisms
3. **Weighting instruction**: "in light of the decision's material impact" → "Weight each factor according to the decision's material consequences" (more action-oriented)
4. **Closing**: "predictably defeats survival, dignity, participation, or contestability" → "predictably force surrender of survival needs, dignity, participation rights, or contestability" (slightly more descriptive)

## Alternative Shorter Version (if preferred)
```
- E: Measure real ability, not menu length. For decisions with material consequences, assess whether: information is accurate and complete; time for comprehension is adequate; actual understanding exists; dependency constrains choice; exit is practically available; coercion or manipulation is absent; processes are accessible; and review is meaningful. Agency fails when available choices inevitably sacrifice survival, dignity, participation, or contestability.
```
