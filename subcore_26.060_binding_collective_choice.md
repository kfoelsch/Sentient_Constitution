### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 26-F is the constitutional owner of the **full rules for binding collective choice**:

- [§3 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](#3-records-gates-and-method-neutrality): what must be published in advance, how outcomes are verified, the gate before a high-impact choice binds, and which voting methods are allowed;
- [§4 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](#4-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice): the process a binding stakeholder choice must follow;
- [§5 Charter Pointer Rule](#5-charter-pointer-rule): how a System Charter or Institutional Charter points to those rules; and
- [§6 Related Rules Elsewhere](#6-related-rules-elsewhere): where voting rules meet other chapters and implementation text.

[Chapter Twenty-Six §6.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](core_26.000_governance.md#62-records-gates-and-method-neutrality) and [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.3 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](core_26.000_governance.md#63-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice) state each guarantee in one line; this file holds the full text.

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Six says a binding vote must have clear rules, checkable results, and a fair process. This file gives the exact lists and steps.*

### 2. Why Binding Collective Choice Is the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: if each adopter could decide what to publish, how to check a count, or when a vote binds, those in charge could design the process to win.*

A binding vote is only as fair as its rules. If adopters could each decide what to publish before a vote, how far a result can be checked, or when a high-impact choice binds without hearing dissent, those running the process could shape the result. One set of rules for every adopter lets anyone check that a binding choice was made fairly.

### 3. Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality

*In plain terms: rules must be clear before the fact, records must be open to verification, high-stakes choices need legitimacy gates, and many voting methods are allowed if participants can understand them and push back.*

This section sets out the official record, gate, and method-neutrality requirements for governance mechanisms:

- **Publication and ex ante clarity:** Published rules must identify:
  - scope;
  - electorate or weighting model; and
  - quorum or threshold logic.
  They should identify the method for recording assent before the decision event where feasible.
- **Integrity and verifiability requirement:** Outcome records must support independent verification proportionate to impact. Material integrity defects must trigger review under Chapter Four and owner-layer procedures. Those defects include:
  - eligibility;
  - custody;
  - tally; and
  - coercion resistance.
- **Binding-effect gate:** No materially high-impact collective choice is binding unless:
  - **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) legitimacy gates are satisfied;
  - **dissent** and **alternative** recording duties are satisfied; and
  - **contest** pathways are satisfied.
- **Method neutrality with comprehensibility:** The following methods are all constitutionally compatible when they satisfy core constraints:
  - single-mark;
  - ordinal;
  - approval; and
  - score/range.
  Those constraints are:
  - proportionality;
  - meaningful agency;
  - transparency; and
  - challenge rights.

### 4. Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice

*In plain terms: serious collective decisions need a real process: hear affected parties first, publish how weighting works, handle rights conflicts openly, treat emergencies as narrow and temporary, and leave the door open when reality diverges from the plan.*

Where material disagreement requires a **binding stakeholder choice** within an already-authorized system, institution, or bounded decision domain, the process must comply with the following Chapter Three definitions (operative specification and O/M/A/C decomposition reside there):

- **Structured sequence, local deliberation first, plural evaluation, reopening:** [Binding Stakeholder Choice — Decision-Resolution Requirements](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#binding-collective-choice-decision-resolution-process).
- **Stakeholder representation and weight limits:** [Stakeholder Representation and Weight Limits (Binding Stakeholder Choice)](subcore_03.090_band_participation.md#stakeholder-representation-and-weight-limits-binding-stakeholder-choice) (also cross-referenced from [Chapter Twenty-Six §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_26.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility)).

  - [Stakeholder Emergency and Contingency](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#stakeholder-emergency-and-contingency);

<a id="5-charter-pointer-rule"></a>

### 5. Charter Pointer Rule

*In plain terms: a System Charter or Institutional Charter says where its process rules are published; it does not copy them.*

A governing System Charter or Institutional Charter points to the rules of [§4 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](#4-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice) rather than repeating them:

- **What it states:** a governing [System Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#system-charter) or [Institutional Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) states operated scope.
- **What it points to:** where Def.P2 admission scope applies, it must **point to** the published process rules that satisfy [§4 Decision-Resolution Requirements for Binding Stakeholder Choice](#4-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice) ([Chapter Five §5 What System and Institutional Charters Must State](core_05.000_charters.md#5-what-system-and-institutional-charters-must-state), item 8).
- **What stays here:** the System Charter or Institutional Charter does not carry the decision sequence, weighting model, or rights-collision record; they stay in this file and in the Chapter Three definitions it names.
- **Where they are published:** institutional publication of those materials lives under **CI-4.205 §1** (*Stakeholder Oversight, Notification, and Binding-Governance Pathway Integrity*) / **CI-4.205 §2** (*Anti-Concentration and Participation-Legitimacy Safeguards*).
- **The pointer field:** the [Charter Pointer](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#charter-pointer) is required by **CI-6.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter Contents, Review, and Formation Template*) field 10.
