# Chapter One Principle-to-Definition Mapping Evaluation

Date: 2026-04-29

Status: working drafting evaluation; not operative constitutional text.

## Purpose

This note evaluates the proposal to map Chapter One principles and constraints to the Chapter Five definitions stack. The goal is to expose:

- definitions that are load-bearing for a Chapter One principle;
- definitions that are only contextual, evidentiary, or collision-related;
- ambiguity caused by overlapping definitions;
- places where Chapter One may be over-indexed, under-indexed, or using a definition at the wrong layer.

The exercise should be treated as an audit before any constitutional text is changed.

## Recommendation

Proceed.

The plan is substantively sound because Chapter One is the Constitution's value and constraint layer, while Chapters Two through Five supply the meaning, evaluation, burden, traceability, and satisfaction conditions for those values. A principle-to-definition map will help distinguish constitutional direction from operational satisfaction tests.

The plan should use confidence and function labels. A flat list of definitions under each principle is less useful, because broad terms such as Materiality, Proportionality, Necessity, Foreseeability, Safety, Truth, Trust, and Wellbeing can attach almost everywhere.

## Mapping Labels

Use these labels when auditing each Chapter One section:

- Primary: the section cannot operate coherently without this definition.
- Supporting: often relevant, but context-dependent.
- Collision: mainly relevant when principles, rights, or constraints conflict.
- Evidence: governs burden, traceability, observability, verification, audit, or proof.
- Boundary: prevents the section from becoming overbroad or becoming a substitute for another chapter.
- Possible overlap: indicates a possible definitional merge, split, hierarchy note, or cross-reference clarification.
- Intentional non-map: related definition is deliberately not attached because another chapter owns the operative rule.

## High-Level Findings

1. The current Chapter One already implements much of the proposed structure through Trace blocks and Definitions / Evaluation / Compliance blocks.
2. The mapping is not yet normalized. Some sections list only load-bearing definitions; others list a mixture of primary terms, evidence terms, adjacent rights terms, and downstream implementation terms.
3. The most valuable next pass is not adding more definitions. It is sorting existing links by function.
4. Chapter One should avoid becoming a second definitions directory. Its maps should answer "what controls this principle?" rather than "what could ever be relevant?"
5. Several areas are especially good candidates for ambiguity exposure: Wellbeing / Productive Capacity / Constitutional Efficiency; Trust / Trustworthiness / Truth / Epistemic Integrity; Freedom / Meaningful Agency / Consent / Non-Imposition; Safety / Risk / Harm / Irreversible Harm / Existential Risk; Stewardship / Distributed Understanding / Transparency / Auditability / Accessibility.

## Ambiguity and Overlap Targets

### A. Wellbeing, Productive Capacity, Constitutional Efficiency, Avoidable Burden

Question:

When does efficient or productive system behavior become constitutionally good, and when is it merely throughput?

Recommended clarification:

- Wellbeing is the end.
- Productive Capacity is an instrumental good.
- Constitutional Efficiency is disciplined resource use toward constitutional outcomes.
- Avoidable Burden is waste or burden without traceable constitutional benefit.
- Proxy Divergence invalidates metrics that detach from the underlying end.

### B. Truth, Epistemic Integrity, Transparency, Auditability, Trustworthiness

Question:

Which concept governs honest method, which governs access, which governs reliance, and which governs proof?

Recommended clarification:

- Truth is the non-negotiable constraint.
- Epistemic Integrity is the structure and practice of reliable knowing.
- Transparency is access to relevant information.
- Auditability is capacity to inspect and verify.
- Trustworthiness is the demonstrated basis for justified reliance.

### C. Freedom, Meaningful Agency, Consent, Self-Determination, Non-Imposition

Question:

Is Chapter One using "freedom" as moral agency, political agency, choice architecture, consent, or non-domination?

Recommended clarification:

- Freedom (Bounded Agency) is the principle-layer concept.
- Meaningful Agency is the operational agency condition.
- Consent is a specific permission/authorization condition.
- Self-Determination is rights-layer and identity/life-direction oriented.
- Non-Imposition governs cooperative interaction boundaries.

### D. Safety, Harm, Risk, Irreversible Harm, Existential Risk, Cascading Failure

Question:

Which terms define the forbidden object, which terms scale scrutiny, and which terms describe system dynamics?

Recommended clarification:

- Harm is the adverse object.
- Risk is likelihood/severity exposure.
- Safety is the constitutional constraint.
- Irreversible Harm and Existential Risk are escalation categories.
- Cascading Failure is a system-dynamics pattern.

### E. Distributed Understanding, Accessibility, Transparency, Auditability, Educational Agency

Question:

What must be understandable by whom, through what channel, and for what constitutional purpose?

Recommended clarification:

- Distributed Understanding is the principle-layer stewardship aim.
- Accessibility is ability-profile and modality access.
- Transparency is information availability.
- Auditability is inspectability for verification.
- Educational Agency is capacity-building and learning autonomy.

## Proposed Next Pass

1. Build a table from every Chapter One heading to its current definition links.
2. Assign each link one of the mapping labels above.
3. Mark missing primary definitions and over-attached supporting definitions.
4. Identify overlaps where two definitions appear to perform the same job in a section.
5. Draft only the smallest changes needed:
   - reorder definition lists by function;
   - add missing primary anchors;
   - remove or demote merely adjacent terms;
   - add short boundary notes where overlap is persistent;
   - avoid expanding Chapter One into a second Chapter Five.

## Most Promising Immediate Edits

1. In section 5, add or foreground Freedom (Bounded Agency) as a primary definition alongside Meaningful Agency.
2. In section 5.1, sort Productive Capacity's definition list into instrumental-good, collision, evidence, and boundary terms.
3. In section 5.2, split the large definition map into understanding, stewardship, evidence, and boundary groups.
4. In section 6.4, decide whether Avoidable Burden belongs as a supporting rights-collision term.
5. In section 7.1, test whether Systemic, System Boundaries, Cascading Failure, and Residual Risk / Misalignment should be included.
6. In section 9, consider shifting emphasis from anti-capture definitions toward interpretive mechanics and authority-stack definitions.

## Bottom Line

The proposed mapping exercise is not just useful; it is likely the right next development move. It will show where Chapter One is conceptually mature, where Chapter Five definitions overlap, and where the document is relying on intuitive terms that should either be formally anchored or intentionally left as principle-layer language.

The key discipline is to map definitions by function, not by association.
