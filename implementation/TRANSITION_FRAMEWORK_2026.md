## 2. What the corpus does not supply (embedding boundary)

The corpus defines **rights floors**, **classification-scaled obligations**, **audit and contestability**, and **adoption hooks**. It does **not**, by itself, constitute a complete municipal legal order. The following remain governed by **applicable external law** and **instruments chosen by the adopter** unless and until established through legitimate political and legal process:

- Who may exercise **public coercion** or **criminal sanction**
- **Courts**, judgment recognition, and enforcement mechanics
- **Elections, sortition, or federated ratification** infrastructure (credentials, ballots, dispute rules) once chosen as the legitimacy mechanism
- **Taxation, incorporation, securities listing**, and analogous licensing

Full editorial treatment: [archive/ARCHITECTURE_ADOPTION_APPENDIX_ARCHIVED_2026-05-08.md](../archive/ARCHITECTURE_ADOPTION_APPENDIX_ARCHIVED_2026-05-08.md) **section 18** (*Constitutional-political embedding boundary*).

## Annex A — Adopter scope tracks

### A.1 Territorial polity (public authority)

Use when the target **viable constitutional state** claims **territorial** governance and public-law enforcement. **Lean heavily** on section 2 (embedding boundary): parallel charters, electoral or sortition infrastructure, recognition under external law, and explicit sequencing for **coexistence** with legacy state institutions until lawful transfer is achieved.

**Artifacts to maintain:** Instrument of adoption; conflict matrix (section 9); legitimacy mechanism documentation (section 5); interim authority charter (section 7) scoped to **lawful** delegation only.

### A.2 Federation, organization, or network adoption

Use when adoption is by **stewards, organizations, federations, or network participants** without claiming full territorial sovereignty. Legitimacy may emphasize **federated ratification**, **compact**, or **charter** paths under Chapter Twelve §1. Baseline (section 4) emphasizes **contractual**, **platform**, and **cross-border** dependencies; **Protocol C** (especially subsection **8**) and **Article XVIII** / **CJS-5.17** (*interoperability, portability, and exit-integrity terms*) often figure prominently in dependency and exit planning.

**Artifacts:** Same structural sections; scale **Protocol T** evidence and **CS-4** classification to organizational scope.
