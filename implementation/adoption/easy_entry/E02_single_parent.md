*In plain terms: unpaid care is real work, survival cannot be billed as the price of parenting, and family form is not a morality test — and this still will not make the other adult disappear or award every living-arrangement preference.*

## Raising a child mostly alone is ordinary

These figures describe a recognizable situation. They do not change the Rights Floor that applies to you.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Alive in it now** | about **8 in 100** households worldwide are headed by one parent. About **7 in 100** children live with one parent and no other adults. At least **100 million** mothers live that way as the only adult; many more if relatives in the same house are counted. |
| **Counted in world output** | If unpaid cooking, cleaning, and looking-after hours worldwide were paid at a local hourly minimum wage, ILO (2018) would count them as about **9%** of world GDP — hours national books usually omit. This household form does a large share of that work, plus waged work. There is no published “one-parent” slice. |
| **Meet it in a full life** | a large share of parents spend years as the main carer. A large share of children spend years in this household form. |

These figures are approximate — nearest 1-in-n or whole percent. Sources: [`SCALE_NOTES.md`](SCALE_NOTES.md).

## Your situation

You are raising a young dependent largely alone. Shifts, school pickup, and a housing clock overlap.

If a body that actually runs the systems in your life adopted this Constitution:

**Care labor is in scope, not a private leftover.**
- **Today:** Unpaid care is treated as your private hobby: no rest-break, no backup when you are sick. The job that keeps the child alive does not count until it is waged.
- **With this Constitution:** A body that has agreed to follow this Constitution has to name who routes rest-breaks, who counts unpaid care, and who takes reports of pressure.

See: [CI-16](../../../corpus_institutions/ci_16_care_labor_dependent_support_respite_care_economy_fairness.md).

**Survival is not the wage for parenting.**
- **Today:** If you cannot make the schedule work, you are told to accept a household that will “help” by taking decisions over the child. Keep the child and lose the basic protections, or keep those protections and surrender care.
- **With this Constitution:** Essential survival access cannot be made to depend on working, buying premium services, or unpaid care labor.

See: [Article III-A](../../../core_06_rights_part_a.md#article-iii-a-survival); [CI-9.4](../../../corpus_institutions/ci_09_classification_linked_institutional_obligations.md) as CI-16 names it.

**Family form is not a lesser tier.**
- **Today:** Someone hints that a single-parent home is already a risk. Single parent, chosen family, or non-marital care is treated as incomplete and therefore easier to override.
- **With this Constitution:** You may form, keep, and leave family and care relationships of your choosing. A one-parent home is not itself a reason to take the child. A lasting split from a child you care for is allowed only if it is actually needed to prevent harm, no bigger than that harm, decided fairly, and open to challenge. A long split has to be looked at again.

See: [Article VII-D](../../../core_06_rights_part_b.md#article-vii-d-family-care-relationships-reproductive-autonomy-and-non-separation); [Non-Separation](../../../core_05_band_participation.md#non-separation-constitutional).

**The child’s interests are not a spare argument for the other adult’s convenience.**
- **Today:** “For the child” is used to move the child, change who they live with, or put another adult in charge — without asking what actually helps this child, and without asking them as far as they can show what they want.
- **With this Constitution:** Those decisions have to be about this child’s own interests, including what they can show they want — not about a quieter house, a “complete” family, or an easier adult schedule. Calling it “for the child” is not enough.

See: [Article V-F](../../../core_06_rights_part_b.md#article-v-f-developing-sentients-best-interest-and-graduated-capability).

## What you might reasonably object to

- **“I still have to do all of it. Naming care labor doesn’t feed anyone.”** A model constitution does not print a paycheck. What it requires of a body that has agreed to follow it is that unpaid care not be invisible, that rest-breaks have an owner, and that survival not be conditioned on that labor. Whether a particular body has taken those duties up is a separate, honest question.

- **“They’ll use ‘best interest’ to take my kid because I’m tired.”** What is actually in the child’s interests is not operator or household convenience, and it is not a license to treat exhaustion from unsupported care as neglect. Verified harm is a different showing from “the other adult has a quieter house.”

- **“This isn’t law where I live.”** Correct until a qualifying body records a formal agreement to follow this Constitution. It does not override applicable family or housing law ([Chapter Fourteen §5](../../../core_14_expansion_supremacy.md#5-relation-to-applicable-external-law)).

- **“I want the other parent out of the picture.”** Protected care relationships run both ways. This text will not make another adult disappear to settle a preference contest.

- **“Work already treats parenting as my scheduling problem.”** The labor protection is in play when scheduling is used to defeat healthcare or survival access ([Article III-D](../../../core_06_rights_part_a.md#article-iii-d-labor-and-economic-floor)). It does not rewrite every gig platform’s shift board for comfort.

## What this will not pretend to give you

- Winning every living-arrangement or custody preference.
- A rule that the other adult is a harm-doer for existing, offering help, or disagreeing.
- A quieter neighborhood as a basic protection.
- Enforcement against an employer, school, or relative who has not adopted it.
