*In plain terms: the opening headline and the three numbers under it are rounded, year-stamped, and here so a situation looks as ordinary as it is — not so anyone can be sorted into a named group.*

Each brief opens with a **thesis headline** (this situation is ordinary, large, or already in everyone's path), then three lines:

1. **Alive in it now** — share of everyone counted worldwide (living humans in a census or labor survey), unless the brief says it is counting jobs, households, or businesses instead.
2. **Counted in world output** — share of what national books count as production, or the closest honest analog when that share is not published (wealth, food grown, unpaid care, systems already in the path of daily life).
3. **Meet it in a full life** — how ordinary it is to pass through this situation, or to be the child, helper, customer, or dependent in one.

A fourth line appears only when the interesting fact is a **gap** (many living it, little counted), a **trend**, or **who already depends on it**.

These figures do not create a legal type, a trust score, or a duty. Source text binds. Census and labor counts are of living humans; machine minds are not in those counts ([E07](E07_synthetic_sentient.md)).

## How the numbers were chosen

- **Denominator.** United Nations *World Population Prospects 2024*: about **8.2 billion** living humans in 2024. Shares use that year unless a source is older and named.
- **Rounding.** Headlines use “about” and 1-in-*n* or whole percents. Exact source figures live in the table below.
- **Do not mix units.** “Share of workers” is not “share of everyone counted.” The brief says which.
- **No invented GDP line.** Pregnancy, disability, teens, displacement, and machine minds do not have a clean production share. The brief says so and uses unpaid care, food, wealth, or reach instead of guessing.
- **The unpaid-care 9%.** ILO *Care work and care jobs* (2018): 16.4 billion unpaid care hours a day, from time-use surveys in 64 countries covering about 67% of the working-age population. If those hours were paid at an hourly minimum wage, they would equal about 9% of world GDP (about $11 trillion PPP 2011, for 2016). Women do more than three-quarters of the hours. National GDP usually omits unpaid household production. This is not a “pregnant GDP,” a “one-parent GDP,” or an “older-human GDP.”
- **Lifetime lines are not forecasts of your life.** They say how common the situation is across a full human life, given current demography. They are not a promise that this Constitution has been adopted anywhere.

Lived-situation packets may reprint a short form of the matching row. Sitters do not score it.

## What we did not do

- Did not treat a snapshot share as more important than a lifetime share. Pregnancy is ~1 in 100 now and nearly universal across a full life. That contrast is the point.
- Did not invent a “pregnant GDP,” a “teen GDP,” or a “machine-mind GDP.”
- Did not use these figures as an official record, a report waiting to be looked at, or proof that anyone has agreed to follow this Constitution. They are outreach scale, not findings.
