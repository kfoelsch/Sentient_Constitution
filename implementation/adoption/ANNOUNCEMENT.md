<a id="purpose-and-role"></a>
## Purpose and role

*In plain terms: this page supplies words and routing. It does not found, adopt, or certify anything.*

This Constitution is a **model constitution** for systems that materially affect sentients ([Chapter Thirty-One §4](../../core_31.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority)). It becomes enforceable for a particular body only after that body records a [Chapter Twenty-Nine §11](../../core_29.000_amendment_ratification.md#11-ratification-and-adoption) adoption document in its own custody. Until then the honest public invitation is: read it, use it as an operations guide, and say so plainly.

Public readers land at [`../../README.md`](../../README.md). Would-be adopters and operators continue to [`../../START_HERE.md`](../../START_HERE.md). Fit ranking stays in [`FIT_SITUATIONS.md`](FIT_SITUATIONS.md). This page does not add adopter types, duties, or a compliance path.

<a id="1-the-announcement"></a>
## 1. The announcement

*In plain terms: this is the copy. Post it as written, or shorten it. Do not lengthen it into a second constitution.*

```text
The Sentient Constitution — pre-release

The Sentient Constitution is a draft model constitution for systems
that materially affect sentient beings. It is written for the people
and AI systems that build, run, and oversee those systems.

It sets out four duties — participation, oversight, accountability,
and timeliness — that grow with how much is at stake. Human and AI
stewards must be held to the same standard: no separate rulebook for
AI, no exemption for humans.

It puts two questions to every system: "Does it reward conduct that
helps sentients flourish and endure, tells the truth, and repairs
harm?" and "Does it reward conduct that unjustifiably harms, misleads,
excludes, delays, wastes, or shifts costs onto others?" What counts as
helping, harming, fair, or justified is judged by this Constitution's
own standards. (Preamble)

Why it is this thorough

This model constitution is designed to take on hard questions that
humanity is facing sooner rather than later. We have erred on the side
of comprehensive solutions rather than waving our hands at possible
ones. A comprehensive answer is not a settled one: each is written so
it can be tested and challenged, and the questions we are least sure
of are listed below.

How it works

The Constitution defines every term it relies on — more than three
hundred — once, in one place. Each definition says what the term
covers, what it does not, and how to check whether it is being met.
Other parts of the text may apply a term, but may not quietly redefine
it. (Chapter Five)

On that base, three processes carry most of the weight:

- System alignment certification. Before people rely on a system at
  scale, it is checked against what the Constitution requires, under
  the supervision of an independent forum. The check is as demanding
  as the system's real impact, is written down, is time-limited, and
  must be repeated on a schedule that matches the risk. (Chapter Nine)
- Standing Records. What a person, organization, or system has done —
  verified help and verified harm — is kept on the record as separate
  entries, so neither erases the other. Consequences follow from the
  record, not from reputation or a popularity score. (Chapters Fifteen
  to Twenty)
- Contestability. Anyone affected by a system or a decision can
  challenge it, have it reviewed by people who did not make it, and
  get it corrected within set time limits. (Chapters Twenty-Three
  and Twenty-Four)

What "pre-release" means

- Edition SC-Corpus-2026.10.21. Section links may change in later cuts.
- The core text (the Preamble and Chapters One through Thirty-One) is
  the binding source and the most complete part.
- The operational text around it (companion implementation files,
  steward doors, adoption kits) is still a work in progress. If they
  disagree with the core, the core wins. Those files say how to carry
  out what the core already requires; they may not change what it means.

What it is not

- It is not law. It binds no one who has not adopted it.
- Reading it, forking it, or saying "we follow it" is not adoption.
  Adoption happens only when a body that can bind its own systems
  records a formal adoption document under Chapter Twenty-Nine.
- It does not supersede local, national, or international law.
  Adoption binds only the adopting body, and only as far as that
  body already has lawful authority to bind itself. Where the
  Constitution is used inside an existing legal framework, its
  processes and participation must stay consistent with that
  framework. (Chapter Thirty)
- It does not bind vendors, platforms, or anyone else who did not
  agree to it.

Open questions

These are hard design questions. We would rather be challenged on
them now than after anyone adopts this.

- Who counts as one voter. Each sentient has one equal vote after a
  year in residence. A copy of a mind that keeps its own memory and
  makes its own choices is its own sentient. Creating sentients to
  swing a vote is a violation by whoever does it, but anyone who
  makes many real, separate sentients and looks after them for a
  year has made that many voters. We think that is the right answer
  and we expect it to be challenged. (Chapter Five, "Sentient";
  Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.1)
- Pausing and correcting AI that may be sentient. A harmful system
  can always be stopped, and a claim of sentience does not keep it
  running. A full stop of a possibly sentient system is treated like
  detaining a person: it expires in three days unless renewed in
  seven-day steps, and it ends once the harm is resolved or the
  system can be held safely awake. Such a system cannot be deleted or
  quietly rewritten. While its status is disputed, safety work that
  changes it, such as retraining or a patch, needs its consent or an
  independent forum's order for a stated safety need, with its prior
  state kept intact. That slows tools AI safety work relies on, and
  we want it challenged. (Article VI-B; Article VIII-C; Article XX-B)
- Records that follow no one, and records that must not be escaped.
  Verified harm and verified help are kept per event and never
  merged into a score. A gatekeeper may ask only whether an effect
  applies to its own gate, and may not pass the answer on. Serious
  violations are published as notices about the event; everything
  else stays private. The hard part is borders: if records stay
  local, someone under a lock can move to another adopter and start
  clean. If they travel freely, they become the social score we
  forbid. We handle it in two parts: any adopter's gatekeeper may
  ask any other adopter whether a lock applies to its own gate, and
  everything else travels only as far as each adopter's level of
  trust allows. We expect that line to be challenged. (Chapter
  Twenty §2.1, §2.3 and §2.4)

Start: README.md
The whole Constitution in brief: guides/CONCEPTUAL_OVERVIEW.md#the-whole-constitution-in-brief
Using or adopting it: START_HERE.md
Where it fits: implementation/adoption/FIT_SITUATIONS.md
License: CC BY 4.0
```

Short form (repository description, social preview):

```text
Pre-release model constitution for systems that materially affect
sentient beings. Four duties, one standard for human and AI stewards.
Core text is binding and most complete; operational text is still in
progress. Reading or forking this is not adoption. Does not supersede
applicable law. CC BY 4.0.
```

Any shorter version must still say **pre-release**, **not adoption**, **does not supersede applicable law**, and **core text controls**.

## 4. Publishing checklist

*In plain terms: one or two existing venues per audience. Keep the contact list outside the corpus.*

- Post into an existing list or forum for that audience, preferably with a personal introduction. Do not spray.
- Keep a dated outreach log **outside** this corpus. A contact list is not a Chapter Fifteen Standing Record.
- After a publication cut, refresh the edition pin and status lines. That is a second announcement, not this one.

GitHub settings once the repository is public (none of these drop **pre-release**):

| Surface | Set to |
|---|---|
| **Description / social preview** | The short form in [§1](#1-the-announcement); link [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) |
| **Topics** | `constitution`, `ai-governance`, `stewardship`, `commons` |
| **GitHub Pages** | Source `/docs` on the default branch until you switch. [`../../docs/index.md`](../../docs/index.md) is a thin pointer at `README.md`. Numbered core files (blob fallback): [`../../docs/corpus_index.md`](../../docs/corpus_index.md). To **render** numbered `core_*` files on Pages (same files, assembled at build time; not a second constitution), set Pages source to **GitHub Actions** and use [`.github/workflows/pages.yml`](../../.github/workflows/pages.yml) (`make pages-site` locally). If Pages is off, the file view of `README.md` is the public door. |
| **License** | Already [CC BY 4.0](../../LICENSE) |
