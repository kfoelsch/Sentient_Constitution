### 15.3 G20/OECD Principle III — explicit scope decision (editorial)

This subsection records the custodian **in-scope / out-of-scope** decision for [G20/OECD Principle III](https://www.oecd.org/corporate/principles-corporate-governance/) (*The role of key corporate governance participants, including institutional investors and market intermediaries*). Operational classification hooks for adopters are stated in [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) — **Interpretation — Capital markets, traded instruments, and financial intermediaries**; this **15.3** text is the **rationale and scope log** kept in the architecture appendix.

**Out of corpus scope (by design).**

- Listing rules, mandatory board composition, shareholder meeting mechanics, takeover bids, and **corporate-law-specific** fiduciary doctrine for listed issuers, **as standalone subjects**.
- Regulation of credit-rating methodology, retail brokerage advertising, or fund prospectus **format** where those topics do not implicate a classified **system** or **steward** under the annex.

**In scope (by classification and stewardship analogy).**

- **Trading venues, clearing/settlement systems, critical pricing or index infrastructure**, and other **market-structuring** systems that meet **Chapter S2** criteria (typically Class A, B, or C depending on coordination, dependency, and risk): full classification, disclosure, audit, integrated risk governance, and **Chapter S3** steward conduct apply.
- **Asset managers, broker-operated platforms, mutual funds, pension administrators**, and similar actors **in their capacity as operators of high-impact technical or organizational systems** are governed by the same **rights, capture, concentration, transparency, and cross-jurisdiction** hooks as other stewards. Examples of such activity include automated order routing, portfolio optimization at scale, and **info-sphere**-shaping research distribution. Illustrative corpus hooks include Sentient Constitution Arts III, IX, XIV, XV, XVII, XVIII, and XIX; [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) Chapters S2 and S3; Protocol S5; Protocol C; PROT1/PROT3/PROT6.
- **Beneficial ownership, hidden control, and incentive paths** that affect governance integrity are already **non-compliant** under **System Capture** and **PROT1** anti-concentration language when materially relevant; market labels do not create exemptions.

**Custody note.** National supervisors and stock exchanges remain authoritative under **external law** where applicable; see **Sentient Constitution Chapter Twelve** (*Supremacy Relative to Other Binding Norms*).

## 16. External assurance and attestation (implementation note)

This section satisfies checklist **21** in [archive/ARCHITECTURE_WORKLIST_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md](archive/ARCHITECTURE_WORKLIST_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md). It is **non-normative** and **not part** of the Sentient Constitution corpus (the numbered `core_*` files listed in [README.md](README.md), plus [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md), [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md), [corpus_institutions.md](corpus_institutions.md), and [corpus_forum.md](corpus_forum.md) within valid incorporation scope). Nothing here creates, amends, or interprets obligations in those files unless a steward or adopter **separately** chooses to adopt assurance processes under applicable law or contract.

**Purpose.** Organizations that operate or certify systems under this constitution may wish to obtain **independent external assurance** over claims such as “controls exist and operate for constitutional alignment” (for example, for participants, regulators, funders, or counterparties). Assurance is a **reporting and governance practice** layered on top of the corpus; the corpus already requires **observability, auditability, and contestability** (e.g. Sentient Constitution Chapters Two through Six, Chapter Nine **Article XII-B** and **Article XV**, CP PRIM9–PRIM11, PROT3, and CS classification governance and protocols). For **NIST AI RMF**, **ISO/IEC 42001**, and **EU AI Act** theme-level criteria anchors, see **section 15.4–15.7**. For what still depends on **external law** or a **separate adopting instrument** (courts, coercion, elections), see **section 18**.

**Illustrative patterns (not prescribed).** International attestation frameworks—commonly discussed under labels such as **ISAE 3000** (assurance engagements other than audits of historical financial information) and related **SOC-style** reports—offer a familiar structure: a **responsible party** asserts a subject matter (e.g. a description of controls or outcomes); an **assurance practitioner** evaluates that subject matter against **suitable criteria**; the report expresses **reasonable** or **limited** assurance. Adopters may map **criteria** to explicit corpus hooks (stable IDs such as **SYS-CH-S2**, **SYS-PROTO-A**, **PRIM/PROT** codes, Chapter Five definitions, and Chapter Nine articles) so that scope and exceptions are traceable. Criteria selection remains the adopter’s responsibility; this document does not endorse a single assurance standard or firm.

**Relationship to document control.** Effective assurance depends on **document control**, **versioning**, and clarity of the **authoritative edition** of Sentient Constitution / CP / CS — see **section 17** and checklist **8** / **22** in [archive/ARCHITECTURE_WORKLIST_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md](archive/ARCHITECTURE_WORKLIST_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md). Assurance reports should state which **corpus version** and **cut-off date** they address.

**Optional future split.** If the project later adds an `implementation/` directory (e.g. adoption guides, assurance mapping worksheets), this note may move or be mirrored there; until then, **section 16** remains the single editorial location for this topic.

## 18. Constitutional-political embedding boundary (non-normative)

This section satisfies the **embedding boundary note** item in [archive/TODO_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md](archive/TODO_ARCHIVED_2026-05-01.md). It is **editorial** and **not part** of the normative Sentient Constitution corpus (the numbered `core_*` files listed in [README.md](README.md), plus incorporated companion files within valid adoption scope).

**Purpose.** Assurance readers, adopters, and external mappers should not treat the corpus as a **complete municipal legal order** or as **self-executing public authority** in every jurisdiction. The files specify **constitutional norms, definitions, companion operational law, and adoption hooks** for entities that **choose** to align; they do not, by themselves, **constitute** a legislature, court registry, police power, or revenue authority.

**What still requires an adopting polity or separate legal instrument.**

- **Legitimacy and coercion:** **Who may govern**, **how leaders are selected**, and **when physical coercion or criminal sanction** is lawful remain governed by **applicable external law** and by instruments of **adoption, federation, or charter** chosen by the adopter. The corpus’s **Chapter Ten** (*Governance Legitimacy, Authorization, and Stewardship*) and **Chapters Eleven through Thirteen** frame those choices. They do not replace domestic constitutional law or corporate charter law unless separately adopted there.
- **Courts and enforcement:** **Binding civil and criminal process**, **judgment recognition**, and **cross-border enforcement** depend on **treaties, arbitration statutes, private international law, and regulator practice**. **Chapter Twelve** (*Disputes Involving External Legal Orders*), **Chapter Six** (*Enforcement Realism Anchors*), **Protocol C**, and **Protocol S5** **anticipate** realistic execution paths; they do not **create** jurisdiction where none exists.
- **Taxation, incorporation, and market licensing:** **Corporate personality**, **securities listing rules**, **tax treatment** of pools or indemnity vehicles, and **insurance regulation** are **external**. The **capital-markets interpretation** in [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) and **section 15.3** record scope decisions; they do not grant exemptions from national supervisors.
- **Political embedding:** **Elections, sortition, federated ratification**, or **treaty-of-parties** mechanisms referenced in **Chapter Ten** require **operational polities** (members, ballots, credentials, dispute rules). The corpus sets **proportionality and publication** expectations; it does not run an election.

**What the corpus does supply.** Substantive **rights floors**, **classification-scaled obligations**, **audit and contestability** requirements, **remedy-capacity** hooks, **supremacy** *among adopted internal norms*, and **non-regression** constraints for amendments — so that adopters can **implement** alignment with **traceable** criteria (see **section 16** for assurance patterns).

## 19. Rights Layer Interpretation Bridge (non-normative)

This section is explanatory only. It does **not** add rights beyond **Chapter Nine** in [core_09-09_rights_part_a.md](core_09-09_rights_part_a.md) through [core_09-09_rights_part_d.md](core_09-09_rights_part_d.md) and does **not** incorporate external instruments as independent sources of obligation.

### 19.1 Layer ownership for rights interpretation

- **Constitutional owner:** **Chapter Nine** defines rights floors and rights-level interpretive scope. **Chapter Five** supplies Interdependent and Clustered Definitions that constrain how rights terms are applied but does not replace the rights articles.
- **Implementation owner:** [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md) and [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) operationalize those rights through procedures, protocols, and controls.
- **Anti-relocation rule:** rights meaning and scope remain in **Chapter Nine**; implementation layers must not redefine or narrow rights floors.

### 19.2 Rights exercise constraints and systemic evaluation

All rights in **Chapter Nine** are exercised subject to Chapter One constraints, conflict-resolution logic, and systemic evaluation requirements.
Evaluation should include local and systemic conditions, including aggregate, delayed, and cross-system effects.
No right should be interpreted as absolute where its exercise would violate non-negotiable constraints or produce disproportionate systemic harm.

### 19.3 Domain extension and implementation home

**Chapter Nine** spans **Articles I**–**XXV** in **planet-first** presentation order (**Parts A–D**; see the Part A opening in [core_09-09_rights_part_a.md](core_09-09_rights_part_a.md)). **Articles VI**–**XXIV** extend the stack into education capability, likeness and publication, agency and cooperation, stakeholder governance, trustworthy systems, info-sphere integrity, audit and verification, lifecycle and innovation, standing, portability, complexity stewardship, diagnostics, interpretive review, justice, and constitutional evolution; **Article XXV** carries transition, re-baselining, and interim-governance bounds. **Article V** states **dignity** (**V-A**); **nondiscrimination** (**V-B**); **full inclusion and equality in adjudication and operations** (**V-C**); and **freedom of conscience, religion, and comparable worldview** (**V-D**). **Article III** states **survival and equal educational access** (**III-A**, **III-B**). **Article XXV-D** bounds transition-phase **seizure** and **voluntary turnover incentives** (including **bounties**) against arbitrary deprivation and bounty-hunting pathologies.
Operational profiles, taxonomies, and checklists for these themes appear in [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md) (Implementation Groups One through Four) and [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) (Chapters S1-S3 and annex protocols).
Those materials implement but do not replace **Chapter Nine** rights or **Chapter Five** definitional meaning where applicable.

### 19.4 Due-diligence cycle (interpretive orientation)

Systems and institutions that materially affect sentients should address rights-relevant impacts through a recurring cycle that:

- identifies salient risks
- prevents and mitigates through design, operation, and governance
- tracks measure effectiveness through observability and verification
- communicates material outcomes and limits in proportionate form
- enables remedy through contestable processes

For orientation only, this sequence aligns with commonly used human-rights due-diligence framing.

### 19.5 Canonical substantive hooks

Substantive requirements are supplied by **Chapter Nine** rights articles and **Chapter Five** definitions, including Harm, Material, Risk, Redress and Remediation, Protected Characteristics, and Substantive Fairness.
The **Chapter Fourteen** incorporation bridge governs when companion-file obligations are constitutionally operative within valid adoption scope.
Cross-file procedural hooks include [corpus_joint_structure.md](corpus_joint_structure.md) Implementation Group Four (including PROT6) and relevant [corpus_systems.md](corpus_systems.md) provisions where adopted and applicable.
