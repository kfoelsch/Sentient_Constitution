### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this section says what this chapter is for, where it sits beside its subcore files on structure, integrity, and proof, and what it covers and leaves out. The rule that keeps each term in one place follows it.*

**Purpose:** Chapter Three answers what each foundational term means, including the Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy and the Corpus, and where its one official home is (**section 2**).

**Role:** The chapter is the naming layer of the definition stack, and it holds the terms the other layers work on:

- The chapter's subcore supplies the mechanics, which this chapter takes as fixed:
  - [Subcore 3-A](subcore_03.010_definition_structure.md#subcore-3-a-definition-structure-and-component-requirements) says how every definition is built;
  - [Subcore 3-B](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#subcore-3-b-definition-integrity-evasion-and-non-compliance) says what it means to fail an applicable definition; and
  - [Subcore 3-C](subcore_03.030_burden_traceability_verification.md#subcore-3-c-burden-of-proof-traceability-and-verification) says who must prove compliance, and how.
- Later chapters and implementation text apply Chapter Three terms under the rule in **section 2**.
- [Chapter Four](core_04.000_rights_part_a.md#chapter-four-foundational-rights) holds the Rights Floor. Read it for the rights themselves, then return here for term precision in interpretation, audit, and adjudication.

Two rules govern how another chapter meets a Chapter Three definition once it invokes it:

- an independent definition, once invoked, is met in full across its Ontological, Measurement, Assessment, and Compliance parts (**section 3**); and
- the members of a dependent cluster are met together, and no member is a shortcut around the rest (**section 4**).

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- the canonical home and non-duplication rule (**section 2**);
- the meta rules for independent definitions (**section 3**) and for dependent clusters (**section 4**);
- how definitions and proof work, stated in one line for each guarantee, with the full rules in the chapter's subcore files (**section 5**);

- the alphabetical directory, the compass and definition map, and the measurement crosswalk, which say where each definition is found.

Chapter Three is the overview; the full text sits in its subcore files:

- how a definition is built, in Subcore 3-A;

### 2. Why Definitions Have One Home
*In plain terms: every defined term has one official home. That home decides what the term means. Other files may apply the term, but they may not quietly redefine it.*

A constitution is only as firm as the meaning of its words. If the same term could be defined in two places, a rule could be narrowed without anyone amending it, simply by applying the weaker definition. Giving each term one home means every reader, forum, and adopter can find the controlling meaning, and any change to it is visible and goes through the amendment process that governs that home.

Three rules keep every defined term in one place:

- Each constitutional definition, definition description, and clustered traceability concept must have one **canonical home** in this corpus — one official place that controls what the term means, how far it reaches, and what counts as satisfying it.
  - No incorporated or implementation text may create a second definition, a competing explanation, or a narrower restatement of a term whose canonical home is already assigned by this Constitution or by the corpus architecture map adopted under it.
- Incorporated implementation text may put a canonical term to work only by pointing back to that home and then stating layer-specific criteria, procedures, classifications, controls, or conditions inside its assigned scope.
  - If such text uses a local label or shorthand, it must say so clearly. That local wording is not a new definition of the term. It becomes official only when the term's canonical home is amended to include it.
- If wording outside the canonical home seems to broaden, narrow, replace, or compete with the canonical definition, the canonical definition governs. Read that outside wording as implementation detail if you can. If you cannot, the competing wording does not apply to the extent of the conflict.

### 3. Independent Definitions

*In plain terms: independent definitions are reusable building blocks. They stand on their own unless a cluster requires them to be met together.*

Independent Definitions are reusable semantic building blocks that can be invoked across multiple definitions. They define shared ontological, measurement, assessment, and compliance constructs. Apply them wherever they are materially relevant to system behavior, outcomes, evaluation scope, or compliance determination.

Independent and Semi-independent definitions do not require joint satisfaction with other definitions unless a Dependent-cluster definition explicitly requires it ([section 4 — Dependent-Cluster Meta Rules](#4-dependent-cluster-meta-rules)). [§3.1 Invocation, Satisfaction, and Compliance](#31-invocation-satisfaction-and-compliance) apply whenever an Independent Definition is invoked.

<a id="31-invocation-satisfaction-and-compliance"></a>

#### 3.1 Invocation, Satisfaction, and Compliance
*In plain terms: when an independent definition applies, it must be met in full — no picking the convenient pieces.*

Failing to invoke or correctly apply any materially required Independent Definition invalidates the associated compliance determination. All Independent Definitions invoked in system evaluation must appear explicitly in Definition Traceability mappings (Subcore 3-C, section 3 — Definition Traceability Requirement). Those mappings must include their Ontological (O), Measurement (M), Assessment (A), and Compliance (C) components. Traceability must show how each invoked Independent Definition contributes to observable system behavior and to compliance outcomes under full functional system conditions.

When an Independent Definition is invoked, it must be satisfied in full across its O, M, A, and C components. That satisfaction must be consistent with the Requirements for Definition Structure (Subcore 3-A §2 (*Definition Components and Joint Satisfaction*)). Partial satisfaction or selective application is non-compliant.

Independent Definitions must still be applied consistently with all applicable definitions under shared functional system scope, evaluation conditions, and temporal context. Subcore 3-B, sections 2 and 3 — Definition Integrity and Anti-Evasion Constraints; Non-Compliance from Observable System Behavior — require that consistency.

Selective invocation, omission of materially relevant definitions, and application that alters, weakens, or bypasses compliance determination are evasion. Subcore 3-B, sections 2 and 3 — Definition Integrity and Anti-Evasion Constraints; Non-Compliance from Observable System Behavior — govern that conduct.

Independent Definitions must not fragment, isolate, or redistribute compliance in a way that blocks full-system evaluation under required assessment conditions.

### 4. Dependent-Cluster Meta Rules

*In plain terms: cluster members must be read and satisfied together — no member is a standalone shortcut around joint requirements.*

A **dependent cluster** is a group of definitions that must be met together, inside the scope each cluster states. [§4.1 Joint Invocation and Satisfaction](#41-joint-invocation-and-satisfaction) and [§4.2 Standalone Definitions Interaction and Full Context](#42-standalone-definitions-interaction-and-full-context) apply to every cluster. Dependent clusters **Def.O1–Def.I1** live in the constitutional band files — see [Clusters A-Z](#clusters-a-z) and the [compass](#chapter-three-compass-and-definition-map).

<a id="41-joint-invocation-and-satisfaction"></a>

#### 4.1 Joint Invocation and Satisfaction
If a definition — or any part of one — belongs to a dependent cluster, it may not be applied, satisfied, or judged on its own. When cluster members are pieces of one requirement, all of them must be met. Meeting only some of them is not compliance.

This is the cluster form of the [Anti-Segmentation Principle](core_02.030_constitutional_interpretation.md#1511-anti-segmentation-principle). Cluster-local applications appear under **Anti-bypass** (not a second "joint invocation" header). Each lists the cluster's compartments and protected duties and ends with a cite to that principle. They must not reopen with `Under Joint invocation and satisfaction, …`.

<a id="42-standalone-definitions-interaction-and-full-context"></a>

#### 4.2 Standalone Definitions Interaction and Full Context
Calling a definition Independent or Semi-independent does not let it skip cluster membership. Dependent clusters must still be met together in the full real-world system context. No piece may be peeled off, reclassified, or applied alone in a way that changes the compliance result.
<a id="5-how-definitions-and-proof-work"></a>
### 5. How Definitions and Proof Work

*In plain terms: a word in this Constitution has to mean something you can check, nobody can win by keeping the word and dodging its point, and whoever says they comply has to prove it. The full rules are in this chapter's three subcore files.*

These guarantees hold for every definition and every compliance claim under this Constitution:

- **Every definition can be checked:** each definition says what the term is, how it is measured, how it is assessed, and what must hold in practice, and all four parts must be met for the system as it actually runs ([Subcore 3-A](subcore_03.010_definition_structure.md#subcore-3-a-definition-structure-and-component-requirements), *Definition Structure*).
- **No winning by evasion:** keeping a definition's words while defeating its purpose, by narrowing it where it applies, splitting a harm into pieces too small to count, or testing only where the problem is absent, is failing it ([Subcore 3-B](subcore_03.020_definition_integrity.md#subcore-3-b-definition-integrity-evasion-and-non-compliance), *Definition Integrity*).
- **Whoever claims compliance proves it:** the burden of proof is on the one who claims to comply, not on the sentient affected, and the proof must be traceable, open to inspection, and checkable by others, even where security limits how or when it is shown ([Subcore 3-C](subcore_03.030_burden_traceability_verification.md#subcore-3-c-burden-of-proof-traceability-and-verification), *Burden of Proof, Traceability, and Verification*).

Each subcore file gives the full rule. None of them may narrow a line in this section.

<a id="61-integrative-terms"></a>
#### 6.1 Integrative Terms

These terms must be met across several Tetrad legs at once. Their full definitions are in [Subcore 3-D](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#subcore-3-d-integrative-definitions), *Integrative Definitions*.

- **[Non-Compliance](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#non-compliance)**: A system or definition is non-compliant when its requirements are not met in practice — judged by what it actually does and produces, through the measures that apply, when the system is running as a whole.
- **[Constitutional Contract Layer](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#constitutional-contract-layer)**: The layer that answers who may govern, by what legitimacy, and under what lasting terms — not ordinary consultation, usage, or branding.
- **[Foundational Constitutional Choice](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#foundational-constitutional-choice)**: Decisions about who holds governing authority, by what mechanism, and on what lasting terms — one-person-one-voice at that founding layer, not impact-weighted ordinary stakeholder voting.
- **[Documented Legitimacy Mechanism](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#documented-legitimacy-mechanism)**: The written, published rule an adopter uses to decide how governing power is granted, renewed, and handed on — such as elections, a civic lottery, federation votes, or a treaty, compact, or Constitutional Charter adopted with recorded agreement.
- **[Innovation Reward and Anti-Enclosure](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#innovation-reward-and-anti-enclosure)**: Real invention and useful creation are rewarded, but the reward may not harden into lasting lock-up of repair, reuse, research, or public-interest use.
- **[No-Bypass](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#no-bypass)**: A constitutional requirement follows the act, not its packaging.
- **[Constitutional Collision](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#constitutional-collision)**: A collision is when two things this Constitution requires cannot both be fully done in the same act, decision, or design, so honoring one limits the other.
- **[Constitutional Collision Record](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#constitutional-collision-record)**: A Collision Record is the written account of how one material Constitutional Collision was decided.
- **[Derived Information](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#derived-information)**: Derived information is anything produced by combining, linking, inferring from, modeling, or de-identifying other information.
- **[Abstract Strictness](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#abstract-strictness)**: Abstract strictness means choosing the harshest, most demanding, or most restrictive reading of a text just because it is harsher, without showing that it protects anyone more.
- **[Fullest Protective Effect](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#fullest-protective-effect)**: When two readings or two rules apply to the same thing, the one that protects sentients most, taken as a whole, is the one that applies.
- **[Incentive Alignment, Proxy Integrity, and Contingent Settlement](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment-proxy-integrity-and-contingent-settlement)**, a group of related terms:
  - **[Incentive Alignment](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment)**: Rewards, targets, and governance signals must push agents and systems toward real constitutional outcomes — not toward proxies, loopholes, or capture.
  - **[Incentive Alignment — Baseline Constitutional Alignment](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment--baseline-constitutional-alignment)**: Incentives must stay aligned with safety, truth, trust, and meaningful agency over time — not only in the short term.
  - **[Incentive Alignment — Strategic, Scaled, and Adversarial Evaluation](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment--strategic-scaled-and-adversarial-evaluation)**: Incentives are checked under gaming, scale, coordination, and deliberate misuse, not only under polite lab conditions.
  - **[Incentive Alignment — Contingent Claims, Games of Chance, and Event-Contract Markets](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#incentive-alignment--contingent-claims-games-of-chance-and-event-contract-markets)**: Bets, prediction markets, and chance-based payouts must not reward unlawful harm, insider power, or twisting of public or fiduciary decisions.
  - **[Misalignment Detection](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#misalignment-detection)**: No one gets to be the only one who can spot, check, or challenge when governance goes wrong.
  - **[Open Systems, Data, and Auditing](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#open-systems-data-and-auditing)**: The information needed to check governance must stay open.
  - **[Misaligned Reward Correction](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#misaligned-reward-correction)**: Once misalignment is verified, rewards that came through a misaligned or corrupt reward pathway cannot be quietly kept.
- **[Corpus and Authority Stack](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#defi1-corpus-and-authority-stack)**: This cluster answers which text is binding, which source wins in a conflict, whether claimed duties can actually be checked and enforced, and — with the Chapter One owner register — who owns which topic.
  - **[Corpus](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#corpus)**: The Corpus is the official, versioned set of binding constitutional texts — not drafts, notes, or unofficial copies.
  - **[Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#authority-stack-and-internal-hierarchy)**: The order for settling what binds: first which source layer is speaking and whether it binds, then which chapter owns the topic, and only then, for a real conflict, the last-resort hierarchy.
  - **[Constitutional Constraint](subcore_03.045_band_integrative.md#constitutional-constraint)**: A constitutional constraint is a binding limit that decides whether a claim, action, or practice is valid under this Constitution — not optional advice.
