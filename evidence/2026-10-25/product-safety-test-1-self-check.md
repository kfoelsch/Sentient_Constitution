# Product safety and Commercial Compliance: Test 1 non-regression self-check (2026-10-25)

**Test.** [Chapter Fifteen Test 1](../../core_15.000_non_regression.md#2-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity): the change must not weaken a Chapter One constraint, a Chapter Two to Four integrity rule, a Chapter Six Rights Floor, or a Chapter Fourteen legitimacy rule, including by indirect narrowing through definitions, standing gates, evidentiary burden, observability degradation, or emergency re-labeling.

**Scope.** New Article III-F; Article III-A "safe to consume"; Article III-D and XIII-A Read-withs; Article XI-B compelled-speech bullet; Article XI-E sentence; Chapter Five *Commercial Compliance* and the *Expression* out-of-scope note; Chapter Nine and CJS follow-ons.

| Change | Direction | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article III-F (new Rights Floor) | Adds a floor | Nothing existing is removed. A known, disclosed, chosen hazard stays the sentient's decision under Article VII-A, which is the existing position. |
| Article III-A "safe to consume / safe to use" | Strengthens | A duty on suppliers. It is not a condition on recipients, and a safety doubt may not cut off survival-critical access. Where safe supply cannot be had, access continues, the risk is disclosed, and the duty to restore safe supply continues. |
| Article III-D sentence, Article XIII-A and Safe Conditions Read-withs | Neutral | Routing only. Article III-D's own scope is unchanged. |
| Article XI-B compelled-speech bullet | Strengthens | Chapter Five said only that compelled speech is "not protected expression". The Article now treats a rule that makes a sentient state, endorse, or carry a message of opinion, belief, identity, or affiliation as a limit based on what is said: presumed non-compliant, highest scrutiny (136th move). It adds protection and removes none. Verifiable facts, such as what a product contains or who is accountable, are not such messages. |
| Article XI-E: "Each is a case of Commercial Compliance" | Neutral | No condition on practice licence or clearance changed. The four questions restate what XI-E already requires (risk finding, Avoidable Burden, contestable, burden on the imposer, same safeguards). |
| Chapter Five *Commercial Compliance* | Neutral to strengthening | It classifies conditions. It confers no new power to impose them, keeps the burden on the imposing party, and names the guards (no silencing, no cut-off of survival-critical access, no use against dissenters, reporters, or organizers, no blocking formation of an enterprise). |
| *Expression* out-of-scope parenthetical | Neutral | Clarifies; the exclusion of compelled speech from protected expression is unchanged. |

**Indirect-narrowing check.** The one place a reader could worry is Commercial Compliance as a general label that lets any market-entry condition escape XI-B scrutiny. It does not: a rule qualifies only if it answers yes to Offer, Fact, Risk, and Least, and the failure list in the entry names relabeling a rule that demands opinion or affiliation as a failure.

**Result.** No weakening found. Settled in the 136th move: the XI-B bullet uses the highest-scrutiny standard instead of an absolute bar. One judgment remains for the steward to confirm: the "safe to consume" carve-out in Article III-A, as worded above, does not excuse a supplier from the safe-supply duty.
