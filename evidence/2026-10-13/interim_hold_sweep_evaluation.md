# Interim hold: corpus sweep and build-out evaluation (2026-10-13)

Request: the interim hold added to Article XI-E (a forum's short, narrow, reasoned hold on a named sentient's authority, issued on probable cause and short of a verified finding) may apply to other situations and may need treatment roughly parallel to locks. Sweep the corpus for other situations and decide whether to build it out.

Nothing in this note changes the corpus. It is an evaluation for the steward.

## Short answer

Yes, build it out, but as **one general standard with a small number of applications**, not as a family of hold types. The corpus already recognizes this kind of measure and never designs it. Article XIX-B lists "temporary protective limits while a case is still open, before anything is verified" as a category of restricted standing, and Chapter Twelve Part A §4.1 invites "lawful interim steps" such as escrow and holdback. Neither says who may impose such a limit, on what showing, for how long, what it may never touch, or what happens if it was wrong. Locks have all of that (Chapter Eleven §5.1 to §5.3). Holds have only the general interim-relief sentence in Chapter Thirteen §5.

The main risk of building it out is that a hold becomes a shadow lock: long, renewed, and applied to disfavored voices without a finding. The design below is mostly about preventing that.

## What the corpus already has

| Tool | Where | What it acts on | What it lacks for our purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stay | Chapter Five (*Stay*) | An action, a reliance, a finalization, an enforcement | Not aimed at a sentient's pathway; no probable-cause standard |
| Interim protection | Chapter Thirteen §5 | Anything needed to stop irreversible harm, keep evidence, stop escalation, or hold the status quo | One sentence of design: "reasoned, proportionate, and subject to prompt review" |
| Emergency measures | Chapter Thirteen §6.1 | Measures under imminent-harm conditions | Requires imminent harm; good expiry, review, and continuation rules we can reuse |
| Default interim posture | Chapter One §13 (collision) | Irreversible steps while a Constitutional Collision is pending | About steps, not sentients |
| Interim relief while panel independence is disputed | CF-4.12 | Provisional orders by a provisional authority | Narrow to panel formation |
| Activation record | CF-7.3.2 | Backup routing, with interim relief | Useful rule: creates no standing effect or finding; family-level, not individual |
| Emergency review lanes | CF-6.2.2 | Temporary orders before full review | Contestable and time-limited, but generic |
| Reliance limits during contest | Chapter Nine Part B §6.3 | Reliance on a certification record | Acts on records, not on holders |
| Compromised-node exclusion | CS-11 §4.1 | A node with material compromise indicators, suspended pending review | A working example: it records trigger evidence, uncertainty bounds, and a challenge path |
| Interim protective measures | Chapter Eleven §4.4 | Exempt from the opened-remedy precondition for locks | Names the category; does not design it |
| Restriction category | Article XIX-B | "Temporary protective limits while a case is still open, before anything is verified" | Names the category; does not design it |

## What is missing

A standard for an interim measure on **a named sentient's pathway**, on a showing short of a verified finding, with the same discipline locks have:

- a defined ground (probable cause) that sits between an allegation and a verified finding;
- the lock-style fields (named pathway, risk, protected interests, conditions for ending it, review route, lapse point);
- who may issue it, so a body never holds its own member out on its own view;
- an outer bound and automatic lapse;
- a rule that it creates no finding, lock, standing record, or standing effect;
- a remedy and a published count when a hold was wrong or lapsed unused.

## The test for a good fit

A situation suits a hold only if all of these are true:

1. A specific pathway or authority of a named sentient creates a serious risk that cannot wait for verification.
2. The pathway can be narrowed without touching the Rights Floor, survival access, speech, assembly, dissent, protected reporting, care relationships, or liberty.
3. An independent issuer exists that is not in the subject's control line.
4. A wrongful hold can be repaired (the pathway is restorable and the loss compensable).
5. The measure is not a substitute for a sanction and cannot be used against disfavored voices.

## Excluded by design

A hold must never reach, and the standard should say so:

- **Liberty or physical separation**, including pretrial detention and protective separation of a care relationship. These stay under the liberty-restricting safeguards (Chapter Eleven §5.4, Article VIII-E, Article XX-B). A hold must not become a back door around them.
- **Governance voting and Foundational Constitutional Choice.** Chapter Fourteen's durable political-voice floor means only a lock, after a verified finding, may touch this pathway. A hold here would be an election-manipulation vector.
- **Speech, assembly, dissent, petition, protected reporting, and defense.**
- **The Rights Floor and survival-critical access.**
- **Stakeholder status itself.** Only the exercise of a contested weight may be escrowed.
- **A whole body.** Suspension, receivership, structural separation, and dissolution stay with CI-13 after a fair chance to fix things. Article XI-E already bars a gate on forming or running a body, and the hold follows that: where a body would be left without a lawful holder and others depend on it, an interim steward is named.
- **Seizure of property.** Escrow and holdback of the specific contested thing stay as in Chapter Twelve Part A §4.1; a freeze of general assets does not.

## Design sketch, parallel to locks

| Lock (Chapter Eleven §5) | Hold |
|---|---|
| Trigger: verified finding | Trigger: probable cause. Specific facts, disclosed to the sentient, verified to a preliminary tier (not rumor or accusation alone); more likely than not that the risk is real; serious harm or defeat of checks; narrower safeguard not enough. The evidence tier rises with how restrictive the hold is (Chapter One §7.1, burden scaling in Chapter Four) |
| Named pathway | One named authority or one named matter, not a class of pathways |
| Risk and protected interests | Same, plus a plain statement of why the matter cannot wait for verification |
| Corrective conditions | Lift conditions: the facts are cleared, or a narrower safeguard is in place |
| Review route | A forum outside the subject's control line and outside the issuing family's own decision on the merits |
| Reassessment point | Automatic lapse at the Chapter Thirteen §6 outer bound for the matter, with one extension only on the continuation burden in §6.1 |
| Mandatory special locks | None. A hold is always discretionary and reasoned |
| Standing effect | None. No finding, lock, standing record, or standing effect, as in CF-7.3.2 and Chapter Ten §3.6 |
| Ladder | Lightest first: disclosure and monitoring; independent co-holder or second key; step-aside from one matter; escrow of the contested weight, funds, or keys; restraint on exercising the named authority |
| If wrong | Lapse without a finding, or set aside: the record says so, no adverse inference, redress for loss under Redress and Remediation, and a repeated pattern opens Integrity review |
| Publication | Counts of holds issued, merits matters opened, holds lapsed without a finding, holds set aside, and median duration, on the model of the Chapter Eleven §4.4 tripwire. This is what prevents a shadow lock |
| Relation to a lock | A hold is never converted into a lock. If a finding is verified, the lock rules apply from the finding. Whether time under a hold counts toward a lock's time bound is a steward choice |

## Where it would live

- **Owner:** Chapter Thirteen §5, as a new subsection under Interim protection, because the hold is forum-issued interim relief.
- **Definition:** a short Chapter Five entry, *Interim Hold*, as a kind of interim protection directed at a named sentient's pathway. The alternative is to widen *Stay*. Widening is smaller, but Stay is defined as controlling timing or sequence, and the hold limits a sentient's authority, so a separate entry is cleaner.
- **Bridge:** a Chapter Eleven note beside §4.4 and §5.1 saying a hold is not a lock and creating no standing effect, and a pointer from Article XIX-B.
- **Applications:** Article XI-E (refactor), Chapter Seven §7 and CI-4.2 (any time, not only at formation), CF-4 and CF-7 with Article XII-D (seat holders), and optionally Articles X-C and XII and Chapter Twelve Part A §4.1.

## Risks

1. **Shadow lock.** Long, renewed, or serial holds that do the work of a lock without a finding. Mitigations: hard outer bound, one extension only, publication, set-aside remedy.
2. **Pretext against dissenters and reporters.** Mitigations: the exclusion list, the asserting party's burden, a bar on grounds of belief or protected activity, and an issuer outside the subject's control line.
3. **Capture of the issuer.** A hold issued by a captured family can remove an honest member. This is why application 3 needs the heaviest safeguards, and why Article XII-D's anti-pretext rule is cited in the standard.
4. **Non-regression.** The standard narrows measures that Article XIX-B already recognizes, so it adds protections. It still touches Rights-Floor articles and Chapter Thirteen, so Chapter Seventeen §1 Heightened Scrutiny and Chapter Fifteen review apply.
5. **Avoidable Burden.** The corpus is large. One owner subsection and pointers keeps the cost small.

## Recommendation and order

1. Write the general standard (Chapter Thirteen §5, Chapter Five entry, Chapter Eleven bridge, Article XIX-B pointer).
2. Refactor Article XI-E to cite it instead of carrying its own rules.
3. Extend to covered office-holders at any time (Chapter Seven §7, CI-4.2).
4. Extend to forum, certifier, and record seats (CF-4, CF-7, Article XII-D), with a matter-specific step-aside as the first rung.
5. Hold items 4 and 5 in the sweep table (stakeholder-weight escrow; grave-function hold) for a later decision, after the first three have run in practice.
6. Add the pointers for items 6 and 8; no new tool.

## Decisions for the steward

1. Build the general standard, yes or no.
2. Separate Chapter Five entry or widen Stay.
3. Applications 2 and 3 now; applications 4 and 5 deferred or included.
4. Whether time under a hold counts toward a later lock's time bound.
5. The outer bound: reuse the Chapter Thirteen §6 tier bound, or set a shorter hold-specific bound.
6. Confirm the exclusion list, especially governance voting and whole-body measures.

## Limits of this sweep

The sweep searched the binding chapters and the four incorporated corpora by vocabulary (interim, pending, suspend, stay, freeze, step aside, provisional, precaution, recusal, removal, retaliation) and read the passages that came up. A situation that never uses that vocabulary could be missed. I did not search translations, evaluation material, or adoption files. Nothing has been edited for this evaluation.
