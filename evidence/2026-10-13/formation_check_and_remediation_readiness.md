## Request

Add two checks. (1) In Chapter Seven (institutional formation): a check on staff standing and authenticity, to see whether they are actually aligned in purpose with the institution's goals. (2) In Chapter Nine: a similar check during the alignment process when there is misalignment, to determine whether the system or institution is actually interested in and capable of fixing the problems. Denial and revocation should be concrete possibilities.

## What changed

- **Chapter Seven §7 Formation Check: Office-Holder Standing and Authenticity** (new; the old §7 Relationship to Other Chapters is now §8). Covered offices are checked on two limbs. *Standing:* no lock, competency bar met, conflicts cleared, from verified records only. *Authenticity:* the holder holds the office in their own right and for the stated purpose, found from control, interests and rewards, conduct, and protected voice; holding a belief or belonging to a group does not count by itself (see the belief-rule note below). Other staff get a structural limb only. The result is a published statement. It is not a gate (Article XI-E). Chapter Seven §1, §2 Timing, §4, the opening note, the chart, and the new §8 relationships are updated.
- **Chapter Nine Part B §7.1 Remediation Readiness: Willingness and Capacity to Correct** (new). Run whenever a review finds misalignment. Willingness and capacity are found from conduct and evidence, with a verified proof step. Findings: ready, ready with support, willing not able, able not willing, neither, not yet shown (one bounded deferral). A conditional certification may not rest on a plan the check did not find ready.
- **Chapter Nine Part B §7.2 Denial and Revocation** (new). Denial is the existing outcome non-certification. Revocation is the existing outcome withdrawal. No new outcome word. Denial is required on three grounds and revocation on five. Protection first: a Class A survival-essential system changes status at once and keeps operating only under separately authorized interim reliance. Not a permanent bar. Not a licence.
- **Part B §8.1 and §9:** a Remediation readiness record item; a missed proof step or false readiness evidence requires timely review.
- **Part D:** §12.2 item 7 (so seven items, not six); §12.4; §13.3 and §13.4; new §13.5 for Institutions; §14.2 triggers; §15 bridge and evasion. §13.5 effects stay inside the no-gate rule: status, notice, adverse weight in its Systems' certifications, no seat in others' certifications while Not Certified, referral to CI-13, standing bridge.
- **Elsewhere:** Chapter Nine index (run sheet, record checklist, institution row); CI-3.6 (formation check statement template and a periodic-review item); doc_architecture (Thirteenth move; Twelfth move now says seven items); TODO; MEMLOG.

## Choices for sign-off

1. Covered offices are the governing authority, Role Map seats, and offices that bind others or hold records, keys, or funds. Other staff get a structural check only, because screening every member of staff would be disproportionate and rights-invasive.
2. "Denial" and "revocation" are plain names for non-certification and withdrawal.
3. The five revocation grounds in Part B §7.2.
4. A Not Certified Institution may not hold a seat in another body's certification or host a forum. This is the one real teeth for an institution that Article XI-E allows, because it is not a bar on forming or operating.

## Gates in Article XI-E and the Institutional Authority Standing Lock

Request: update Article XI-E, which bars any gate on forming or operating an institution, to include a gate for outstanding locks and for sentients who have probable cause for misalignment. Follow-up: add an institutional lock for institution-related wrongdoing if none exists. None existed: Chapter Eleven §5.5 had only the Forum-Service, Stakeholder-Participation, and Anti-Constitutional Trust locks, and §10.6 routed institutional evasion to general locks.

**What changed**

- **Article XI-E:** keeps the bar on any gate on forming or operating, and adds two narrow gates on a named sentient's taking or exercising governing, controlling, or owning authority. (1) Outstanding lock: no authority within the named pathway of an unresolved Institutional Authority Standing Lock, Anti-Constitutional Trust Lock, or other authority lock; the gate reaches only as far as the lock. (2) Probable cause: a forum with jurisdiction under Chapter Thirteen §5 Interim protection may issue a reasoned interim hold on named authority in a named body, on specific verified facts disclosed to the sentient, more likely than not misaligned (conduct, control, rewards; never belief), a real risk of serious harm or of defeating the body's checks, and a narrower safeguard not enough. The hold is time-bound and contestable. It is not a finding, lock, standing record, or standing effect. The imposing party bears the burden. The gates do not bar wages, the Rights Floor, survival access, speech, defense, or protected reporting, and they may not be used against dissenters, reporters, or organizers. No sentient needs clearance before forming or running a body.
- **Chapter Eleven §5.5:** new Institutional Authority Standing Lock (trigger, named pathway with look-through to successors and nominee-held vehicles, what it does not restrict, risk, protected interests, corrective conditions including a verified authenticity finding, review route, reassessment point, gate effect, no lock from probable cause, non-substitution). §4.2 and §10.6 point to it.
- **Chapter Five:** pointer in the Standing Lock entry; note in the creation-rights limits that the only gates are the two narrow gates.
- **Chapter Seven §7:** limb one checks both gates; probable-cause facts go to a forum, and the body may not hold a sentient out on its own view; the "No gate" result is now "No gate on the body".
- **Part D:** §11.3 notes that the gates attach to the sentient; §13.5 referral to the lock or the hold, never on the Institution; §14.2 trigger; §15 evasion by nominee.
- **CI-3.6:** template and anti-evasion text.

**Authentic formation (added at the custodian's request).** Article XI-E now says a sentient may form or run a body only when authentically aligned with its stated purpose, and that forming or running one with intent to mislead, or with probable cause to find that intent, is not allowed. It is a condition of the right and not a licence: no clearance up front, judged on conduct, control, and rewards and never belief, a founder with no record presumed authentic, the asserting party bearing the burden. The Limits clause and the Chapter Five creation-rights note also name bodies formed to mislead. Probable cause of intent to mislead falls under the same interim hold; a body left without a lawful holder gets an interim steward.

**Why probable cause is a hold and not a lock.** A lock attaches only to a verified finding (Chapter Eleven §5.1). Probable cause is not a finding, so it can only support an interim, narrow, reviewable measure under Chapter Thirteen interim protection.

**Later (same day):** the interim hold described above was generalized into the Interim Hold standard (Chapter Thirteen §5, Chapter Five, Chapter Eleven §5.6). See [interim_hold_sweep_evaluation.md](interim_hold_sweep_evaluation.md).

**Intent to harm (added at the custodian's request):** Article XI-E's authentic-formation condition, the probable-cause hold ground, and the Limits clause now also cover forming or running a body with intent to harm others. It means harm this Constitution forbids; ordinary competition, lawful criticism, and lawful dissent are not harm in this sense. Intent is found from verified conduct, design and control, rewards, and verified statements (see the notes below). The Chapter Five creation-rights note and Chapter Seven §7 say the same.

**Statements count (correction at the custodian's request):** the first wording said intent is never found from what a sentient says. That was too broad. A verified statement, call, or plan that others deserve harm, or discriminatory treatment forbidden under Article VI-C, is conduct and counts against a founder or holder (Article XI-E; Chapter Seven §7 *What a holder has said*). Private belief, ideology, and professed enthusiasm still do not count against them, and a profession of good intent does not count in their favor. Safeguards: the statement must itself state or call for harm or discriminatory treatment (criticism, dissent, satire, quotation, reporting, and scholarship do not); it is verified and read in context; it comes from public material, the body or office, or a protected report, with no surveillance of private speech; the holder may answer and show by action that it no longer holds; and it is evidence about authority, not a penalty for the speech.

**Belief rule, threaded (second correction at the custodian's request):** the "never judged on belief, ideology, politics, religion, association, identity, or professed enthusiasm" wording was an absolute, and discrimination is often justified in religious or political terms. Now: holding a belief, or belonging to a faith, party, or group, does not count by itself, and a profession of good intent does not count in a founder's favor. A religious, political, or ideological label is no shield for a statement, call, plan, or act that harms others or treats them in ways forbidden under Article VI-C; that is judged on what it states, calls for, or does. The basis is the Constitution's own limit in Article XI-A (belief is protected in manifestation only consistently with the rights of others, and accommodation does not exempt compliance), Article XI-G (offense or disagreement alone is not harm), and Article XI-F. Chapter Seven §7 gives three rules (*Holding is not enough*, *Labels are no shield*, *Control is not membership*) and says the section decides only what evidence the check may use; it does not widen Article VI-C. Article XI-E, the Interim Hold failure list in Chapter Five, Part D item 7, and the Chapter Eleven restoration condition use the same threaded wording. Review at Chapter Seventeen §1 Heightened Scrutiny should look at this rule specifically, since it sits where Article XI-A and Article VI-C meet.

**Principle added (Expression and the Harm Line, at the custodian's request):** the repeated wording about statements, labels, and harm is now anchored in Chapter One §7.5. It restates existing rules and adds none. Expression is protected as expression; speech, publication, design, and policy that are the means of harm, harassment and bullying, cruelty, or discrimination are judged on what they state, call for, or do, the same way for a sentient and for an institution, and whatever label they carry. Criticism, offense, and disagreement are not harm, and calling criticism of an institution bullying is a limit on expression that carries the burden. Limiting expression is distinguished from using it as evidence. Article XI-B, Article XI-G, Article XI-E, Chapter Seven §7, Part D item 7, the Chapter Five Interim Hold failure list, and the Chapter Eleven restoration condition point to it. The Chapter Seven §7 evidence-use safeguards remain. Review at Chapter Seventeen §1 Heightened Scrutiny should confirm there is no new restriction and that the limit-versus-evidence distinction fits Article XI-B.
