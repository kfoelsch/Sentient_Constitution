# Incentive Alignment: Chapter 1 vs Chapter 5 Alignment Analysis

**Date**: May 6, 2026  
**Scope**: Comparison of incentive alignment treatments at principle (Chapter 1 §7.2) and foundational definition (Chapter 5) levels

---

## Executive Summary

Chapter 1 §7.2 and Chapter 5 Incentive Alignment are substantially aligned but operate at different layers with different depth and scope. **Three significant gaps** require clarification:

1. **Stewardship Incentive Operationalization**: Chapter 1 provides detailed lists of lawful incentive targets; Chapter 5 defers to implementation corpus
2. **Active Misalignment Correction Duty**: Chapter 1 requires active correction and treats capture as escalation; Chapter 5 definition lacks this operational layer
3. **Proportionate Controls Detail**: Chapter 1 §7.2.5 specifies concrete controls (necessity, concentration limits, resolution procedures); Chapter 5 definition-layer scope is narrower

These gaps appear **intentional** (layering between principle and definition) but warrant **explicit cross-reference** or clarifying language.

## Detailed Gap Analysis

### Gap 1: Stewardship Incentive Operationalization

#### Chapter 1 §7.2.2 Specifies (Explicit Detail):

**Lawful Incentive Targets:**
- Safety
- Truth
- Auditability
- Timely remediation
- Anti-capture conduct
- Preservation and durable expansion of productive capacity (consistent with Chapter constraints and Chapter Nine rights)

**Prohibited Incentive Targets:**
- Raw throughput
- Utilization
- Headcount targets
- Narrow financial targets where they predictably conflict with:
  - Chapter One constraints
  - Chapter Nine rights floor
  - Underlying constitutional outcomes

**Explicit Negative:** Must not reward creation or maintenance of **avoidable burden** (per Chapter One §6.1.4)

#### Chapter 5 Definition (Implicit):

The Chapter 5 **Baseline Constitutional Alignment** subsection requires alignment with:
- Safety, Truth, systemic stability, and meaningful agency

But does **NOT explicitly list** what stewardship incentives should or should not target.

#### Assessment:

- **Intentional**: Chapter 5 definitions establish foundational concepts; Chapter 1 principles operationalize them
- **Gap Type**: Definition-principle layering is appropriate, but **cross-reference clarity needed**
- **Recommendation**: Add cross-reference in Chapter 5 Trace section or in the Implementation Routing subsection directing readers to Chapter 1 §7.2.2 for detailed stewardship operationalization

**Proposed Language**: Consider adding to Chapter 5 Incentive Alignment E-subsection under **Baseline Constitutional Alignment**:
> "Stewardship operationalization—including detailed lists of lawful and prohibited incentive targets—is specified in Chapter One §7.2.2. This definition layer supplies the foundational concept; Chapter One supplies the operative guidance."

### Gap 2: Active Misalignment Correction Duty and Capture Escalation

#### Chapter 1 §7.2.4 Specifies (Explicit Operational Mandate):

**Active Correction Requirement:**
- "Where constitutional misalignment is identified, systems must modify, constrain, or override such incentives to restore alignment."

**Capture Detection & Escalation:**
- "Systems must detect, disclose, and mitigate materially relevant system-capture conditions."
- "Concentrated or obscured control structures that undermine challenge rights, oversight, or accountability are incompatible with this chapter. They must be treated as escalation conditions under **Chapters Two through Five**."

#### Chapter 5 Definition (Not Explicitly Addressed):

The **Baseline Constitutional Alignment** C-subsection states:
- "Sustained incentives that predictably undermine Chapter One values without mitigation, disclosure, or feasible override are non-compliant."

This implies active response ("without mitigation, disclosure") but does **NOT explicitly mandate**:
- Active correction/override processes
- System duty to detect and disclose
- Escalation procedures for concentrated control

#### Assessment:

- **Gap Type**: Chapter 5 defines **non-compliance condition**; Chapter 1 defines **active operational duty**
- **Issue**: The definition layer is reactive (stating when something is non-compliant) rather than prescriptive (stating what systems must do)
- **Severity**: Medium — the C-subsection implies remediation, but readers may not understand the affirmative active-correction duty

**Recommendation**: Strengthen Chapter 5 C-subsection to reference the **active correction mandate** and **capture escalation**

**Proposed Language**: Consider revising Chapter 5 **Baseline Constitutional Alignment** C-subsection:
> "Sustained incentives that predictably undermine Chapter One values without mitigation, disclosure, or feasible override are non-compliant. Systems must actively detect, disclose, and correct such misalignments (Chapter One §7.2.4). Concentrated or obscured control structures defeating challenge or oversight are escalation conditions (Chapter One §7.2.4; Chapters Two–Five)."

---

### Gap 3: Proportionate Controls Detail — Contingent Claims/Games/Markets

#### Chapter 1 §7.2.5 Specifies (Detailed Control Framework):

**Prohibited Incentive Pathways** (four explicit categories):
1. No unlawful harm reward/normalization
2. No coercion of protected decisions
3. No corrupt office use or non-public power
4. No structural distortion of fiduciary/public/rights-relevant decisions without mitigation

**Proportionate Controls Specification** (operational detail):
- Apply Necessity and Proportionality to permitted underlying events
- **Govern resolution procedures** and outcome-resolution sources (independence, multi-source rules where feasible, contestable dispute pathways)
- **Set concentration, leverage, exposure limits** appropriate to vulnerability, dependency, systemic stability
- **Evaluate misuse** consistent with section 7.1 (including coordination to affect resolving events, scale dynamics)

**Epistemic Boundary** (explicit):
- Prices, odds, pool sizes, aggregated signals are **NOT, without more, sufficient evidence** for truth/probability/compliance determinations
- Where adopting instruments reference such signals, same Truth/contestability/evidence expectations apply as elsewhere

#### Chapter 5 Definition (Narrower Scope):

The **Contingent Claims, Games, Markets** subsection states:
- O: Structures in scope where materially relevant
- E: Must satisfy Chapter One §7.2.5; include outcome-manipulation risk, capture, privileged information, truth treatment
- C: Settlement structures rewarding unlawful harm, corrupt advantage, or material distortion **without proportionate mitigation** are non-compliant

But does **NOT detail**:
- What "proportionate controls" means operationally
- Concentration/leverage/exposure limit framework
- Multi-source resolution procedures
- Necessity/Proportionality application to underlying events
- How to evaluate epistemic status of prices/odds

#### Assessment:

- **Gap Type**: Chapter 1 provides **control-design framework**; Chapter 5 provides **boundary condition** (non-compliant if no mitigation)
- **Intentional**: Definition layer appropriately references Chapter One; implementation corpus would supply operational detail
- **Issue**: Readers seeking how-to guidance on controls won't find it in Chapter 5; they must navigate back to Chapter 1 §7.2.5

**Recommendation**: Strengthen cross-reference or add evaluation guidance in Chapter 5

**Proposed Language**: Consider adding to Chapter 5 **Contingent Claims...** E-subsection:
> "Apply Necessity and Proportionality tests to underlying events. Governance must address resolution procedures (independence, multi-source rules, contestability), concentration/leverage/exposure limits scaled to vulnerability and systemic stability, and misuse evaluation including coordination pathways (Chapter One §7.2.5). Prices and odds are insufficient, without more, for truth/compliance determinations; apply same evidence standards as comparable high-impact decisions."

## Additional Observations

### Strong Alignments (No Action Required)

1. **Core Alignment Mandate**: Both layers agree on the fundamental principle—incentives must pull toward Chapter One values, not undermine them
2. **Safety/Truth/Agency Primacy**: Both layers prioritize these as non-negotiable constraints
3. **Aggregated Effects Assessment**: Both require looking at delayed, indirect, and cumulative impacts
4. **System Capture as Core Risk**: Both identify capture as a material threat requiring active mitigation
5. **Epistemic Integrity on Markets**: Both reject price-based shortcuts to truth determinations

### Layering Clarities (Appropriate Differentiation)

1. **Definition vs. Principle**: Chapter 5 appropriately stays at foundational concept level; Chapter 1 appropriately provides principle-level operationalization
2. **O/E/C Structure**: Chapter 5 uses O/E/C discipline to establish compliance boundaries; Chapter 1 uses principle prose to establish values
3. **Corpus Routing**: Both appropriately defer detailed operational rules to corpus_systems.md and corpus_institutions.md

## Conclusion

**Overall Assessment**: Chapter 1 §7.2 and Chapter 5 Incentive Alignment are **well-aligned**. The differences reflect appropriate layering: principles (Chapter 1) provide operationalization; foundational definitions (Chapter 5) provide boundary concepts and compliance conditions.

**Key Gaps**:
1. Stewardship operationalization detail in Chapter 5 (intentional; needs explicit cross-reference)
2. Active correction duty in Chapter 5 definition (should be explicit in C-subsection)
3. Proportionate controls detail in Chapter 5 contingent claims (should be expanded in E-subsection)

**Recommendation**: Implement Priority 2 and Priority 3 actions to strengthen internal cross-references and clarify the definition layer's relationship to the principle layer, particularly around:
- Active correction/detection/disclosure duty
- Proportionate controls framework for contingent claims
- Stewardship operationalization guidance location

This will improve readability and reduce navigation burden for readers seeking to understand complete Incentive Alignment requirements.
