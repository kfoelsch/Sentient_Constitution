This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-4.270** (*Scientific publication, peer review, replication, and evidence stewardship*).

*In plain terms: scientific claims need evidence that others can check, review that can be scrutinized, and a visible path to correction. Shared floors live in **CJS-3.110 §5** (*Oversight: Independent Verification and Claim-Integrity Terms*). This file names who maintains the local records, review routes, incentives, and referrals.*

## CI-4.270 §1: Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this file covers institutional responsibilities for making scientific claims reviewable and correctable.*

This file assigns institutional ownership for scientific publication intake, evidence and review records, correction and access, incentive criteria, and specialist referral.

**Quick orientation:**

- **The basic idea** — material empirical or analytical claims must be publishable with enough method, provenance, uncertainty, and evidence detail for independent checking — not locked behind prestige gates or opaque editorial black boxes.
- **What institutions should rely on** — evidence quality, critique, replication, correction history, and long-run reliability — not journal brand, closed ranking, or impact-factor proxies.
- **What must stay easy** — good-faith correction, amendment, supersession, null results, and replication-oriented work must remain publishable and citable without prestige punishment.
- **What is out of bounds** — suppressing inconvenient results, retaliating against reviewers or replicators, or quietly manipulating the scientific record.
- **CI-4.270 §3** — publication intake and correction records: who receives claims, how versions and retractions are tracked, and what reliance status they carry.
- **CI-4.270 §4** — evidence, review, and reviewer custody: evidence packages, structured review, reviewer independence, and replication status.
- **CI-4.270 §5** — challenge, correction, and access: challenge lanes, correction history, verification access, and bundling justification.
- **CI-4.270 §6** — incentive criteria: rewards tied to evidence quality, not prestige proxies or hidden correction history.
- **CI-4.270 §7** — ombuds, escalation, and technical forum referral: dispute review, **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) escalation, and **CF-22.440** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*) specialist routes.
- **Read with** — **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*) for local procedure maps; **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) for cross-institution escalation; **CF-22.440** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*) for technical specialist forum referral.

## CI-4.270 §2: Why Scientific Publication Integrity Exists

*In plain terms: scientific claims are more useful when others can inspect, test, challenge, and correct them.*

Publication and review practices shape which evidence others can inspect and rely on. Clear institutional responsibility for methods, review, replication, and correction helps expose errors and protects the paths by which claims improve.

**Grounded in:**

- Chapter Two principles:

## CI-4.270 §3: Publication Intake and Correction Records
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-4.270 §3** (*Publication Intake and Correction Records*); **CI-4.270 §4** (*Evidence, Review, and Reviewer Custody*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: scholarly and empirical claims tracked for institutional reliance need a clear intake trail — what was submitted, what changed, what was withdrawn, and whether the institution still treats it as trustworthy.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **publication intake owner** — who receives and tracks versions of incoming scholarly or empirical claims for institutional reliance;
- **versioned intake and correction record** — a searchable history of submissions, amendments, supersessions, retractions, and expressions of concern; and
- **reliance-status field** — whether the institution treats a claim as provisional, verified, contested, superseded, or withdrawn.

## CI-4.270 §4: Evidence, Review, and Reviewer Custody
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-4.270 §4** (*Evidence, Review, and Reviewer Custody*); **CI-4.270 §3** (*Publication Intake and Correction Records*); **CI-4.270 §5** (*Challenge, Correction, and Access*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: a claim is only as good as the evidence behind it and the review that tested it — methods, data access, reviewer independence, and whether anyone tried to replicate it must be on the official record.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **evidence-package file** — methods, provenance, uncertainty statements, funding and conflict disclosures, data or lawful access limits, and materials needed for proportionate verification;
- **review file** — structured review dimensions separating method quality, data integrity, inferential validity, uncertainty, reproducibility, and fit between evidence and conclusion;
- **reviewer-custody record** — who reviewed what, under what independence and conflict controls, including signed, pseudonymous, confidential, or specialist review modes where used; and
- **replication and adversarial-review status** — whether independent replication or adversarial review was attempted or completed for high-impact, safety-relevant, policy-relevant, or dependency-relevant claims.

## CI-4.270 §5: Challenge, Correction, and Access
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-4.270 §5** (*Challenge, Correction, and Access*); **CI-4.270 §4** (*Evidence, Review, and Reviewer Custody*); **CI-4.270 §6** (*Incentive Criteria*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: sentients who try to replicate, reanalyze, or correct a claim need a real door in — and if publication, funding, and editorial power are bundled together, that bundling must be justified and auditable.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **challenge-lane owner** — who receives good-faith replication attempts, reanalysis, methodological critique, and correction requests;
- **correction file** — auditable record of corrections, retractions, clarifications, and superseding claims, with reasons and dates;
- **access file** — how qualified independent reviewers obtain underlying materials or justified substitutes for verification, subject only to narrow lawful limits; and
- **bundling-justification record** — where publication, review, curation, funding, archival custody, reputation, or reliance functions are bundled, the justification and independent audit route for that bundling.

## CI-4.270 §6: Incentive Criteria
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-4.270 §6** (*Incentive Criteria*); **CI-4.270 §5** (*Challenge, Correction, and Access*).

<br>

*In plain terms: promotions, grants, and hiring should reward honest evidence and visible correction — not hype, citation games, or burying mistakes.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

## CI-4.270 §7: Ombuds, Escalation, and Technical Forum Referral
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with:
  - **CI-4.270 §7** (*Ombuds, Escalation, and Technical Forum Referral*);
  - **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*);
  - **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*);
  - **CF-22.440** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: when publication disputes stall or the local reviewer is conflicted, there must be a named ombuds lane, an escalation path, and — where needed — a specialist technical forum that is not the same shop that benefits from the original claim.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **ombuds or review-lane office** — a named body for publication, review, and replication disputes that is separated from revenue-dependent or prestige-dependent editorial control where material;
- **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*) / **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) **escalation route** — the published path when local review stalls, is captured, or cannot act independently; and
- **CF-22.440** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*) **technical-forum referral route** — where science-facing specialist technical review under **CJS-R09** is required or appropriate.

Read **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*) and **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) for local procedure maps and cross-institution escalation interfaces.
