Core home: 06

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-6.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*).

*In plain terms: **CI-6.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*) is the institutions layer's honesty-and-independence rulebook — how institutions prevent capture, corruption, and hidden conflicts; what they must disclose; and where problems go when local review is not trustworthy. Shared anti-capture floors live in **CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*). What this file adds is local: what each institution must name and maintain locally.*

**Purpose and role:** This file applies shared anti-capture floors to institutional control maps, disclosure and escalation routes, and integrity-trigger handling.

**Quick orientation**

- **CI-6.210 §1** — shared minimum anti-capture and civic-lottery integrity rules (**CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*)).
- **CI-6.210 §2** — named contacts and local records: who publishes disclosures, who tracks fix deadlines, who logs serious violations, and where to escalate when self-review is not trustworthy.
- **CI-6.210 §3** — moved to [Subcore 4-B §3 Integrity Trigger Categories](../subcore_04.060_integrity_triggers_gifts_funding.md#3-integrity-trigger-categories): the trigger categories for corruption, fraud, capture, and related failures, and where each routes.
- **CI-6.210 §4** — moved to [Subcore 4-B §4 Gifts, Apparent Conflicts, and Funding Integrity](../subcore_04.060_integrity_triggers_gifts_funding.md#4-gifts-apparent-conflicts-and-funding-integrity): the gift register, gift threshold, apparent-conflict clearance, and funding-change log that carry the **Article XIII-C** (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*) and **Article XIII-D** (*Funding Integrity*) floors.

*In plain terms: each institution must write down who does what when honesty or independence is at stake — not leave it implied. That means a published map of anti-corruption safeguards, named roles for publishing disclosures and tracking fix deadlines, records when serious violations trigger forfeiture, oversight of prediction-market-style systems, and a clear path to outside review when the institution cannot credibly investigate itself.*

Each institution must maintain and publish:

- an integrity control map naming who owns each safeguard;
- disclosure and resolution records — what must be published and the deadlines to fix problems;
- grave-violation forfeiture records when serious misconduct triggers loss of benefits or assets;
- supervision rules for contingent-claim and event-market systems; and
- escalation paths to **CI-6.215 §2** (*External Assurance Triggers*) or **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) when local review lacks credible independence.

Each institution must also name:

- who publishes each disclosure category, who keeps the resolution-window file, and where sanctions cases escalate (**CI-4.215** (*Resource stewardship and incentive integrity*), **CI-18.210** (*Institutional failure and dissolution*), **CI-25.205** (*Institutional accountability and sanctions*));
- who keeps grave-violation forfeiture records and the contest route under **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*); and
- for supervised contingent-claim or event-market systems: who governs resolution sources, how decision power is separated, and how insider-information abuse is prevented.
