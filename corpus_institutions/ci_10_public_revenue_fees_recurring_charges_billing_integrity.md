This subsection states **orientation** for how institutions—and adopting authorities where they have discretion—should think about **taxes, charges, and similar revenue measures** in relation to `corpus_systems.md` **Chapter S2** system classes. It does **not** specify tax bases, rates, credits, enforcement, or cross-border rules; those remain **governing law** outside this file (see `doc_architecture.md`, **constitutional and political** embedding boundary).

**Aim.** Fiscal and quasi-fiscal design should **avoid** making **survival-relevant or normal-operation-critical access** the **primary** source of public extraction. It should **prefer** instruments that are **lawful, predictable, disclosed, and contestable** enough to keep administration from becoming a **de facto barrier** to rights, remedies, or essential participation.

**Class A and Class B.** These classes describe systems on which sentients or dependent systems **depend for survival (Class A)** or **normal functioning within survival-relevant timeframes (Class B)**. Revenue policy should **default to placing the burden of general taxation and similar charges elsewhere**. Examples include **discretionary, commercial, premium, or externality-generating** activity, or **bases that do not single out** the **core delivery** of essential inputs such as food, water, energy, healthcare, environmental stability, and comparable foundations. Where **user charges** exist for A/B services, they should be **proportionate**, **transparent**, and **structured** so they do **not** deny **minimum access** to what the classification already treats as indispensable.

**Class L and Class P.** These classes describe **bounded, replaceable, localized, or private** scopes. **Access fees**, **subscriptions**, **usage-based charges**, and similar **transaction-linked** revenue are **generally appropriate** fiscal tools for such systems when they are **proportionate**, **disclosed**, and **consistent** with contestability and pathway-access expectations elsewhere in this file. Relevant provisions include **CI-10.2** (*Recurring charges, renewals, and commercial billing integrity*) and **CI-12.3** (*Digital self-service pathway integrity*) where institutions supervise or set compliance expectations.

**Class C.** These systems **materially shape coordination** at scale but are **not**, by definition, **operational prerequisites** for Class A or Class B. Fiscal **planning** should reflect that difference:
- **Avoid** structures that make **ordinary, baseline participation** in widely relied-on coordination **prohibitively costly** or **administratively exclusionary** for typical participants.
- **Prefer** placing fees or assessed contributions on **commercial-scale activity**, **concentration or gatekeeper rents**, **advertising or attention markets**, **high-volume business interfaces**, or other activity where **externalized costs** or **discretionary upside** are concentrated. Avoid putting that burden on **bare access** to coordination that is approaching infrastructure-like reliance for large populations.
- **Re-evaluate classification and fiscal treatment together** when evidence shows a Class C system has become **de facto operationally necessary** for dependents that would otherwise meet Class B or Class A criteria. In that case, **essential-like access** should receive the same protective fiscal default as Class A/B core access, not serve as the **primary** extraction point.

**Commercial API, compute, and high-volume interface surcharges.** Where governed systems expose APIs, model access, compute, bandwidth, queue priority, or comparable high-demand interfaces, institutions may authorize **commercial-use surcharges**, premium service levels, or assessed contributions for **commercial-scale**, **automated bulk**, **low-latency**, or **revenue-generating** use. Those charges should be structured so that the systems providing value from shared information, inference, coordination, or compute can recover costs and fund additional capacity when demand rises. They must remain **published**, **proportionate**, **non-discriminatory except by lawful and disclosed use category**, and **contestable** where material. They must not make **baseline**, **rights-pathway**, **Class A**, or **Class B** access practically unavailable. For **Class C** systems, commercial surcharges are especially appropriate where they place cost on high-volume business interfaces rather than ordinary participation.

**Peak-load priority and revenue use.** Institutions supervising Class A, Class B, or Class C interfaces must require operators to publish priority rules for constrained periods, consistent with `corpus_systems.md` **Chapter S2** (*Scarce-capacity, API, and traffic-priority handling for Class A/B/C systems*). Revenue from commercial-use surcharges should be traceably allocated to operations, security, resilience, compute or bandwidth expansion, remedy capacity, and ecosystem/public-good support under `corpus_systems.md` **Protocol S5**, with enough reporting for **Article XV-A** auditability and challenge.

**Purpose.** This subsection records institutional requirements for **recurring and transaction-linked charges**, including subscriptions, memberships, paid tiers, and trial-to-paid conversion, where institutions **supervise, charter, or set compliance expectations** for governed systems. It does **not** restate constitutional rights. Constitutional meaning remains authoritative in **Sentient Constitution Chapter Ten, Article II-D** (*Post-Sale Access and Subscription Integrity*), **Article XIV**, **Article X-A**, and related **Chapter Five** definitions such as **Meaningful Agency**, **Consent**, **Coercion and Manipulation** (including **predatory choice architecture** as described in its evaluative examples), and **Trust Degradation and Misleading Reliance** where **material reliance** on represented terms, renewal, or exit conditions is at issue. **Chapter Six** **contribution / violation / standing** classification remains authoritative where offense-linked evaluation applies.

**Eligibility for class-appropriate subscription-style revenue.** For supervised scope, **access fees**, **subscriptions**, and **usage-based charges** are **institutionally aligned** with the **Class L and Class P** orientation in **CI-10.1** (*Public revenue, user fees, and class-aligned burden*) only when the requirements in this subsection are met. Those requirements scale to **class**, **tier**, and **material impact** under `corpus_systems.md` **Chapter S2** and **Chapter S3**.

**Shared charge-pathway floor.** Commitment, renewal, trial-conversion, bundled-offer, cancellation, downgrade, and billing-cessation integrity are governed by `corpus_joint_structure.md` **CJS-5D.2** (*Implementation and cross-implementation interoperability, portability, and exit-integrity terms*), including its **Digital self-service pathway integrity** and **Commitment, renewal, and charge-exit integrity** rules. **Class C** surfaces and higher stewardship tiers carry stronger attestation and audit expectations than localized **Class P** scope, consistent with proportionality and dependency signals in **CI-9** (*Classification-linked institutional obligations*) and **CI-10.1** (*Public revenue, user fees, and class-aligned burden*). Where governing law sets a stricter billing, notice, renewal, cancellation, or disclosure standard, supervised institutional scope must meet or exceed that stricter standard.

Institutions must document, for governed scope, how **revenue and fee choices** align with **published class/tier assignments** and how **misclassification or drift** toward essentiality triggers **review** under `corpus_systems.md` **Chapter S2** reclassification expectations.
