<br>

*In plain terms: **CI-3** is the institutions layer's separation-of-powers rulebook — how authority is divided, what the top governing body cannot hand off, how power flows through delegation, what a governing [Charter](../core_05defs_continuity.md#charter) must state, and what happens in emergencies. Shared lane categories and separation floors live in **CJS-5.11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*); this file states what each institution must publish and maintain locally.*

**Quick orientation**

- **CI-3.1** — decisions that stay with the governing authority and cannot be delegated.
- **CI-3.2** — required functional lanes (direction, execution, challenge, assurance, publication) and who hosts each one.
- **CI-3.3** — authority chain from source authorization through every delegated role.
- **CI-3.4** — emergency delegation limits and rapid post-event review.
- **CI-3.5** — plain-language risk appetite statement, reviewed on cadence and after serious incidents.
- **CI-3.6** — Charter contents, periodic review, amendment, and formation template.

*In plain terms: some decisions are too important to hand to a subordinate — the governing body must keep them. At minimum that means staying aligned with the constitution, accepting serious risk, deciding to dissolve the institution, and adopting or amending its [Charter](../core_05defs_continuity.md#charter). The following are non-delegable by the governing authority:
- constitutional alignment,
- material risk acceptance,
- dissolution decisions,
- [Charter](../core_05defs_continuity.md#charter) adoption and material charter amendment.
Additional non-delegable duties must be listed.

*In plain terms: power must be split into distinct jobs so no one office runs everything unchecked. Required lanes include setting direction and policy, carrying decisions out, challenging and reviewing them, auditing and assuring integrity, and publishing evidence. Each institution must publish who holds each lane, note when one body hosts more than one lane, and document safeguards that keep those lanes independent — so outsiders can audit the map, challenge it, and see that power has not quietly pooled in one place.*

Apply [corpus_joint_structure.md](../corpus_joint_structure.md) **CJS-5.11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*) **Constitutional lane and functional separation** for the shared lane categories and separation floor.

This subsection states institutional owner duties: each institution must publish which local office, body, role, or process hosts each required lane; identify any merged hosting arrangement; document independence safeguards where one institution hosts more than one lane; and keep the lane map auditable, contestable, and current enough to prevent capture, unchecked concentration, and silent failure.

<br>

*In plain terms: every institution needs a published **Charter** that says what it is for, what it may do, what it must not do, who it affects, how the Charter can change, and how often it is re-checked. The paperwork can evolve — silent scope creep cannot. This section states the minimum fields and review duties; it does not invent a new creation right or let paper scope defeat real impact.*

**Owner boundary.** This subsection states institutional Charter **publication, contents, review, and amendment** duties. Meaning lives in Chapter Five [Charter](../core_05defs_continuity.md#charter). Creation floors remain under [System Creation](../core_05defs_participation.md#system-creation-constitutional) and [Business Creation](../core_05defs_participation.md#business-creation-constitutional) and must not be narrowed here. Shared formation-burden scaling lives in **CJS-5.11** (*Formation and authorization proportionality floor*). System alignment certification consumes Charter fields under [Chapter Seven](../core_07_b_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#111-minimum-record-contents); it does not replace this institutional duty.

**When a Charter is required.** Each institution must publish and maintain a governing Charter. Where an institution operates, stewards, or materially controls a system or commercial form that is itself materially impactful, the institutional Charter must identify that scope or point to the system's or business's Charter (or equivalent published scope instrument) so reviewers can reconstruct the full claim.

**Minimum Charter fields (formation template).** At formation and after each material amendment, the published Charter must state, in plain language sufficient for affected sentients and auditors:

1. **Identity** — legal or organizational name; steward or governing body; version and effective date.
2. **Purpose** — what the institution exists to do.
3. **In-scope activities** — functions, services, authorities, and geographic or network domains claimed.
4. **Out-of-scope limits** — material activities, authorities, or domains the institution is **not** authorized to exercise.
5. **Affected communities and dependencies** — who relies on the institution, and which systems or infrastructures it depends on or gates, where material.
6. **Classification assumptions** — system class, stewardship tier, or equivalent burden assumptions relied on, with a pointer to **CS-4** / **CS-5** where applicable.
7. **Amendment authority** — who may amend, what counts as material amendment, notice and contest paths, and that material amendments are non-delegable under [CI-3.1](#ci-31-non-delegable-governing-duties).
8. **Periodic-review cadence** — next review date or interval, scaled to [material stake](../core_00_preamble.md#material-stake), class, dependency, and risk under [Proportionality](../core_05defs_accountability.md#proportionality) and [Classification-Scaled Governance](../core_05defs_oversight.md#classification-scaled-governance).
9. **Alignment and contest hooks** — how charter–behavior mismatch, overdue review, or operation outside chartered scope routes to internal correction, [Chapter Seven](../core_07_b_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#16-reopening-drift-and-non-evasion) reopening where certification applies, and [Chapter Eleven §4.3](../core_11-11_forum.md#43-institutional-forums) Institutional forums.

**Periodic review.** On the published cadence, and sooner after material incident, dependency growth, class change, credible challenge, or observed operation outside stated scope, the governing authority must review whether:

- stated purpose and in-scope / out-of-scope limits still match observed operation and [System Boundaries](../core_05defs_continuity.md#system-boundaries);
- classification assumptions remain honest;
- amendment and contest paths still work in practice;
- any material mismatch is corrected by amendment, remediation, recertification, or scope contraction — not by silent expansion.

**Anti-evasion.** Charter text does not establish classification, defeat [System Boundary Integrity](../core_05defs_continuity.md#system-boundary-integrity), or substitute for forum-supervised system alignment certification where Chapter Seven requires it. Paper-only Charters, overdue reviews treated as current authorization, and delegations that expand scope beyond the Charter are non-compliant under this subsection and [CI-3.3](#ci-33-authority-chain-and-delegation-controls).

**Systems and businesses.** Non-institutional systems and commercial forms that require a Charter under Chapter Five [Charter](../core_05defs_continuity.md#charter) should use the same minimum field set. Certification-record duties remain with Chapter Seven; institutional adoption, custody, and non-delegable amendment for institutions remain with this subsection.
