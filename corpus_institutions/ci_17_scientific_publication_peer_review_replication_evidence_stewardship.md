<br>

*In plain terms: **CI-17** is the institutions layer's rulebook for how science and scholarship actually work in practice — publishing claims, peer review, replication, corrections, and keeping evidence trustworthy enough to rely on. Science is public verification infrastructure, not a prestige contest. Evidence quality, critique, replication, and correction behavior must matter more than journal brand, impact factor, or who you know. Correcting an error must always be easier than hiding one. Shared claim-testing, review, reliance, correction, and incentive-integrity floors live in **CJS-5.11**; constitutional meaning lives in **Article XVII-E** (*Scientific Publication, Review, and Replication Integrity*); this file states what each institution must name, file, and maintain locally.*

**Quick orientation**

- **The basic idea** — material empirical or analytical claims must be publishable with enough method, provenance, uncertainty, and evidence detail for independent checking — not locked behind prestige gates or opaque editorial black boxes.
- **What institutions should rely on** — evidence quality, critique, replication, correction history, and long-run reliability — not journal brand, closed ranking, or impact-factor proxies.
- **What must stay easy** — good-faith correction, amendment, supersession, null results, and replication-oriented work must remain publishable and citable without prestige punishment.
- **What is out of bounds** — suppressing inconvenient results, retaliating against reviewers or replicators, or quietly manipulating the scientific record.
- **CI-17.1** — publication intake and correction records: who receives claims, how versions and retractions are tracked, and what reliance status they carry.
- **CI-17.2** — evidence, review, and reviewer custody: evidence packages, structured review, reviewer independence, and replication status.
- **CI-17.3** — challenge, correction, and access: challenge lanes, correction history, verification access, and bundling justification.
- **CI-17.4** — incentive criteria: rewards tied to evidence quality, not prestige proxies or hidden correction history.
- **CI-17.5** — ombuds, escalation, and technical forum referral: dispute review, **CI-8** escalation, and **CF-10** specialist routes.
- **Read with** — **CI-6** for local procedure maps; **CI-8** for cross-institution escalation; **CF-10** for technical specialist forum referral.

*In plain terms: a paper in a famous journal is not automatically true, and a correction in an obscure venue is not automatically worthless. If your institution cannot show what evidence supported a decision, who reviewed it, whether replication was attempted, or how a challenge was handled, it is not stewarding science — it is borrowing its reputation.*

*Shared rules live elsewhere.*

- **[CJS-5.11](../corpus_joint_structure.md)** (*Implementation and cross-implementation independent verification and claim-integrity terms*) — independent verification, reproducibility, anti-single-authority verification, structured claim-testing, reliance and correction integrity, structured review and incentive-integrity floors.
- **Article XVII-E** (*Scientific Publication, Review, and Replication Integrity*) in **Chapter Ten** — science as public verification infrastructure, open publication and evidence sufficiency, review and replication over prestige, correction and contestability.
- **Chapter One** (*Truth*, epistemic disclosure constraints) and **Article XVI-A** — narrow limits on publication where privacy, biosafety, security, or comparable justification applies.
- **CF-10** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*) in `corpus_forum.md` — specialist science-facing technical forum routes under **CJS-R09**.
- **CI-17 scope** — does not repeat those floors; states **Article XVII-E** publication owner files and local routing duties.

**Apply**

- Apply **CJS-5.11** for the shared claim-testing, review, reliance, correction, and incentive-integrity floors.
- **CI-17** states only local **Article XVII-E** publication owner files, ombuds routing, and **CF-10** referral interfaces across **CI-17.1** through **CI-17.5**.

*In plain terms: every claim an institution might rely on needs a clear intake trail — what was submitted, what changed, what was withdrawn, and whether the institution still treats it as trustworthy.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **publication intake owner** — who receives and versions incoming scholarly or empirical claims for institutional reliance;
- **versioned intake and correction record** — a searchable history of submissions, amendments, supersessions, retractions, and expressions of concern; and
- **reliance-status field** — whether the institution treats a claim as provisional, verified, contested, superseded, or withdrawn.

### CI-17.2: Evidence, review, and reviewer custody
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-17.2**; **CI-17.1**; **CI-17.3**.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: a claim is only as good as the evidence behind it and the review that tested it — methods, data access, reviewer independence, and whether anyone tried to replicate it must be on the record.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **evidence-package file** — methods, provenance, uncertainty statements, funding and conflict disclosures, data or lawful access limits, and materials needed for proportionate verification;
- **review file** — structured review dimensions separating method quality, data integrity, inferential validity, uncertainty, reproducibility, and fit between evidence and conclusion;
- **reviewer-custody record** — who reviewed what, under what independence and conflict controls, including signed, pseudonymous, confidential, or specialist review modes where used; and
- **replication and adversarial-review status** — whether independent replication or adversarial review was attempted or completed for high-impact, safety-relevant, policy-relevant, or dependency-relevant claims.

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-17.3**; **CI-17.2**; **CI-17.4**.

<br>

*In plain terms: people who try to replicate, reanalyze, or correct a claim need a real door in — and if publication, funding, and editorial power are bundled together, that bundling must be justified and auditable.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **challenge-lane owner** — who receives good-faith replication attempts, reanalysis, methodological critique, and correction requests;
- **correction file** — auditable record of corrections, retractions, clarifications, and superseding claims, with reasons and dates;
- **access file** — how qualified independent reviewers obtain underlying materials or justified substitutes for verification, subject only to narrow lawful limits; and
- **bundling-justification record** — where publication, review, curation, funding, archival custody, reputation, or reliance functions are bundled, the justification and independent audit route for that bundling.

### CI-17.5: Ombuds, escalation, and technical forum referral
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

<br>

*In plain terms: when publication disputes stall or the local reviewer is conflicted, there must be a named ombuds lane, an escalation path, and — where needed — a specialist technical forum that is not the same shop that benefits from the original claim.*

Each institution in scope must name and maintain locally:

- **ombuds or review-lane office** — a named body for publication, review, and replication disputes that is separated from revenue-dependent or prestige-dependent editorial control where material;
- **CI-6 / CI-8 escalation route** — the published path when local review stalls, is captured, or cannot act independently; and
- **CF-10 technical-forum referral route** — where science-facing specialist technical review under **CJS-R09** is required or appropriate.

Read **CI-6** and **CI-8** for local procedure maps and cross-institution escalation interfaces.
