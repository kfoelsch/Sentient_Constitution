Core home: 08

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*).

*In plain terms: **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*) is the institutions layer's separation-of-powers rulebook — how authority is divided, what the top governing body cannot hand off, how power flows through delegation, what a governing [Institutional Charter](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) must state, and what happens in emergencies. Shared lane categories and separation floors live in **CJS-5.125 §11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*). What this file adds is local: what each institution must publish and maintain locally.*

**Quick orientation**
- **CI-8.205 §1** — decisions that stay with the governing authority and cannot be delegated.
- **CI-8.205 §2** — required functional lanes (direction, execution, challenge, assurance, publication) and who hosts each one.
- **CI-8.205 §3** — authority chain from source authorization through every delegated role.
- **CI-8.205 §4** — emergency delegation limits and rapid post-event review.
- **CI-8.205 §5** — plain-language risk appetite statement, reviewed on cadence and after serious incidents.
- **CI-8.205 §6** — System and Institutional Charter contents, periodic review, amendment, and formation template.

## CI-8.205 §1: Non-delegable governing duties

*In plain terms: some decisions are too important to hand to a subordinate — the governing body must keep them. At minimum that means staying aligned with the constitution, accepting serious risk, deciding to dissolve the institution, and adopting or amending its [Institutional Charter](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter). Any other non-delegable duties must be written down in a list.*

The following are non-delegable by the governing authority:
- constitutional alignment,
- material risk acceptance,
- dissolution decisions,
- [Institutional Charter](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) adoption and material Institutional Charter amendment.
Additional non-delegable duties must be listed.

## CI-8.205 §2: Functional separation lanes

*In plain terms: power must be split into distinct jobs so no one office runs everything unchecked. Required lanes include setting direction and policy, carrying decisions out, challenging and reviewing them, auditing and assuring integrity, and publishing evidence. Each institution must publish who holds each lane, note when one body hosts more than one lane, and document safeguards that keep those lanes independent — so outsiders can audit the map, challenge it, and see that power has not quietly pooled in one place.*

Apply **CJS-5.125 §11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*) **Constitutional lane and functional separation** for the shared lane categories and separation floor.

This subsection states institutional owner duties: each institution must publish which local office, body, role, or process hosts each required lane; identify any merged hosting arrangement; document independence safeguards where one institution hosts more than one lane; and keep the lane map auditable, contestable, and current enough to prevent capture, unchecked concentration, and silent failure.

**Segregation of duties on the lane map.** Principle-layer floor: [Chapter Eight §2 *Four-seat constitutional floor*](../core_08000_functional_independence_segregation_of_duties.md#2-four-seat-constitutional-floor). The published lane map must place the four standing-record seats under [Chapter Ten §3.7 Segregation of duties](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-segregation-of-duties) — request (execution), verify (assurance and audit), enter and hold (publication and evidence), contest (challenge and review) — for the institution and for each system it operates or stewards. The office that runs a system holds the execution lane for it and never verifies records about it. Where the institution's [Institutional Charter](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) is silent on record custody (a **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*) field 10 defect), this lane map is the operative fallback: the assurance lane verifies, the publication lane enters and holds, and neither is the execution lane for the system concerned. A merged-hosting safeguard may pair two seats for a small adopter; it never pairs verify with enter, or verify with contest. For each system, the lane map also names a **substitute holder** in the publication lane outside the reporting line of the office that runs it, so that a custodian excluded on a single record under [Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-conflicted-custodian-on-a-single-record) (*Conflicted custodian on a single record*) has somewhere to pass it; a lane map that leaves every custodian for a system inside that reporting line is defective, and records route to the forum until it is corrected. The challenge-and-review lane is the **contest seat** that receives a challenge logged on a record before any filing ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-challenge-received-on-the-record) *Challenge received on the record*).

**Seats the lane map places.** Beyond the four standing-record seats, the lane map names, for the institution and for each Class A or Class B system it operates or stewards, which office holds the **containment seat** (incident command), the **participation-terms seat**, and the **release-control seat** for incident-window evidence, and which office is the substitute for each. Seat types, their boundaries, and the wrong-seat rule are fixed in [CI-14.205 §6](ci_14205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-14205-6-seat-catalog--process-role-archetypes-and-operational-boundaries) (*Seat catalog*); the lane map places them and may not enlarge them. A seat the lane map leaves unplaced is a **CI-8.205 §2** (*Functional separation lanes*) defect to log and correct, and until it is corrected the act routes as an empty seat under CI-14.205 §6 (*Seat catalog — process-role archetypes and operational boundaries*) shared rule 6, not to whoever is present.

**Fallback for certification seats.** Where the Charter Role Map is silent on a seat in a system alignment certification or an institutional alignment certification (a **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*) field 11 defect), this lane map is the operative fallback. The office holding the execution lane for the system holds the initiating seat. The publication lane holds the record seat, through an office outside the Material Control Line of the subject of the certification. The challenge-and-review lane holds the contest seat. The lane map does not place the verify-or-authorize seat, which belongs to the forums assigned under [Chapter Nine Part B §6.2 Supervisory sequence](../core_09010_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#62-supervisory-sequence) and Chapter Thirteen. Silence is a defect to log and correct, not a reason the pass cannot move, but it runs on a clock: the Chartered Party has the response window and resolution window of **CI-11.210 §3** (*Notice, response, and resolution*), counted from the logged notice of the gap, to place the seat by a dated amendment of the map, and a seat still unplaced when the resolution window ends makes the System Charter or Institutional Charter invalid on the same terms as a gap in field 10 ([Chapter Seven §4.7 Gaps in the map for operator standing records](../core_07000_charters.md#47-gaps-in-the-map-for-operator-standing-records)). The lane map keeps placing the seat for any pass already open, so the pass is not stranded. In an institutional alignment certification the same fallback applies with the institution's governing authority as the initiating seat, and the record seat is an office outside the institution's own reporting line ([Chapter Nine Part D §13.1 Four seats in an institutional certification](../core_09020_institutional_alignment_certification.md#131-four-seats-in-an-institutional-certification)).

## CI-8.205 §3: Authority chain and delegation controls

*In plain terms: every institution must show a clear line of authority from the original grant of power down to each delegated role — and every hand-off of power must be written down with its boundaries.*

Institutions must maintain a clear authority chain from source authorization through each delegated role.

Delegations must state:
- scope,
- limits,
- duration,
- review trigger,
- revocation trigger,
- accountable reviewer.

Delegations must remain consistent with the institution's governing [Institutional Charter](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter). A delegation may not expand institutional scope beyond the Institutional Charter, silently amend the Institutional Charter, or defeat periodic review duties.

## CI-8.205 §4: Emergency delegation

*In plain terms: in a crisis an institution may temporarily hand authority to someone else — but only when truly necessary, only as much as the situation requires, and only with a fast review afterward to confirm the emergency grant was justified.*

Emergency delegation is permitted only for necessity and proportionality reasons and must include rapid post-event review.

## CI-8.205 §5: Risk profile requirement

*In plain terms: each institution must publish, in everyday language, how much risk it is willing to accept — within constitutional limits and scaled to its class and tier duties — and must revisit that statement on a regular schedule and again after any serious incident.*

Each institution must maintain a plain-language risk appetite and tolerance statement aligned to [Constitutional Constraints](../core_05045_band_integrative.md#constitutional-constraint) and class/tier obligations. That statement must be reviewed on a fixed cadence and after material incidents.

*In plain terms: every institution needs a published scope document that says what it is for, what it may do, what it must not do, who it affects, how that document can change, and how often it is re-checked. For an institution that runs one system, that document is the system's **System Charter**. For one that runs several, it is an **Institutional Charter** that holds each System Charter and explains why the institution runs those systems. Where binding stakeholder decisions will stick inside that scope, the document must point to the published process and weighting rules — it does not swallow those rules. The paperwork can evolve — silent scope creep cannot. This section states the minimum fields and review duties; it does not invent a new creation right or let paper scope defeat real impact.*

  - Shared formation-burden scaling lives in **CJS-5.125 §11** (*Accountability: distributed and proportional authority terms*).
  - System alignment certification consumes System Charter fields under [Chapter Nine](../core_09010_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#81-minimum-record-contents); it does not replace this institutional duty.
  - Binding stakeholder-choice **substance** — decision sequence, weighting model, and rights-collision record — remains under [Def.P2 Binding Stakeholder Choice](../core_05055_band_participation.md#defp2-binding-stakeholder-choice) and [Chapter Fourteen §4.3 Decision-resolution requirements for binding stakeholder choice](../core_14000_governance.md#43-decision-resolution-requirements-for-binding-stakeholder-choice), with institutional publication under **CI-6.205 §1** (*Stakeholder oversight, notification, and binding-governance pathway integrity*) / **CI-6.205 §2** (*Anti-concentration and participation-legitimacy safeguards*).
- **Adoption fill-in:** Process-support fill-in for cooperating sentients on an adoption path lives in [`implementation/adoption/INSTITUTION_FOUNDATION_KIT.md`](../implementation/adoption/INSTITUTION_FOUNDATION_KIT.md); filling that template is not a published System Charter or Institutional Charter, not Chapter Seventeen adoption, and not **CS-6.350** (*Transition constitution and migration governance*) phase entry.

### CI-8.205 §6.2: Who must publish
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.2** (*Who must publish*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

Whether a System Charter or Institutional Charter is required depends on class and on what the body runs:

- **Systems:** Each system of Class L or higher must publish and maintain a System Charter.
- **Institutions:** Each institution of Class L or higher publishes the one that fits the systems it runs ([Chapter Seven §1 When System and Institutional Charters Are Required](../core_07000_charters.md#1-when-system-and-institutional-charters-are-required)): its System Charter when it runs exactly one system, which then also serves as its Institutional Charter, and an Institutional Charter when it runs more than one system or none.
- **Class P:** For Class P it is voluntary: institutions and systems of Class P are encouraged to publish one, and it becomes required on reclassification to Class L or higher or when the operator asserts constitutional compliance.
- **Held systems and businesses:** Where an institution operates, stewards, or materially controls a system or commercial form that is itself materially impactful, the Institutional Charter must hold that system's or business's System Charter (or hold a [Charter Pointer](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#charter-pointer) to it) and state why the institution runs it, so reviewers can reconstruct the full claim.

### CI-8.205 §6.3: Minimum fields (formation template)
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.3** (*Minimum fields (formation template)*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

**Minimum fields (formation template).** At formation and after each material amendment, the published System Charter or Institutional Charter must state, in plain language sufficient for affected sentients and auditors:

1. **Identity** — legal or organizational name; steward or governing body; version and effective date.
2. **Purpose** — what the institution exists to do.
3. **In-scope activities** — functions, services, authorities, and geographic or network domains claimed.
4. **Out-of-scope limits** — material activities, authorities, or domains the institution is **not** authorized to exercise.
5. **Affected communities and dependencies** — who relies on the institution, and which systems or infrastructures it depends on or gates, where material.
6. **Classification assumptions** — system class, stewardship tier, or equivalent burden assumptions relied on, with a [Charter Pointer](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#charter-pointer) to **CS-9.305 and CS-9.310** (*System classification machinery*) / **CS-14.305** (*Critical system stewardship*) where applicable.
7. **Amendment authority** — who may amend, what counts as material amendment, notice and contest paths, and that material amendments are non-delegable under [CI-8.205 §1](#ci-8205-1-non-delegable-governing-duties) (*Non-delegable governing duties*).
8. **Alignment and contest hooks** — how a mismatch between the System Charter or Institutional Charter and behavior, overdue review, or operation outside chartered scope routes to internal correction, [Chapter Nine](../core_09010_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#7-certification-outcomes) reopening where certification applies (for an institution, [Chapter Nine Part D §14.2 Reopening triggers](../core_09020_institutional_alignment_certification.md#142-reopening-triggers)), and [Chapter Thirteen §4.3](../core_13000_forum.md#43-institutional-forums) Institutional forums.
9. **Binding stakeholder-choice process Charter Pointers** — where [Def.P2 Binding Stakeholder Choice](../core_05055_band_participation.md#defp2-binding-stakeholder-choice) admission scope applies, live Charter Pointers to the published decision-sequence / participation-window materials (**CI-6.205 §1** (*Stakeholder oversight, notification, and binding-governance pathway integrity*)), weighting publication file (**CI-6.205 §2** (*Anti-concentration and participation-legitimacy safeguards*)), and rights-collision-record rules used for binding stakeholder choice inside chartered scope. The System Charter or Institutional Charter points; those files carry the substance.
10. **Standing-record custody** — for [Standing Records](../core_05035_band_accountability.md#standing-record) concerning the institution, its chartered scope, and each system it operates or stewards:
    - the **record-opening authority** that verifies the factual basis and authorizes opening, correction, expiry, or supersession of the record;
    - the separate **record custodian** that enters each authorized version, holds it, and controls versioning, with the **substitute holder** that takes custody of a single record on which the named custodian is excluded under [Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-conflicted-custodian-on-a-single-record) (*Conflicted custodian on a single record*);
    - the **contest seat** to which a challenge raised on a record is routed before any filing ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-challenge-received-on-the-record) *Challenge received on the record*); and
    - the **independent route** that applies when no qualified substitute is available, or when the named authority or custodian is itself the subject or sits inside the subject's Material Control Line — a forum under [Chapter Ten §3.6](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#36-forum-boundary) (*Forum boundary*), the independent investigative service under **CF-13.435 §6** (*No self-investigation*), or a pre-designated backup body
    - **Where the offices sit:** The record-opening authority, the record custodian, and the contest seat must each sit outside the [Material Control Line](../core_05035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line) of the subject, the claimant, and the challenger for any record they act on, and must be different seats from one another and from the execution lane for the system concerned ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-record-custody-and-opening-authority) *Segregation of duties*).
    - **Naming them:** It may name the offices or point to the published document that names them — commonly the **CI-8.205 §2** (*Functional separation lanes*) lane map; it may not leave them unnamed.
    - **Who may be named record custodian:** A System Charter or Institutional Charter may name any of these as record custodian, or as the substitute holder for one record, if it passes every test below:
      - the Backup Record Custodian (**CF-13.415 §9** (*Backup Record Custodian*)), named by reference;
      - a public archive, library, registry, or records service;
      - a separate Institution with no control relationship to the subject, a claimant, or a challenger;
      - a host, cooperative, or association that holds seats in common for the scope under [Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_08000_functional_independence_segregation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting), where it has no power over the subject, a claimant, or a challenger beyond membership; or
      - a records service paid under published terms by a fund the Chartered Party does not control, and over which neither the subject, a claimant, nor a challenger has practical power through its contributions, the choice of trustees, or otherwise.

      The list is not closed. A body of another kind may be named if it passes every test. The first time a System Charter or Institutional Charter names a kind of body that this list does not, that is an amendment under field 7, with notice and a contest path. Naming a further body of a kind the System Charter or Institutional Charter already uses is a holder change. The tests decide.
    - **The tests for each seat holder:** These apply to the record custodian and its substitute holder, and to the record-opening authority and the contest seat, except the tests that speak only of custody.
      - **Outside the line:** It sits outside the [Material Control Line](../core_05035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line) of the subject, each claimant, and each challenger. Appointment, funding, discipline, a shared governing body, and common control count where they give one of those parties practical power over the body for that record; membership alone does not. A body that can admit, discipline, or expel the subject or a claimant over the conduct the record concerns, or is funded by one, fails. A professional association therefore cannot hold a standing record about its own member's conduct where it holds that power. This narrows only these standing-record seats: [Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_08000_functional_independence_segregation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting) (*Joint professional practice*) still governs the seats for the acts themselves.
      - **Separate seats:** No body holds two of the three seats for the same record. No body both verifies and enters.
      - **No reciprocal or circular hosting:** Two adopters may not hold each other's records, as a closed pair at once or in turn within the same periodic review of the System Charter or Institutional Charter, and a ring of three or more that returns control to the subject fails for the same reason.
      - **Continuity:** It publishes how long it keeps records, preserves the audit trail under [Evidence Preservation](../core_05050_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation), and transfers custody with the full trail if it ends, leaves, or is replaced.
      - **Challenge path:** It publishes the challenge path and routes a challenge to the contest seat on the day it is received.
      - **No fee:** It charges the subject and any claimant nothing for a record.
      - **Disclosure:** Before it acts, it publishes who funds it, who governs it, and any dealing it has with the Chartered Party, a claimant, or a challenger, so that outsiders can test the other tests.
      - **Named in advance:** It is named in the Charter Role Map before it acts, and a change of holder is a new dated version of the map that takes effect under [Chapter Seven §4.5 Changing seat rules and seat holders](../core_07000_charters.md#45-changing-seat-rules-and-seat-holders).
      - **Checked before it acts:** Except for the Backup Record Authority and the Backup Record Custodian, named by reference, the Backup Record Authority (**CF-13.415 §7** (*Backup Record Authority*)) or a forum confirms that a holder passes these tests before the holder first acts, and the confirmation is published with the map version. The Chartered Party carries the burden of showing that each test is met. A later finding that a holder fails stops it acting at once, and its records move with their full trail to the Backup Record Custodian.
    - **If silent:** A System Charter or Institutional Charter that leaves any of these four unnamed is invalid, and a change that would do so is not allowed. After an unforeseeable loss of the last qualified substitute, the Chartered Party has the usual response window and resolution window, and until then its own offices cannot issue a valid record for the affected scope; the record goes to the Backup Record Authority (**CF-13.415 §7** (*Backup Record Authority*)) to verify and the Backup Record Custodian (**CF-13.415 §9** (*Backup Record Custodian*)) to hold, or to a forum ([Chapter Ten §3.7 Record custody and opening authority](../core_10000_standing_assessment.md#37-charter-gap-invalid)). Custody goes back to the named holders only after the Backup Record Authority or a forum confirms that they pass the tests and the notice and chance to contest in [Chapter Seven §4.5 Changing seat rules and seat holders](../core_07000_charters.md#45-changing-seat-rules-and-seat-holders) have run.
    - **Systems with their own System Charter:** Where the institution stewards a system that holds its own System Charter, that System Charter carries this field for records concerning that system and the Institutional Charter points to it.
    - **Charter Role Map:** These offices are the [Charter Role Map](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map)'s entries for standing records ([Chapter Seven §4 The Charter Role Map and Standing-Record Custody](../core_07000_charters.md#4-the-charter-role-map-and-standing-record-custody)). A System Charter or Institutional Charter that holds or points to a Role Map covering standing records satisfies this field, and need not list the offices a second time.
11. **Charter Role Map** — the [Charter Role Map](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map): the published, versioned placement of seats for the chartered scope, or a Charter Pointer to the published document that is the Charter Role Map. It is the map that [Chapter Eight §5 Published Placement, Vacancy, and Substitution](../core_08000_functional_independence_segregation_of_duties.md#5-published-placement-vacancy-and-substitution) requires.
    - **What it names:** For each seat on the materially binding acts the document covers, including system alignment certification, it names the eligible offices, the stewards now authorized to hold them, the substitute for each, any permitted merged-hosting safeguard, and the independent route.
    - **How it stays current:** Two layers keep the map current without making every staffing change an amendment:
      - *Eligibility* (which offices may hold a seat, serve as a substitute, or share a hosting) changes only under the amendment authority field 7 names, with notice and a contest path.
      - *Current holders* change by dated version under [CI-14.205 §5](ci_14205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-14205-5-authorized-roles-and-accountability-chains) (*Authorized roles and accountability chains*). For the record, contest, and verify-or-authorize seats, the new version takes effect only after notice and a chance to contest ([Chapter Seven §4.5 Changing seat rules and seat holders](../core_07000_charters.md#45-changing-seat-rules-and-seat-holders)).
    - **Seats outside the control line:** The verify-or-authorize, record, and contest seats must sit outside the [Material Control Line](../core_05035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line) of the party whose act it is, and the office that runs a system cannot alone change the entries for those seats.
    - **Certification seats:** For a system alignment certification the map places the initiating seat, the record seat, and the contest seat. It does not place the verify-or-authorize seat, which belongs to the forums assigned under [Chapter Nine Part B §6.2 Supervisory sequence](../core_09010_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#62-supervisory-sequence) and Chapter Thirteen, so an operator cannot choose its own verifying authority.
    - **Standing-record offices:** The standing-record offices in field 10 are entries in this map and may be carried there.
    - **If silent:** For the standing-record offices, silence is the invalid case in field 10. For a certification seat, silence is a defect the **CI-8.205 §2** (*Functional separation lanes*) lane map covers in the meantime, not a reason a pass cannot move. It runs on the **CI-11.210 §3** (*Notice, response, and resolution*) response and resolution windows from the logged notice of the gap, and a seat still unplaced when the resolution window ends makes the System Charter or Institutional Charter invalid, as for field 10 ([Chapter Seven §4.7 Gaps in the map for operator standing records](../core_07000_charters.md#47-gaps-in-the-map-for-operator-standing-records)).

### CI-8.205 §6.4: Institutional Charter additions
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.4** (*Institutional Charter additions*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

An Institutional Charter states fields 1 to 11 for the institution itself and also holds:

- every System Charter in force for the systems the institution operates, stewards, or materially controls (in full, or by a Charter Pointer that resolves to the current version); and
- the **portfolio rationale**: why the institution runs those particular systems, how they serve its purpose and relate to one another, and which dependencies they share ([Chapter Seven §3 What System and Institutional Charters Must State](../core_07000_charters.md#3-what-system-and-institutional-charters-must-state)).

Adding, retiring, or merging a system is a material amendment of the Institutional Charter.

### CI-8.205 §6.5: Formation check statement template
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.5** (*Formation check statement template*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

[Chapter Seven §5 Formation Check: Office-Holder Standing and Authenticity](../core_07000_charters.md#5-formation-check-office-holder-standing-and-authenticity) holds the floor. This subsection is the template and procedure. It may be stricter and may not narrow the floor. The statement is published with the System Charter or Institutional Charter and carries:

- **Covered offices:** each by title and by the dated Charter Role Map version that names it.
- **For each covered office, the standing limb:** the lock check, including any Institutional Authority Standing Lock, and the check for an [Interim Hold](../core_05035_band_accountability.md#interim-hold) under [Chapter Thirteen §5 Interim hold on a named sentient's pathway](../core_13000_forum.md#interim-hold-on-a-named-sentients-pathway) ([Chapter Eleven §4.2 Prevention — general standing locks](../core_11000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks) and [§5.5 Special locks](../core_11000_standing_integration.md#55-special-locks)), the competency bar and clearance relied on ([Chapter Eleven §6.2 Competency bars and clearances](../core_11000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances)), the disclosed conflicts and the independent seat that cleared each (**CI-8.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*)), and any cooling-off period observed (**CI-14.205 §2** (*Role criteria, appointment standards, and removal pathways*)).
- **For each covered office, the authenticity limb:** who can instruct, fund, replace, or reward the holder; the holder's disclosed interests and rewards, to the level of their materiality; the verified conduct record, including verified statements that others deserve harm or discriminatory treatment, or for a holder with none the commitments shown in action; and the protected route to refuse an instruction and to report, with the date it was last tested.
- **The structural limb for other staff:** the appointment, pay, promotion, and removal rules relied on, the protected reporting route, and the finding that no one who used it or refused a wrongful instruction was penalized.
- **Verifying authority and date:** who verified each limb, outside the Material Control Line of the holder, and when. Where no independent seat exists yet, the statement says so and names the first certification as the point of verification.
- **Next re-check:** the date and the triggers that bring it forward.

*Procedure:*

- **Timing:** The statement is published before the Chartered Party first exercises authority over others.
- **New holder:** A new holder of a covered office is checked before taking the seat.
- **Emergency substitution:** For an emergency substitution the adopting document sets the window for completing the check, which may not exceed the tier window for the matching materiality tier in [Chapter Nine Part B §9.2](../core_09010_system_alignment_certification_record_process.md#92-provisional-and-full-certification). Where the matching tier is Tier A and the Emergency level applies, the default window is 3 days, the protective-step bound in [Chapter Thirteen §6 Timely resolution, materiality tiers, and anti-delay discipline](../core_13000_forum.md#time-scale-levels), and an adopting document may set a shorter window and never a longer one. The substitute takes no materially binding act alone before the check is done.
- **Changes:** A new holder or a changed fact is a new dated version of the statement and not an amendment.
- **Privacy and records:** The holder is named only as far as each limb needs, and the records of the check are held under the standing-record custody field.
- **Anti-evasion:** a nominee or proxy in a covered office, a covered office that exists only on paper, splitting an office so that no part is covered, and hiding who controls a holder, and seating through a nominee a sentient who is under an outstanding lock or an Interim Hold are non-compliant under this section and [CI-8.205 §3](#ci-8205-3-authority-chain-and-delegation-controls) (*Authority chain and delegation controls*).

### CI-8.205 §6.6: Periodic review
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.6** (*Periodic review*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

On the cadence its class sets in [Chapter Thirteen §6 Timely resolution, materiality tiers, and anti-delay discipline](../core_13000_forum.md#review-cadence) (*Review cadence*), and sooner after material incident, dependency growth, class change, credible challenge, or observed operation outside stated scope, the governing authority must review whether:

- stated purpose and in-scope / out-of-scope limits still match observed operation and [System Boundaries](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#system-boundaries);
- classification assumptions remain honest;
- amendment and contest paths still work in practice;
- Def.P2 process Charter Pointers still resolve to current published rules where binding stakeholder choice is in scope;
- the standing-record custody field still resolves to live offices outside the institution's own control line for records about itself, and the independent route has actually been usable;
- the Charter Role Map still resolves to live offices and current holders, its versions are dated and attributable, and no verify-or-authorize, record, or contest seat sits inside the control line of the party whose act it is;
- the formation check statement still matches the holders of the covered offices and the controls, rewards, and protected routes it describes;
- any material mismatch is corrected by amendment, remediation, recertification, or scope contraction — not by silent expansion.

### CI-8.205 §6.7: Anti-evasion
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Read with: **CI-8.205 §6.7** (*Anti-evasion*); **CI-8.205 §6** (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*).

</details>

<br>

System Charter and Institutional Charter text does not:

- establish classification;
- defeat [System Boundary Integrity](../core_05040_band_continuity.md#system-boundary-integrity);
- substitute for forum-supervised system alignment certification where Chapter Nine requires it; or
- stand in for [Def.P2 Binding Stakeholder Choice](../core_05055_band_participation.md#defp2-binding-stakeholder-choice) process substance.

These are non-compliant under this section and [CI-8.205 §3](#ci-8205-3-authority-chain-and-delegation-controls) (*Authority chain and delegation controls*):

- paper-only System Charters and Institutional Charters;
- overdue reviews treated as current authorization;
- dead Charter Pointers; and
- delegations that expand scope beyond the document.
