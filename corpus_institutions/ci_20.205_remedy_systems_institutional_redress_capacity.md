Core home: 20

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-20.205** (*Remedy systems and institutional redress capacity*).

*In plain terms: **CI-20.205** (*Remedy systems and institutional redress capacity*) is the institutions layer's home for making challenge and repair real — staffing, funding, backlog, succession, and high-impact remedy domains. The constitutional floor lives in [Chapter Twenty §4 Enforcement Realism and Remedy Systems](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems) and [Remedy System](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#remedy-system). Forum clocks live in **CF-23.445** (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*). Technical Forums publish measurement standards; they do not run these offices as bosses.*

**Quick orientation**
- **What this family owns** — institutional capacity to deliver practical redress: intake, preservation, review, repair, monitoring, safeguard enforcement, and lock review.
- **What it does not own** — forum-family routing (**Chapter Twenty-Three** / **CF**), standing measurement (**Chapter Sixteen**), or technical measurement standards (**Technical Forum Domains** / **CF-23.440** (*Technical specialist forums and specialist chambers*)).
- **Read with** — **CI-23.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*) for local procedure maps; **CI-8.215** (*Oversight, assurance, controls, and evidence*) for oversight and assurance; **CI-6.205** (*Transparency, participation, and accessible challenge and service pathways*) for accessible challenge and service pathways; **CI-23.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) for cross-institution escalation; **CI-19.210** (*Institutional failure and dissolution*) when chronic under-capacity becomes institutional failure; **CF-23.445** (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*) for tier milestones and backlog publication; **CF-23.415** (*Routing operations, transfer, questions for ruling, and representative treatment*) for capacity-failure routing.

## CI-20.205 §1: Capacity, Funding, and Succession

*In plain terms: Every remedy system must name who owns it, where its funding comes from, and who takes over if that owner disappears. A remedy route with no named owner fails silently.*

Each remedy system must name, in plain view:

- the institutional owner responsible for staffing, funding, independence, records capacity, accessibility, and succession planning;
- the funding source and reauthorization cycle scaled to risk, case volume, vulnerability, and rights sensitivity;
- the succession plan that keeps intake and records continuous through leadership change, reorganization, or technology outage; and
- the route by which affected sentients contest under-capacity, inaccessible intake, or cost-shifting.

Expense, insolvency, inconvenience, receivership, dissolution, asset transfer, or restructuring does not by itself defeat a verified obligation under [Chapter Twenty §4.1 Remediation Capacity and Funding](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#41-remediation-capacity-and-funding). Successors, estates, receivers, and comparable transferees inherit proportionate duties — whether the responsible actor is an individual, an institution, or a system — unless a less harmful lawful transfer is demonstrated. Lock-to-remedy funding parity for the same system class is in **CI-20.205 §5** (*Remedy Parity and Capacity-Failure Interface*).



## CI-20.205 §2: Durability, Backlog, and Publication

*In plain terms: Remedy systems publish their own waiting times and backlog ages, so a route that has quietly stopped working in practice becomes visible from outside.*

Each remedy system must publish, on a fixed schedule compatible with **CF-23.445** (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*):

- current intake and acknowledgment windows;
- backlog size and age distribution for open matters;
- milestone compliance against [Chapter Twenty-Three §6 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](../core_23.000_forum.md#6-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier defaults; and
- any designed backlog, single-office dependency, or chronic underfunding risk that could foreseeably nullify challenge or remedy.

Designed backlog, inaccessible intake, chronic underfunding, dependence on a single fragile office, or chronic milestone failure violates [Chapter Twenty §4.2 Remedy-System Durability](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability) where it foreseeably nullifies challenge or remedy.

Each remedy system must also publish, for each lock class it administers or funds, the [Chapter Nineteen §2.4 Remedy Parity and Lock Preconditions](../core_19.000_violation_consequences.md#24-remedy-parity-and-lock-preconditions) tripwire figures — general locks issued, remedies commenced, remedies completed, restorations decided, and reassessments pending — on a schedule compatible with **CF-23.445** (*Forum performance, backlog requirements, publication timeliness, and accessibility*). A ratio of locks issued to remedies commenced that widens over successive periods is evidence of a [Chapter Twenty §4 Enforcement Realism and Remedy Systems](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#4-enforcement-realism-and-remedy-systems) paper-pathway failure, not a neutral operations metric.



## CI-20.205 §3: High-Impact Remedy Domains

*In plain terms: Areas where a broken remedy route causes the most damage — child protection, environmental enforcement, benefits, detention oversight, public health — and so require durable systems.*

The following domains, and comparable high-impact systems, require durable remedy systems capable of timely intake, preservation, review, and enforcement:

- child protection;
- environmental enforcement;
- benefits administration;
- detention oversight;
- public-health response; and
- other rights-sensitive administrative systems named by adopting documents.

Domain-specific operational detail may live in other **CI** families (**CI-6.220** (*Neurodiversity, disability justice, and trauma-informed participation*) through **CI-6.260** (*Place-based stewardship, Indigenous continuity, and consultation routes*), **CI-6.235** (*Collective public health, epidemic response, and addiction-informed care*), and cognates). **CI-20.205** (*Remedy systems and institutional redress capacity*) states the shared capacity minimum those domains must meet.



## CI-20.205 §4: Standards Interface and Accountability Routing

*In plain terms: Technical Forums set the capacity and timeliness standards remedy systems must meet, but they do not run those systems. Setting a standard is not the same as commanding compliance.*

Remedy systems must meet published capacity, measurement, and milestone standards under [Technical Forum Domains](../core_23.000_forum.md#42-technical-forum-domains). Technical Forums set and maintain those standards; they are not the command hierarchy for remedy systems or institutions.

Accountability for staffing, funding, backlog, succession, and performance remains with the [primary-stakes](../core_23.000_forum.md#2-default-venue-and-primary-stakes) forum family — ordinarily [Institutional](../core_23.000_forum.md#43-institutional-forums), or [Integrity](../core_23.000_forum.md#45-integrity-forums) where capture, process failure, or system-alignment collapse is primary. Cross-institution stall or handoff failure routes through **CI-23.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*). Chronic institutional failure routes through **CI-19.210** (*Institutional failure and dissolution*). A claim that this family or a remedy system it funds is itself under capacity routes under **CI-20.205 §5** (*Remedy Parity and Capacity-Failure Interface*) and **CF-23.415 §4** (*Capacity-Failure Routing*), not as a self-judged staffing dispute.



## CI-20.205 §5: Remedy Parity and Capacity-Failure Interface

*In plain terms: Lock offices may not outrun the repair offices that make those locks lawful. When remedy or restoration work falls behind its clock, the starved office does not get to decide that it is fine.*

This subsection implements, and must not narrow, [Chapter Nineteen §2.4](../core_19.000_violation_consequences.md#24-remedy-parity-and-lock-preconditions) (*Remedy Parity and Lock Preconditions*), [Chapter Twenty §4.2](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-parity-funding-minimum) (*Remedy-parity funding minimum*), and [Chapter Twenty-Three §3](../core_23.000_forum.md#capacity-failure-routing) (*Capacity failure is heard outside the starved body*).

- **Funding minimum:** For each system class an adopter administers, the capacity funded for remedy, restoration, and reassessment must be no less than the capacity funded for lock administration, enforcement, and safeguard imposition on the same class. An adopter departing from parity must record the reasons under [Chapter Twenty-Four §4.1 Stewardship Direction](../core_24.000_governance.md#41-stewardship-direction) and show on the integration record that remedy commencement and restoration review still meet their tier windows. The ratio must be published with the [Chapter Nineteen §2.4 Remedy Parity and Lock Preconditions](../core_19.000_violation_consequences.md#24-remedy-parity-and-lock-preconditions) tripwire figures. Budget parity does not excuse a tripped tripwire, and a met tripwire does not excuse silent departure from parity. When an adopter first stands up lock machinery for a class, it must hold a reserve for remedy, restoration, and reassessment on that class, enough to meet the parity minimum until ongoing funding is in place.
- **Opened-remedy precondition:** Institutional lock offices may not treat a general standing lock as attached until the [Chapter Nineteen §2.1 Remedy and Correction](../core_19.000_violation_consequences.md#21-remedy-and-correction) integration record for the same verified violation finding is open, except for interim protective measures under [Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Escalation and Questions for Ruling](../core_23.000_forum.md#interim-protection) (*Interim Protection*). Completion of remedy is not required; an opened remedy record is.
- **Tripwire:** Where remedy commencement or restoration review for a lock class exceeds its [Chapter Twenty-Three §6 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](../core_23.000_forum.md#6-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier outer bound, the capacity failure is a [Chapter Twenty §4.2 Remedy-System Durability](../core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#42-remedy-system-durability) durability failure. New ordinary locks and existing-lock reassessment follow **Chapter Nineteen §2.4** (*Remedy Parity and Lock Preconditions*); this family does not invent a lighter or heavier tripwire.
- **Routing:** A capacity-failure claim about a remedy system, lock office, or forum family is heard outside the starved body under **CF-23.415 §4** (*Capacity-Failure Routing*). **Integrity** is the default lead. The body in question supplies its published tripwire and backlog figures and may give evidence, but must not be the sole finder of fact on its own capacity.

This subsection does not relocate standing measurement, forum-family routing, or Chapter Twenty-Three clocks.
