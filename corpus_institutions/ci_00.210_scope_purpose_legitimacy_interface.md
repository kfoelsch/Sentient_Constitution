This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-0.210** (*Scope, purpose, and legitimacy interface*).

*In plain terms: **CI** is the institutions rulebook — how bodies are formed, authorized, overseen, corrected, and dissolved within constitutional boundaries.*

**Purpose and role:** This file states the Institutions layer’s scope and boundaries, explains how it uses anti-constitutional-misconduct terminology, and sets its institutional proportionality baseline.

**What this layer owns**

- institutional formation, design, appointment, conflict integrity, and procedure (**CI-6.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*) through **CI-22.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*));
- oversight, transparency, participation, and classification-linked institutional obligations (**CI-6.215** (*Oversight, assurance, controls, and evidence*) through **CI-8.210** (*Classification-linked institutional obligations*));
- public revenue, resource stewardship, cross-institution coordination, failure, sanctions, dissolution, and Adoption Transition (**CI-4.210** (*Public revenue, fees, recurring charges, and billing integrity*) through **CI-4.275** (*Adoption Transition governance and institutional evolution*));
- domain institutional interfaces (**CI-4.220** (*Neurodiversity, disability justice, and trauma-informed participation*) through **CI-4.270** (*Scientific publication, peer review, replication, and evidence stewardship*));
- compliance mapping and stable registry (**CI-0.215** (*Compliance mapping and stable registry*));
- remedy-systems institutional capacity (**CI-25.210** (*Remedy systems and institutional redress capacity*)).

**What this layer does not own**

- constitutional term meaning and O/M/A/C definition mechanics — those remain in the Sentient Constitution (Chapter Three, including its subcore);
- joint operational definitions, and joint cross-layer interfaces — those belong to **CJS** (reusable joint operational definitions in **CJS-3.105 (*Operational cluster library (oDef)*) through CJS-3.135 (*Integrative cross-leg operations (oDef.22–oDef.23)*)**);
- Rights Floors — those remain in Chapter Four;
- system-class and steward taxonomies — those belong to **CS**;
- forum-family operational doctrine under Chapter Twenty-Two — those belong to **CF**;
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designation (ACMD) — that remains in Chapter Twenty (see **CI-0.210 §1** (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct Usage*) for how this layer uses the label).

<br>

*In plain terms: heavier institutional impact means stronger duties — setup, oversight, transparency, and accountability must scale with how much the institution affects sentients and dependencies.*

Institutional burden must scale with impact. This subsection states the CI layer's institution-specific baseline.

Apply **CJS-3.125 §11** (*distributed and proportional authority terms*), **CJS-3.125 §12** (*burden-of-justification and constraint terms*), and **CJS-3.125 §13** (*procedural integrity and adjudication terms*) for shared proportionality, burden, and procedural read-with terms where materially applicable.

- Low-impact institutional scopes should be simple, accessible, and inexpensive to establish and operate.
- High-impact institutional scopes must satisfy stronger diligence, assurance, transparency, and accountability duties.
- Scaling factors include material impact, dependency footprint, reversibility limits, and stewardship criticality.
- If impact is uncertain, institutions must apply precautionary provisional controls and revalidate after evidence is collected.
