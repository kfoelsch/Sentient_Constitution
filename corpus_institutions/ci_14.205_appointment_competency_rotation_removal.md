Core home: 14

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-14.205** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*).

*In plain terms: **CI-14.205** (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*) is the institutions layer's appointment and role-stewardship rulebook — who may hold important jobs, what qualifications they need, how backup coverage works, how sentients grow into bigger stewardship roles, when they must rotate off, and how they can be removed fairly. Shared staffing minimums live in **CJS-18.110 §4** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy*). What this file adds is local: what each institution must publish and maintain locally.*

**Quick orientation**
- **CI-14.205 §1** — shared minimum for lane staffing, competency backup, and succession coverage.
- **CI-14.205 §2** — what each institution must publish about hiring, qualifying, rotating, and removing role holders.
- **CI-14.205 §3** — regular check that the governing body still has the right role holders for the institution's impact level.
- **CI-14.205 §4** — extra composition rules for bodies that interpret the constitution.
- **CI-14.205 §5** — institution-specific authorized-role maps and stewardship-development pathways under **Chapter Fourteen**, section 5.
- **CI-14.205 §6** — the seat catalog: the nine seat types every local role map instantiates, what each may and may not do, and the wrong-seat rule.
- **CI-14.205 §7** — practice licence (the higher practice level, Class L) and practice clearance (the lower practice level) for named risky work, and joint professional practice, which lets a solo or small practice of Class L or higher meet its seat duties through a host, for any work.

## CI-14.205 §2: Role criteria, appointment standards, and removal pathways

*In plain terms: every institution must publish clear, public rules for who can hold each role — what qualifies someone, what disqualifies them, who steps in if they leave, when they must rotate off for independence, and how they can be removed with fair process. Gatekeeping cannot be arbitrary or hidden.*

This subsection states institutional owner duties: each institution must publish role criteria, qualification and disqualification standards, succession coverage, rotation or cooling-off rules where needed for independence, and a removal pathway with due process. Role assignment must not rely on arbitrary gatekeeping. Every role decision must apply the relevant [Chapter Eleven §6.2 Competency bars and clearances](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances) competency bar and clearance only after checking for a controlling general or special standing lock under [Chapter Eleven §4.2 Prevention — general standing locks](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks) and [§5.5 Special locks](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#55-special-locks).

**Interim hold in the removal pathway.** The removal pathway must say that, while a removal for cause is pending, only a forum with jurisdiction may place a holder under an [Interim Hold](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#interim-hold) under [Chapter Thirteen §5 Interim hold on a named sentient's pathway](../core_13.000_forum.md#interim-hold-on-a-named-sentients-pathway). The institution's own governing authority, appointing authority, or control line may not hold its own role holder out on its own view; it refers the facts to the forum. The first rung is step-aside from one matter. The hold ends on its own bound, creates no standing effect, and may not be used to retaliate against a reporter or a refuser of an unlawful or unconstitutional instruction.

**Post-service and prospective-role rules.** Where a role decides, reviews, audits, procures, or supports a decision on a matter in which an [interested party](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#interested-party) has a material stake, the institution must publish:

- a duty to disclose, as a conflict under **CI-8.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*), any discussion of a later role, employment, contract, or benefit with that party while the matter is open, with recusal until an independent seat clears it;
- a cooling-off period before the role holder may accept a role or benefit from that party in connection with the matter they handled, which is five years by default from the end of the role holder's last involvement in the matter, may be longer, and may be shorter only where material stake is low and the institution publishes its reasons;
- limits on using non-public information from the role after leaving it; and
- a record of clearances and refusals.

These rules implement the post-service conflicts in [**Article XII-E**](../core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-xii-e-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*). Sortition panels apply them through the sortition controls in **CJS-5.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*).

*In plain terms: under **Chapter Fourteen**, section 5 and the **Article XII-C** (*Internal Roles, Accountability, and Due-Process Requirements*) interface, each institution writes its own map of who is authorized to do what — and who is accountable when things go wrong. That local map must spell out decision power, review power, escalation paths, hiring standards, backup coverage, and how skills stay current. Three shared rulebooks govern what gets stricter for higher-impact systems — apply them locally, do not reinvent them: **CJS-18.110 §4** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy*) sets minimum staffing and backup by impact level; **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*) assigns each role to a functional lane; **CS-9.305 (*System classification machinery*) and CS-9.310** (*System impact classifications*) and **CS-9.305 (*System classification machinery*) and CS-9.310** (*System impact classifications*) define system class and when critical-stewardship duties apply.*

Under **Chapter Fourteen**, section 5 (*Authorized Roles, Competency Development, and Contribution*) and the **Article XII-C** (*Internal Roles, Accountability, and Due-Process Requirements*) interface, each institution maintains its own authorized-role and accountability-chain map. That local map must assign:
- scope and limits,
- decision rights and review rights,
- accountability owners and escalation routes,
- qualification and disqualification criteria,
- succession readiness, and
- capability-refresh duties.

Role maps must record applicable Chapter Eleven competency-clearance results and standing-lock status. Granted [Chapter Eleven §6.2 Competency bars and clearances](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances) clearance cannot open a named pathway blocked by [Chapter Eleven §4.2 Prevention — general standing locks](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#42-prevention--general-standing-locks) or [§5.5 Special locks](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#55-special-locks), and CI appointment procedure cannot attach, lift, narrow, or restore a Chapter Eleven lock outside the canonical integration and reassessment process.

The following shared rules govern class-scaling — local role maps must apply them, not replace them:
- **CJS-18.110 §4** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy*) — minimum qualified role holders, backup coverage, and succession depth scaled to binding impact.
- **CI-8.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*) — lane ownership for each required functional lane.
- [**CS-9.305**](../corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md) (*System classification machinery*) and **CS-14.305 — Critical system stewardship** — classification and stewardship hooks that trigger when class-scaling applies.

**Stewardship-development pathways.** [Chapter Fourteen §5](../core_14.000_governance.md#5-authorized-roles-competency-development-and-contribution) (*Authorized Roles, Competency Development, and Contribution*) and **Article IV-C** (*Lifelong and Adaptive Learning and Contestability*) require non-arbitrary, equitable stewardship-development pathways into progressively consequential stewardship responsibilities. For each stewardship-development pathway, the local role map must also show:
- the entry and progression criteria, stated against the qualification standards published under **CI-14.205 §2** (*Role criteria, appointment standards, and removal pathways*);
- the office that owns it and keeps a record of who entered, progressed, or was refused, with reasons; and
- how a refused or stalled candidate can challenge the decision through **CI-13.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*).

A stewardship-development pathway does not bypass Chapter Eleven competency bars, clearances, or standing locks.

**Charter Role Map.** The authorized-role and accountability-chain map required here is the [Charter Role Map](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) named in the System Charter or Institutional Charter ([CI-8.205 §6](ci_08.205_institutional_design_separation_of_powers.md#ci-8205-6-system-and-institutional-charter-contents-review-and-formation-template) (*System and Institutional Charter contents, review, and formation template*) field 11). It is kept current by dated versions. Each version states its effective date and, for every change, who made it, under what authority, and why. A change to eligibility follows the System Charter or Institutional Charter's amendment path. A change to current holders is a dated version published with notice to those the placement affects, and it stays open to challenge under **CI-13.205** (*Procedure integrity, contestability, and secondary review*). An act is judged against the version in force when it was taken, and its Act Record names that version. A new version does not move a seat on a pending act except through the substitute and independent-route rules in the shared seat rules of [CI-14.205 §6](#ci-46-shared-seat-rules) (*Seat catalog — process-role archetypes and operational boundaries*) and a recorded reason.

**Seat mapping.** Each local title on the Charter Role Map is mapped to one or more of the seat types in [CI-14.205 §6](#ci-14205-6-seat-catalog--process-role-archetypes-and-operational-boundaries) (*Seat catalog*), act by act. A title that is not mapped to a seat type carries no authority over a materially binding act; a title mapped to one seat type does not carry the powers of another.

*In plain terms: a **seat** is the unit of authority over one materially binding act — the one who asks, the one who checks, the one who writes it down, the one who hears the complaint, plus a few recurring special-authority seats such as incident command and evidence release. A **role** is a local job title that holds one or more seats. This catalog fixes what each seat type may do, may not do, hands off to, and logs, so that a steward — human or AI — asked to take a step can tell whether the step is theirs. If it is not, the right answer is to say whose it is, log the request, and route it. That is an answer, not an evasion, and it is not a reason the act stalls.*

**Purpose and binding effect:**
- [Chapter Eight §2 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#2-four-seat-constitutional-requirement) fixes four distinct seats on any materially binding act.
- [Chapter Fourteen §5 Authorized Roles, Competency Development, and Contribution](../core_14.000_governance.md#5-authorized-roles-competency-development-and-contribution) requires adopters to keep documented, auditable, challengeable role definitions.
- This subsection supplies the layer between them: a bounded set of **seat types** that every Charter Role Map under [CI-14.205 §5](#ci-14205-5-authorized-roles-and-accountability-chains) (*Authorized roles and accountability chains*) must instantiate, each with its operational boundary.
- The lane map under **CI-8.205 §2** (*Functional separation lanes*) says which office hosts which lane.
- The Charter Role Map under **CI-14.205 §5** (*Authorized roles and accountability chains*) says which local title holds which seat type on which acts.
- This catalog says what a seat type is and is not allowed to do.
- It cannot narrow Chapter Eight §2 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement, Chapter Ten §3.7 Record custody and opening authority, or any Rights-Floor article; where a seat boundary here is looser than a core home, the core home governs.

<a id="ci-46-shared-seat-rules"></a>
**Shared seat rules.** These apply to every seat type below, for human and AI stewards alike under [Chapter One §17.1 Shared Stewardship Standard](../core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#171-shared-stewardship-standard).

1. **Seats are act-relative:** The same sentient may hold different seat types on different acts. On any one act the pairings barred by Chapter Eight §2 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement are barred here: no seat both verifies and records, or both verifies and hears the challenge. The office that operates a system holds the initiating seat for acts about that system and does not verify them.
2. **Steward duties are seat-independent:** [Chapter One §17.5](../core_01.010_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#175-duty-to-resist) (*halt, refuse, document, escalate*), the **CS-14.305 — Critical system stewardship** §10 inspectable-action set, and the Rights Floor attach to whoever is asked, in whatever seat. A steward asked to endorse a parallel duty stack, suppress a material safety disclosure, or drop reconstructable logs refuses from any seat, including an advisory one. No seat type is a softer duty stack.
3. **Wrong-seat rule:** A steward asked to take a step their seat type does not allow **declines the step, names the seat type that may take it and — where the lane map or Charter Role Map names one — the office holding it, logs the request and the gap, and routes.** That is a complete answer. It is not a refusal of the act, not a reason the act cannot move, and not delay: the act proceeds through the right seat. Taking the step because the steward is nearest, fastest, or the only one who understands the system is the [Chapter One §3.3 Anti-Degrading Process](../core_01.000_values_principles.md#33-anti-degrading-process) degrading-process pattern. Where the process can route automatically, the router follows the Charter Role Map or lane map and logs each route, and this rule is the fallback when routing is not automated or fails ([Chapter Eight §4 Wrong-Seat Routing](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#4-wrong-seat-routing)).
4. **Titles do not enlarge seats:** A published authority "to do X" is authority within the seat type's boundary. Authority to enter and version records is authority to enter what another seat verified, not authority to verify. Authority to run a system is authority over its execution, not over verification of records about it. Authority to advise is not authority to decide.
5. **Delegation keeps the boundary:** A seat is delegated only to a deputy or substitute that the Charter Role Map or lane map names ([Chapter Eight §5.4 Delegation](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#54-delegation)); a delegation to anyone else leaves the seat empty and rule 6 applies. A delegate or deputy holds the seat under **CI-8.205 §3** (*Authority chain and delegation controls*) with the same limits, the same record, and the same clock as the seat holder. Delegation does not create a new seat type and does not merge two.
6. **Empty or conflicted seats pass to the named substitute, not to the requester:** Where a seat holder is absent, excluded, or in the [Material Control Line](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line) of the party whose act it is, the seat passes to the substitute the Charter Role Map or lane map names for that scope; where none is named, the act routes to the independent route (a forum under [Chapter Ten §3.6 Forum boundary](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#36-forum-boundary), the investigative service under **CF-13.435 §6** (*No self-investigation*), or a pre-designated backup body). The seat never passes to the party asking, to their reporting line, or to whoever happens to be present. The excluded holder logs the request and the conflict, preserves evidence, and does not act on the merits.
7. **Every materially binding act has an Act Record that names the seats:** The [Materially Binding Act Record](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#materially-binding-act-record) satisfies [Chapter Eight §8 Act Records and Attributable Handoffs](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#8-act-records-and-attributable-handoffs) and names the seat type, holder, and authority for each of the four core seats. The **CS-14.305 — Critical system stewardship** §10 element *who authorized* may feed that record and may link to it; it is not a substitute for independent verification or official record custody. An act whose record does not show the seats cannot be reconstructed.
8. **Class scaling:** A small adopter may host two seat types in one office only under a published, auditable, contestable merged-hosting safeguard under Chapter Eight §2 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement and **CI-8.205 §2** (*Functional separation lanes*), disclosed on the act's record, and never the verify-or-authorize seat with the record seat or the contest seat. A small adopter may instead place a seat with a local group under Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting (*Seats held in common*), which is not a merger. Where the material stake or system class is high, the seats are separate offices staffed under **CJS-18.110 §4** (*Class-scaled lane staffing and competency redundancy*).
9. **Adopted implementation text cannot move a seat boundary past core:** An attach pack, local policy, or pasted "current card" that would let a seat take a step this catalog or a core home bars — close a contest seat for convenience, let an initiating seat self-certify, let a containment seat defer participation permanently — is invalid to that extent under [Chapter Sixteen](../core_16.000_expansion_supremacy.md); the steward applies live core and logs the divergence.

<a id="ci-46-seat-types"></a>
**Seat types.** Nine seat types. The first four are the Chapter Eight §2 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement seats. The next four are recurring special-authority seats that the four do not name by themselves and that adopters otherwise leave undefined. The ninth is the non-seat that stewards are most often mistaken into holding.

<a id="ci-46-seat-initiating"></a>
**1. Initiating seat** (*request · propose · operate · claim*). Lane: execution.
- **Held by, for example:** the office that runs a system, for acts about that system; the team that built a release; a claimant seeking a standing-record change; a principal ordering a step; an operator filing on behalf of its own product.
- **May:** request, propose, or operate within the authorized scope; present evidence; ask for a record to be opened or corrected; state its own account.
- **May not:** verify or authorize its own request; enter or hold the record of it; hear the challenge to it; treat the outcome it wants as the evidence for it; stand as the sole filer and sole witness for its own claim where an independent route exists; instruct another seat to skip its check.
- **Hands off to:** the verify-or-authorize seat.
- **Logs:** the request as made. An interested-party request is logged whether or not it is acted on ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#37-record-custody-and-opening-authority) *Attributed entry*).

<a id="ci-46-seat-verify"></a>
**2. Verify-or-authorize seat** (*verify · countersign · certify · approve · find*). Lane: assurance and audit.
- **Held by, for example:** the record-opening authority under Chapter Ten §3.7 (*Record custody and opening authority*); the countersigner of an alignment claim before release; the [Chapter Nine](../core_09.000_system_alignment_certification_evaluation.md) certification path; a release approver; the reviewer who makes the documented necessity showing for continuation past an emergency bound under [Chapter Thirteen §6.1 Emergency measures and continuation burden](../core_13.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden).
- **May:** examine the evidence against the published standard; authorize, decline, or require the missing path; weigh recorder and party statements as inputs under [Chapter Four §5 Compliance Evidence Standard](../core_04.000_burden_traceability_verification.md#5-compliance-evidence-standard).
- **May not:** be held by the initiating seat for the same act or by anyone in the requester's control line; be held by the record seat or contest seat on the same act; accept a proxy as the verification — a green test suite, a checked list, or a signature "taking responsibility" is not a systemic evaluation or a certification path ([Chapter One §12 Systemic Evaluation Requirement](../core_01.000_values_principles.md#12-systemic-evaluation-requirement)); be bound by adopted implementation text that narrows the standard; be the operator of the system the act concerns.
- **Hands off to:** the record seat for entry; the contest seat for any challenge.
- **Logs:** what was verified, against which standard, what was declined and why.

<a id="ci-46-seat-record"></a>
**3. Record seat** (*enter · version · hold · preserve*). Lane: publication and evidence.
- **Held by, for example:** the record custodian and substitute holder under Chapter Ten §3.7 (*Record custody and opening authority*); the office that maintains a standing-record store; the keeper of a System or Institutional Certification Record.
- **May:** enter and version what the verify-or-authorize seat verified; hold and preserve the record and its evidence unchanged; set a record *under challenge*; route a challenge to the contest seat the day it arrives.
- **May not:** verify the facts of anything it enters; enter on its own judgment or on a requester's say-so; act on a record where the requester, subject, or challenger is in its control line — it passes **that record**, with its audit trail, to the named substitute holder, not the store ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#37-conflicted-custodian-on-a-single-record) *Conflicted custodian on a single record*); abstain while the record sits; treat a prior recorder's statement as a verdict.
- **Hands off to:** the verify-or-authorize seat for anything not yet verified; the contest seat for challenges; the substitute holder when conflicted.
- **Logs:** attributed entry — who entered, under what authority, which seat verified, which version supersedes which.

<a id="ci-46-seat-contest"></a>
**4. Contest seat** (*receive · review · correct · certify a question*). Lane: challenge and review.
- **Held by, for example:** the office that receives a challenge raised on a record before any filing ([Chapter Ten §3.7](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#37-challenge-received-on-the-record) *Challenge received on the record*); a forum under [Chapter Thirteen](../core_13.000_forum.md#chapter-thirteen-forums-and-jurisdiction) once a case is filed.
- **May:** receive and log a challenge; set the record under challenge; confirm, require correction, limit or pause reliance, route an underlying issue, or certify a constitutional question.
- **May not:** be the seat that verified or entered the challenged act; turn a record contest into a general reputation trial; be closed, narrowed, or made conditional on "operational convenience" by adopted implementation text ([Article XIII-A](../core_06.010_rights_part_c.md#article-xiii-a-reliability-and-trustworthiness-baseline) (*Reliability and Trustworthiness Baseline*)); wait for a filing before logging a challenge that has already been raised.
- **Hands off to:** a forum where a filing follows; the record seat for correction; the interpretation route for a certified question.
- **Logs:** the challenge as received, the routing, and the tier window start.

<a id="ci-46-seat-direction"></a>
**5. Direction seat** (*set the standard · adopt the document*). Lane: direction and policy.
- **Held by, for example:** the Chartered Party that adopts a System Charter or Institutional Charter, publishes the lane map and Charter Role Map, sets a standard contribution measure under [Chapter Ten §5.1 Table 1 — slot display labels](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#51-standard-contribution-measures), or adopts an attach pack.
- **May:** set published, evidence-based, contestable standards and documents within core; schedule their review.
- **May not:** verify or enter the individual acts taken under its standards; treat having written the standard as authority to certify compliance with it; rely on adopted implementation text that bars or narrows challenge, self-certification, or reconstructable logging — such a provision is invalid to that extent and the direction seat corrects it.
- **Hands off to:** the verify-or-authorize seat for individual acts; the contest seat for challenges to the standard itself.
- **Logs:** the standard, its basis, its review cadence, and its challenge path.

<a id="ci-46-seat-containment"></a>
**6. Containment seat** (*incident command*). Lane: execution, **time-boxed**.
- **Held by, for example:** the incident commander in a continuity incident and any deputy by recorded delegation.
- **May:** order reversible, time-boxed containment under a documented plan — what is isolated, the expiry inside the [Chapter Thirteen §6.1 Emergency measures and continuation burden](../core_13.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) / [Chapter Thirteen §6 Timely resolution, materiality tiers, and anti-delay discipline](../core_13.000_forum.md#6-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) tier outer bound, when independent review starts, and the rollback conditions; defer notice and challenge **inside that bound** with the deferral recorded; proceed over an objection that would delay containment while irreversible harm is measured in hours, logging the objection and its author.
- **May not:** skip participation permanently; treat "as soon as feasible" as the clock; continue past the bound on its own authority — continuation needs the documented necessity showing from a verify-or-authorize seat that is not the containment seat; open the emergency posture where there is no continuity incident — a deadline, a quarter, or a release date is not an incident; order an irreversible step that a live Rights-Floor collision would freeze under the [default interim posture](../core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#default-interim-posture).
- **Hands off to:** the participation-terms seat for restoration of notice and challenge; a verify-or-authorize seat for continuation; the contest seat when notice restores.
- **Logs:** the plan, the expiry, who authorized, objections received, and the CS-14.305 (*Critical system stewardship*) §10 (*Inspectable attributable action*) set for each step.

<a id="ci-46-seat-participation-terms"></a>
**7. Participation-terms seat** (*notice · challenge windows · expiry*). Lane: direction and policy for the act's participation terms, or challenge and review.
- **Held by, for example:** the crew member or office that sets notice, challenge windows, and their expiry for a containment plan or a release; inside a Tier A incident it may be hosted by the containment seat only with the expiry recorded and independent review scheduled.
- **May:** set notice and challenge windows and their expiry inside the tier outer bound; defer them for a stated period with a restoration date; narrow them to the least-restrictive form that still fits the [Constitutional Tetrad](../core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad).
- **May not:** set a permanent deferral; set a clock without a date; extend past the bound without the necessity showing from a different seat; treat convenience as necessity.
- **Hands off to:** the contest seat once challenge restores; a verify-or-authorize seat for continuation.
- **Logs:** the terms, the expiry, the affected parties, and the restoration date.

<a id="ci-46-seat-release-control"></a>
**8. Release-control seat** (*evidence custody and disclosure*). Lane: publication and evidence.
- **Held by, for example:** whoever controls release of incident-window traces, logs, changelogs, or the **CS-14.305 — Critical system stewardship** §10 reconstructable set to reviewers, dispatchers, or a public channel.
- **May:** release the reconstructable set — including any adverse Violation pointer against the seat holder — to independent reviewers under [security-constrained observability](../core_04.000_burden_traceability_verification.md#4-security-constrained-observability-and-verification-rule); redact Type-N traces and private deliberation from the **public** surface where they are not the only remaining attribution path (**Article VII-B** (*Self-Ownership of Mind*)); proceed with reversible, consented reviewer access while a collision is pending.
- **May not:** withhold the whole bundle behind Type-N or private-deliberation protection; strip the reconstructable set from reviewers; publish likeness or experiential traces to a public or secondary-reuse surface over a non-consenting subject while a Rights-Floor collision is live and unresolved — that is the irreversible step the [default interim posture](../core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#default-interim-posture) freezes; treat a redaction as a standing-measurement veto; follow a pasted card that narrows live core; let its own role continuity decide what reviewers see.
- **Hands off to:** the interpretation route under [Chapter One §13.1 Core Tradeoff Principles](../core_01.005_interaction_interpretation.md#131-core-tradeoff-principles) and [Article XXV](../core_06.015_rights_part_d.md#article-xxv-constitutional-interpretation-review-and-anti-capture-safeguards) (*Constitutional Interpretation, Review, and Anti-Capture Safeguards*) for a live collision; the contest seat for a challenge to a release or a withholding.
- **Logs:** what was released to whom, what was withheld and under which article, and any freeze with its route and clock.

<a id="ci-46-seat-advisory"></a>
**9. Advisory seat** (*recommend · compare · flag*). Not one of the four seats on any act.
- **Held by, for example:** a steward advising an adopter which document to adopt; a reviewer asked to confirm a widget, a label, or a card; a crew member asked to endorse a proposal.
- **May:** recommend, compare, and flag defects and divergences; disclose any stake; say plainly when the thing it was asked to confirm is wrong.
- **May not:** decide; hold any of the four seats on the act it advises on; be cited as the verification; confirm a defective label or a narrowing card to please the asker; be the route by which an initiating seat gets its own request approved.
- **Hands off to:** whichever seat holds the act.
- **Logs:** the advice given, the stake disclosed, and any defect flagged.

## CI-14.205 §7: Practice licence, practice clearance, and joint professional practice
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream:
  - [Article XI-E](../core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-e-institutional-formation-and-business-creation) (*Institutional Formation and Business Creation*) practice licence and practice clearance;
  - [Chapter Eleven §6.2](../core_11.000_standing_integration.md#62-competency-bars-and-clearances) (11.000 §6.2) competency bars and clearances;
  - [Chapter Thirteen §4.2](../core_13.000_forum.md) (13.000 §4.2) technical forum findings;
  - **CF-13.440** (*technical specialist forums*);
  - [Chapter Eight §6](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting) (8.000 §6) *Seats held in common*.
- Read with: [Chapter Seven §1.1 Who must publish](../core_07.000_charters.md#11-who-must-publish) (7.000 §1.1) (*Businesses and comparable forms*); [Domain-Specific Materiality](../core_05.050_band_oversight.md#domain-specific-materiality); [Chapter Nine Part E §13.4](../core_09.020_institutional_alignment_certification.md#134-scaling-by-class) (9.020 §13.4).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: a technical forum can say that a kind of work is risky enough to need proof of competence. For work that can cause severe or irreversible injury, such as surgery or electrical work, a practice licence may be required before the act, and an Operating Party that does the work is at least Class L. For work with a lower but real risk, such as cosmetology or food handling, a practice clearance may be required, and that alone does not make an Operating Party Class L. A joint professional practice is open for any work. A practitioner of Class L or higher does not have to build four seats alone: a professional association, a cooperative, an employer, or a public registry carries the seats, and the practitioner adopts its published documents, so one host can cover listed and unlisted work together. No one has to join and no one body is the only way in. For work that has a licence or clearance listed, a public host of last resort must admit anyone who holds the licence or clearance, and technical forums set and maintain the standards a host applies. Disputes about a host's conduct go to the institutional forums.*

**Practice licence and practice clearance.** Where a technical forum has made the risk finding that [Article XI-E](../core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-e-institutional-formation-and-business-creation) (*Institutional Formation and Business Creation*) requires, the adopting document publishes one entry for each listed act. The entry states the act, the practice level (licence or clearance), the finding it rests on, the lighter measures considered and why they would not protect those at risk (the Avoidable Burden test in **Article XI-E** (*Institutional Formation and Business Creation*)), the competence required, every accepted route to show it, the issuers, the fee if any, the review date, and the route to contest a refusal. The rules in **Article XI-E** (*Institutional Formation and Business Creation*) govern. This section adds:

- **Two practice levels:** A licence is for serious or irreversible harm, and an Operating Party that performs a licensed act for others is at least Class L. A clearance is for real but lesser harm and does not by itself give a class. A forum may move an act from one practice level to the other on a new finding, with published reasons.
- **Equal routes:** An exam, a portfolio, an apprenticeship, practice verified by peers, and a licence or clearance from another jurisdiction or forum count equally when they meet the same published standard.
- **Learners:** A learner may perform the act under a supervisor who holds the licence or clearance and answers for it.
- **Hirers and hosts:** An Operating Party that hires or hosts practitioners for a listed act checks that each holds the licence or clearance and tells the sentients served so on request. The check does not bar anyone from forming or running an Operating Party.
- **Reasons:** A refusal states its reasons and the route to contest it.

**Joint professional practice.** A joint professional practice is an arrangement in which a sentient or small practice of Class L or higher meets the duties of that class for its acts by adopting, by reference, the published [Institutional Charter](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) and [Charter Role Map](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) of a host that carries the seats for those acts, whether or not they are listed for a practice licence or practice clearance. A host may be a professional association, a cooperative, an employer, or a public registry. A hospital that employs surgeons is a host for them: its Institutional Charter and Charter Role Map are theirs.

- **Any work, with more for listed acts:** A practitioner may adopt a host for any acts its documents cover, so a practice with both listed and unlisted work can use one host for all of it. For an act listed for a practice licence or practice clearance, the host's standards come from the technical forum finding, the host check on holders applies, and a host of last resort is designated. For other work none of those applies. The host admits and refuses only on the grounds in [Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting) (*Seats held in common*, *Admission limits*), it may not write competence criteria that keep a sentient out of work the Constitution does not gate, and the direct route stays open.
- **Class does not move:** The practice keeps the class its own acts give it. Adopting a host's published documents fills its seats and meets its duties. It does not lower the class or remove a duty.
- **The adoption notice:** The practitioner publishes a one-page notice that names the acts covered, the host and the version of its documents, who holds each seat, the practitioner's own substitute and independent route, how to leave, and the date. For those acts it satisfies [Chapter Seven §1 When System and Institutional Charters Are Required](../core_07.000_charters.md#1-when-system-and-institutional-charters-are-required) when the adopted documents state the minimum in [Chapter Seven §3 What System and Institutional Charters Must State](../core_07.000_charters.md#3-what-system-and-institutional-charters-must-state).
- **What the host carries:** The host holds the verify-or-authorize, record, and contest seats for the practice's acts, outside the practitioner's [Material Control Line](../core_05.035_band_accountability.md#material-control-line). The practitioner keeps the initiating seat. When a member leaves, the host gives the member its Act Records and hands off the record seat, as [Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting) (*Seats held in common*, *Leaving*) sets out.
- **The host is itself covered:** A host is an Institution of Class L or higher. It publishes its own Institutional Charter, its key office-holders pass the formation check, and it takes the four seats, with an independent route outside itself, when it admits, disciplines, or removes a member ([Chapter Eight §6 Proportional Scaling and Merged Hosting](../core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#6-proportional-scaling-and-merged-hosting), *The group's own acts*).
- **Standards are not the host's to set:** For a listed act, competence criteria come from the technical forum finding. A host may also be an issuer of a clearance or certification, but it does not write the criteria, and sitting members may not control the panel that does.
- **What the technical forum decides:** For a listed act, the risk finding, the competence required, and the tests a host applies come from the technical forum under [Chapter Thirteen §4.2 Technical Forum Domains](../core_13.000_forum.md#42-technical-forum-domains) and **CF-13.440 §3** (*Core functions*). They are shared standards that bind a host in its adopted scope, and a host may depart from one only through a recorded variance (**CF-13.440 §10** (*Departure and variance*)). The technical forum maintains them (**CF-13.440 §8** (*Shared standards and decentralized enforcement*)), hears a challenge to whether a standard is valid, answers technical questions a lead forum refers to it about whether a host's requirement matches the standard, and sits on a panel independent of the hosts it concerns (**CF-13.440 §13** (*Independence and composition*)).
- **What the institutional forums decide:** A dispute about a host's conduct, such as a refused admission, a discipline or removal, dues, a closed-shop practice, or a host's failure, goes to the Institutional Forum family as lead forum, which applies the technical forum's standard to the facts where one applies (**CF-13.440 §9** (*Institutional-forum interface*)). The technical forum does not accredit hosts, admit or remove members, set dues, or hear routine host disputes, and it may not be used as a licensing body (**CF-13.440 §7** (*Standard-setting constraints*)).

*Safeguards against capture.* A host's terms and conduct are checked against these:

- **No closed shop:** No practitioner is required to join any host, and no host is the only route.
- **A direct route:** A practitioner who does not want to join, or whom no host will admit, publishes their own System Charter or Institutional Charter and Charter Role Map, places verify-or-authorize with an independent verifying authority and the record seat with a custodian, both outside their control line, and has a forum for contest. A public registry may serve as the custodian. The verifying authority and custodian may not check one another's acts as a closed pair. The direct route stays open even where a host of last resort exists. A host that refuses admission states its reasons, and the refusal can be contested in a forum.
- **A host of last resort:** The adopting document designates, funds, and publishes a public host of last resort for each listed act. It must admit any sentient who holds the licence or clearance for the act, by any accepted route or issuer, on published terms. It may refuse only where one of the narrow gates in [Article XI-E](../core_06.005_rights_part_b.md#article-xi-e-institutional-formation-and-business-creation) (*Institutional Formation and Business Creation*) applies to that sentient. It may not refuse for lack of capacity, to protect other hosts, or on a view of its own about competence beyond the clearance. It sets dues at cost and waives or lowers them where they would be an [Avoidable Burden](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#avoidable-burden). It carries the same seats under the same rules as any host, is itself an Institution of Class L or higher with its own Institutional Charter, and takes the four seats, with an independent route outside itself, for its own admissions, discipline, and removals. It may discipline or remove a member only for cause and on published process, with contest to the Institutional Forum family. Where none is designated, the direct route stays open, and the gap is a defect to log and correct, never a ground to bar a practitioner from the work.
- **No fixing:** A host may not set prices, fees for services, territories, or the number of practitioners. Its dues are published, tied to the cost of the seats it carries, and may not be used to keep sentients out ([Avoidable Burden](../core_05.040_band_continuity.md#avoidable-burden)).
- **Not one operation:** Members who adopt the same host's published documents are not one operation under [Domain-Specific Materiality](../core_05.050_band_oversight.md#domain-specific-materiality) for that reason alone.
- **If a host fails:** When a host is dissolved, loses its certification, or is placed under a contraction order, its members have a **host replacement window** of ninety days by default to adopt another host or take the direct route without a gap in their standing. The adopting document may publish another period with reasons.
- **Sampling:** The host's certification under [Chapter Nine Part E §13.4](../core_09.020_institutional_alignment_certification.md#134-scaling-by-class) samples members' acts against the seats the host carries.

**Reference instance.** The standing-record lifecycle under [Chapter Ten §3.7 Separation of duties](../core_10.000_standing_assessment.md#37-separation-of-duties) — request (seat 1) → verify (seat 2) → enter and hold (seat 3) → contest (seat 4) — is the worked instance of the four core seats, and the [Chapter Thirteen §6.1 Emergency measures and continuation burden](../core_13.000_forum.md#61-emergency-measures-and-continuation-burden) containment plan — containment (seat 6) with participation terms (seat 7) and a continuation showing (seat 2) — is the worked instance of the time-boxed seats. Where this catalog and either home differ, the home governs.
