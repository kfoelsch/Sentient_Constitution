Core home: 04

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-4.260** (*Place-based stewardship, Indigenous continuity, and consultation routes*).

*In plain terms: decisions about land and ecosystems can affect communities with durable connections to a place, including Indigenous continuity of language, culture, governance, and knowledge. Shared floors live in **CJS-3.120 §7** (*Participation: Quorum and Participatory Legitimacy Terms*). This file names who keeps consultation records, routes continuity impacts, and escalates ineffective consultation.*

## CI-4.260 §1: Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: this file covers how institutions identify affected communities and make consultation reviewable.*

This file assigns institutional ownership for identifying affected communities, documenting consultation, routing Indigenous-continuity impacts, and escalating inadequate responses.

**Quick orientation:**

- **The basic idea** — place-based stewardship decisions must identify affected communities, publish consultation pathways, and keep records that support accountable reconstruction — not symbolic listening sessions with no effect.
- **Indigenous continuity** — where community continuity of language, culture, governance practice, or knowledge transmission is materially implicated, institutions must route the response under:
  - **Chapter Three** *Indigenous Continuity*;
  - the owner floors in **Article VI-C** (*Nondiscrimination*) and **Article I-A** (*Environmental Preconditions and Ecological Integrity*).
- **Consultation records** — who was consulted, what was said, what changed, and what did not must be custodied and auditable.
- **Compliance theater** — funded projects that box-check consultation without meaningful risk response must escalate through **CI-6.215 §2** (*External Assurance Triggers*) and **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*).
- **What this is not** — no unbounded territorial-restitution mandate; no constitutional-layer adjudication of historical territorial claims; adoption-scope and adopter-jurisdiction discipline stay in **Chapter Thirty-One**.
- **Hard limits** — symbolic consultation cannot be presented as binding governance; binding governance cannot be hidden as optional consultation; participation records must support later challenge.
- **Read with** — **CI-6.215 §2** (*External Assurance Triggers*) and **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) for external assurance and cross-institution escalation.

## CI-4.260 §2: Why Place-Based Consultation Matters

*In plain terms: decisions about a place can affect the communities and living systems connected to it.*

Land and ecosystem decisions can affect community continuity, including language, culture, governance, and knowledge. Identifying affected communities and documenting the response helps make consultation meaningful and open to challenge.

**Local place-based stewardship and consultation role map**

Each institution in scope must name the office or body responsible for:

- **consultation-record custody** — who receives, stores, tracks versions of, and publishes consultation notices, submissions, responses, and decision rationales, subject to applicable privacy and security limits, so participation can be reconstructed and challenged later;
- **stakeholder and affected-community identification** — who determines which place-linked, Indigenous, tenure-holding, ecosystem-dependent, or comparable communities are materially affected, and so must receive notice and practical participation routes under **CJS-3.120 §7** (*quorum and participatory legitimacy terms*);
- **Indigenous-continuity response** — who evaluates and routes decisions that materially implicate **Chapter Three** *Indigenous Continuity*, including impacts on language, cultural practice, governance practice, and knowledge transmission, under **Article VI-C** (*Nondiscrimination*), **Article I-A** (*Environmental Preconditions and Ecological Integrity*), and **Chapter Thirty-One** discipline where territorial-continuity questions arise;
- **advisory-versus-binding pathway publication** — who publishes whether a consultation route is advisory, binding, or relied on for governance effect, and what challenge or correction paths follow; and
- **compliance-theater escalation** — who escalates funded projects or supervised programs where consultation occurs without meaningful risk response to **CI-6.215 §2** (*External Assurance Triggers*) external-assurance triggers and **CI-22.210** (*Cross-institution coordination and escalation*) cross-institution escalation.
Read **Chapter Three** *Indigenous Continuity* alongside these articles where materially applicable:

- **Article I-A** (*Environmental Preconditions and Ecological Integrity*);
- **Article VI-C** (*Nondiscrimination*);
- **Article XXIII-C** (*Non-Statelessness*).
