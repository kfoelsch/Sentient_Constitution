Core home: 04

This file is the institutional implementation home for **CI-4.225** (*Care labor, dependent support, respite, and care-economy fairness*).

*In plain terms: paid and unpaid care work supports dependents, elders, disabled sentients, families, and communities. Shared floors live in **CJS-3.130 §16** (*Continuity: Dependency Integrity and Disclosure Terms*). This file names who routes respite, tracks care work, and receives coercion reports.*

**Purpose and role:** This file assigns institutional ownership for care and respite pathways, care-work reporting, coercion referrals, and related budgets and labor routes.

**Quick orientation:**

- **The basic idea** — care labor is operational capacity; institutions that depend on it must name who routes respite, measures unpaid care, handles coercion reports, and funds reproductive-labor pathways.
- **Respite** — carers and dependents need substitution, crisis coverage, and relief without abandonment, retaliation, or impossible eligibility hoops.
- **Visibility** — unpaid and paid care must appear in governance metrics and dependency maps, not stay off the books.
- **Coercion in care** — intake and escalation routes must connect to **CI-4.245** (*Relational coercive control, intimate power, and anti-domination routing*) without dissolving lawful association or conscience protections that **Chapter Four** guarantees.
- **Reproductive-labor fairness** — metrics, budgets, service pathways, and labor routes must treat reproductive and maintenance labor as governable, not as a private default obligation.
- **Hard limits** — survival floors cannot be conditioned on care labor (**CI-8.210 §4** (*Survival floors, voluntary exchange, and markets*)); billing and charges cannot quietly block care access (**CI-4.210** (*Public revenue, fees, recurring charges, and billing integrity*)); funding allocation must stay auditable (**CS-4.335** (*Resource allocation and funding stewardship*)).
- **Read with** — **CI-8.210 §4** (*Survival floors, voluntary exchange, and markets (Article III-A (Survival) interface)*), **CI-4.210** (*Public revenue, fees, recurring charges, and billing integrity*), **CI-4.205 §4** (*Governance Disclosure Schema*), **CI-4.245** (*Relational coercive control, intimate power, and anti-domination routing*), and **CI-4.240** (*Vulnerable personal services markets — general regulation and Article VII-E (Adult consensual commercial sexual services and sexual exploitation) interface*) where applicable.

**Local care-economy and support-dependency role map**

Each institution in scope must name the office or body responsible for:

- **respite pathway** — who routes substitution care, crisis coverage, and relief for carers and dependents, including eligibility, scheduling, accessibility, and contest routes;
- **unpaid-care metrics** — who maintains the **CI-4.205 §4** (*Governance Disclosure Schema*) governance-disclosure fields that make unpaid care, dependency concentration, and carer burden visible in periodic reports rather than treating them as private background; where a carer seeks a contribution record for demonstrable informal care, this or another independent body with published authority to rely on the Standing Record acts as **record-opening authority** under [Chapter Fourteen §7.9](../core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#79-informal-and-small-scope-records) (*Informal and Small-Scope Records*) and [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](../core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement). The carer is the claimant; attestations from dependents or household members are inputs. A separate custodian enters and holds the Standing Record, and no formal-institution credential is required where the body can verify;
- **coercion-intake and escalation** — who receives reports of coercion, exploitation, or dependency abuse in care, household, or service relationships and routes them to **CI-4.245** (*Relational coercive control, intimate power, and anti-domination routing*), labor, housing, benefits, or comparable remedy lanes without retaliation against reporters;
- **reproductive-labor metrics** — who tracks embodiment-related, reproductive, and maintenance labor burdens as governable inputs to planning and oversight, not invisible default obligations;
- **reproductive-labor budget and service pathways** — who owns budget lines, service enrollment, and referral routes for reproductive healthcare, pregnancy-related support, elder care, disability support, substrate maintenance, and comparable dependency services; and
- **reproductive-labor and care-work labor routes** — who connects paid care workers, family carers, and platform or agency intermediaries to fair-compensation, collective-organization, safe-conditions, and billing-integrity pathways under **Articles III-C** (*Labor and Economic Floor*) and **III-D** (*Safe Working Conditions*), **CI-4.210** (*Public revenue, fees, recurring charges, and billing integrity*), and **CS-4.335** (*Resource allocation and funding stewardship*).
