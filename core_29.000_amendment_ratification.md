Chapter Twenty-Nine is the constitutional owner of **procedural and custody validity** for amendment and adoption (Procedural Tests 2 through 4), review triggers, provisional effect, invalid-change handling, referral of serious bad-faith conduct, ratification documents, and amendment procedure requirements.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: a change can be substantively fine and still fail because of how it was made. This chapter is the procedural and paperwork spine for legitimate change, and it holds amendments to a procedure that is real: a published proposal, a clear chain of authority and custody that proves the text is the real one, and a contest path that works, so that independent reviewers and the public can challenge the change. A rights-affecting update must not snap irreversibly into place before those guardrails have actually run. Ticking procedural boxes cannot save a change that fails the substance check or the publication, custody, or contestability tests.*

**Purpose:** This Constitution, including its [subcore](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#subcore), and the implementation text in its [Corpus](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#corpus) may be amended to improve protection, clarity, binding effect, or institutional fitness under changing conditions. All three are amended by the one procedure in **section 3**. Every amendment is subject to **Chapter Twenty-Eight** (non-regression), **Chapter Thirty** (supremacy and external-order interface), and the validity conditions in this chapter.

**Role:** The chapter is the procedural half of change validity. It covers whether a change was made the right way, and what happens when it was not:

- **How a change is made:** the one procedure by which a change is proposed, heard, answered, decided, and recorded (**section 3**).
- **How closely a change is examined:** changes that affect rights get a harder review (**section 4**), whoever proposes a change must justify it in public (**section 5**), and a change that fails **Chapter Twenty-Eight** or any procedural test is invalid even if every step was followed (*Validity floor* below).
- **The three procedural tests:** each asks a different question about how the change was made.
  - **Procedural Test 2:** was the proposal properly published, with enough detail and time for **affected** **parties** to respond? (**section 6**)
  - **Procedural Test 3:** can anyone tell which text is authoritative, who approved it, and when it took effect? (**section 7**)
  - **Procedural Test 4:** did **affected** **parties** get a real chance to object, with independent review where this chapter requires it? (**section 8**)
- **When a change needs review or fails:** some kinds of change automatically get the harder review (**section 9**), a high-stakes change cannot become irreversible before review has run (**section 10**), and an invalid change is unwound and remedied, with any serious bad faith referred to the chapters that decide it (**section 11**).
- **How a body adopts:** how a body adopts this Constitution (**section 12**).

<a id="validity-floor"></a>
**Validity floor:** This chapter states minimum constitutional **procedural and custody** validity conditions for amendment and adoption actions, together with **Chapter Twenty-Eight** substantive conditions. Any amendment that fails **Chapter Twenty-Eight** or any procedural test in **sections 6 through 8** of this chapter is invalid regardless of procedural completion, and is handled under **section 11**.

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- amendment procedure requirements (**section 3**);
- heightened review and the burden of justification (**section 4** and **section 5**), and the validity floor (*Validity floor* above);
- Procedural Tests 2 to 4 (**section 6** to **section 8**);
- review triggers, the provisional effect rule, continuity of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment, invalid-change handling, and the referral of serious bad-faith conduct (**section 9** to **section 11**);
- ratification and adoption (**section 12**), where a body binds itself to this Constitution only by adopting through this chapter (**Chapter Thirty section 7**).

The chapter leaves:

- numeric Contribution and Violation slots to [Chapter Fifteen](core_15.000_standing_measurement.md#chapter-fifteen-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it), which measures serious bad-faith conduct and assigns any numeric slot;
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designations to [Chapter Twenty](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation), which decides any anti-constitutional-misconduct designation;
- the consequences of that conduct to [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#chapter-sixteen-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows), which applies them;
- substantive non-regression to [Chapter Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-eight-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity), which states Substantive Test 1 (procedural steps cannot save a change that fails it, *Validity floor* above); and
- custody, editions, and operative effect to [Chapter Thirty-One](core_31.000_incorporation.md#chapter-thirty-one-incorporation-bridge).

When amendment or adoption conduct shows serious bad faith, this chapter opens a referral record and sends it on (**section 11**). This chapter does not label, rank, or sanction the conduct itself.

### 2. Why Amendment Procedure Exists

*In plain terms: a change can be right in substance and still fail if it was made in the dark. This chapter exists so every change is published, traceable, and open to challenge before it takes effect.*

A constitution that cannot change will fail, and one that can be changed quietly will be captured. This chapter sets how a change is proposed, published, reviewed, adopted, and recorded, with harder review for changes that affect rights and a contest path that actually works. It also sets how a body adopts this Constitution in the first place.

**Grounded in:**

- Preamble:

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: anyone can put a change to this Constitution on the Forum Case Record. To get the Constitutional Forum to take it up, it needs backing: at least 1% of the sentients entitled to vote sign on, a majority of the members of any forum votes for it, a majority of the stewards of a Class A, B, or C system or institution does, or the body that governs the community that adopted the text formally proposes it by a recorded decision. Everyone then gets at least 30 days (the Tier C outer bound) after the last change to the wording to respond, and any edit to the wording restarts that window. Only then does the forum rule on whether the change weakens a protection, or is a correction of a real error in it.*

This subsection sets out how a proposed change to the text of this Constitution is put forward, reaches the **Constitutional** forum, and is open to public response:


##### 3.2.1 Proposal

Any sentient, group, or body may put a proposed change on the Forum Case Record. The proposal is published on the Forum Case Record with its exact wording and the justification required by **section 5**. Every later change to that wording is recorded with its date.

##### 3.2.2 Hearing Gate

The **Constitutional Forum** takes up a proposal for determination when it has any one of these:
- sign-off by at least 1% of the electorate for governance voting under [Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_27.000_governance.md#61-entitlement-and-eligibility), counted for the community that adopted the text the change would alter;
- a vote in favor by a majority of the members then seated of any forum, taken in a recorded decision of that forum;
- a recorded decision in favor by a majority of the stewards then holding the role in one Class A, Class B, or Class C system or institution, made under [Chapter Two §17.7 Duty to Represent Stakeholder Interests](core_02.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#177-duty-to-represent-stakeholder-interests); or
- the body that holds [governing authority](core_27.000_governance.md#3-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority) for the community that adopted the text the change would alter, including an assembly where it holds that authority, puts the change forward by a recorded decision taken under its [documented legitimacy mechanism](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#documented-legitimacy-mechanism) and entered on the Forum Case Record.

##### 3.2.3 Counting Sign-Off

Sign-off is counted from the electorate record published under [Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](core_27.000_governance.md#62-records-gates-and-method-neutrality), by a party independent of the proposers.
- Each distinct subject signs once, as with one subject, one vote under **Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.1** (*Entitlement and Eligibility*).
- Creating sentients, or inducing branches to elect to be distinct, in order to reach the share is non-compliant conduct by the creator.
- Signing and responding are open petition and public comment on the Forum Case Record, and are protected as such under **Article XIII-B** (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*).

##### 3.2.4 Counting a Forum or Steward Majority

The vote or decision is recorded on the Forum Case Record, with dissent recorded.
- Each distinct subject counts once toward a majority, as with one subject, one vote under **Chapter Twenty-Seven §6.1** (*Entitlement and Eligibility*).
- Creating, appointing, or seating members or stewards in order to form a majority is non-compliant conduct by whoever does it.
- A **Constitutional Forum** panel that voted to bring a proposal to hearing does not decide it. The determination is made by a panel with no overlapping decision-makers, drawn from the constitutional-review reserve roster maintained under **CF-22.470** (*Forum staffing, reserve capacity, shared administration, structural review, and structural records*).

##### 3.2.5 Amendment Comment Window

Any sentient may respond to a proposal on the Forum Case Record. The window for responses is no shorter than **30 days**, the Tier C outer bound in [Chapter Twenty-Three §5 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#5-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline), counted from the last recorded change to the proposal's wording.
- Any later change to the wording, including one made in response to comments, is a recorded change and restarts the count from its date.
- Sign-off may be gathered while the window runs. The forum may not decide until the gate is met and the window has run.
- The forum may extend the window. It may not shorten it.
- Every response, objection, and dissent stays on the Forum Case Record. For a materially high-impact change, participation and objections are also recorded as **Article XII-A** (*Stakeholder System Participation and Representation*) requires.

##### 3.2.6 Determination

When the gate is met and the window has run, the **Constitutional Forum** decides whether the proposal fails Substantive Test 1 under [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4](core_28.000_non_regression.md#4-substantive-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*) and publishes its determination with reasons under **Article XXVI-A** (*Bounded Interpretive Mandate and Public Reasons*). The reasons must say how the responses filed were treated. Review of the determination follows **Article XXVI-B** (*Challenge Rights and Independent Review*).

##### 3.2.7 Corrections

A proposer may mark a proposal as a correction of a protection. The justification required by **section 5** must then show the error in the form that [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4](core_28.000_non_regression.md#4-substantive-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*) requires. The hearing gate and the amendment comment window are the same as for any other proposal. The determination states whether the proposal is found to be a correction and which findings support that.

<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

<br>

*In plain terms: proposals have to show their work — who is affected, what might go wrong, and why the change still clears the non-regression bar — vague hand-waving about "necessity" is not enough. For any significant amendment, the independent Public Policy Office checks that work in public before the decision.*

Proposed amendments must provide publicly auditable justification, including expected effects, affected populations, foreseeable risks, and compatibility with **Chapter Twenty-Eight** non-regression constraints. Unsupported claims of necessity or feasibility are insufficient.

The proposer's claims are tested independently, not taken on trust:

- **Independent evaluation:** a proposed amendment that meets the materiality threshold of [Subcore 7-B §4.1](subcore_07.020_public_policy_office.md#41-the-materiality-threshold) (*The Materiality Threshold*), or whose evaluation is asked for under Subcore 7-B §4.2 (*Evaluation on Request*), is evaluated by the **Public Policy Office** under [Subcore 7-B §4.7](subcore_07.020_public_policy_office.md#47-proposed-amendments) (*Proposed Amendments*) before the adoption decision. The proposer's justification serves as the sponsor's impact statement under Subcore 7-B §5.2 (*The Sponsor's Impact Statement*).
- **Part of the publication record:** the evaluation, the office's rating of the proposer's justification, and the adopting body's reasoned response to each material finding are published with the justification and form part of the publication record under [§6 Procedural Test 2](#6-procedural-test-2--procedural-validity-and-publication-integrity).
- **Evidence, not a ruling:** the evaluation informs the justification and any review of it. It does not decide whether the amendment is valid under **Chapter Twenty-Eight** or this chapter.

### 6. Procedural Test 2 — Procedural Validity and Publication Integrity
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: Procedural Test 2 is the "did you actually publish a real proposal with enough detail and time for **affected** **parties** to respond" test — shadow drafts and last-second rewrites that nobody can review do not pass.*

A proposed amendment is invalid unless its publication meets [Publication Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#publication-integrity). For an amendment:

- **Elements:** the publication includes the exact proposed text, the scope of the amendment, the provisions it affects, its expected impacts, the justification required by **section 5**, and the contest pathways.
- **Response window:** the window is the amendment comment window in **section 3.2**, and a change to the wording restarts it as that subsection provides.
- **Traceability:** the publication satisfies **Subcore 3-C** burden and traceability of definitions to results.

### 7. Procedural Test 3 — Authority-Chain and Custody Validity

- Read with: [Record Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#record-integrity) and [Attribution Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#attribution-integrity) (*the general tests this section applies*); [Chapter Three *Corpus*](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#corpus) (S3-L) and [README.md](README.md) (edition identifiers); [Corpus and Authority Stack](subcore_03.080_band_integrative.md#defi1-corpus-and-authority-stack) (**Def.I1** cluster).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: Procedural Test 3 is the chain-of-custody test — if nobody can tell which text is authoritative, who approved it, or when it took effect, the amendment does not constitutionally exist as binding.*

A proposed amendment is invalid unless the amendment records meet [Record Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#record-integrity) and its chain of authority meets [Attribution Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#attribution-integrity). For an amendment:

- **Records:** the adoption document, edition identifier, effective date, and custodian chain are present, consistent, and reliable, measured against **section 12** (*Ratification and Adoption*) and **Corpus** custody controls;
- **Authority:** anyone can tell which text is authoritative, who approved it, under what authority, and when it took effect.

### 11. Invalid-Change Handling and Remediation Continuity
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§1](#validity-floor)–[§10](#10-provisional-effect-rule) validity and timing rules.
- Subsections: [§11.1](#111-effect-of-an-invalidity-finding)–[§11.3](#113-serious-misconduct-referral).
- Downstream: Referral-style obligations in operative bullets route per [Chapter Twenty-Eight §7](core_28.000_non_regression.md#7-work-this-chapter-leaves-to-other-chapters) (28.000 §7) (*Work This Chapter Leaves to Other Chapters*).
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Two](core_22.000_forum.md#chapter-twenty-two-standards-routing-and-forum-cases) (22.000) forum-family and jurisdiction routing where invalidity determinations implicate forum role.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: when a change is declared invalid, treat it as void for constitutional purposes from that determination forward, spell out what must be rolled back and how **affected** **parties** get a fair remedy — and publish a time-bounded plan that restores protections and preserves evidence. Serious bad-faith conduct in making or adopting a change is referred to the bodies that own labels and sanctions, not relabeled here.*

This section sets what an invalidity finding does (§11.1 *Effect of an Invalidity Finding*), the plan that restores protections afterward (§11.2 *Remediation Plan*), and the referral of serious misconduct in making or adopting a change (§11.3 *Serious-Misconduct Referral*).

<a id="111-effect-of-an-invalidity-finding"></a>
#### 11.1 Effect of an Invalidity Finding
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§1](#validity-floor) validity floor; [Chapter Twenty-Eight §4](core_28.000_non_regression.md#4-substantive-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (28.000 §4) Substantive Test 1 determination.
- Downstream: [§11.2](#112-remediation-plan) remediation plan.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: a change found invalid stops counting as constitutional law from the moment of that finding, and the decision says what it touched and what has to be put right.*

Where a change is found invalid, including by the **Constitutional Forum**'s determination under [**Chapter Twenty-Eight §4**](core_28.000_non_regression.md#4-substantive-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*), it is treated as non-operative for constitutional supremacy purposes from the point of determination.

Decision records must identify scope, affected acts, remediation obligations, and required restoration steps for materially affected parties.

<a id="112-remediation-plan"></a>
#### 11.2 Remediation Plan
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11.1](#111-effect-of-an-invalidity-finding) effect of the finding.
- Downstream: [Evidence Preservation](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation); [Contestability](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#contestability) remedy pathways.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: after a finding of invalidity, the adopter publishes a plan with deadlines that brings back the protections the change removed, keeps the evidence, and gives those affected a fair way to a remedy.*

On invalidity determination, adopters must publish a time-bounded remediation plan that:
- restores controlling prior protections or stricter lawful protections
- preserves evidence under [Evidence Preservation](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation) and audit trails
- provides contestable remedy pathways proportionate to impact and dependency

<a id="113-serious-misconduct-referral"></a>
#### 11.3 Serious-Misconduct Referral
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11.1](#111-effect-of-an-invalidity-finding) the conduct behind an invalid change; [Chapter Twenty §5](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#5-criteria-set-for-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation--accountability-at-constitutional-scale) (20.000 §5) criteria for serious misconduct.
- Downstream: Mandatory referral records route to [Chapter Fourteen](core_14.000_standing_assessment.md#chapter-fourteen-standing-records--question-1-what-happened) (14.000) (separate Contribution Axis and Violation Axis records and numeric measurement on the [Chapter Fifteen §8](core_15.000_standing_measurement.md#8-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes) unified scale), [Chapter Sixteen §4](core_16.000_standing_integration.md#4-automatic-integration-review-and-continuity) (16.000 §4) (integration routing of qualifying Violation Axis slot 7–9 findings), and [Chapter Twenty](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) (20.000) (the corresponding anti-constitutional-misconduct designation for a qualifying Chapter Fifteen Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9 record); this chapter does not classify.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: serious bad faith in making or adopting a change, such as deliberate deception or falsifying records, must be written up in a referral record and sent on.*

Where amendment or adoption conduct indicates serious misconduct, a mandatory referral record must be opened. **Serious misconduct** includes the following (read with [**Chapter Twenty**, **section 5** (*Criteria set*)](core_20.000_misconduct_designation.md#5-criteria-set-for-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation--accountability-at-constitutional-scale), **criterion 1** (*Willful deceptive intent*), **criterion 2** (*Knowing falsification or suppression*), **criterion 3** (*Material rollback effect*), and **criterion 4** (*Causally significant conduct*) on **change** or **record** pathways):
- willful deceptive intent;
- knowing falsification or suppression;
- material rollback effect; and
- causally significant obstruction or degradation.

**Forcible** or **nullification** misconduct routes through **criteria 5** and **6** (*Insurrection or material forcible subversion* and *Operative nullification*) of the same **Chapter Twenty** section where material. Those traits remain character descriptors and anti-constitutional-misconduct designation (ACMD) predicates; they do not move the numeric Violation Axis slot.

The referral record goes to the chapters that measure, integrate, and designate the conduct, as listed in **section 1**. This chapter does not classify it.

*In plain terms: claiming this constitution as a shield without a deliberate, documented adoption that matches impact and class — or letting new parties "join" in a way that strips **existing** **covered** **sentients** of floors — is exactly what these ratification rules forbid.*

#### 12.1 Deliberate Adoption
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§12](#12-ratification-and-adoption) ratification frame.
- Downstream: [§12.2](#122-adoption-document) document requirements; [§12.3](#123-joining-by-additional-parties) joining rules.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3](core_27.000_governance.md#3-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority) (27.000 §3) documented legitimacy mechanism alongside deliberate adoption; [Chapter Thirty-One §5 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_31.000_incorporation.md#5-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority) (31.000 §5) (*who may adopt* — a steward bound by valid adoption is not themselves a qualifying adopter).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: binding adoption has to be explicit, recorded, and auditable to the scale of the system — quiet or partial "we kind of follow it" does not let an operator claim constitutional protection while dodging obligations, and a steward's own-conduct note is not adoption.*

This Constitution binds a steward, organization, federation of bodies, or jurisdiction only when adoption is deliberate, documented, and auditable:

- **Auditable to scale:** auditability must meet a standard proportionate to system class, material impact, and dependency under **[CS-8.305](corpus_systems/cs_08.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-8305-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [Subcore 8-A — System Classification](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#subcore-8-a-system-classification)**.
- **No shield without obligations:** informal, partial, or undisclosed adoption is insufficient to claim constitutional protections as a shield while denying corresponding obligations.
- **When a steward is bound:** a steward is bound when a qualifying adopter records a valid adoption document under this section and [Chapter Thirty-One §5 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_31.000_incorporation.md#5-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority). Listing a steward as a party bound by adoption does not make that steward themselves a qualifying adopter.
- **Jurisdictions and polities:** when the adopter is a jurisdiction or polity, the recorded mechanism must include a published affected-sentient participation check, such as:
  - a contested election;
  - sortition;
  - federated ratification; or
  - another path that complies with [Chapter Twenty-Seven §3.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums](core_27.000_governance.md#32-eligibility-contested-selection-and-democratic-minimums).

  A silent board resolution, an undisclosed executive document, or "we kind of follow it" practice is not enough for a polity to claim constitutional adoption.
- **Other adopters:** organizations, system operators, and system-developer consortia adopt through the deliberate, documented, and auditable path without that polity participation check.

*In plain terms: keep a clear paper trail — who adopted, what scope, when it starts, who holds the master copy of the corpus — so nobody can launder authority through ambiguous version drift.*

Adopters must maintain an identifiable adoption document. #### 12.4 Adoption Transition Record and Adoption Charter
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§12.2](#122-adoption-document) adoption document.
- Downstream: [Article XXIX-A](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) (the Adoption Transition Record's contents and the final gate); [Chapter Nineteen §3.4 Gatekeeper Checks Across Adopters and Levels of Trust](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#34-gatekeeper-checks-across-adopters-and-levels-of-trust).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: the adoption document records that an adopter adopted. Two more published documents record how it goes: the Adoption Transition Record while the adopter moves out of legacy operation, and the Adoption Charter once it is settled. The Adoption Charter is where other adopters find the office to ask about Standing Records and learn how far this adopter trusts them.*

Besides the adoption document, an adopter keeps and publishes two documents: the Adoption Transition Record while it moves out of legacy operation (§12.4.1 *Adoption Transition Record*), and the Adoption Charter once it reaches the steady-state phase (§12.4.2 *Adoption Charter*). Both change and are reviewed the same way (§12.4.3 *Changes, Vacancies, and Review*). The Adoption Transition Record and the Adoption Charter are not the adoption document, are not evidence of certification, and do not by themselves show that an Adoption Transition is complete.

<a id="1241-adoption-transition-record"></a>
##### 12.4.1 Adoption Transition Record

The Adoption Transition Record is kept during the transition:

- **When:** From the day the adopting document takes effect until the steady-state phase begins, the adopter keeps and publishes an [Adoption Transition Record](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-record) with the contents [Article XXIX-A](core_04.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) requires.
- **Provisional entries:** It carries, marked provisional, the forum authority assignment, the record custodian and the office-level contact for gatekeeper checks, and the levels of trust the adopter extends.
- **Trust needs a forum:** An adopter that extends any level of trust above the public level must have at least a provisional forum authority assignment.

<a id="1242-adoption-charter"></a>
##### 12.4.2 Adoption Charter

The Adoption Charter is published at the final gate:

- **The final gate:** When the adopter passes the final gate into the steady-state phase, it publishes an [Adoption Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#adoption-charter) that confirms or replaces the provisional entries, and the Adoption Transition Record is closed and archived. The steady-state phase does not begin until the Adoption Charter is published.
- **Contents:** the Adoption Charter states:
  - the forum authority assignment;
  - the record custodian for Standing Records and the office-level contact for gatekeeper checks;
  - the level of trust extended to each other adopter, with its reasons, the technical advice it rests on, and any reasons for departing from that advice;
  - standards agreed with other adopters; and
  - a [Charter Pointer](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#charter-pointer) to each [Charter Role Map](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) that applies to the adopter's systems and institutions.
- **The gatekeeper contact:** The office-level contact for gatekeeper checks is a routing contact. It either holds the Standing Record or tells the gatekeeper which record custodian does, using the [Charter Role Maps](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) that apply and, where a map names no custodian for stakeholder Standing Records, the Backup Record Custodian ([CF-22.415 §11 Backup Record Custodian](corpus_forum/cf_22.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-22415-11-backup-record-custodian); [Chapter Five §6.6.2 Stakeholder Standing Records](core_05.000_charters.md#662-stakeholder-standing-records)). Its answer times are set by the check-answer window in [Chapter Nineteen §3.1 Anti-Aggregation of Gated-Pathway Effects](core_19.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#31-anti-aggregation-of-gated-pathway-effects).
- **How it is published:** It is published as [Public Oversight Baseline Disclosure](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#public-oversight-baseline-disclosure) and names offices and roles only. A named sentient's address, phone number, or other contact details are never published in it.

<a id="1243-changes-vacancies-and-review"></a>
##### 12.4.3 Changes, Vacancies, and Review

The following rules apply to both documents:

- **Changes:** A change to either document takes effect as a new dated version, published, and the earlier versions stay available.
- **An unplanned vacancy:** If the record custodian or the office-level contact becomes vacant or cannot act, that is a seat vacancy under [Chapter Six §6.3 A Seat the Map Leaves Unplaced, or a Holder Who Cannot Serve](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#63-general-seat-vacancy-rule). The adopter publishes a new dated version naming the replacement within its [Class Repair Window](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#class-repair-window), and meanwhile the Backup Record Custodian ([CF-22.415 §11 Backup Record Custodian](corpus_forum/cf_22.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-22415-11-backup-record-custodian)) answers gatekeeper checks for that adopter.
- **Review:** Once the Adoption Charter is published, the adopter confirms it, with its record custodian and office-level contact, at least once in each **Adoption Charter review interval**, which is twelve months, and publishes the confirmation as a new dated version.
