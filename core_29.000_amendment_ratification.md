Chapter Twenty-Nine is the constitutional owner of **procedural and custody validity** for amendment and adoption (Procedural Tests 2 through 4), review triggers, provisional effect, invalid-change handling, referral of serious bad-faith conduct, ratification documents, and amendment procedure requirements.

### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

*In plain terms: a change can be substantively fine and still fail because of how it was made. This chapter is the procedural and paperwork spine for legitimate change, and it holds amendments to a procedure that is real: a published proposal, a clear chain of authority and custody that proves the text is the real one, and a contest path that works, so that independent reviewers and the public can challenge the change. A rights-affecting update must not snap irreversibly into place before those guardrails have actually run.*

**Purpose:** This Constitution, including its [subcore](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#subcore), may be amended to improve protection, clarity, binding effect, or institutional fitness under changing conditions, subject to **Chapter Twenty-Eight** (non-regression), **Chapter Thirty** (supremacy and external-order interface), subject to the validity conditions in this chapter.

**Role:** The chapter is the procedural half of change validity. It covers whether a change was made the right way, and what happens when it was not:

- **How closely a change is examined:** changes that affect rights get a harder review (**section 2**), whoever proposes a change must justify it in public (**section 3**), and a change that fails **Chapter Twenty-Eight** or any procedural test is invalid even if every step was followed (**section 4**).
- **The three procedural tests:** each asks a different question about how the change was made.
  - **Procedural Test 2:** was the proposal properly published, with enough detail and time for **affected** **parties** to respond? (**section 5**)
  - **Procedural Test 3:** can anyone tell which text is authoritative, who approved it, and when it took effect? (**section 6**)
  - **Procedural Test 4:** did **affected** **parties** get a real chance to object, with independent review where this chapter requires it? (**section 7**)
- **When a change needs review or fails:** some kinds of change automatically get the harder review (**section 8**), a high-stakes change cannot become irreversible before review has run (**section 9**), and an invalid change is unwound and remedied, with any serious bad faith referred to the chapters that decide it (**section 10**).
- **How a body adopts and amends:** how a body adopts this Constitution (**section 11**) and how it is amended (**section 12**).
- **Staying in its lane:** this chapter sends other chapters' work to them and does not take it over (**section 13**).

**Chapter scope:** The chapter covers:

- heightened review, the burden of justification, and the validity floor (**section 2** to **section 4**);
- Procedural Tests 2 to 4 (**section 5** to **section 7**);
- review triggers, the provisional effect rule, invalid-change handling, and the referral of serious bad-faith conduct (**section 8** to **section 10**);
- ratification, adoption, and amendment procedure requirements (**section 11** and **section 12**), where a body binds itself to this Constitution only by adopting through this chapter (**Chapter Thirty section 6**); and
- the owner non-relocation rule (**section 13**).

The chapter leaves:

- numeric Contribution and Violation slots to [Chapter Sixteen](core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#chapter-sixteen-standing-measurement--question-2-how-good-or-bad-was-it), which measures serious bad-faith conduct and assigns any numeric slot;
- anti-constitutional-misconduct designations to [Chapter Twenty-One](core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-one-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation), which decides any anti-constitutional-misconduct designation;
- the consequences of that conduct to [Chapter Seventeen](core_17.000_standing_integration.md#chapter-seventeen-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows), which applies them;
- substantive non-regression to [Chapter Twenty-Eight](core_28.000_non_regression.md#chapter-twenty-eight-non-regression-and-substantive-amendment-validity), which states Substantive Test 1 (procedural steps cannot save a change that fails it, **section 4**); and
- custody, editions, and operative effect to [Chapter Thirty-One](core_31.000_incorporation.md#chapter-thirty-one-incorporation-bridge).

When amendment or adoption conduct shows serious bad faith, this chapter opens a referral record and sends it on (**section 10**). This chapter does not label, rank, or sanction the conduct itself.

<br>

*In plain terms: when a change is declared invalid, treat it as void for constitutional purposes from that determination forward, spell out what must be rolled back and how **affected** **parties** get a fair remedy — and publish a time-bounded plan that restores protections and preserves evidence. Serious bad-faith conduct in making or adopting a change is referred to the bodies that own labels and sanctions, not relabeled here.*

Where a change is found invalid, including by the **Constitutional Forum**'s determination under [**Chapter Twenty-Eight §3**](core_28.000_non_regression.md#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*), it is treated as non-operative for constitutional supremacy purposes from the point of determination. Decision records must identify scope, affected acts, remediation obligations, and required restoration steps for materially affected parties.

On invalidity determination, adopters must publish a time-bounded remediation plan that:
- restores controlling prior protections or stricter lawful protections
- preserves evidence under [Evidence Preservation](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#evidence-preservation) and audit trails
- provides contestable remedy pathways proportionate to impact and dependency

<a id="serious-misconduct-referral"></a>
**Serious-misconduct referral.** Where amendment or adoption conduct indicates serious misconduct, a mandatory referral record must be opened. **Serious misconduct** includes the following (read with [**Chapter Twenty-One**, **section 4** (*Criteria set*)](core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md#4-criteria-set-for-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation--accountability-at-constitutional-scale), **criteria 1** **through** **4** (*Willful deceptive intent*; *Knowing falsification or suppression*; *Material rollback effect*; and *Causally significant conduct*) on **change** or **record** pathways):
- willful deceptive intent;
- knowing falsification or suppression;
- material rollback effect; and
- causally significant obstruction or degradation.

**Forcible** or **nullification** misconduct routes through **criteria 5** and **6** (*Insurrection or material forcible subversion* and *Operative nullification*) of the same section where material. Those traits remain character descriptors and anti-constitutional-misconduct designation (ACMD) predicates; they do not move the numeric Violation Axis slot. The Standing Record must be transmitted to the canonical classification authorities; **this chapter does not itself classify**. **General contribution and violation measurement** (Contribution Axis and Violation Axis), including numeric slot assignment on the shared five-times LEQU bands from verified impact and separate axis records, is owned by the [**Chapter Sixteen §7 unified scale**](core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#7-unified-proportional-lequ-scale--contribution-and-violation-axes) in [core_15.000_standing_assessment.md](core_15.000_standing_assessment.md). [**Chapter Seventeen**](core_17.000_standing_integration.md#chapter-seventeen-standing-integration--question-3-what-follows) owns standing effects and integration without moving either slot. For a final Chapter Sixteen **Violation Axis slot 7, 8, or 9** impact slot, **Chapter Twenty-One** (*Anti-Constitutional Misconduct*) in [core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md](core_21.000_misconduct_designation.md#chapter-twenty-one-anti-constitutional-misconduct-designation) may add the corresponding **ACMD**, subject to its criteria, due-process safeguards, and cross-chapter discipline, as referenced under [Chapter Sixteen §5 Slot Grammar and Shared Scaling](core_16.000_standing_measurement.md#5-slot-grammar-and-shared-scaling) and the [Chapter Seventeen §3 gateway](core_17.000_standing_integration.md#3-automatic-integration-review-and-continuity). This chapter issues **referral and review triggers only**; it does not assign or move a numeric slot or attach, narrow, or expand canonical designations.

#### 11.1 Deliberate Adoption
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11](#11-ratification-and-adoption) ratification frame.
- Downstream: [§11.2](#112-adoption-document) document requirements; [§11.3](#113-joining-by-additional-parties) joining rules.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Seven §2](core_27.000_governance.md#2-authorization-and-legitimacy-of-governing-authority) (27.000 §2) documented legitimacy mechanism alongside deliberate adoption; [Chapter Thirty-One §4 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_31.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority) (31.000 §4) (*who may adopt* — a steward bound by valid adoption is not themselves a qualifying adopter).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: binding adoption has to be explicit, recorded, and auditable to the scale of the system — quiet or partial "we kind of follow it" does not let an operator claim constitutional protection while dodging obligations, and a steward's own-conduct note is not adoption.*

This Constitution binds a steward, organization, federation of bodies, or jurisdiction only when adoption is deliberate, documented, and auditable. Auditability must meet a standard proportionate to system class, material impact, and dependency under **[CS-9.305](corpus_systems/cs_09.305_system_classification_machinery.md#cs-9305-part-a-system-classification-machinery) (*System classification machinery*) and [CS-9.310 — System Impact Classifications](corpus_systems/cs_09.310_system_impact_classifications.md#cs-9310-part-b-system-impact-classifications)**. Informal, partial, or undisclosed adoption is insufficient to claim constitutional protections as a shield while denying corresponding obligations.

A steward is bound when a qualifying adopter records a valid adoption document under this section and [Chapter Thirty-One §4 Adoption Framing and Scope of Authority](core_31.000_incorporation.md#4-adoption-framing-and-scope-of-authority); listing a steward as a party bound by adoption does not make that steward themselves a qualifying adopter.

When the adopter is a jurisdiction or polity, the recorded mechanism must include a published affected-sentient participation check — contested election, sortition, federated ratification, or another [Chapter Twenty-Seven §2.2 Eligibility, Contested Selection, and Democratic Minimums](core_27.000_governance.md#22-eligibility-contested-selection-and-democratic-minimums)-compliant path. Silent board resolution, undisclosed executive document, or "we kind of follow it" practice is not enough for a polity to claim constitutional adoption. Organizations, system operators, and system-developer consortia remain able to adopt through the existing deliberate, documented, and auditable path without that polity participation check.

#### 11.2 Adoption Document
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11.1](#111-deliberate-adoption) deliberate adoption requirement.
- Downstream: [§6](#6-test-3--authority-chain-and-custody-validity) Procedural Test 3 chain checks against document contents.

<br>

*In plain terms: keep a clear paper trail — who adopted, what scope, when it starts, who holds the master copy of the corpus — so nobody can launder authority through ambiguous version drift.*

Adopters must maintain an identifiable adoption document. Examples include **joining** by additional parties (with recorded assent), treaty or compact ratification, corporate or intergovernmental resolution, federated registry entry, or equivalent. The adoption document must state the adopting entity, scope of adoption, and effective date. It must:

- identify the custodian of the authoritative edition of this Constitution and its integrated implementation files.
- be consistent with document-control expectations in [README.md](README.md) and [Chapter Five *Corpus*](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#corpus) (authoritative corpus, edition identifier, custody chain).
- be consistent with **Corpus** in Chapter Five ([core_05.000_definitions_home.md](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#corpus)).
- where [Article I-D](core_06.000_rights_part_a.md#article-i-d-existential-risk-and-ecological-recovery-capacity) (*Existential Risk and Ecological Recovery Capacity*) is in adoption scope, name the reduction, cap, or interruption measures required by that Article in the adoption document or in incorporated implementation it pins — not a core-chosen numeric target.
- point to the adopter's published [Adoption Transition Record](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-record) and, once it is published, its [Adoption Charter](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-charter), so a reader can find them ([§11.4 Adoption Transition Record and Adoption Charter](#114-adoption-transition-record-and-adoption-charter)).

That participation must be consistent with **Article XII-B** (*Weighted Participation and Anti-Token Constraints*) where participation weight applies.

#### 11.4 Adoption Transition Record and Adoption Charter
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§11.2](#112-adoption-document) adoption document.
- Downstream: [Article XXIX-A](core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) (the Adoption Transition Record's contents and the final gate); [Chapter Twenty §2.4 Gatekeeper Checks Across Adopters and Levels of Trust](core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#24-gatekeeper-checks-across-adopters-and-levels-of-trust).
- Read with: [Adoption Transition Record](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-record) and [Adoption Charter](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-charter).

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: the adoption document records that an adopter adopted. Two more published documents record how it goes: the Adoption Transition Record while the adopter moves out of legacy operation, and the Adoption Charter once it is settled. The Adoption Charter is where other adopters find the office to ask about Standing Records and learn how far this adopter trusts them.*

Besides the adoption document, an adopter keeps and publishes two documents:

- **Adoption Transition Record:** From the day the adopting document takes effect until the steady-state phase begins, the adopter keeps and publishes an [Adoption Transition Record](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-transition-record) with the contents [Article XXIX-A](core_06.040_rights_part_e.md#article-xxix-a-phased-adoption-and-rights-floor-continuity) (*Phased Adoption and Rights-Floor Continuity*) requires. It carries, marked provisional, the forum authority assignment, the record custodian and the office-level contact for gatekeeper checks, and the levels of trust the adopter extends. An adopter that extends any level of trust above the public level must have at least a provisional forum authority assignment.
- **Adoption Charter:** When the adopter passes the final gate into the steady-state phase, it publishes an [Adoption Charter](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#adoption-charter) that confirms or replaces the provisional entries, and the Adoption Transition Record is closed and archived. The steady-state phase does not begin until the Adoption Charter is published.
  - The Adoption Charter states the forum authority assignment; the record custodian for Standing Records and the office-level contact for gatekeeper checks; the level of trust extended to each other adopter, with its reasons, the technical advice it rests on, and any reasons for departing from that advice; standards agreed with other adopters; and a [Charter Pointer](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#charter-pointer) to each [Charter Role Map](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) that applies to the adopter's systems and institutions.
  - The office-level contact for gatekeeper checks is a routing contact. It either holds the Standing Record or tells the gatekeeper which record custodian does, using the [Charter Role Maps](core_05.040_band_continuity.md#charter-role-map) that apply and, where a map names no custodian for stakeholder Standing Records, the Backup Record Custodian ([CF-23.415 §11 Backup Record Custodian](corpus_forum/cf_23.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-23415-11-backup-record-custodian); [Chapter Seven §5.6.2 Stakeholder Standing Records](core_07.000_charters.md#562-stakeholder-standing-records)). Its answer times are set by the check-answer window in [Chapter Twenty §2.1 Anti-Aggregation of Gated-Pathway Effects](core_20.000_final_effect_restoration_enforcement.md#21-anti-aggregation-of-gated-pathway-effects).
  - It is published as [Public Oversight Baseline Disclosure](core_05.050_band_oversight.md#public-oversight-baseline-disclosure) and names offices and roles only. A named sentient's address, phone number, or other contact details are never published in it.
- **Changes:** A change to either document takes effect as a new dated version, published, and the earlier versions stay available.
  - **An unplanned vacancy:** If the record custodian or the office-level contact becomes vacant or cannot act, that is a seat vacancy under [Chapter Eight §6.3 A Seat the Map Leaves Unplaced, or a Holder Who Cannot Serve](core_08.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#63-general-seat-vacancy-rule). The adopter publishes a new dated version naming the replacement within its [Class Repair Window](core_05.035_band_accountability.md#class-repair-window), and meanwhile the Backup Record Custodian ([CF-23.415 §11 Backup Record Custodian](corpus_forum/cf_23.415_routing_operations_transfer_certification_representative_treatment.md#cf-23415-11-backup-record-custodian)) answers gatekeeper checks for that adopter.
- **Review:** Once the Adoption Charter is published, the adopter confirms it, with its record custodian and office-level contact, at least once in each **Adoption Charter review interval**, which is twelve months, and publishes the confirmation as a new dated version.
- **What neither document is:** The Adoption Transition Record and the Adoption Charter are not the adoption document, are not evidence of certification, and do not by themselves show that an Adoption Transition is complete.

#### 12.3 Proposal, Hearing, and Comment Window
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

- Upstream: [§2](#2-heightened-review-for-rights-affecting-changes) heightened review; [§12.1](#121-notice-and-contest) notice and contest; [Chapter One §17.7 Duty to Represent Stakeholder Interests](core_01.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#177-duty-to-represent-stakeholder-interests) (stewards' duty to those the system affects); [Chapter Twenty-Eight §3](core_28.000_non_regression.md#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (28.000 §3) Substantive Test 1 and who decides it.
- Downstream: [§9](#9-provisional-effect-rule) provisional effect; [§12.2](#122-recorded-effectiveness) recorded effectiveness.
- Read with: [Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.1](core_27.000_governance.md#51-entitlement-and-eligibility) (27.000 §5.1) and [§5.2](core_27.000_governance.md#52-records-gates-and-method-neutrality) (27.000 §5.2) electorate and records; [Chapter Twenty-Four §4](core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline) (24.000 §4) Tier C outer bound; [CJS-0.105 §4 Time-Window Names](corpus_joint_structure/cjs_00.105_registry_and_reading_rules.md#cjs-0105-4-time-window-names); [Article XIII-B](core_06.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*) for protected petition and public comment on the Forum Case Record.

</details>

<br>

*In plain terms: anyone can put a change to this Constitution on the Forum Case Record. To get the Constitutional Forum to take it up, it needs backing: at least 1% of the sentients entitled to vote sign on, a majority of the members of any forum votes for it, a majority of the stewards of a Class A, B, or C system or institution does, or the governing authority puts it forward. Everyone then gets at least 30 days (the Tier C outer bound) after the last change to the wording to respond, and any edit to the wording restarts that window. Only then does the forum rule on whether the change weakens a protection, or is a correction of a real error in it.*

This subsection sets out how a proposed change to the text of this Constitution is put forward, reaches the **Constitutional** forum, and is open to public response:

- **Anyone may propose:** Any sentient, group, or body may put a proposed change on the Forum Case Record. The proposal is published on the Forum Case Record with its exact wording and the justification required by **section 3**. Every later change to that wording is recorded with its date.
- **Hearing gate:** The **Constitutional Forum** takes up a proposal for determination when it has any one of these:
  - sign-off by at least 1% of the electorate for governance voting under [Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.1 Entitlement and Eligibility](core_27.000_governance.md#51-entitlement-and-eligibility), counted for the community that adopted the text the change would alter;
  - a vote in favor by a majority of the members then seated of any forum, taken in a recorded decision of that forum;
  - a recorded decision in favor by a majority of the stewards then holding the role in one Class A, Class B, or Class C system or institution, made under [Chapter One §17.7 Duty to Represent Stakeholder Interests](core_01.040_stewardship_capacity_principles.md#177-duty-to-represent-stakeholder-interests); or
  - the governing authority of the community that adopted the text puts the change forward, which **section 2** already requires to be heard.
- **How sign-off is counted:** Sign-off is counted from the electorate record published under [Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.2 Records, Gates, and Method Neutrality](core_27.000_governance.md#52-records-gates-and-method-neutrality), by a party independent of the proposers.
  - Each distinct subject signs once, as with one subject, one vote under **Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.1** (*Entitlement and Eligibility*).
  - Creating sentients, or inducing branches to elect to be distinct, in order to reach the share is non-compliant conduct by the creator.
  - Signing and responding are open petition and public comment on the Forum Case Record, and are protected as such under **Article XIII-B** (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*).
- **How a forum or steward majority is counted:** The vote or decision is recorded on the Forum Case Record, with dissent recorded.
  - Each distinct subject counts once toward a majority, as with one subject, one vote under **Chapter Twenty-Seven §5.1** (*Entitlement and Eligibility*).
  - Creating, appointing, or seating members or stewards in order to form a majority is non-compliant conduct by whoever does it.
  - A **Constitutional Forum** panel that voted to bring a proposal to hearing does not decide it. The determination is made by a panel with no overlapping decision-makers, drawn from the constitutional-review reserve roster maintained under **CF-23.470** (*Forum staffing, reserve capacity, shared administration, structural review, and structural records*).
- **Amendment comment window:** Any sentient may respond to a proposal on the Forum Case Record. The window for responses is no shorter than **30 days**, the Tier C outer bound in [Chapter Twenty-Four §4 Timely Resolution, Materiality Tiers, and Anti-Delay Discipline](core_24.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#4-timely-resolution-materiality-tiers-and-anti-delay-discipline), counted from the last recorded change to the proposal's wording.
  - Any later change to the wording, including one made in response to comments, is a recorded change and restarts the count from its date.
  - Sign-off may be gathered while the window runs. The forum may not decide until the gate is met and the window has run.
  - The forum may extend the window. It may not shorten it.
- **Determination:** When the gate is met and the window has run, the **Constitutional Forum** decides whether the proposal fails Substantive Test 1 under [Chapter Twenty-Eight §3](core_28.000_non_regression.md#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*) and publishes its determination with reasons under **Article XXVI-A** (*Bounded Interpretive Mandate and Public Reasons*). The reasons must say how the responses filed were treated. Review of the determination follows **Article XXVI-B** (*Challenge Rights and Independent Review*).
- **Corrections:** A proposer may mark a proposal as a correction of a protection. The justification required by **section 3** must then show the error in the form that [Chapter Twenty-Eight §3](core_28.000_non_regression.md#3-test-1--substantive-non-regression-validity) (*Substantive Test 1 — Substantive Non-Regression Validity*) requires. The hearing gate and the amendment comment window are the same as for any other proposal. The determination states whether the proposal is found to be a correction and which findings support that.
- **Interim measures:** This subsection does not change **section 9**. Narrow interim measures to prevent imminent material harm remain available on the terms stated there.

### 13. Owner Non-Relocation
<details>
<summary><strong><span style="color: #2563eb;">Trace</span></strong></summary>

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Nine owns how amendments are published, chained, reviewed, and ratified — not numeric Contribution or Violation slots under the Chapter Sixteen §7 (*Unified Proportional LEQU Scale — Contribution and Violation Axes*) unified scale or Chapter Twenty-One anti-constitutional-misconduct designations; escalation wording here is routing, not a second accountability-disposition code.*

Under [Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#owner-non-relocation), this chapter must not absorb, restate, or relocate obligations owned by another constitutional owner layer. Any review-trigger, escalation-trigger, or referral-trigger language in this chapter — including mandatory escalation language in **section 8** (*Review Triggers*) and in **section 10** (*Invalid-Change Handling and Remediation Continuity*) — operates as a **trigger or referral heuristic** for routing to canonical owners and is **non-canonical** for final offense classification.

Cross-layer drift that imports classification substance, sanction mechanics, or adjudicative workflow from another owner layer into **Chapter Twenty-Nine** — whether as direct text, as reader-guidance prose elevated to operative effect, or as inference from review-trigger language — is **non-compliant** under this section.

Future additions that touch offense-flavored or sanction-flavored language must include an explicit non-relocation pointer to the canonical owner home in the [Preamble — constitutional owner register](core_00.000_preamble.md#4-principles-definitions-and-rights), read with [Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy](core_05.045_band_integrative.md#owner-non-relocation).
