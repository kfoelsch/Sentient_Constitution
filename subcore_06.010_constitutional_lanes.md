Subcore 6-A is the constitutional owner of the **constitutional lanes**: the required functional separations every institution must publish, and how a lane map is assessed. Chapter Six states the four-seat requirement for each materially binding act; this file states the lanes those seats are hosted in.

*In plain terms: a constitutional lane is a published functional separation with constitutionally binding duties. Splitting the lanes is also the Constitution's **separation of duties** rule: the office that asks for something, the office that checks it, the office that records it, and the office that hears a challenge to it are different seats.*

---

### 1. Constitutional Lanes and Functional Separation

#### 1.1 Required Lanes

A constitutional lane is a published functional separation with constitutionally binding duties. The required lanes are:

- direction and policy;
- execution;
- challenge and review;
- assurance and audit; and
- publication and evidence work.

One institution may host more than one lane only when independence safeguards are documented, auditable, and contestable.

#### 1.2 Separation of Duties on Each Act

**Separation of duties** is the transaction-level form of the same rule, stated in [Chapter Six §3 Four-Seat Constitutional Requirement](core_06.000_functional_independence_separation_of_duties.md#3-four-seat-constitutional-requirement), which this file cannot narrow: for any single [materially binding act](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#materially-binding-act) or record, the request, the verification, the entry and custody, and the contest are held by different seats, and no seat both verifies and enters, or both verifies and hears the contest.

The [Subcore 10-B §7.1 Separation of Duties](subcore_10.020_standing_assessment.md#71-separation-of-duties) Standing-Record lifecycle (request → verify → enter and hold → contest) is one duty set every lane map must place; it is the reference instance of this rule, and the lane map is the fallback when a [System Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#system-charter) or [Institutional Charter](subcore_03.075_band_continuity.md#institutional-charter) is silent on record custody.

#### 1.3 Seats a Lane Hosts

The seat types a lane hosts — the four core seats plus the containment, participation-terms, release-control, and direction seats — and what each may and may not do are fixed in [CI-21.205 §6](corpus_institutions/ci_21.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-21205-6-seat-catalog--process-role-archetypes-and-operational-boundaries) (*Seat catalog*); a lane map places seats, it does not redefine them.

#### 1.4 Assessing a Lane Map

Reviewers assess each lane's published function, host institution, binding duties, independence safeguards, accountable roles, and relationship to any system-class or stewardship burden under [Subcore 8-A](subcore_08.010_system_classification.md#subcore-8-a-system-classification) (*System Classification*), including the steward levels. Institution-specific authority custody, non-delegable duties, and local delegation controls remain with **CI-6.205** (*Institutional design, separation of powers, and authority custody*).

#### 1.5 When a Lane Fails

A lane fails if it is undefined, merged, or staffed in a way that defeats required separation, auditability, challenge, succession, or binding role accountability. A lane map fails if it does not place the Standing-Record request / verify / enter-and-hold / contest seats, or places verification and entry, or verification and contest, in one seat, even under a published merged-hosting safeguard.

### 2. Why Institutions Keep Separate Lanes

*In plain terms: if one office sets policy, carries it out, checks it, and publishes the results, no one else can tell when it goes wrong. Separate lanes keep those jobs apart.*

Chapter Six keeps the four seats apart on each single act. The lanes do the same for an institution as a whole: direction, execution, challenge, assurance, and publication sit in different places, so that a failure in one is visible to the others. Where one institution must host more than one lane, the safeguards have to be published and open to challenge.
