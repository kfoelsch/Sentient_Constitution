Chapter Three, Accountability Tetrad leg, is the constitutional owner of the **Accountability** Tetrad-leg head definition (O/M/A/C) and hierarchy map. Leaf definitions are in the [Accountability band](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md).

*In plain terms: hold actors answerable — attribution, records, and real recourse have to work, not just ceremonies on paper.*

<a id="accountability"></a>

- **What it is**
  - Hold actors answerable — the Tetrad leg this entry names.
  - **In scope:** Material decisions, failures, and behaviors can be attributed to identifiable actors, roles, or system components, who can be answered for through review, [redress](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation), or correction proportional to impact.
  - **Depends on:** [Contestability](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#contestability), [Auditability](subcore_03.085_band_oversight.md#auditability), [Attributable Action](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#attributable-action), [Attribution Integrity](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#attribution-integrity), and [Redress and Remediation](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#redress-and-remediation) — the conditions and channels this leg needs in order to work. Each term's detailed scope and how-to-measure rules live on its own definition; start from [Tetrad Leg Decomposition](#accountability-tetrad-leg-decomposition).
  - **Out of scope:** the leaf-definition details, measurement layers, and implementation mechanics owned by leaf entries and CJS.
<a id="accountability-m"></a>
<a id="accountability-a"></a>
- **How to measure and assess**
  - **Primary measure:** How to tell whether answerability is real in practice. This line only points to the measures; the how-to-measure rules sit on each linked definition. Topic map: [Tetrad Leg Decomposition](#accountability-tetrad-leg-decomposition).
  - **Primary assessment:** Judge under the [Constitutional Tetrad](core_00.000_preamble.md#constitutional-tetrad), scaled to [material stake](core_00.000_preamble.md#material-stake). Ask whether accountability is real — attribution chains, records, and functional recourse work under normal, degraded, and adversarial conditions — not just ceremony.
<a id="accountability-c"></a>
- **What must hold**
  - **Primary failure:** Non-compliant when:
    - material harm is unattributable;
    - accountability pathways are non-functional or illusory where accountability is materially required;
    - labels or ceremonies that do not actually change the governed behavior;
    - attribution exists in form while affected parties lack functional [Contestability](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#contestability) to invoke review, redress, or correction;
    - verified duties are extinguished by receivership, restructuring, asset transfer, dissolution, or comparable formal-structure change alone; or
    - successor or estate arrangements leave material obligations unassigned or practically unenforceable where proportionate transfer or satisfaction was feasible.
