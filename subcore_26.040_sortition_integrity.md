### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 26-D is the constitutional owner of the **standard detail of sortition integrity**: the bodies sortition may serve and the capture routes it guards against, the full list of integrity rules a sortition mechanism must publish, and the operational safeguards that implementation text must cover. [Chapter Twenty-Six §3.1.2 Sortition Integrity and Anti-Capture Use](core_26.000_governance.md#312-sortition-integrity-and-anti-capture-use) states the guarantees: sortition may be used against capture, Independent Bodies must use it, a mechanism must publish its integrity rules, private contact with selected members is barred, and a lottery may not be used as legitimacy theater.

*In plain terms: Chapter Twenty-Six says when sortition may be used and that it must be fair and open. This file lists exactly what a fair, open lottery must publish and protect.*

### 2. Why Sortition Rules Are the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: a lottery is only trusted if no one can rig who is drawn or lean on those drawn, and that has to hold everywhere.*

Sortition protects against capture only while the draw is verifiable and the members it selects are free from pressure. If each adopter decided for itself which rules to publish, the first ones dropped would be the ones that expose a rigged draw or private lobbying. One list, the same for every adopter, lets anyone check a lottery against the same standard.

<a id="3-anti-capture-uses"></a>

### 3. Anti-Capture Uses

*In plain terms: where a lottery can replace elections or appointments that money and favors could buy.*

Sortition may be used as an anti-corruption and anti-capture mechanism for specified governing, oversight, review, agenda-setting, procurement, audit, or constitutional-deliberation bodies where random selection materially reduces predictable capture through:

- campaign finance;
- incumbency dependence;
- patronage;
- factional gatekeeping; or
- pre-selection bribery.

<a id="4-published-integrity-rules"></a>

### 4. Published Integrity Rules

*In plain terms: everything about how the lottery works, and how to challenge it, must be public.*

A sortition mechanism that exercises or materially shapes **governing authority** must publish:

- auditable selection rules;
- eligibility boundaries;
- exclusion grounds;
- replacement rules;
- rotation cadence;
- compensation and access supports;
- conflict controls;
- briefing and evidence rules;
- facilitator or staff independence controls;
- anti-lobbying and anti-intimidation safeguards, which apply the **Influence-contact boundaries** of [**Article XIII-B**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-b-influence-contact-and-lobbying-boundaries) (*Influence-Contact and Lobbying Boundaries*) at their strictest: private contact with selected members about the matter is barred except through the open briefing record; and
- contest routes for:
  - selection failure;
  - manipulation;
  - capture; or
  - procedural distortion.

<a id="5-operational-safeguards"></a>

### 5. Operational Safeguards

*In plain terms: the day-to-day protections that keep a lottery honest.*

Operational safeguards for sortition integrity belong in designated implementation text and **[CI-6.210](corpus_institutions/ci_06.210_conflict_integrity_anti_capture_anti_corruption.md#ci-6210-conflict-integrity-anti-capture-and-anti-corruption)** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*) anti-capture and anti-corruption controls. Those safeguards include:

- randomization verification;
- conflict screening;
- staffing separation;
- disclosure;
- protected escalation; and
- response to capture allegations.
