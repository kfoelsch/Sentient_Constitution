# Chapter 5 §3.11 — Standing State, Contribution, and Violation (Audience Guidance)

## What this cluster does

This cluster provides the vocabulary and rules for how the Constitution tracks and responds to what actors do — both the positive contributions they make and the violations they commit. It connects to [Chapter Six](../core_06-06_standing_classification.md), which handles the technical classification mechanics.

The cluster brings together six interconnected concepts:

1. **Contribution State** — tracking positive outcomes and baseline satisfaction
2. **Verified Violation Findings** — what counts as proven violations
3. **Violation Nature** — how violations are classified by severity and character
4. **Standing Cell** — the bounded record that holds status information
5. **Standing Effect** — the actual consequences applied from that record
6. **Participant Standing** — reputation-based eligibility for roles and access (distinct from Chapter Six mechanics)

*In plain terms:* When someone asks "how is this actor doing?" or "what happens to them now?" this cluster provides the rules for answering fairly, transparently, and without mixing up good behavior with bad behavior.

---

## What it protects

The cluster establishes several interconnected protections that cannot be separated:

1. **Separate tracking of good and bad** — contributions and violations stay on separate axes; one cannot cancel out the other
2. **Verified facts only** — adverse consequences require proof, not just allegations
3. **Bounded records** — status applies to specific subjects, scopes, time periods, and contexts
4. **Transparent consequences** — what happens must trace back to what was actually found
5. **Proportionate responses** — effects must match the nature of findings
6. **Contestability** — affected parties can challenge both the record and its effects

*In plain terms:* You cannot use "they did good things too" to erase violations, or "there were accusations" to impose consequences without proof. Status must be specific (about what, when, and who) and reversible when wrong.

---

## When this cluster applies

The cluster is triggered whenever decisions involve:

**Classification and tracking:**
- Determining contribution levels or baseline satisfaction
- Recording verified violations
- Classifying violation severity or type
- Assigning standing status to actors, institutions, or systems

**Consequences and effects:**
- Trust eligibility or role assignment
- Delegated authority decisions
- Recognition or restriction measures
- Restoration or remediation requirements
- Review or safeguard triggers

**Access and participation gating:**
- Eligibility for audits or oversight roles
- Access to funding challenges
- Participation pathway qualifications
- Reputation-based access controls

**Process requirements:**
- Forum routing decisions (which court or tribunal handles a matter)
- Remedy determinations
- Integration with Chapter Six mechanics

## What stays outside

**Individual definitions operating alone:** Outside the cluster's admission scope, individual Chapter Six hooks may be cited without importing the full cluster discipline.

**Chapter Seven assignment:** Final assignment of the most severe anti-constitutional misconduct (Violation Axis s = 7, 8, or 9) routes to [Chapter Seven](../core_07-07_misconduct.md), not through participant-standing criteria.

**Inherent dignity and baseline rights:** Standing effects cannot extinguish inherent dignity, equal moral standing, baseline rights, or access to challenge and remedy where the Constitution forbids it.

## The cluster members (in logical order)

### 1. Contribution State

**What it is:** Axis I tracks *positive-only* constitutional outcomes — baseline satisfaction and demonstrable improvement beyond baseline. It does **not** track adverse violation findings or non-compliance.

**Why it matters:** Separating positive contributions from violations prevents "netting" where good behavior cancels out bad behavior. Both matter; neither substitutes for the other.

**Key rules:**
- Only demonstrable facts under auditability and contestability count
- Must stay traceable to Chapter Six mechanics
- Cannot be used to relabel adverse violations as "negative contribution"

---

### 2. Verified Violation Findings

**What it is:** Violation Axis inputs that affect standing only when they rest on auditable, contestable findings satisfying Chapters Two through Four.

**Why it matters:** Prevents consequences based on rumors, allegations, or provisional labels. Only verified findings can support adverse standing effects.

**Key rules:**
- Allegations and intake labels are not verified findings
- Must identify finding authority, affected subject, scope, and evidence path
- Must stay separate from contribution state (no offsetting)

---

### 3. Violation Nature

**What it is:** Axis II classification of adverse constitutional outcomes from verified findings — severity, process/response character, and stackable supplements.

**Why it matters:** Not all violations are equal. Classification determines proportionate response and process requirements.

**Key rules:**
- Only classified from verified findings (not allegations)
- Must specify severity, applicable attachments, scope, and review status
- Distinct from contribution state (Axis I, positive-only)
- Top slots (s = 7, 8, 9) route to Chapter Seven

---

### 4. Standing Cell

**What it is:** The bounded operational record applying Chapter Six categories to a defined subject, functional scope, time window, review status, and decision context.

**Why it matters:** Prevents vague, merged, or timeless status labels. Status must be specific and bounded.

**Key rules:**
- Must state subject, scope, time window, review status
- Must keep Contribution Axis and Violation Axis inputs separate
- Must identify verification basis and contestability pathway
- Cannot substitute for dignity, baseline rights, or forum findings

---

### 5. Standing Effect

**What it is:** The consequence layer applying verified contribution state and verified violation findings to trust, roles, authority, recognition, restriction, restoration, and review.

**Why it matters:** Ensures consequences actually trace back to verified findings and follow Chapter Six rules.

**Key rules:**
- Must remain traceable to Chapter Six sections 2–3 and integration sections 4, 7–8
- Must keep axes separate (no netting, averaging, or substitution)
- Violation-linked constraints cannot be time-discounted or softened by contribution upside
- Must respect Article XXIII punishment constraints where applicable

---

### 6. Participant Standing

**What it is:** Reputation-, record-, or qualification-based status used to gate access to audits, roles, funding challenges, or participation pathways. Distinct from Chapter Six Axis I typing.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishes eligibility for specific roles from general dignity and standing. "Good standing" means eligibility for ordinary participation under valid, contestable criteria — it does not extinguish baseline rights.

**Key rules:**
- Cannot permanently exclude from survival-critical systems solely on standing
- Cannot deny audit or challenge access without proportionality and necessity justification
- Must ensure contestability, auditability, and transparency proportional to impact
- Top-end anti-constitutional misconduct routes to Chapter Seven, not participant-standing criteria

---

## Common non-compliant patterns

**Mixing axes:** Using contribution state to "net away," average, or substitute for verified violation findings. *Both matter separately; neither erases the other.*

**Unverified inputs:** Treating allegations, provisional labels, intake tags, or forum-phase narratives as verified findings for adverse standing effects. *Only verified findings count.*

**Relabeling violations:** Calling adverse violation findings "negative contribution" on Axis I to avoid proper violation classification. *Violations stay on Axis II.*

**Undifferentiated labels:** Using vague standing status without a bounded cell specifying subject, scope, time, and review status. *Status must be specific.*

**Opaque criteria:** Hiding axis inputs, evidence, calibration assumptions, version changes, or contestability pathways. *Everything must be auditable.*

**Overreach:** Applying standing effects that extinguish inherent dignity, equal moral standing, baseline rights, or access to challenge and remedy where the Constitution forbids it. *Some protections are unconditional.*

**Time discounting:** Applying recency weighting to soften unresolved violation findings while keeping contribution credit current. *Violations don't expire through delay.*
