# Constitution architecture primer

**Status:** Locked — `2026-04-30` (non-operative companion; substantive edits should be deliberate and traceable).

This document is a **readability companion** to the **Sentient Constitution**: the numbered `core_*.md` files (Chapters One through Fourteen) listed in [README.md](README.md).

It gives extended framing for readers and editors. **It is not a constitutional source** unless an adopting instrument explicitly says otherwise.

For binding meaning, read those core files as **one instrument**. Then read designated incorporated obligations in `corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, and `corpus_forum.md`, limited by whatever adoption and custody rules apply.

---

## 1. Extended framing context (non-operative)

### Starting point

The project rests on one premise: **sentient wellbeing cannot be separated** from the systems sentients build, live in, and rely on.

As systems grow more interdependent and more powerful, risk grows too—in scope and in stakes. That includes cumulative harm that may be hard or impossible to undo. The constitution treats those risks as **real and material**, not as remote hypotheticals.

### Constitutional response

The response is an **enforceable alignment framework**:

- Systems stay aligned with survival, dignity, and long-horizon wellbeing.
- Governance duties scale with impact, dependency, and risk.
- Where effects are material, **epistemic integrity**, **contestability**, and **accountability** are required.

### Two layers of governance

The architecture distinguishes:

| Layer | What it covers |
|--------|----------------|
| **Constitutional Contract Layer** | Authorization of governing authority itself—who may rule, and under what legitimacy constraints. |
| **Stakeholder System Participation** | How materially affected stakeholders participate, are represented, contest decisions, and receive due process **inside** systems or domains that are already authorized. |

This section explains intent only. **Binding effect** stays in the constitutional text and in incorporated obligations the text designates.

## 2. Authority and reading order (non-operative aid)

When you interpret obligations, use this **order**:

1. **Core instrument.** All numbered Sentient Constitution `core_*.md` files from [README.md](README.md) (`core_00-01_principles.md` through `core_14-14_incorporation.md`), read **together as one** instrument.
2. **Incorporated implementation.** Designated obligations in `corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, and `corpus_forum.md`.
3. **Support only.** Architecture, process, and evidence artifacts **unless** an adopting instrument elevates them.

### When several companion files touch the same facts

**When this applies.** Use this subsection when **two or more** step-2 companions—`corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, and `corpus_forum.md`—are **all materially in play for the same situation**. Here “same situation” means the **same structural facts**: the same institution, the same Chapter Eight court family, the same classified system or stewardship chain, or another object of duty that **more than one** companion reaches. If only **one** companion is material, read that owner in the normal way; you do **not** need the joint spine for that pass.

**What to open.** **`corpus_joint_structure.md`** is the **integration spine** for those overlaps.

- **CJS-2** states how to read companions **as a set**: **default read order**, **intentional overlap** (who owns substance versus who states joint glue only), and **stricter-wins**. **Governing standard:** integrated constitutional meaning comes first—the interpretation that **best preserves the Constitution's overall meaning and spirit** read **as one instrument** (including **fullest protective effect as an integrated whole** and alignment with sentient wellbeing, and **not** **maximal restriction** or **abstract strictness** in isolation), per **Chapter One** (*Interpretive Constraints* and **Canonical conflict resolution procedure**). **Within that frame**, where two companions set **different** stringency for the **same** material risk among **coherent**, **adoption-traceable** alternatives, the **stricter clearly adopted** rule applies as **Chapter One** and **Chapter Fourteen** define it—not as a license to pick the harshest reading detached from the integrated instrument (see **`corpus_joint_structure.md` CJS-2** and **Chapter Fourteen**).
- **CJS-2.2** is the **topic router** (stable row IDs such as **CJS-R01** …): each row names a **primary owner** and a **mandatory read-with** list for topics that cross companions.
- **CJS-3** adds **joint structural** obligations on top of domain owners—for example, blocking a claim of compliance that satisfies **one** companion while a **material** duty in **another** companion stays **unmet** for the **same** outcome.

**Incorporation.** **CJS-2**, **CJS-2.2**, and **CJS-3** apply **only** where **`corpus_joint_structure.md`** is **incorporated** under valid adoption and custody (see **Chapter Fourteen** in `core_14-14_incorporation.md`).

### Conflicts across layers

If the **numbered core** text and **incorporated** companion text **read as if they disagree**, **Sentient Constitution** meaning **controls**. Incorporated implementation must **meet or exceed** that floor: it may add detail, procedure, or **stricter** tests, but must **not** treat companion language as license to **undercut** constitutional protective **floors** set by the core instrument. Where **two** companions still **conflict** on the **same** facts **after** the core-integrated read, resolve the overlap through **`corpus_joint_structure.md` CJS-2** (including companion **stricter-wins** and **CJS-3** where applicable) **under the same Chapter One interpretive frame**—preserving overall meaning and spirit and **not** by cherry-picking between annexes or preferring **abstract strictness**.

## 3. Chapter Fourteen walkthrough (non-operative)

**Chapter Fourteen** (`core_14-14_incorporation.md`) is deliberately **narrow**. It is the **constitutional incorporation bridge**.

It says:

- which companion files count as binding implementation text;
- how adoption and custody make that text operative;
- that edits after adoption do **not** silently change constitutional effect.

It is **not** a second copy of the full implementation corpus. The chapter has three numbered sections:

### 3.1 Role (Chapter Fourteen, section 1 in `core_14-14_incorporation.md`)

This section states **what** is incorporated within adoption scope: `corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, and `corpus_forum.md`. It states **how** adoption makes that bundle operative, and **that** companion-file edits do not silently change legal effect.

Optional mergers of companions into fewer Markdown files are **packaging only** while **Corpus** listings in Chapter Five still identify those texts as companion implementation. See **Chapter Five** in `core_05-05_definitions_a_independent.md` and the sibling Part B/C definition files.

### 3.2 Authority stack and invocation (Chapter Fourteen, section 2)

This section summarizes the ladder: **binding constitutional source → binding incorporated source → non-binding process/maps** unless adopted.

Read it together with the **Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy** cluster in Chapter Five. The stack tells you **which layer is speaking**; internal hierarchy resolves **residual** conflict after an integrated read.

Cross-references in the Sentient Constitution to **Chapter Fourteen** or to incorporated companions invoke the matching implementation obligations **where they apply** and **within adoption scope**.

**Conflict handling (high level):** Sentient Constitution supremacy applies first, along with **Chapter Eleven** non-regression and **Chapter Twelve** internal hierarchy. If edition or custody is unclear, the **stricter clearly adopted baseline** applies—**as defined in Chapter One** (*Canonical conflict resolution procedure*, incorporation layer), i.e. **custody discipline** and **anti-drift** tied to what the adoption chain **clearly supports**, **not** substantive ambiguity resolution by preference for **abstract strictness**—until **Chapters Eleven and Thirteen** restore a clean validity picture.

### 3.3 Custody, editions, and operative effect (Chapter Fourteen, section 3)

Edition identifiers and effective dates must be fixed in the adoption chain. Adopters keep an **auditable custody chain**.

Post-adoption edits to companion files have **no operative constitutional effect** unless they are readopted under **Chapter Thirteen**. Architecture notes, TODO lists, regression logs, and evidence files may **support verification**; they do **not**, by themselves, change constitutional effect.

## 5. Chapters Eleven through Thirteen walkthrough (non-operative)

### How this relates to Chapter Nine

**Chapter Nine** states foundational **rights** (Articles **I–XXV**). **Article XXV** covers transition and re-baselining. Those articles are spread across **`core_09-09_rights_part_a.md` through `core_09-09_rights_part_d.md`**, in a **planet-first** order grouped as **Parts A–D**. Section **6** of this primer (and the opening of Chapter Nine in `core_09-09_rights_part_a.md`) spell out presentation order.

**Chapters Eleven through Thirteen** are **not** the rights chapter. They cover **non-regression**, **supremacy and external legal orders**, and **amendment / procedural validity**. Do not merge those topics mentally with Chapter Nine.

### What each chapter owns

- **Chapter Eleven — Non-regression.** The substantive home for the non-regression floor, **Test 1** (substantive non-regression invalidity), anti-evasion, regressive-deception **referral** triggers, and **scope** limits. It is **not** where you find day-to-day adjudication logic, canonical **Violation Axis** slots **s = 7, 8, or 9** for anti-constitutional misconduct (**Chapter Seven**), or the general compliance model (**Chapter Six**).

- **Chapter Twelve — Expansion, supremacy, external law.** **Additive** expansion of protection; **continuity** of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment; **supremacy relative to other binding norms** (linked to the Chapter Five Authority Stack / Internal Hierarchy cluster, stricter external law, conflict disclosure); and **disputes that involve external legal orders**.

- **Chapter Thirteen — Ratification, adoption, procedure.** **Ratification and adoption** instruments; **Tests 2–4** (procedural validity, custody, contestability); review triggers; provisional effect; **invalid-change handling**; and **amendment procedure** requirements.

### Read Eleven–Thirteen as one checklist

1. **Non-regression floor (Ch 11):** Amendments cannot materially weaken foundational constraints, integrity requirements, or Chapter Nine Rights Floors.

2. **Procedural and custody validity (Ch 13):** Amendment attempts fail if publication, authority-chain / custody, or contestability / review requirements are not met.

3. **Supremacy and external interface (Ch 12):** Internal hierarchy and stricter external protections apply, subject to Chapter Eleven.

4. **Review triggers and provisional effect (Ch 13):** Rights-adjacent and authority-adjacent changes get heightened scrutiny; irreversible effect is gated.

5. **Invalid-change continuity (Ch 13):** Invalid changes are non-operative for constitutional supremacy and must trigger restoration, remediation publication, evidence preservation, and contestable remedy paths.

6. **Layer boundaries:** Chapters Eleven and Thirteen may impose immediate validity protection and referral controls. They do **not** replace **Chapter Six** (Axes I and II) or **Chapter Seven** (final **s = 7, 8, or 9** labels) as the **canonical** place for Axis I/II contribution inputs and top-end anti-constitutional misconduct classification.

### Shortcuts for practical reading

These three moves keep Eleven–Thirteen work from drifting into the wrong chapter:

- **Evasion-shaped changes → Chapter Eleven first.** When something **narrows** rights or accountability—relabeling, metadata edits, procedural shortcuts, emergency framing, or similar—treat **Chapter Eleven** as the first stop for anti-evasion and invalidity logic, not as an afterthought.

- **Uncertain validity → preserve the status quo.** Until review finishes, keep prior protections in force and maintain custody-related evidence; do not treat a doubtful amendment as already operative.

- **Validity language ≠ offense slots.** Chapters Eleven through Thirteen govern regression, supremacy, ratification, and procedure. Their tiers and validity wording are **not** a shortcut to final misconduct labeling. For **s = 7, 8, or 9**, use **Chapter Seven** and the general **Chapter Six** framework (contribution, violation, standing)—not inference from Eleven–Thirteen alone.
