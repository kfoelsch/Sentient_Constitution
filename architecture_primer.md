# Constitution architecture primer

This document is a readability companion to the **Sentient Constitution** — the numbered `core_*.md` files (Chapters One through Fourteen) enumerated in [README.md](README.md).

It provides extended explanatory framing for readers and editors, but it is not a constitutional source unless explicitly incorporated by a valid adopting instrument.

For authoritative constitutional meaning, read those files together as one instrument, then designated incorporated obligations in `corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, and `corpus_forum.md`, as scoped by adoption and custody rules.

---

## 1. Extended framing context (non-operative)

The constitutional project starts from a simple condition: sentient wellbeing is inseparable from the systems sentients build, inhabit, and depend on.

As systems become more interdependent and more powerful, risks scale in scope and consequence. Those risks include cumulative and potentially irreversible harms. They are treated as present and materially relevant, not hypothetical.

The constitutional response is an enforceable alignment framework:
- systems must remain aligned with survival, dignity, and long-horizon wellbeing
- governance obligations scale with impact, dependency, and risk
- epistemic integrity, contestability, and accountability are required where system effects are material

The governance architecture separates the **Constitutional Contract Layer** from **Stakeholder System Participation**. The first concerns authorization of governing authority itself; the second concerns participation, representation, contestability, and due process for materially affected stakeholders inside already-authorized systems or bounded domains.

This framing is explanatory. Binding legal effect remains in the constitutional text and incorporated obligations designated there.

## 3. Chapter Fourteen walkthrough (non-operative)

**Chapter Fourteen** in `core_14-14_incorporation.md` is intentionally narrow. It is the **constitutional incorporation bridge**. It states what companion files count as binding implementation text, how adoption and custody make that text operative, and that post-adoption edits do not silently change constitutional effect. It is **not** a second place to restate the full implementation corpus. The file uses three numbered sections:

### 3.1 Role (Chapter Fourteen, section 1 in `core_14-14_incorporation.md`)

States **what** is incorporated (`corpus_joint_structure.md`, `corpus_systems.md`, `corpus_institutions.md`, `corpus_forum.md`, `corpus_joint_structure.md` within adoption scope), **how** adoption makes it operative, and **that** companion-file edits do not silently change effect. Optional merger of companions into the core file is packaging only while **Corpus** designations remain (see **Chapter Five** in `core_05-05_definitions_a_independent.md` and sibling Part B/C files).

### 3.2 Authority stack and invocation (Chapter Fourteen, section 2)

Summarizes binding constitutional source → binding incorporated source → non-binding process/map support unless adopted. Read this source-layer order with the **Authority Stack and Internal Hierarchy** cluster in Chapter Five: the stack identifies which layer is speaking, and internal hierarchy resolves genuine residual conflict after integrated reading. Sentient Constitution references to **Chapter Fourteen** or the incorporated companion files invoke corresponding implementation obligations **where applicable** and within adoption scope. Conflict order applies Sentient Constitution supremacy, **Chapter Eleven** non-regression constraints, and **Chapter Twelve** internal hierarchy rules first; edition/custody ambiguity falls back to the stricter clearly adopted baseline until **Chapters Eleven and Thirteen** restore validity.

### 3.3 Custody, editions, and operative effect (Chapter Fourteen, section 3)

Edition identifiers and effective dates must be pinned in the adoption chain. Adopters maintain an auditable custody chain. Post-adoption edits to companion files are non-operative unless readopted under **Chapter Thirteen**. Architecture, TODO, regression, and evidence artifacts may support verification but do not alter constitutional effect on their own.

## 5. Chapters Eleven through Thirteen walkthrough (non-operative)

**Scope note:** **Chapter Nine** states foundational **rights** (Articles **I–XXV**; **Article XXV** houses transition and re-baselining). Those articles appear across **`core_09-09_rights_part_a.md` through `core_09-09_rights_part_d.md`** in a **planet-first presentation order** grouped as **Parts A–D**; see **section 6** of this primer (and the opening of **Chapter Nine** in `core_09-09_rights_part_a.md`). **Chapters Eleven through Thirteen** are about **non-regression**, **supremacy and external legal orders**, and **amendment and procedural validity**—do not conflate with the rights chapter.

**Chapter Eleven** is the substantive **non-regression** home: the non-regression floor, **Test 1** (substantive non-regression invalidity), anti-evasion, regressive-deception **referral** triggers, and **scope** limits. It is **not** the home for operational adjudication or canonical final **Violation Axis s = 7, 8, or 9** slot labels for anti-constitutional misconduct (**Chapter Seven**), nor for the general compliance model (**Chapter Six**).

**Chapter Twelve** covers **additive expansion** of protection, **continuity** of supremacy and challenge rights during amendment, **supremacy relative to other binding norms** (the Chapter Five Authority Stack/Internal Hierarchy cluster, stricter external law, conflict disclosure), and **disputes involving external legal orders**.

**Chapter Thirteen** covers **ratification and adoption** instruments, **Tests 2–4** (procedural, custody, contestability), review triggers, provisional effect, **invalid-change handling**, and **amendment procedure requirements**.

Read the cluster as connected checks:

1. **Non-regression floor (Ch 11):** amendments cannot materially weaken foundational constraints, integrity requirements, or **Chapter Nine** rights floors.
2. **Procedural and custody validity (Ch 13):** amendment attempts fail if publication, authority-chain/custody, or contestability/review requirements are not met.
3. **Supremacy and external interface (Ch 12):** internal hierarchy and stricter external protections apply subject to **Chapter Eleven**.
4. **Review triggers and provisional effect (Ch 13):** rights-adjacent and authority-adjacent changes trigger heightened scrutiny; irreversible effect is gated.
5. **Invalid-change continuity (Ch 13):** invalid changes are non-operative for constitutional supremacy and must trigger restoration, remediation publication, evidence preservation, and contestable remedy pathways.
6. **Layer boundary discipline:** **Chapters Eleven and Thirteen** may impose immediate validity-protection and referral controls but do **not** replace **Chapter Six** (Axes I and II) or **Chapter Seven** (final **Violation Axis s = 7, 8, or 9** slot labels) as canonical classification authority for **Axis I / II** contribution inputs and top-end anti-constitutional misconduct.

Practical interpretation shortcut:
- if a proposed change narrows rights or accountability through relabeling, metadata edits, procedural shortcuts, or emergency framing, apply **Chapter Eleven** anti-evasion invalidity logic first
- if validity is uncertain, preserve prior protections and custody evidence while review completes
- do not infer final offense classification from **Chapters Eleven–Thirteen** tier or validity language alone; route final **s = 7, 8, or 9** slot labeling through **Chapter Seven** and general **Chapter Six** classification (contribution, violation, standing)
