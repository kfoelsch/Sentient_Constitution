### 1. Purpose, Role, and Scope

Subcore 27-E is the constitutional owner of the **published rules for role holders** (who qualifies for a role, who is disqualified, who succeeds, when holders rotate, how they are removed, and what they may do after leaving) and of the **composition controls for bodies that interpret the Constitution**. Staffing depth, periodic review, each institution's role map, the seat catalog, and practice licences stay in [CI-27.205](corpus_institutions/ci_27.205_appointment_competency_rotation_removal.md#ci-27205-appointment-competency-rotation-and-removal) (*Appointment, competency, rotation, and removal*).

*In plain terms: every institution, everywhere, has to say in public who can hold its important jobs and how they can be removed, and the bodies that interpret the Constitution must not be stacked by one group.*

### 2. Why These Rules Are the Same for Every Adopter

*In plain terms: if an adopter could keep its qualifications secret or let one bloc fill an interpretive body, it could quietly take over the bodies meant to check it.*

Who holds a role decides how power is used. If role criteria, removal pathways, and post-service limits were optional, an institution could appoint loyalists, remove critics without due process, and reward decision-makers afterward with jobs from the parties they favored. A body that interprets the Constitution is the most valuable to capture of all. Rules that every adopter shares keep these routes closed everywhere.

### 3. Role Criteria, Appointment Standards, and Removal Pathways

*In plain terms: every institution must publish clear, public rules for who can hold each role — what qualifies someone, what disqualifies them, who steps in if they leave, when they must rotate off for independence, and how they can be removed with fair process. Gatekeeping cannot be arbitrary or hidden.*

This section states institutional owner duties: each institution must publish role criteria, qualification and disqualification standards, succession coverage, rotation or cooling-off rules where needed for independence, and a removal pathway with due process. Role assignment must not rely on arbitrary gatekeeping. Every role decision must apply the relevant [Chapter Seventeen §3.2 Competency Bars and Clearances](core_17.000_contribution_consequences.md#32-competency-bars-and-clearances) competency bar and clearance only after checking for a controlling general or special standing lock under [Chapter Eighteen §3.2 Prevention — General Standing Locks](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#32-prevention--general-standing-locks) and [§4.5 Special Locks](core_18.000_violation_consequences.md#45-special-locks).

**Independent Bodies.** For a decision seat in an Independent Body, the role criteria and qualification standards published under this section are the competency gate for the draw under *Appointment by sortition* in [Chapter Seven §3 Independent Bodies](core_07.000_independent_bodies.md#3-what-makes-a-body-independent). Everyone who passes the gate and clears the conflict screen enters the pool, and the seat is filled by lot, not by choice among them.

**Interim hold in the removal pathway.** The removal pathway must say that, while a removal for cause is pending, only a forum with jurisdiction may place a holder under an [Interim Hold](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interim-hold) under [Chapter Twenty-Three §4 Interim Hold on a Named Sentient's Pathway](core_23.000_forum_families_escalation_timeliness_support.md#interim-hold-on-a-named-sentients-pathway). The institution's own governing authority, appointing authority, or control line may not hold its own role holder out on its own view; it refers the facts to the forum. The first rung is step-aside from one matter. The hold ends on its own bound, creates no standing effect, and may not be used to retaliate against a reporter or a refuser of an unlawful or unconstitutional instruction.

**Post-service and prospective-role rules.** Where a role decides, reviews, audits, procures, or supports a decision on a matter in which an [interested party](subcore_03.070_band_accountability.md#interested-party) has a material stake, the institution must publish:

- a duty to disclose, as a conflict under **CI-6.210** (*Conflict integrity, anti-capture, and anti-corruption*), any discussion of a later role, employment, contract, or benefit with that party while the matter is open, with recusal until an independent seat clears it;
- a cooling-off period before the role holder may accept a role or benefit from that party in connection with the matter they handled, which is five years by default from the end of the role holder's last involvement in the matter, may be longer, and may be shorter only where material stake is low and the institution publishes its reasons;
- limits on using non-public information from the role after leaving it; and
- a record of clearances and refusals.

These rules implement the post-service conflicts in [**Article XIII-C**](core_04.010_rights_part_b.md#article-xiii-c-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-benefits) (*Conflicts of Interest, Gifts, and Benefits*). Sortition panels apply them through the sortition controls in **CJS-3.110 §6** (*integrity assurance and resilience operations*).

<a id="4-interpretive-body-composition-controls"></a>

### 4. Interpretive-Body Composition Controls

*In plain terms: bodies that interpret the constitution face extra rules. Terms must be fixed and public, members must rotate, no single appointing bloc may control back-to-back cycles, conflicts must be disclosed, and members who hide bias, abuse recusal, or carry capture risk can be challenged and removed.*

Interpretive-body composition controls (**Article XXVI** (*Constitutional Interpretation, Review, and Anti-Capture Safeguards*) — constitutional interpretation and review — interface):

- Membership terms, role pathways, and renewal limits must be fixed, transparent, and rotation-based.
- No single appointing authority, institution, or stakeholder bloc may control appointment outcomes across consecutive cycles.
- Members must disclose material conflicts and recuse where impartiality is reasonably contested.
- Members remain subject to challenge for non-disclosure, bias, capture risk, or dependency-linked influence.
- Repeated or strategic recusal abuse is non-compliant and triggers corrective/removal review.
